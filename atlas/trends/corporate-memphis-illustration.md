# Corporate Memphis Illustration

**Essence:** Flat vector people with tiny heads, long bendy limbs, non-naturalistic skin colors and minimal faces, cheerfully collaborating around oversized laptops and potted plants.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Representation without stereotype | Abstract figures avoid tying a product to one age, gender or ethnicity. | Show real people (consented photography of actual users) or go non-figurative (objects, hands, places). If illustrated, draw specific situations from the product's actual use, not generic collaboration. |
| G2 · Humanise the abstract | Payroll or cloud storage has nothing to picture; people make it warm. | Picture the outcome, not an actor: the paid invoice, the finished document, the delivered parcel, one concrete object per empty state, drawn in the product's own icon language. |
| G3 · One system, many scenes | Alegria was a kit: modular bodies and props scaled to thousands of assets. | Build an illustration kit with three fixed rules (line weight, ≤5-color palette, one projection) and document it like a component library, so scale never falls back to stock. |

## Why it reads as slop now

Buck's Alegria system for Facebook (2017) was a sincere answer to a real problem: illustration anyone could see themselves in, at platform scale. It borrowed the 1980s Memphis Group's color and shapes and a cut-paper simplicity. Stock libraries and in-house teams cloned it; by 2020 to 2021 critics labelled it "Corporate Memphis" or the "Big Tech art style" and read it as optimistic gloss over controversial companies. It has receded from flagship brands but persists in HR tools, fintech onboarding and free illustration packs. The cheap version: a purple-skinned figure with legs twice the torso length waving beside a floating chart, on an empty state reading "Nothing here yet!".

## Subvert

- **Commission a voice.** One illustrator with an identifiable hand, allowed to be specific, texture, odd perspective, real places, real objects.
- **Diagram instead of mascot.** Explain the product with precise pictograms and annotated drawings (the Isotype logic of Otto Neurath and Gerd Arntz, or `otl-aicher`'s pictograms), warmth through clarity.
- **Photograph the work.** Documentary photography of the people and objects involved builds more trust than any universal figure.

## Visual facts

- **Color:** flat and saturated, no gradients, coral #FF6F61, blue #3D5AFE, yellow #FFC93C, purple or blue skin tones, pale pink grounds.
- **Type:** paired with rounded geometric sans (Circular-like faces, Poppins, Graphik).
- **Form:** no or thin outlines, exaggerated limbs, tiny or absent facial features, blobby plants and floating shapes.
- **Composition:** figures interacting with oversized UI fragments, centered vignettes on white.

## Attitude

Technology is friendly and for everyone. Sincere at the start; as a default it erases specificity, nobody in particular doing nothing in particular.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `pill-buttons-with-arrows`, `centered-hero-three-cards-template`, `isometric-illustration`, the friendly-startup page of 2018 to 2021.
- **Productive tension with:** `otl-aicher`, universal pictogram figures with a strict information purpose instead of decoration.
- **Clashes with:** `linear-style-dark-ui`, cartoon cheer against instrument seriousness.

## Don't copy

Facebook's Alegria characters and Buck's original assets. Free packs (Humaaans, Open Peeps, unDraw) are licensed for use, but adopting them as a brand voice guarantees the generic look.
