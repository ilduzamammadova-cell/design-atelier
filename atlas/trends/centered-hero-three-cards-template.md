# Centered Hero + Three Cards Template

**Essence:** The landing-page stack everyone ships: pill badge → centered headline → subline → two buttons → product shot → "Trusted by" logo strip → three icon feature cards → testimonial → pricing → FAQ → final CTA.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Value proposition first | Say what it is and for whom before anything else. | Headline states a concrete outcome in the user's words with a noun competitors can't claim ("Close your books in 2 days, not 10"), ≤10 words; the subline names the audience. |
| G2 · Scannable benefits | A few short claims are easy to absorb. | Order benefits by the user's decision sequence, not a fixed count — 2 or 5 is fine. Each claim carries proof (screenshot, number, quote), not an icon. |
| G3 · Borrowed trust | The logo strip transfers credibility from known customers. | Replace the anonymous logo wall with one named, specific proof — a customer quote with role and metric — placed next to the claim it supports. |
| G4 · One path to action | A single repeated CTA reduces choice. | One primary action per page, phrased as the real next step ("Import your Figma file"), repeated at decision points; secondary actions as text links. |

## Why it reads as slop now

Bootstrap's early examples (2011–2013: hero/jumbotron plus three columns) taught a generation the pattern; template marketplaces, Webflow and Framer polished it; conversion folklore froze it. AI site generators reproduce the statistically most probable page — same hero, same gradient, same three-column row — and 2025–2026 commentary on vibe-coded sites and Show HN launches treats this stack as the leading tell of AI-built pages. The cheap version: a small pill badge "✨ New: AI features" over a centered H1 with one gradient word, a solid and a ghost button, a gray row of logos that may not even be customers, then three identical cards with icons in rounded squares titled "Fast", "Secure", "Scalable".

## Subvert

- **Lead with the thing itself.** Open on the product working (live demo, interactive sample, a real document) instead of a headline about it.
- **Asymmetric, editorial structure.** Left-aligned headline on a real grid, a long-form argument with pull-quotes and footnoted proof — a page you read, not a funnel you scroll.
- **Proof inline.** Drop the logo strip; put each customer's evidence beside the claim it proves, with names and numbers.

## Visual facts

- **Color:** whatever the template default is — usually white, indigo primary, gray logos at 50% opacity.
- **Type:** Inter/Geist; centered H1 56–72px, gray subline 18–20px.
- **Form:** pill badge, rounded buttons, 40px rounded-square icon tiles, 3×1 card grid.
- **Composition:** fully centered hero column, symmetric three-up features, alternating image/text rows below.

## Attitude

The page as conversion funnel: minimise thought, maximise clicks. Rational — but when every page uses the same funnel, the funnel stops persuading.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `purple-blue-gradient-saas`, `pill-buttons-with-arrows`, `ai-sparkle-iconography`, `marquee-tickers` (as the animated logo strip).
- **Productive tension with:** `editorial-web`, `new-york-school` — editorial pacing, scale shifts and spreads instead of modules.
- **Clashes with:** `kinetic-type-everywhere` — choreography fights the template's scan-and-click logic.

## Don't copy

Bootstrap's example pages; named commercial templates; real company logos you have no permission to display.

- Anthropic, "Improving frontend design through Skills" (Nov 2025) — on predictable layouts and component patterns.
