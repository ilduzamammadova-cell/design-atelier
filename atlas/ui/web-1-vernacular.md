# Web 1.0 Vernacular (GeoCities era)

**Essence:** The amateur web before professionals arrived — browser defaults, tiled wallpapers, animated GIFs and visitor counters, made by people who simply wanted to be seen.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Browser defaults as palette | Early pages accepted the user agent: serif body text, blue underlined links, purple visited links, grey or white page. | Ship with almost no reset: system serif body, links `#0000EE` underlined, visited `#551A8B`, page `#FFF` or `#C0C0C0`. Let the default *be* the style; deviate only with purpose. |
| G2 · The 88×31 badge economy | Tiny standard-size buttons ("best viewed with…", webring and friend badges) linked sites into a visible social network. | A footer wall of fixed-size 88×31 px badges linking to partners, tools and friends; each badge a 2–4 frame animation at most. A format, not a logo slot. |
| G3 · Animated punctuation | Under-construction signs, spinning envelopes, rainbow dividers and flaming bars separated sections and shouted news. | Section dividers are small looping images; "new!" markers are 2-frame flashes. Cap at one moving element per viewport so it reads as punctuation. |
| G4 · Visible visitors | Hit counters, guestbooks and webring prev/next links made the audience and neighbours part of the page. | Public visit counter, a guestbook component instead of comments, and ring navigation (← previous site · random · next site →) linking peer sites. |
| G5 · Tiled ground, table stage | Seamless background tiles (stars, marble, clouds) behind centred content laid out in bordered tables. | Body background = small repeating tile (≤64 px); content in a centred column with visible 3D table borders (`border: 2px outset`). |
| G6 · Proudly unfinished | Sites announced themselves as "under construction" — permanently in progress, updated by hand. | Show "last updated" dates and openly mark incomplete sections. A changelog page is part of the navigation. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** default link blue `#0000EE`, visited `#551A8B`, grey `#C0C0C0`; the 216-colour "web-safe" cube (steps of `#33`); neon text on black or starfields.
- **Type:** Times New Roman, Arial, Courier New, Comic Sans MS (Vincent Connare, 1994); `<font>` sizes 1–7. Free: **Tinos**, **Arimo**, **Cousine**, **Comic Neue**.
- **Form:** GIFs with jagged transparency, bevelled table borders, horizontal rules, `<blink>` (Netscape) and `<marquee>` (Internet Explorer) motion.
- **Composition:** centred stacks; frames with a left navigation column; 640×480 assumptions; everything visible at once.

## Attitude

Olia Lialina named this the "vernacular web" (2005): a folk visual language invented by users, not designers, full of enthusiasm and mutual linking. GeoCities (founded 1994, closed in the US in 2009) hosted millions of these pages. The spirit to borrow is self-publishing as joy and community — not irony about bad taste.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `punk-graphics`, `brutalist-web`, `windows-95` — DIY honesty and default materials.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a rigorous grid carrying vernacular badges, counters and GIF punctuation: order that welcomes the handmade.
- **Clashes with:** `material-design` — tokenised polish applied to vernacular parts produces a parody of both.

## Don't copy

- Individual GeoCities pages and the specific GIFs preserved in archives (each had an author); the Netscape and Internet Explorer logos; famous viral animations. Draw new badges and dividers in the spirit, not lifted files.
