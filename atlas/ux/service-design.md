# Service Design

**Essence:** The experience is the whole service over time, every channel, every handover, and the backstage organisation that makes the visible moments possible, so design the journey and the machinery together.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Line of visibility | Lynn Shostack's service blueprint (*HBR*, 1984) separates what customers see from the processes behind it | Surface backstage status when it matters, honestly: "Your claim is with the assessment team, usually 2 working days", with the stage shown on a simple tracker |
| G2 · One journey across channels | People move between app, web, email, phone and counter; the service must remember them | Shared state everywhere: drafts resumable on any device; support agents see the same screen and history as the customer; nobody re-enters information already given |
| G3 · Design the fail points | Blueprints mark where services typically break; those are the moments that define trust | Failure states designed first-class: rejected payment, delayed delivery, missing document, each with a clear next step, a time expectation and a route to a human |
| G4 · Name services by verbs | Government Digital Service practice: services named for what users need to do, in plain language | Entry points titled as tasks ("Renew your passport", "Report a lost card"); one thing per page; reading age around 9 to 12 for public services |
| G5 · Every touchpoint is the product | Emails, letters, SMS and receipts are part of the interface | Transactional emails use the same tokens, voice and primary button as the app; every message ends with the single next action and a reference number |
| G6 · Staff are users too | Frontline and back-office people deliver the service; their tools shape the customer's experience | Internal tools use the same design system and receive usability testing; staff screens show the customer's view alongside their own |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** public-service plainness, black on white, strong blue links, a single status colour set (e.g. GOV.UK's approach); colour signals state, not brand mood.
- **Type:** large, highly legible sans (GOV.UK uses its own restricted face; free analogues: Public Sans, Atkinson Hyperlegible).
- **Form:** status trackers, step lists, reference numbers, journey maps and blueprints as working artefacts.
- **Composition:** one task per page, linear and calm; consistent across channels.

## Attitude

Shostack, a banker, argued in 1984 that services could be designed as rigorously as products. Birgit Mager at the Köln International School of Design and consultancies like live|work (London, 2001) shaped service design into a discipline; the UK Government Digital Service turned it into public policy with its design principles (2012) and service standard. The belief: services are co-produced with people over time, so good design must reach into operations, policy and staff, not just screens.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `jobs-to-be-done`, `ideo-design-thinking`, `calm-technology`, needs-led, iterative, low-friction.
- **Productive tension with:** `atomic-design`, component thinking against journey thinking; the hybrid adds journey-level patterns (status trackers, handovers) to the design system.
- **Clashes with:** `postmodern-new-wave`, reinventing the visual language per touchpoint breaks recognisability across channels.

## Don't copy

The GOV.UK crown and branding, its restricted typeface, and Shostack's original shoeshine blueprint drawing as published. Draw blueprints of your own service.
