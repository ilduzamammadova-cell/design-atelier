# Persuasive Technology and Behaviour Design

**Essence:** Behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt meet at the same moment, so make the desired action tiny, prompt it at the right time, and celebrate it; legitimate only when the goal is the person's own and leaving is as easy as joining.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Make it tiny (ability first) | Fogg Behavior Model (2009): B = MAP. Raising ability is more reliable than pumping motivation | The first action takes under 30 seconds and one tap ("Do 1 exercise", not "Start your plan"); every step that can be removed is removed before any motivational copy is written |
| G2 · Prompt when they can act | A prompt only works when motivation and ability are already above the action line | Reminders are scheduled by the user and anchored to their routine ("after my morning coffee"); no prompts at times the user marked as off; one prompt per day maximum by default |
| G3 · Celebrate right after | An immediate positive feeling wires the habit ("Shine" in *Tiny Habits*) | A 300 to 600ms success animation plus haptic right at completion, sized to the effort (small tick for small acts, bigger moment for milestones); never celebrate trivia |
| G4 · Self-monitoring the user owns | Showing people their own data supports change; Fogg lists reduction, tunnelling, tailoring, self-monitoring among persuasive tools | Personal trend graphs and streaks visible only to the user by default; export available; comparisons are with one's past self, not leaderboards, unless opted in |
| G5 · The ethics gate | Persuasion is defensible only toward goals the person chose, transparently, with easy exit | User sets the goal in their own words; streaks can be paused without penalty ("rest day"); no variable-ratio rewards tied to spending; turning off every nudge takes one screen |
| G6 · Natural stopping points | Endless feeds and autoplay remove the moment to decide to stop | Sessions end with a summary and a clear "done for today"; autoplay off by default; feeds paginate or end; weekly usage report offered |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** bright, optimistic saturation, fresh green ~#58CC02-like or coral, on white; progress in a single strong hue; celebratory confetti in 3 to 4 colours.
- **Type:** rounded, friendly sans → Nunito, Baloo 2, DM Sans; big numbers for streaks and counts.
- **Form:** progress rings and bars, checkmarks, mascots and badges; bouncy, springy motion.
- **Composition:** one action per screen, the button large and central, the reward appearing where the action happened.

## Attitude

BJ Fogg founded the Stanford Persuasive Technology Lab (now the Behavior Design Lab) and coined "captology" for computers as persuasive technologies; *Tiny Habits* (2019) reframes the model as helping people do what they already want to do. The same toolkit fed engagement-maximising social products, provoking a critique led by former industry insiders and the Center for Humane Technology (co-founded by Aza Raskin, who is widely credited with designing infinite scroll and has publicly regretted it). This card keeps only the self-directed, transparent half.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `microinteractions`, `kano-model`, crafted feedback and earned delight.
- **Productive tension with:** `calm-technology`, motivation through quiet periphery: gentle ambient progress instead of push notifications.
- **Clashes with:** `dark-patterns`, the same levers used against the user's interest; the ethics gate (G5) is the boundary.

## Don't copy

Specific mascots (e.g. Duolingo's owl), Apple's activity rings, Snapchat's streak flame and any branded celebration animation. Invent your own reward language.
