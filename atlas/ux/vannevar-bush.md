# Vannevar Bush (1890 to 1974)

**Essence:** Memory should work the way the mind does, by association, so the tool's real product is the path a person blazes through what they have read.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Associative trails | In "As We May Think" (*The Atlantic*, July 1945) the memex lets a reader chain any items into a named trail and replay it later, with side trails branching off. | Users string notes, pages and search hits into a named, ordered trail with next/previous stepping. An item can sit on many trails; its page lists every trail that passes through it (backlinks with order, not just a bag of links). |
| G2 · Tie two things together | Two items are shown side by side on the desk's screens and joined with a single action; the link is the unit of thought. | Default reading posture is two panes: source left, note or second source right. One "tie" command (button and shortcut) links whatever is in the two panes, asking at most for a short label. |
| G3 · Trails are gifts | A trail, with its annotations, can be copied and handed to a colleague to insert into their own machine; Bush imagined a profession of "trail blazers". | Share a trail as one URL that preserves order, annotations and authorship; the recipient can fork and extend it. Show "trail by …" credit on every forked copy; popular trails become browsable guides. |
| G4 · Against the filing cabinet | He criticised alphabetical and hierarchical indexing as artificial, the mind does not work by classification. | No mandatory folders at capture; items are found by links, full-text search and recency. A "folder" is only a saved query or a trail, so one item can live in many places without copies. |
| G5 · Capture without breaking thought | The essay pictures recording on the spot, a small forehead-worn camera, dictation, notes photographed straight onto film. | Global one-key capture that saves the selection with source URL, time and context and returns focus within a second. Filing, tagging and linking happen later, never at the moment of capture. |
| G6 · Annotation in the margin | The memex user writes comments beside an item and those comments travel with the trail. | Annotations anchor to a passage and live in a right-hand margin column (≈ 240 to 280px), visible while reading, exported with the trail; never in a modal or a separate notes page. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** the memex was imagined as office furniture: walnut ≈ #5A3E2B, translucent screen glow ≈ #EDE6D3, microfilm amber ≈ #D8A34A, steel grey ≈ #8C8F91.
- **Type:** none designed → for trail-and-note tools: Source Serif 4 for reading, Courier Prime for captured fragments.
- **Form:** a desk with slanted translucent screens, keys and levers, a platen for photographing new material.
- **Composition:** side-by-side reading surfaces on a single desk; the archive hidden inside the furniture.

## Attitude

Bush, an MIT engineer who built the differential analyzer and directed the wartime Office of Scientific Research and Development, feared that knowledge was growing faster than anyone could use it. His answer was not more storage but better paths: a personal machine that extends memory and lets scholars hand on how they found things, not just what they found.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `douglas-engelbart`, `richard-saul-wurman`, augmenting intellect, and organising information around how people actually look for it.
- **Productive tension with:** `edward-tufte`, Bush's sprawling associative webs disciplined by Tufte's density and clarity give knowledge maps that are rich but readable.
- **Clashes with:** `persuasive-technology`, engagement loops pull attention to the feed; Bush's tool exists to protect the reader's own line of thought.

## Don't copy

The 1945 *Life* magazine illustrations of the memex desk and camera as product imagery. Take the trail, the tie and the margin, not the furniture.
