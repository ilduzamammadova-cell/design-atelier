# Calm Technology

**Essence:** Technology should inform without demanding attention, living in the periphery, moving to the centre only when it matters, and settling back so that people stay in their own lives.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Centre ↔ periphery | Weiser and Brown (1995): calm tech moves easily between the edge of attention and its centre, and back | Three escalation tiers: ambient (a colour dot, a thin status line) → badge → interruption. Each event type is assigned a tier; only safety, money or time-critical events may interrupt |
| G2 · Smallest possible attention | Amber Case's first principle: require as little attention as the task allows | All non-critical notifications off by default; daily digest instead of real-time pings; no badges for marketing; one-glance status screens with no required interaction |
| G3 · Speak without speaking | Communicate through light, tone, touch and position rather than words or voice | Status as a single hue or soft glow; a haptic tick on completion; a short tone only for finished long tasks; text appears on demand, not by default |
| G4 · Work even when it fails | Graceful degradation: failure should drop to a usable state, not a broken one | Offline mode shows last known data with a timestamp and queues actions; if the smart layer fails, the manual control still works; never a blank error screen |
| G5 · Minimum technology | The right amount of technology is the minimum needed to solve the problem | Replace a screen with an indicator light, a setting with a sensible default, a feature with nothing; every release must remove something as well as add |
| G6 · Respect social norms | Technology enters shared spaces and must behave politely there | Silent by default in shared contexts; camera, microphone and location indicators always visible while sensing; no surprise voice output in public |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** paper and stone neutrals, warm paper #F3EFE7, graphite #2E2E2C, with one soft status hue (sage ~#8FA88A or amber ~#D9A441); low contrast between surfaces.
- **Type:** humanist text at medium weights → Source Serif 4, IBM Plex Sans; no shouty all-caps alerts.
- **Form:** e-ink, fabric, wood and matte surfaces; glows and fades rather than pop-ups; motion slow (400 to 800ms) and rare.
- **Composition:** large quiet fields with information at edges and corners; one thing visible at a time.

## Attitude

Mark Weiser, who coined "ubiquitous computing" at Xerox PARC, saw the goal of computing everywhere as *less* attention, not more screens; his 1995 paper with John Seely Brown used Natalie Jeremijenko's "Dangling String", which twitched with network traffic, as the model of information felt in the periphery. Amber Case codified eight principles in *Calm Technology* (2015) and founded the Calm Tech Institute in 2024 to certify products against them. The belief: attention is a finite human resource and technology must earn every claim on it.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `kenya-hara`, `kenya-hara`, `jef-raskin`, `dieter-rams`, emptiness, reduction and protected attention.
- **Productive tension with:** `microinteractions`, expressive feedback forced into whisper-level signals gives precise, tiny, satisfying cues.
- **Clashes with:** `persuasive-technology`, engagement loops and calm periphery are opposite theories of attention.

## Don't copy

Jeremijenko's "Dangling String" installation, specific ambient products' forms (e.g. the Ambient Orb), and the Calm Tech Certified mark, which is a registered trademark. Take the periphery principle.
