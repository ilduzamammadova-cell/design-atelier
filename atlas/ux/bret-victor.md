# Bret Victor

**Essence:** Creators need an immediate connection to what they create — every change visible as it is made, every hidden state shown, every abstraction reachable from a concrete example.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Immediate connection | *Inventing on Principle* (2012): any gap between making a change and seeing its effect kills ideas | Every parameter edit re-renders in under 100ms; source and result side by side, linked both ways (click result → highlight source); no Run or Apply button in creative tools |
| G2 · Scrubbable values | Explorable explanations (2011): numbers in a text can be dragged, and the argument updates live | Numeric values in prose, tables and inspectors are drag-to-scrub (horizontal drag changes value, Shift for fine steps); manipulable values marked with a distinct colour and dotted underline |
| G3 · Ladder of abstraction | *Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction* (2011): move from one concrete case, to a family of cases, to the general pattern | Show one instance → small multiples across one parameter → aggregate plot, each a click apart; a time slider can collapse into a trace of all moments at once |
| G4 · Information software shouldn't need interaction | *Magic Ink*: most software is for learning answers; infer context and show the likely answer first | Open on the most probable answer for the user's context (next departure from where they stand, today's relevant items); interaction exists to refine, not to begin |
| G5 · Show the invisible | *Learnable Programming* (2012): make data, flow and time visible instead of making people simulate them in their heads | Hidden state rendered in place: variable values beside code, history as onion-skin or timeline, data flow as visible links; never require mental simulation |
| G6 · Invent on principle | Choose one principle you believe is right and let it judge every decision | Write the product's single governing principle in one sentence; every feature review asks whether it serves or violates it; violations are removed, not compromised |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** light, quiet grounds with a single bright highlight reserved for what can be manipulated; diagrams in restrained 2–3 colour palettes.
- **Type:** book-like essay typography with interactive figures inline → Source Serif 4 for text, IBM Plex Sans for labels, a mono for code.
- **Form:** precise vector diagrams beside hand-drawn sketches; at Dynamicland, paper, coloured dots, projection and physical objects.
- **Composition:** long-form essays where text and live models interleave; editor and output in adjacent panes.

## Attitude

After prototyping future interfaces at Apple (2007–2010), Victor turned to independent research and then founded Dynamicland (from 2014), building Realtalk, a computing environment in physical rooms where paper and bodies are the interface. His "brief rant" (2011) objected to "pictures under glass" for ignoring human hands. The belief, inherited from Kay and Engelbart: the point of computing is to extend human thought, so the medium must make reasoning visible and tangible.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `alan-kay`, `edward-tufte`, `ben-shneiderman` — live, explorable, evidence-rich media.
- **Productive tension with:** `conversational-and-ai-ux` — opaque generation against visible models; push AI output to arrive as manipulable, explorable structures rather than final prose.
- **Clashes with:** `persuasive-technology` — tools for understanding and tools for nudging want opposite things from the user's attention.

## Don't copy

His essay illustrations and interactive figures, the platformer game and demos from *Inventing on Principle*, and Dynamicland's visual language of dots and paper programs. Take immediacy and visibility.
