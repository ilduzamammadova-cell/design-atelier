# IDEO and Design Thinking

**Essence:** Learn by making: go to the people, build rough things fast, show many options before choosing, and keep work visibly unfinished so others feel invited to improve it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Build to think | Rough prototypes are thinking tools, not deliverables; fidelity rises only as confidence rises | Draft states look deliberately draft: sketchy or wireframe rendering for unconfirmed content, "Draft" chip, comments open by default; polish appears only at publish |
| G2 · Diverge before converging | Generate many options, then narrow (later formalised by the Design Council's Double Diamond) | Creation flows show 3 to 4 variants side by side before asking for a choice; a "more like this" action on each; the chosen variant keeps its siblings in history |
| G3 · Extreme users first | Fieldwork with people at the edges (experts, novices, people with constraints) reveals needs the average hides | Test and design each core flow with a first-timer and a power user present; defaults must survive both; edge cases get named personas, not footnotes |
| G4 · How might we… | Reframing a problem as an optimistic, open question widens the solution space | Empty states and prompts phrased as invitations ("What do you want to try?") with 3 example starts, instead of blank fields or instructions |
| G5 · Defer judgement | Brainstorm rules: build on others' ideas, go for quantity, judge later | Collaborative spaces where nothing is deleted outright, ideas are archived, recoverable, and comments start with "build on" before "critique" options |
| G6 · Three lenses | Desirability, feasibility, viability must all hold for an idea to survive | Every feature card in the product backlog carries three visible checks; a prototype is shown to users before it is costed, and costed before it is polished |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** sticky-note saturation, canary yellow ~#FFE66D, pink ~#FF8FB1, orange ~#FFB347, on white walls; marker black.
- **Type:** handwriting in fat marker on notes → Permanent Marker or Caveat for draft states, paired with a clean grotesque (Work Sans, Public Sans) for finished states.
- **Form:** foam-core, tape, paper prototypes, photos of real people in context.
- **Composition:** affinity walls, clusters of notes grouped by theme; storyboards in frames.

## Attitude

IDEO formed in 1991 from the merger of David Kelley's studio with Bill Moggridge's ID Two and Mike Nuttall's Matrix; Moggridge, designer of the GRiD Compass laptop, is credited with coining "interaction design" with Bill Verplank. Tim Brown's 2008 *Harvard Business Review* article spread "design thinking" into management, and the Stanford d.school (2005) taught it widely. The belief: everyone can be creative, and empathy plus iteration beats genius. Critics (e.g. Natasha Jen's 2017 talk) warn that it can turn into sticky-note theatre without craft or evidence.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `jobs-to-be-done`, `service-design`, `kano-model`, needs-first, outcome-oriented innovation.
- **Productive tension with:** `edward-tufte`, evidence-dense rigour checks optimistic ideation; the mix gives playful process with honest proof.
- **Clashes with:** `ulm-school`, Ulm's scientific, systematic method distrusts sticky-note ideation; the hybrid often yields process theatre with neither rigour nor play.

## Don't copy

The IDEO wordmark, the Stanford d.school hexagon process diagram, the Design Council Double Diamond graphic as drawn, and famous IDEO artifacts (Apple's early mouse, the *Nightline* shopping cart). Draw your own process.
