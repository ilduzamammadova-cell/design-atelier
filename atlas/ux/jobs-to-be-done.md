# Jobs to Be Done

**Essence:** People don't buy products, they hire them to make progress in a specific circumstance — so design around the struggling moment and the progress sought, not around features or demographics.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Progress, not product | The unit of value is the progress the person makes, not the feature they use | The home screen shows the user's progress toward their job ("3 of 5 invoices paid · €2,400 outstanding"), not a menu of modules; empty states describe the outcome, not the feature |
| G2 · Circumstance over demographic | The same person hires different solutions in different situations; the situation predicts behaviour better than age or role | Entry points tuned to moments: the phone view answers the "quick check on the go" job in one glance; desktop serves "sit down and sort it out"; notifications fire on situations, not schedules |
| G3 · Job stories | When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome] — replaces "As a [role]…" | Each screen spec opens with one job story; acceptance criteria test the outcome ("user can tell in 5s whether they will be paid this week") |
| G4 · Your rival is the workaround | Competition includes spreadsheets, paper, phoning a friend, and doing nothing | First-run imports from the workaround (paste from spreadsheet, CSV, photo of paper); early screens mimic the familiar structure before introducing new concepts |
| G5 · The four forces | Switching happens when push (pain) + pull (new promise) outweigh anxiety (of the new) + habit (of the old) | At the moment of commitment, reduce anxiety: "try with your own data", free import, one-click export, visible undo of the trial; never add new friction at the switch point |
| G6 · Three dimensions of a job | Jobs have functional, emotional and social sides — how I want to feel and be seen | Output that makes the user look good (polished shareable reports, professional invoice templates); copy that acknowledges the emotional stake ("No more chasing payments") |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** none intrinsic; JTBD products tend toward clear, low-chroma interfaces with one accent marking progress.
- **Type:** plain-language labels; outcome numbers set large (tabular figures) as the visual centre.
- **Form:** progress meters, checklists, before/after comparisons.
- **Composition:** the user's outcome at the top; tools below it, ordered by the steps of the job.

## Attitude

Clayton Christensen popularised the theory in *The Innovator's Solution* (2003) — the milkshake study with Bob Moesta is its parable — and expanded it in *Competing Against Luck* (2016). Tony Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation developed a quantitative branch; Moesta and Alan Klement a switch-interview and progress-making branch. The shared belief: understand causation (why people switch) rather than correlation (who they are).

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `alan-cooper`, `kano-model`, `service-design` — goals, satisfaction drivers and whole journeys.
- **Productive tension with:** `alan-kay` — one focused job against an open-ended medium; the mix gives a focused entry that opens into authoring once the job is done.
- **Clashes with:** `persuasive-technology` used for engagement — JTBD measures time-to-done, engagement design measures time-in-app.

## Don't copy

Strategyn's trademarked ODI process materials and the milkshake anecdote as a presentation crutch. Interview your own users about their last switch.
