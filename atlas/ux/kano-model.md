# Kano Model

**Essence:** Satisfaction is not linear — basics earn no praise but their absence enrages, performance pays in proportion, and delight comes from small unexpected touches that slowly harden into expectations.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Must-be quietly | Must-be attributes go unnoticed when present and cause fury when missing | Never celebrate basics: autosave shows a small inline "Saved" in muted text, not a toast; security and reliability get engineering, not marketing copy, inside the product |
| G2 · Performance on a visible dial | One-dimensional attributes (speed, storage, battery) satisfy in proportion to how much you get | Expose them as measurable quantities — load time, storage meter, battery estimate — and improve them steadily; pricing tiers differ on these, not on basics |
| G3 · Delight unannounced | Attractive qualities are unexpected; their absence disappoints nobody | One small, discovered delight per core flow (a smart prefill, a thoughtful empty state, a well-timed shortcut hint); never gated, never explained in a tutorial |
| G4 · Delight decays | Kano's life-cycle work: today's attractive feature becomes tomorrow's expectation | Review delighters yearly; once users expect one, make it robust and quiet (move it to G1 treatment); invent the next one rather than inflating the old |
| G5 · Indifferent is clutter | Attributes users don't care about either way still cost attention | Kano survey (paired "if present / if absent" questions) on candidate features; indifferent ones are removed or moved deep into settings |
| G6 · Some people hate it | Reverse attributes satisfy some users and annoy others (sounds, gamification, social sharing) | Polarising features are opt-in toggles, default off, offered once in context; never forced on everyone because a segment loves them |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** none intrinsic; the visible consequence is restraint on basics and a few precise moments of colour or motion for delighters.
- **Type:** quiet confirmations in secondary text styles; bold reserved for performance figures the user cares about.
- **Form:** meters for performance attributes, small surprise details for delight.
- **Composition:** basics invisible but solid; performance visible; delight placed at the end or at a moment of effort.

## Attitude

Noriaki Kano and colleagues (1984) adapted Herzberg's two-factor theory of motivation to product quality, showing that "good" has several shapes. The belief: not every improvement pays equally, and quality strategy means knowing which kind of quality you are building — while remembering that categories drift as expectations rise.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `jobs-to-be-done`, `microinteractions`, `ideo-design-thinking` — needs-led priorities and crafted delight.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams` — "less, but better" against delight; the hybrid finds delight in perfected basics rather than in extras.
- **Clashes with:** `dark-patterns` — fake delighters (guilt-driven streaks, confetti over missing basics) mask failed must-bes.

## Don't copy

Consultancies' branded Kano diagrams and survey templates. Draw your own curves from your own survey data.
