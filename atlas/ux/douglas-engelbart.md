# Douglas Engelbart

**Essence:** The computer as an instrument for augmenting human intellect, a tool worth learning, like a violin, that raises what skilled people and teams can think and do together.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Augment, don't dumb down | Engelbart refused to trade power for first-minute ease; the measure was what an expert could achieve after practice | Keyboard-first command grammar (verb + object) learnable in an hour, deepening for years; every action has a shortcut shown beside it; no feature removed merely to look simple |
| G2 · Structure is a view | NLS stored text as a hierarchy and let users change "view specs", show only top levels, first lines, or everything | Any long document or list has an outline-level control (1 · 2 · 3 · all) and "first line only" mode; collapse/expand on every heading, keyboard-driven |
| G3 · Everything addressable | Every statement in NLS had an address that could be linked and jumped to | Each block, paragraph and comment has a stable ID and copyable deep link; links can point at a block, not just a page; backlinks listed under the target |
| G4 · Two hands, two channels | Mouse in one hand for pointing, five-key chord keyset in the other for commands, selection and action in parallel | Pointer selects, keyboard acts: modifier-click, hold-key tools and a command palette that works on the current selection without moving the pointer |
| G5 · The screen is a shared room | The 1968 demo showed two people editing the same file live, with linked video | Real-time co-presence as default: named live cursors, visible selections, follow-mode, comments anchored to blocks |
| G6 · Bootstrap | His lab used its own tools to build those tools, so every flaw was felt daily | The product's help, changelog and roadmap live inside the product as ordinary documents; the team runs its own work in it |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** monochrome, bright vector text on a dark CRT. A faithful modern echo: near-black #101410 background, pale green-white #D8F0D8 text, no second hue.
- **Type:** monospaced terminal text → IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono.
- **Form:** no icons, no chrome; text, cursor, and the occasional split frame.
- **Composition:** dense hierarchical text, split windows, everything driven by structure rather than decoration.

## Attitude

Engelbart framed computing as a response to humanity's growing, urgent, complex problems: if collective intelligence could be raised, everything else would follow. He accepted a learning curve as the price of power and treated the human, the tool, the language and the method as one co-evolving system. Much of the industry later adopted his mouse and windows while discarding his insistence on expert depth.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `alan-kay`, `bret-victor`, `ben-shneiderman`, the augmentation lineage of tools for thought.
- **Productive tension with:** `steve-krug`, a self-evident surface for newcomers layered over Engelbart's expert grammar yields tools that are both approachable and deep.
- **Clashes with:** `persuasive-technology`, optimising for engagement contradicts optimising for capability; the hybrid becomes a power tool that nags.

## Don't copy

The 1968 demo footage and stills, the original wooden SRI mouse form, and the chord keyset as a decorative motif. Take the principles of structure, addressability and expert depth.
