# Kenya Hara

**Essence:** Emptiness as a vessel, design that offers a receptive space for people to fill, rather than a message to receive.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Emptiness, not simplicity | Hara distinguishes Western "simple" (reduction toward function) from Japanese emptiness: a vessel whose value is its capacity to receive meaning. Muji (founded 1980 by Ikko Tanaka and others as "no-brand quality goods") is his main case; he has been its art director since the early 2000s. | **Product:** design for what users will bring, unlabelled surfaces, neutral packaging that takes their use. **UI:** blank-canvas starts with almost no prompts; content areas at least 70% of the viewport; generous empty margins (min 64px desktop) treated as part of the product, not leftover. |
| G2 · "This will do" over "this is what I want" | Muji's stance offers a reasonable satisfaction rather than intense desire, products that suffice without seducing. | **Product:** aim for sufficiency; fewer SKUs, no hero features. **UI:** no persuasive patterns (no countdowns, badges, "only 2 left"); defaults chosen to be good enough for most; settings exist but are quiet. |
| G3 · White as an active material | In *White* (2009/2010) Hara treats white not as a colour but as a state of possibility, paper, salt, rice, snow. | **Product:** natural off-whites and unbleached materials. **UI:** a ladder of 4 to 5 warm and cool whites (#FFFFFF, #FAF8F3, #F2EFE8, #E8E4DA) used to separate regions instead of lines or shadows; type in soft black (#2A2826). |
| G4 · Haptic design | The *HAPTIC* exhibition (2004) explored how materials and textures awaken the senses, e.g. packaging that feels like the product inside. | **Product:** texture and touch as primary information. **UI:** carry sensation through micro-feedback: subtle haptics on toggles, paper-grain texture at 2 to 3% opacity on reading surfaces, press states that feel soft (scale 0.99, 80 ms). |
| G5 · Re-design the everyday | *Re-Design: Daily Products of the 21st Century* (2000) asked designers to rethink toilet paper, matches, tea bags, the overlooked ordinary. | **Product:** redesign the commodities nobody notices. **UI:** spend design effort on the most mundane moments, loading, empty results, receipts, date pickers, error copy, rather than on hero pages. |
| G6 · Horizon as identity | Muji's horizon campaign (2003), photographed at a salt flat and other vast landscapes, framed the brand as an empty horizon line. | **Product:** an identity that recedes to a single line. **UI:** a brand expressed by one horizontal rule and a word, no logo lockup in-product; the brand is the calm. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** whites and off-whites (#FFFFFF, #F7F4EE), kraft brown (#B49A7A), Muji maroon (#7F0019) used sparingly, soft black (#2A2826), natural linen (#E3DDD0).
- **Type:** quiet Japanese gothic and Mincho, small sizes, wide spacing. Free equivalents: Noto Sans JP (Light/Regular), Zen Kaku Gothic New, Shippori Mincho; Latin: Karla or Figtree at small sizes.
- **Form:** unbleached paper, clear PP, aluminium, plain cylinders and boxes, unprinted surfaces.
- **Composition:** small type, vast margins, horizon lines, centred stillness.

## Attitude

Hara sees design as the act of awakening people to what they already know about everyday life, making the familiar unknown again. He believes emptiness gives people freedom and that a brand can be a vessel rather than a voice.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `naoto-fukasawa`, `jasper-morrison`, `isamu-noguchi`.
- **Productive tension with:** `ulm-school`, Ulm's systematic grid holding Hara's emptiness yields structured calm.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`, `verner-panton`, saturation and spectacle fill the vessel before the user arrives.

## Don't copy

The Muji logotype and maroon label system, specific Muji horizon campaign photographs, his Nagano 1998 Olympic programmes, Muji product forms. Take emptiness as capacity and the white ladder.
