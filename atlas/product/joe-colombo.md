# Joe Colombo

**Essence:** The home as a compact machine for living, modular, mobile units that pack an entire life into reconfigurable blocks.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Total furnishing unit | Colombo replaced furniture with self-contained living blocks: the Rotoliving and Cabriolet-Bed (1969), the Visiona habitat for Bayer (Cologne, 1969) and the Total Furnishing Unit shown at MoMA's *Italy: The New Domestic Landscape* (1972, after his death). Each block holds a whole activity, kitchen, sleep, bath, storage. | **Product:** bundle a full activity into one movable unit with everything inside. **UI:** self-contained "activity modules", a compose module, a review module, each carrying its own tools, state and history, that can be docked, floated or stacked; no global toolbar needed. |
| G2 · Mobile storage on wheels | The Boby trolley (Bieffeplast, 1969 to 70) is an injection-moulded tower of swivelling trays and drawers on castors, rolling to wherever the work is. | **Product:** storage that moves to the task, with swivel-out compartments. **UI:** a floating, draggable tool tray that follows the user's focus area; trays swivel open to reveal nested tools (radial or fan-out menus); position remembered per workspace. |
| G3 · One chair, many heights | The Universale chair (Kartell, 1965, model 4860) is an injection-moulded plastic chair whose legs can be swapped for different lengths, dining, bar or child height. | **Product:** a single moulded body with interchangeable parts for different contexts. **UI:** one component with swappable "legs", size variants (compact/regular/comfortable density) that change spacing and control height but not the body; one prop switches the whole app density. |
| G4 · Nesting and coupling | The Tube chair (Flexform, 1969 to 70) is made of foam-covered tubes that nest inside each other for transport and join with clips into different seat configurations. | **Product:** parts nest for storage and couple in several configurations. **UI:** nested containers that collapse into each other (an expanded panel folds into a chip, chips stack into a single badge) and can be recombined, progressive collapse driven by available width. |
| G5 · Space-age gloss and rounded mass | Glossy ABS, white plus one strong colour, large radii and soft-cornered volumes, an image of a clean, automated future. | **Product:** high-gloss plastic, one bold colour per unit, generous radii. **UI:** cards with large radii (24 to 32px), a white or near-black base and one saturated unit colour (#E2231A or #FF8A00), subtle specular highlight gradient on hero elements, used with restraint. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** white ABS (#F4F4F2), orange (#FF7A00), red (#E2231A), black (#121212), space-age yellow (#F6C700), chrome accents.
- **Type:** 1960s futurist lettering; for his register use Michroma or Orbitron for very sparse display, Red Hat Display for text.
- **Form:** rounded rectangles and cylinders, moulded plastic, castors, swivel compartments, built-in lighting.
- **Composition:** compact blocks placed freely in open space; the room as a field of machines.

## Attitude

Colombo believed furniture should adapt to changing ways of living and that design should anticipate the future rather than refine the past. Living space was a system to be optimised, flexible and mobile, ready for a world of smaller homes and new technologies.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `verner-panton`, `italian-radical-design`, `sony-design`.
- **Productive tension with:** `charlotte-perriand`, Colombo's machine-like units softened by Perriand's warmth yield compact living tools that feel human.
- **Clashes with:** `thonet`, the nostalgic bentwood silhouette and Colombo's sealed plastic units speak opposite languages.

## Don't copy

The Boby trolley form, Universale silhouette, Tube chair, Elda armchair shell, Optic clock, Spider lamp; the Visiona and Total Furnishing Unit layouts. Take the activity-module and swappable-density ideas.
