# Konstantin Grcic

**Essence:** Structure made visible, faceted, process-driven objects that look engineered, slightly strange and entirely deliberate.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Faceted structure | Chair_One (Magis, 2004) is die-cast aluminium built from flat triangulated facets, like a football's panels or a low-poly mesh; the structure is the form. | **Product:** surfaces resolved into flat planes and triangles; no smoothing. **UI:** low-polygon geometry as a visual motif, faceted icons and illustrations built from triangles; containers with chamfered corners (clip-path 8px cuts) instead of radii; data visualisations rendered as planar meshes. |
| G2 · Push the process | Myto (Plank, 2008) is a cantilever chair moulded in one piece from a new high-flow engineering plastic, with a perforated seat that flows into the frame, developed with a materials manufacturer. | **Product:** co-develop with material suppliers; the new capability shapes the form. **UI:** let a new platform capability drive form, variable fonts animated by weight, container queries enabling truly component-level responsiveness, GPU-shaded backgrounds, designed from the capability outward. |
| G3 · Tool-like utility | The Mayday lamp (Flos, 1999; Compasso d'Oro 2001) is a portable work light with a hook-shaped handle to hang anywhere and a cord you wind around it. | **Product:** a handle, a hook, a winding point, explicit tool affordances. **UI:** components that look graspable, explicit drag handles, pinnable/hangable panels with visible hooks, collapsible panels with a "wind-up" affordance; utility first, styling second. |
| G4 · Raw, unpolished honesty | He often leaves surfaces unfinished: raw aluminium, rough plastic, visible fixings, provisional-looking colour. | **Product:** finish only where touch requires it. **UI:** "unpolished" UI mode, visible grid, plain system fonts, hard 1px borders, flat fills, for expert or developer surfaces, where honesty beats polish. |
| G5 · Slightly wrong on purpose | His proportions and angles often look a little off (an awkward tilt, an extra leg) until you use them and they make sense. | **Product:** one deliberately uncomfortable visual element resolved by use. **UI:** one intentional asymmetry per screen (a tilted badge, an offset column) that later reveals its logic (e.g. pointing to the next action). |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** raw aluminium (#BFC2C4), anthracite (#33363A), signal colours used flatly, orange (#F26A1B), lime (#B5D334), off-white (#EDEDE8).
- **Type:** not typographic; exhibitions use plain sans and technical labelling. Free equivalents: Archivo, Space Mono for annotations, Barlow Condensed.
- **Form:** facets, triangulation, die-cast and moulded single pieces, hooks, perforations, cantilevers.
- **Composition:** angular, structural, slightly off-balance; objects photographed as specimens.

## Attitude

Grcic, trained as a cabinetmaker before the Royal College of Art, treats design as research into how things are made and used. He accepts awkwardness if it comes from honest structure and dislikes style for its own sake; his 2014 Vitra Design Museum retrospective *Panorama* extended this into speculative environments.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `jasper-morrison`, `ulm-school`, `marcel-breuer`.
- **Productive tension with:** `thonet`, continuous bent curve against faceted die-cast plane: two process-honest languages that together make strong contrast systems.
- **Clashes with:** `streamline-moderne`, polished teardrop shells hide the structure he insists on revealing.

## Don't copy

Chair_One faceted frame, Myto, Mayday lamp, Miura stool, 360° chair forms. Take facet logic, process-first form and tool affordances.
