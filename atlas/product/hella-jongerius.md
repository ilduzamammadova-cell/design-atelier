# Hella Jongerius

**Essence:** Industrial production with the soul of craft, colours that breathe, and repetition that is never quite identical.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Imperfection inside repetition | Jongerius builds deliberate variation into industrial runs: her Repeat textiles for Maharam (2002) use a long pattern repeat so cut pieces differ, and her porcelain and B-Set ceramics (1997) embrace firing irregularities. | **Product:** each unit off the line is subtly different. **UI:** seeded per-instance variation in repeated components, avatar backgrounds, card textures, placeholder illustrations, generated deterministically from an item ID (hue ±6°, grain offset), so lists feel alive yet stable. |
| G2 · Colour that breathes | Her research (book *I Don't Have a Favourite Colour*, 2016; exhibition *Breathing Colour*, Design Museum London, 2017) shows how colours mixed from several pigments shift and stay rich under changing light, while flat single-pigment colours go dead. | **Product:** formulate colours from multiple pigments so they change gracefully with light. **UI:** build colour tokens with slight complexity, mix each brand hue with a touch of its complement and a neighbour in OKLCH (e.g. chroma reduced 10 to 15%, hue nudged) and define separate values for light, dark and high-glare modes instead of inverting one value. |
| G3 · Colour library as system | As art director for colours and materials at Vitra (from 2007) she built the Vitra Colour and Material Library, palettes, textiles and finishes that work together across a catalogue. | **Product:** one curated colour and material library across all products. **UI:** a colour library with harmonious families (each with 6 to 8 values) where any family combination is pre-tested; documentation shows them on real materials (photos, illustrations), not just swatches. |
| G4 · Visible making | The Polder sofa (Vitra, 2005) carries oversized, mismatched buttons and visible stitching; craft traces stay on the industrial product. | **Product:** leave a few visible marks of making on mass products. **UI:** expose small craft details, hand-drawn underline on a key link, visible "stitch" dashed borders on editable zones, slightly irregular icon strokes, at one or two points per screen. |
| G5 · Old and new held together | Her "Beyond the New" manifesto with Louise Schouwenberg (2015) argues against novelty for its own sake, favouring continuity, archetypes and craft knowledge. | **Product:** refine and re-colour proven designs instead of replacing them. **UI:** iterate on existing components (re-colour, re-texture) rather than redesigning; release notes that frame changes as continuity. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** complex, multi-pigment tones, muted coral (#D9826B), sage (#9DAA8C), petrol blue (#2F5D6A), ochre (#C99A3B), plum (#6E4A5E), warm greys; colour combinations with slight dissonance.
- **Type:** not typographic; for her register use a humanist sans with warmth such as Figtree or Karla, and a characterful serif like Fraunces for headings.
- **Form:** soft upholstery with buttons, weaving, glazed ceramics with drawn or embroidered decoration, mixed-material vessels.
- **Composition:** collections in which no two pieces are identical; layered colours; textiles as architecture (KLM cabin, 2012 to 13; UN North Delegates' Lounge with Rem Koolhaas, 2013).

## Attitude

Jongerius believes industrial production can carry character, history and imperfection, and that colour is a material, not a coating. She questions the endless demand for new products and works for continuity and deeper value in what already exists.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `ronan-and-erwan-bouroullec`, `scandinavian-modern`, `charlotte-perriand`.
- **Productive tension with:** `jonathan-ive`, precise, seamless digital surfaces carrying her breathing colours.
- **Clashes with:** `ulm-school`, systematic grey neutrality leaves no room for her colour complexity and irregularity.

## Don't copy

The Polder sofa, Repeat textile patterns, B-Set and Coloured Vases, the KLM cabin textiles, specific Vitra colour-library swatches. Take seeded variation and multi-pigment colour logic.
