# Ettore Sottsass

**Essence:** Design as a way of discussing life, machines, totems and furniture charged with colour, ritual and irony.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Objects as totems | His ceramic series (e.g. *Ceramiche delle Tenebre*, 1963, and the stacked *Totems* of the 1960s) build vertical objects from stacked, coloured elements with ritual presence. | **Product:** stack distinct primitive volumes, each a different colour/material, into one upright figure. **UI:** vertically stacked component "totems", a profile or status card built from 3 to 5 visibly different bands (colour, pattern, type size), each band a separate data layer. |
| G2 · Anti-office machines | With Olivetti he designed the Elea 9003 (1959) and, with Perry King, the red Valentine typewriter (1969), framed as a playful object for anywhere but the office. | **Product:** take a serious tool category and give it leisure colour and casual posture. **UI:** enterprise tools with one consumer-grade gesture, a saturated hero colour, a casual voice in empty states, a personal theme picker, without compromising density. |
| G3 · Laminate as image | In the *Superboxes* (1966) and later furniture, plastic laminate turned cabinets into flat, striped or patterned images. Surface became graphic. | **Product:** treat flat sheet surfaces as a canvas for pattern. **UI:** large flat panels carrying bold stripe patterns (repeating-linear-gradient, 8 to 16px bands) as the only decoration, used for category headers or empty-state backgrounds. |
| G4 · Colour as emotion and meaning | Sottsass used colour to speak of sensuality, memory and culture rather than as neutral coding; his travels in India fed a sense of colour as spiritual. | **Product:** palettes chosen for emotional temperature, documented with meaning. **UI:** name colour tokens by feeling and use ("dusk-violet: reflection, archive"), and map product moods to palette shifts (calm tasks cooler, celebration warmer). |
| G5 · Architecture of everyday ritual | His furniture and interiors frame actions, drinking, writing, storing, as small ceremonies, often with plinths, frames and gateways. | **Product:** add a threshold or plinth to mark a meaningful act. **UI:** "ceremonial" moments get a framed composition (centred, generous margin, distinctive border), e.g. publish, sign, complete; routine actions stay plain. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Valentine red (#D2232A), sottsass pinks and violets (#C46AA8, #6E3F8F), turquoise (#2AA6A0), yellow (#F5C518), black-and-white laminate stripes.
- **Type:** mostly not typographic; Memphis-era graphics used playful sans. For his register: Syne or Unbounded for display, Hanken Grotesk for text.
- **Form:** stacked primitive volumes (cylinders, cones, discs), plinths, laminate-clad boxes, ceramic and glass totems.
- **Composition:** frontal, symmetrical, monumental at small scale; strong vertical axes.

## Attitude

Sottsass resisted design as a purely functional or commercial service; he saw objects as carriers of feelings, ritual and cultural memory. He moved between industry (Olivetti) and provocation (Memphis) without contradiction, seeing both as ways to talk about how people live.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `memphis`, `olivetti`, `italian-radical-design`.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams`, strict grid plus Sottsass' colour and ritual yields expressive systems.
- **Clashes with:** `kenya-hara`, Hara's emptiness and Sottsass' totemic colour cancel each other.

## Don't copy

The Valentine typewriter form and case, the Carlton bookcase, the Bacterio laminate pattern, specific Totem ceramics and Superbox designs. Take totemic stacking and anti-office colour.
