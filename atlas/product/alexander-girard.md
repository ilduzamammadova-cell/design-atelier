# Alexander Girard (1907–1993)

**Essence:** Modernism allowed to celebrate — clean geometry in festival colours, folk art as a living source, and every matchbook designed as carefully as the building.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · A family of colours, not one brand colour | For Braniff International's "End of the Plain Plane" (1965) each aircraft was painted in one of a set of solid fuselage colours — ochres, oranges, blues, turquoise, lemon — with white wings and tail. | Identity built on a set of 6–8 saturated solid colours rotated across instances (each workspace, route, team or product line owns one). Every colour is contrast-checked for its text; the logotype stays constant while the colour changes. |
| G2 · Design the matchbook first | At La Fonda del Sol (New York, 1960) and for Braniff he designed everything from the architecture to the smallest printed and handheld items — at Braniff, from aircraft livery to ticket counters. | Keep a "matchbook list" of the smallest surfaces (receipt, favicon, 404, email footer, loading state, invoice PDF) and design them from the same motif set before the big screens. No surface ships in default styling. |
| G3 · Simple geometric repeats as yardage | As head of Herman Miller's textile division (1952–1973) he produced bright, simple woven and printed repeats — stripes, checks, small geometric motifs — to dress modern furniture. | Three to five pattern tokens (stripe, check, triangle, dot) built in CSS/SVG at 8–24px repeats, used like upholstery on non-content surfaces: avatars without photos, card backs, placeholders, packaging. Patterns use two colours from the family at a time. |
| G4 · Art panels for neutral work spaces | His Environmental Enrichment Panels (designed 1971, released 1972) were large printed textiles made to humanise the movable walls of Herman Miller's Action Office cubicles. | Utilitarian tools (dashboards, admin, IDE-like products) get one large, rotating art panel in otherwise neutral chrome: a header band or empty-state wall that changes weekly from a curated set. Never behind data. |
| G5 · Folk objects in dense tableaux | He and Susan Girard collected folk art from many countries, gave it to the Museum of International Folk Art in Santa Fe (1978) and installed it as crowded miniature scenes in *Multiple Visions* (opened 1982). | Collection and catalogue views as composed scenes: many small objects at varied scale arranged on a shared ground, rather than a uniform thumbnail grid; tap any object for its full record and credited origin. |
| G6 · A cast of simple figures | His carved and painted Wooden Dolls (1952) are blocks with a few painted features — characters built from almost nothing. | Mascots and avatar sets as a family of blocky figures: one body colour each, two or three painted features, no gradients or outlines; the whole cast shares one silhouette grammar. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** magenta ≈ #D6336C, hot orange ≈ #F26B21, lemon ≈ #F6D33C, turquoise ≈ #1FA3A6, sky blue ≈ #7FB8E0, ochre ≈ #C98F2B, off-white ≈ #F5EFE3, black accents.
- **Type:** custom lettering and logotypes per project (La Fonda del Sol, Braniff's italic capitals) → free: Josefin Sans for geometric display, Syne for playful headings, Karla for text.
- **Form:** suns, hearts, eyes, triangles and stripes; carved blocky figures; sunken seating and textile-lined interiors.
- **Composition:** strong colour fields, generous pattern, objects clustered in collected abundance against calm architecture.

## Attitude

Girard believed modern design had become joyless and that the cure lay in colour, ornament and the vitality of folk art from many cultures. He treated an environment as one complete experience and sweated the smallest object because people actually touch it. Joy, for him, was a functional requirement.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `charles-and-ray-eames`, `mexican-otomi-and-papel-picado`, `henri-matisse` — colleagues at Herman Miller, folk colour, cut-out silhouette, joy as a design value.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams` — Rams' restrained product geometry dressed in Girard's colour family gives calm hardware with festive personality.
- **Clashes with:** `swiss-international` — when the grid insists on neutrality, Girard's pattern and colour read as decoration fighting the system.

## Don't copy

Braniff's livery, BI logotype and campaign materials; La Fonda del Sol's sun mark and tableware; specific Herman Miller/Maharam textile and Environmental Enrichment Panel designs; the Wooden Dolls (produced by Vitra). When drawing on folk sources, credit the culture and maker and avoid sacred or ceremonial motifs.
