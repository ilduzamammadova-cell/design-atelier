# Charlotte Perriand

**Essence:** Modern equipment for living that moves from chrome to wood, from machine to body, without losing its system.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Equipment, not furniture | Perriand thought of the home as fitted "equipment" (*équipement de l'habitation*): storage walls, kitchens, built-in units sized to the activities they serve. The prefab kitchen for Le Corbusier's Unité d'habitation (prototype 1950) and the standard bathroom/kitchen pods at Les Arcs (from 1967) are the clearest cases. | **Product:** design around activities (cook, store, rest), then derive units; standardise the pod, vary the arrangement. **UI:** model screens as activity stations — each with its own fixed toolkit (compose station, review station) — rather than as pages of content; the toolkit docks in the same place every time. |
| G2 · The body is the brief | The B306 chaise longue (1928, with Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret) follows the reclining body and lets the cradle slide on its base to change angle. | **Product:** adjustable posture from a single movable element, no knobs. **UI:** one continuous control for a continuous need (a single slider or drag handle for density, zoom or text size) instead of a menu of fixed presets. |
| G3 · Translate, don't import | In Japan (1940–42, as an industrial-design adviser) she studied local craft and rebuilt her own pieces in bamboo and wood rather than exporting French chrome. Tradition was treated as a set of techniques, not motifs. | **Product:** when localising, rebuild with local materials and making methods. **UI:** localisation adapts structure, not only strings — vertical text support, different reading orders, culturally appropriate density — while the interaction grammar stays constant. |
| G4 · Modular open shelving | Her shelving systems (e.g. the *Nuage* bookcases, 1950s, with sliding panels and coloured sheet-metal dividers, partly produced by Jean Prouvé's workshop) mix open and closed compartments in a rhythmic grid. | **Product:** a grid of uprights with interchangeable infills (open, door, drawer, colour panel). **UI:** a dashboard grid where each cell can be open (content visible), collapsed (title only) or accented (one colour plate); allow users to slide panels to hide/reveal, keeping the grid rhythm intact. |
| G5 · Free-form surfaces for gathering | Later wooden tables with irregular, rounded outlines invite people to sit at different points without a head of table. | **Product:** non-hierarchical shapes for shared surfaces. **UI:** collaborative canvases without a fixed "owner" corner; avatars and cursors distributed around content rather than queued in a top bar. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** chrome and black leather (late 1920s); then warm pine and oak (#C99C62), straw (#D9C08C), with flat accent plates of sheet-metal blue (#2D5DA1), red (#B33A2B), yellow (#E3B23C) and black.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; for her register try Work Sans or Jost for labels, Fraunces (low contrast setting) for warmer headings.
- **Form:** tubular steel to solid wood; slatted benches, thick rounded table tops, sliding panels, bamboo strips.
- **Composition:** horizontal emphasis, low furniture, modular rhythm along walls, open centre of room.

## Attitude

Perriand believed design should serve everyday life and pleasure — eating, conversation, the mountains, the body at rest. She rejected the idea that modernism must be cold, and kept refining the system across materials and cultures for seventy years.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `scandinavian-modern`, `naoto-fukasawa` — warm functionalism, body-led objects.
- **Productive tension with:** `ulm-school` — her sensual, convivial modules against Ulm's methodological neutrality yield systems that are rigorous but inviting.
- **Clashes with:** `streamline-moderne` — styling for speed and sales opposes her activity-led equipment logic.

## Don't copy

The LC2/LC3 Grand Confort and B306/LC4 chaise longue outlines (produced by Cassina), the Ombra Tokyo chair, the exact *Nuage* bookcase configuration. Take the equipment logic and the material-translation method.
