# Richard Sapper

**Essence:** The black box that opens: closed, mysterious objects that transform into precise tools, with one red point of contact.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Black box, one red point | The IBM ThinkPad 700C (1992) broke with IBM grey for a matte black rectangle (often linked to a lacquered bento box) with a small red pointing stick in the keyboard. All visual energy concentrates in one tiny accent. | **Product:** a neutral dark body and one small saturated control where the hand acts. **UI:** dark, matte chrome (#1C1C1C) with a single red accent (#E1251B) reserved for focus/active pointer state; everything else greyscale; accent covers under 1% of pixels. |
| G2 · Closed until opened | The TS 502 radio (Brionvega, 1965, with Marco Zanuso) is a hinged box that opens to reveal speaker and dial; the Grillo phone (1965) folds its mouthpiece. Transformation is the interaction. | **Product:** a closed state that hides function, an open state that presents it fully. **UI:** collapsed-by-default panels that open with a hinged motion (rotateX or a clamshell split, 240 ms) to reveal controls; the closed state shows only identity and one status. |
| G3 · Exposed balance | The Tizio desk lamp (Artemide, 1972) balances arms with counterweights and passes low voltage through the arms themselves, so no wires show; the mechanism is the form. | **Product:** structure, balance and conduction combined in visible members. **UI:** show the system's logic — node editors, visible constraints in layout tools, live connectors — as the main visual; no hidden "magic" in configuration screens. |
| G4 · Sound and sense as feature | The Alessi 9091 kettle (1983) has a two-tone brass whistle; the 9090 espresso maker (1979) opens with a lever at the base. Small sensory events make daily tools memorable. | **Product:** one signature sensory event per product (sound, click, motion). **UI:** a single signature earcon or haptic for the most meaningful completion event (send, save, finish), designed in two pitches; never on routine actions. |
| G5 · Compact first | With Zanuso he made the Doney 14 (Brionvega, 1962), an early compact transistorised TV, and the K1340 children's chair (Kartell, 1964), an early all-plastic chair. New technology was used to shrink and simplify. | **Product:** use each new technology to remove volume and parts. **UI:** when adopting a new capability (AI, sync), use it to remove steps and screens, not to add a feature panel. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** matte black (#1A1A1A), anthracite (#2B2B2B), TrackPoint red (#E1251B), polished steel, Brionvega orange and white plastics in the 1960s.
- **Type:** IBM's Paul Rand-era wordmark and Helvetica labels on ThinkPads. Free equivalents: IBM Plex Sans (open source), Arimo for Helvetica metrics.
- **Form:** rectangular boxes with crisp edges, hinges, counterweights, slim lever arms, rubberised soft-touch finishes.
- **Composition:** closed monoliths that open into layered planes; mechanisms arranged as visible diagrams.

## Attitude

Sapper believed objects should have character and a bit of mystery, and that technology should be compact, functional and intriguing to discover. He liked the moment an object reveals how it works.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `olivetti`, `dieter-rams`, `bang-and-olufsen`.
- **Productive tension with:** `verner-panton` — Sapper's black monolith with one red dot against Panton's colour immersion yields dark tools that bloom into colour when opened.
- **Clashes with:** `hella-jongerius` — her imperfect craft colour fights his machined black precision.

## Don't copy

The ThinkPad 700C form and red pointing-stick layout, the Tizio arm geometry, the 9091 kettle silhouette with its whistle, the TS 502 and Grillo forms. Take the black-box-plus-red-point and open/close logic.
