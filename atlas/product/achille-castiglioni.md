# Achille Castiglioni

**Essence:** The redesign of the ready-made — finding an existing part, observing real behaviour, and assembling the smallest witty object that solves it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Ready-made components | The Mezzadro stool (designed 1957, produced by Zanotta from 1970) uses a tractor seat; the Sella stool a bicycle saddle; the Toio lamp (Flos, 1962) a car headlight bulb with fishing-rod guides. Existing industrial parts are recombined into new types. | **Product:** start from catalogue parts, design only the connections. **UI:** build new features from existing platform pieces (system share sheet, native pickers, OS notifications, existing APIs) and invest design effort in the glue — the transitions and copy between them. |
| G2 · Observe the actual gesture | The inline *Rompitratta* switch (VLM, 1968) was shaped for the hand and foot after watching how people fumble for cord switches; it sold in the millions. | **Product:** design from watched behaviour, not stated preference. **UI:** instrument and observe real interaction (session replays, field studies) before redesigning a control; put the control where hands already are (e.g. inline actions on the row being read, not in a distant toolbar). |
| G3 · The handle hidden in the problem | The Arco lamp (Flos, 1962) needs a heavy marble base to balance its arc; a hole through the marble lets two people slide a broomstick through to carry it. | **Product:** every heavy or awkward feature gets a built-in way to handle it. **UI:** every powerful but heavy feature (bulk edit, large import, complex filter) ships with its own "handle": a save/load preset, an undo stack, a progress indicator, a cancel. |
| G4 · Minimum means, maximum effect | Parentesi (Flos, 1970, after an idea by Pio Manzù) is a steel cable from floor to ceiling with a sliding lamp; Lampadina (1972) is a bulb, a reel and a base. | **Product:** fewest parts that deliver the function, no housing for its own sake. **UI:** features expressed as the minimal UI possible — a single inline control instead of a modal; the "design" is the clever reduction, not added chrome. |
| G5 · Irony that serves | Objects such as Snoopy (Flos, 1967) and the Allunaggio seat (1966) carry humour without sacrificing function. | **Product:** one witty reference that does not compromise use. **UI:** humour in microcopy and small animations only where it never blocks task flow — e.g. a playful empty state, never a playful error on payment. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** mostly natural materials — chrome and steel (#C2C5C8), Carrara marble (#ECEBE7), enamel reds and yellows (#D7262E, #F2B705) on stools, black rubber.
- **Type:** not typographic; for his register use a friendly neutral grotesk such as Instrument Sans or Schibsted Grotesk for labels.
- **Form:** borrowed industrial parts, thin rods, cables, arcs, counterweights, visible bulbs.
- **Composition:** few elements, unexpected relationships, wide gestures (Arco's arc) supported by small, clever points.

## Attitude

Castiglioni designed by curiosity and observation, collecting anonymous objects in his studio as teachers. He believed good design comes from the right relation between object and user, often with a smile, and that a designer should start from what already exists rather than from a blank sheet.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `naoto-fukasawa`, `jasper-morrison`, `bruno-munari` — observation-led, ready-made thinking.
- **Productive tension with:** `ulm-school` — Ulm's method with Castiglioni's wit yields rigorous yet charming tools.
- **Clashes with:** `streamline-moderne` — styling shells over mechanisms opposes his visible-parts reduction.

## Don't copy

The Arco lamp (arc, marble base), Mezzadro and Sella stools, Toio, Taraxacum, Snoopy and Parentesi forms — all in production and protected. Take ready-made recombination and the "handle in the problem".
