# Jasper Morrison

**Essence:** Super Normal, objects so well-tuned to everyday use that they improve the atmosphere of a room without asking to be looked at.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Super Normal | With Naoto Fukasawa he curated *Super Normal* (Axis Gallery, Tokyo, 2006), showing around 200 ordinary, often anonymous objects whose quality is revealed only through long use. | **Product:** benchmark new designs against the best anonymous existing object of the type. **UI:** before designing a component, collect the best "normal" versions (native OS controls, long-lived apps) and match their behaviour; deviations require evidence. |
| G2 · Atmosphere over attention | Morrison argues that objects should contribute to the atmosphere of a place rather than draw attention to themselves. | **Product:** measure success by how calm a room feels with the object in it. **UI:** a screen-level "atmosphere audit": count competing focal points (max 1 per view), saturated pixels (<5%), and simultaneous motions (max 1); reduce until the screen feels quiet. |
| G3 · The archetype refined | The Air-Chair (Magis, 1999), gas-injection-moulded in one piece of polypropylene, is a recognisable plain chair made cheaply and robustly; the Glo-Ball lamp (Flos, 1998) is a softly flattened sphere of light. | **Product:** start from the archetype and refine proportions and production, not concept. **UI:** use archetypal patterns (list, table, tabs) with obsessive refinement, line height 1.5, row height 44px, tabular numerals, perfect alignment, rather than novel widgets. |
| G4 · Things that do their job | His photo essays and books (*Everything but the Walls*, 2002; *The Good Life*, 2014; *A Book of Things*, 2015) document ordinary, improvised and useful objects as design references. | **Product:** a reference library of real-world use, not of designer objects. **UI:** a research board of real user setups (screenshots of their spreadsheets, sticky notes, workarounds) as the primary inspiration source, design from improvised solutions. |
| G5 · Quiet range | Morrison's catalogues (Vitra, Magis, Muji, Punkt phones, the Hannover TW 2000 tram, 1990s) show a range that is consistent across scales without a visual signature. | **Product:** consistency through attitude, not logo or styling motif. **UI:** a design system whose identity comes from spacing, proportion and restraint rather than a signature colour or illustration; remove brand marks from in-product chrome. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** natural plywood (#D7B98E), white (#F4F3EF), light grey (#CFCFCB), black (#1C1C1C), occasional muted greens and blues.
- **Type:** modest grotesks in his books. Free equivalents: Work Sans, Karla, Hanken Grotesk.
- **Form:** archetypal silhouettes, single-piece plastics, plywood, cast aluminium, softened edges, frosted glass spheres.
- **Composition:** objects placed with breathing room; low visual weight; symmetry where useful.

## Attitude

Morrison believes design should serve daily life rather than the designer's ego or the market's demand for novelty. The best designs are often anonymous and long-lived; the designer's job is to add a bit of quality to the normal.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `naoto-fukasawa`, `kenya-hara`, `dieter-rams`, `thonet`.
- **Productive tension with:** `konstantin-grcic`, Grcic worked in Morrison's studio; together they balance quiet archetype with structural edge.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`, Morrison's early career was partly a reaction against postmodern spectacle.

## Don't copy

The Air-Chair, Thinking Man's Chair, Glo-Ball and Ply-Chair outlines; Punkt phone forms. Take the Super Normal benchmark and the atmosphere audit.
