# Philippe Starck

**Essence:** The object as character and conversation, theatrical, anthropomorphic forms that sell an idea and a story as much as a function.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The object tells a story | The Juicy Salif lemon squeezer (Alessi, 1990) is more talked about than used; its spider-like legs make it a sculpture and a conversation piece. Starck openly acknowledged its role as a starter of conversations. | **Product:** distinguish "talking objects" from working objects and design each honestly. **UI:** reserve one narrative, expressive element per product (an animated mascot moment, an unusual landing hero) and keep the working surfaces plain; label which is which in the system. |
| G2 · Ghosts of history in new material | The Louis Ghost chair (Kartell, 2002) reproduces a Louis XVI silhouette in clear injection-moulded polycarbonate; La Marie (Kartell, 1998) was an early fully transparent polycarbonate chair. Familiar form, impossible material. | **Product:** take a historic archetype and render it in an unexpected material. **UI:** "ghosted" classics, a familiar control (rotary dial, ledger table, card catalogue) rendered as translucent glass-like layers (backdrop blur 16px, 8% white fill, 1px inner highlight) so recognition is instant but the surface feels new. Use once per product. |
| G3 · Anthropomorphic character | Many Starck objects have faces, legs or postures (the tri-legged chairs, the gnome stools, Hot Bertaa kettle). Character invites affection. | **Product:** one subtle body-like cue (legs, a "face" of controls). **UI:** components with a hint of posture, a progress bar that leans, an empty state where elements are arranged like a face, limited to onboarding and celebratory moments. |
| G4 · Democratic design | Through the *Good Goods* catalogue (La Redoute, 1998) and a collection for Target (2002), Starck pursued good design at mass-market prices. | **Product:** design for mass channels without diluting form. **UI:** premium visual quality on the free tier, no deliberately degraded "cheap" styles for lower plans; the paywall is functional, never aesthetic. |
| G5 · Hospitality as theatre | His hotels (Royalton, New York, 1988; Delano, Miami, 1995) and Café Costes (Paris, 1984) staged spaces as scenes, with dramatic lighting, oversized furniture and surreal juxtapositions. | **Product:** design the arrival as a scene. **UI:** first-run and empty states staged like a set: dramatic contrast, one oversized element, a surprising juxtaposition, then the working UI calms down. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** polished aluminium (#C9CCCF), crystal-clear and smoked polycarbonate, white (#FFFFFF), black (#0F0F0F), occasional jewel tones (amber #D9A100, ruby #9E1B32).
- **Type:** theatrical serif and condensed display in hotel graphics. Free equivalents: Playfair Display (sparingly), Bodoni Moda, Oswald.
- **Form:** organic, horn-like, tapering legs, transparent historic silhouettes, gnome and figure forms, polished cast aluminium.
- **Composition:** single dramatic object as protagonist; spaces as stage sets with surreal scale shifts.

## Attitude

Starck sees design as a tool for humour, poetry and political ideas (ecology, democratic design), and he has long argued that objects should give more service with less material. He is also a reminder of how quickly expressive design becomes spectacle and brand.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `memphis`, `italian-radical-design`.
- **Productive tension with:** `jasper-morrison`, theatrical character with Morrison's normality gives products that are calm in use, witty in detail.
- **Clashes with:** `enzo-mari`, Mari's ethic of the anonymous type opposes design as celebrity performance.

## Don't copy

Juicy Salif, Louis Ghost, La Marie, Hot Bertaa, Miss Sissi lamp, Dr. Glob, all protected and instantly attributable. Take the ghosted-archetype idea and the talking-versus-working split.
