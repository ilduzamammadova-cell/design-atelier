# Enzo Mari

**Essence:** Design as an act of conscience, simple, essential forms that anyone could understand, build and own, set against consumerist excess.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Self-design (Autoprogettazione) | *Autoprogettazione* (1974) published plans for 19 pieces of furniture made from standard pine boards and nails, free for anyone to build, inviting people to understand how things are made. | **Product:** publish build plans; use standard stock materials and one fastener. **UI:** "view source" design, open, documented components, remixable templates and readable configs; every generated artefact exposes how it was made (inspectable layers, exportable code). |
| G2 · Form as essential type | Mari pursued the archetypal form of an object, the essential chair, the essential tray, stripped of personal styling. | **Product:** design the type, not a variation. **UI:** default components as archetypes, the plain list, the plain form, refined for years rather than restyled; no decorative variants in the library. |
| G3 · Puzzle from one block | *16 Animali* (Danese, 1957) cuts sixteen animals from a single block of wood with no waste; the pieces interlock back into a rectangle. | **Product:** cut many parts from one stock with zero waste. **UI:** layouts where tiles of different shapes interlock into one rectangle (a bento grid that always resolves to a clean outer edge); icon sets cut from a shared master grid so every glyph fits its neighbours. |
| G4 · Manual state you can see | The Timor perpetual calendar (Danese, 1967) uses flip-cards turned by hand to show day, date and month, state is physical and visible. | **Product:** state changes by direct manipulation, legible at a glance. **UI:** controls that show their state as large, legible values the user flips directly (tap to advance a date, swipe to change a period), no hidden state in menus. |
| G5 · Dignity of labour | Mari repeatedly framed design around the worker who makes the object and the user who maintains it, critical of design as status marketing. | **Product:** design that respects makers (simple to produce) and users (simple to repair). **UI:** design for the operators too, admin, support and moderation tools get the same design care as consumer screens; accessible, documented, efficient. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** raw pine (#E5C99A), black (#141414), white (#F5F5F2), primary accents in graphics and puzzles (red #D62828, yellow #F2C200, blue #1F4E9A).
- **Type:** neutral grotesks in Danese graphics and calendars. Free equivalents: Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk, Arimo (Helvetica metrics).
- **Form:** planks, nails, sheet steel, interlocking blocks, plain trays and vases, flip-card mechanisms.
- **Composition:** orthogonal, frontal, constructional; joints and fixings visible.

## Attitude

Mari believed design should help people understand the world and resist alienation, not stimulate consumption. He was combative about the industry and defended the designer's role as one of knowledge and ethics. His work keeps open the question of who design is for.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `bruno-munari`, `jasper-morrison`, `ulm-school`.
- **Productive tension with:** `teenage-engineering`, Mari's open, ethical self-design with TE's flat-pack, hacker-friendly hardware: playful products users can open and rebuild.
- **Clashes with:** `philippe-starck`, design as celebrity spectacle is exactly what Mari attacked.

## Don't copy

The Autoprogettazione drawings themselves (republished by Corraini and produced in editions), the 16 Animali and 16 Pesci cutting patterns, the Timor calendar form, the Delfina chair. Take the open-plans principle and the zero-waste puzzle logic.
