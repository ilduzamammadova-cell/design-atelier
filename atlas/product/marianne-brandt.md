# Marianne Brandt

**Essence:** Everyday metal objects assembled from pure geometric primitives, then tuned for factory production.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Assembly of primitives | Her small tea infuser (MT 49, 1924, Bauhaus metal workshop) is a hemisphere on a cross-shaped foot, with a disc lid, a semicircular handle and a cylindrical knob — each part a distinct geometric solid. | **Product:** build objects from 3–5 named primitives (sphere, cylinder, disc, cone), each with one job. **UI:** components composed from a tiny shape vocabulary: circle (avatar, status), half-disc (handle, tab), rectangle (container), line (divider). No other shapes allowed; each shape always means the same role. |
| G2 · Contrast of materials as meaning | Silver or brass body with an ebony handle and knob: the dark insulating material marks exactly where the hand goes. | **Product:** a different material/colour at every touch point. **UI:** every interactive hotspot inside a component is rendered in one contrasting "touch" token (e.g. dark #1E1E1E handles on a light metallic #D8D4CC body) — drag handles, toggles, close buttons share it. |
| G3 · From workshop prototype to industry | With Hin Bredendieck she designed the Kandem bedside lamp (Körting & Mathiesen, 1928), with an adjustable reflector and a push switch, made for mass production. The Bauhaus metal workshop shifted from handmade silver to industrial lighting under her watch. | **Product:** prototype by hand, then redesign each part for stamping, spinning or pressing. **UI:** move from bespoke one-off screens to production components: every prototype element is either mapped to a library component or explicitly created as a new one before shipping. |
| G4 · Adjustability as the core feature | Her lamps tilt and swivel so light lands where it is needed, using the simplest joints. | **Product:** one or two degrees of freedom, smooth and visible. **UI:** panels with one resize axis and one tilt-like option (e.g. orientation toggle), handles always visible; nothing configurable that cannot be seen. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** brushed brass (#B8955A), silver/nickel (#C8C8C4), ebony black (#1A1612), white enamel reflector (#F2F0EA), occasional red-lacquer accents in lamps.
- **Type:** Bauhaus lower-case sans in workshop documents. Free equivalents: Jost, Josefin Sans (sparingly), Red Hat Text.
- **Form:** hemispheres, discs, cylinders, cross feet, cantilevered reflectors, push-buttons.
- **Composition:** strong central axis; parts read separately at the joints; small objects with monumental geometry.

## Attitude

Brandt treated domestic objects as an exercise in clarity: find the simplest geometric form that does the job, then make it manufacturable. She was the first woman to lead the Bauhaus metal workshop (acting head, 1928–29), and later worked in industry (Ruppelwerk, Gotha, 1929–32); her photomontages show a sharper, more critical side that belongs to the graphic side of the atlas.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `bauhaus`, `dieter-rams`, `ulm-school`.
- **Productive tension with:** `isamu-noguchi` — strict primitives with Noguchi's organic glow give geometric lamps with a soft light.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — whiplash ornament dissolves the readable primitive parts.

## Don't copy

The MT 49 tea infuser silhouette, the Kandem lamp form, her specific ashtrays and ceiling fittings (reissued under licence), and her photomontages. Take the primitive vocabulary and the touch-point material rule.
