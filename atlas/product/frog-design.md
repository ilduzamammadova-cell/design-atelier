# frog design

**Essence:** A design language as a rulebook — measurable surface rules applied across a product family, in the service of emotion rather than pure function.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The design language spec | For Apple's "Snow White" language (1984–1990) frog fixed measurable rules: shallow grooves 2 mm wide and deep, spaced 10 mm on centre; corner radii of 2 mm at the front and 3 mm at the rear; zero-draft walls; silk-screened names; recessed port icons. | **Product:** write the form language as numbers, not adjectives, so any engineer can apply it. **UI:** a token spec in the same spirit — rule pitch, radius per edge, stroke width, icon inset — published as numbers with tolerances; lint designs and code against it automatically. |
| G2 · Stripes that make volume lighter | Snow White's parallel grooves broke up large plastic surfaces, visually reducing bulk and doubling as vents. | **Product:** a functional linear texture that also slims the silhouette. **UI:** a repeating fine-line motif (1px lines at 10px pitch, 6–8% contrast) on large empty surfaces (sidebar backgrounds, headers) to reduce apparent mass; may double as a scroll or drag affordance. |
| G3 · One family, many products | Snow White unified the Apple IIc (1984), Macintosh SE and II, LaserWriter II and Macintosh Portable, so a whole range read as one brand. | **Product:** a shared vocabulary that spans every SKU and accessory. **UI:** one component vocabulary across web app, desktop app, mobile, docs and marketing; product-specific additions must reuse the family's rhythm and radius rules. |
| G4 · Form follows emotion | The studio's motto ("form follows emotion") reframed modernist functionalism: products should also be loved, not just used. | **Product:** add one deliberately emotional cue (colour, gesture, sound) on top of a rational base. **UI:** a rational token system plus one documented "emotion layer" — celebratory motion, warm illustration, voice — with rules for where emotion is allowed. |
| G5 · From hardware to strategy and software | Starting with Wega TVs (from 1969) and Sony's product image (from 1974), the studio expanded into branding and software interfaces for clients such as Lufthansa, SAP and Microsoft Windows. | **Product:** design the object, brand and interface as one engagement. **UI:** treat the hardware language, the logo and the software chrome as one spec — device corner radius equals app icon radius equals card radius family. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Apple "Fog" off-white (#E8E4D8), later "Platinum" warm grey (#D3D1CB), "Smoke" grey cables (#8C8A86), Wega black (#151515), occasional signal colours.
- **Type:** Apple Garamond on 1980s Apple products; frog's own identity uses lower-case sans. Free equivalents: EB Garamond (condensed tracking) for period flavour, Manrope or Hanken Grotesk for labels.
- **Form:** rectilinear housings with small, precise radii, parallel grooves, zero-draft walls, recessed icons.
- **Composition:** horizontal banding on fronts, strict alignment of slots and ports, consistent margins across a product family.

## Attitude

Hartmut Esslinger argued that design is a strategic tool for companies and that emotion — not only function — makes people bond with technology. frog turned the visual language of a brand into a reproducible, numeric system, an idea that maps directly onto today's design tokens.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `ulm-school`, `jonathan-ive`, `sony-design`.
- **Productive tension with:** `hella-jongerius` — numeric rules hosting irregular, breathing colour.
- **Clashes with:** `italian-radical-design` — corporate design-language machinery is what the radicals critiqued.

## Don't copy

The Snow White product forms (Apple IIc, Macintosh SE housings), Apple's Fog/Platinum trade dress as a pastiche, frog's logo, Wega System 3000 form. Take the numeric design-language spec.
