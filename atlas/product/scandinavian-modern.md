# Scandinavian Modern

**Essence:** Modernism made warm, everyday objects of wood, glass and clay, shaped for ordinary homes, long winters and the human hand.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Glare-free light | Poul Henningsen's layered shade lamps (PH series, from the 1920s; PH5, 1958) are calculated so the bulb is never visible and light falls soft and even. | **Product:** never expose the raw source; reflect it through layers. **UI:** no pure #FFFFFF backgrounds or pure #000 text, use warm off-white (#FAF7F2) and soft black (#22201D); shadows built from 2 to 3 stacked low-opacity layers; dark mode uses dimmed, not inverted, surfaces. |
| G2 · Open stock, no fixed sets | Kaj Franck's Kilta tableware for Arabia (launched 1953) was sold as separate pieces in a few colours to mix freely, instead of formal matching services. | **Product:** modular pieces sold individually, all colours compatible. **UI:** a component palette where any surface colour pairs with any other (every pairing AA-tested); users compose their own sets, themeable widgets with no "official" combination. |
| G3 · Soft geometry of the hand | Hans Wegner's chairs (Wishbone CH24, 1949 to 50; "The Chair", 1949) carve wood into rounded joints and armrests that invite touch. Geometric order, softened at every contact point. | **Product:** radius increases where the hand lands. **UI:** corner radius scales with interaction intensity: static containers 4px, clickable cards 12px, primary buttons fully rounded; tactile press states (scale 0.98, 120 ms). |
| G4 · Research-based proportions | Kaare Klint's furniture school at the Royal Danish Academy measured bodies and the objects people store (cutlery, linens) to size furniture. | **Product:** size containers to the real objects they hold. **UI:** card and table cell sizes derived from real content measurements (median title length, typical number of digits) rather than arbitrary breakpoints. |
| G5 · More beautiful everyday things | Swedish Werkbund campaigns (Gregor Paulsson's 1919 call for "more beautiful things for everyday use") framed good design as a right for ordinary households, later carried by firms such as Gustavsberg, Arabia and, later, IKEA. | **Product:** design for the median income, not the collector. **UI:** design first for low-end Android at 3G and 360px width; premium features never degrade the base experience. |
| G6 · Total room, unified hotel | Arne Jacobsen's SAS Royal Hotel, Copenhagen (1960), building, furniture (Egg, Swan), cutlery and fittings by one hand. | **Product:** one designer specifies every item down to the spoon. **UI:** one system governs app, help centre, emails and receipts, no third-party widgets with alien styling. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** pale woods, oak (#C8A27A), birch (#E4D3B5), teak (#A0623A); textile white (#F4F1EA); muted blues (#5A7A99) and greens (#6E8B6A); Marimekko-era bold red (#D3302F) and black as occasional punctuation.
- **Type:** quiet humanist and geometric sans. Free equivalents: Albert Sans, Figtree, Karla; for Danish-modern elegance, Jost.
- **Form:** organic-tapered wood, moulded plywood (Jacobsen's Ant, 1952; Series 7, 1955), layered shades, thick-walled ceramics, clear coloured glass.
- **Composition:** low, horizontal, uncluttered rooms; objects spaced so each can be seen; daylight as material.

## Attitude

The Scandinavian designers held that good form should be affordable, durable and pleasant to live with daily. Their modernism was moderated by craft traditions, climate and a social-democratic idea that design serves the many.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `charlotte-perriand`, `jasper-morrison`, `alvar-aalto`, warm functional modernism.
- **Productive tension with:** `verner-panton`, the Danish rebel against Danish calm; together they make warm interfaces with one immersive colour space.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`, Memphis' antagonistic surfaces fight the movement's quiet domestic contract.

## Don't copy

Wegner's Wishbone and The Chair silhouettes, Jacobsen's Egg, Swan, Ant and Series 7 outlines, Henningsen's PH5 and Artichoke shade geometry, Marimekko's Unikko poppy pattern. Take glare-free light, open stock and soft geometry.
