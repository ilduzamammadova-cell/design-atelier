# Olivetti

**Essence:** A company that treated office technology as culture — sculpted machines, colour-coded intelligence, and a brand built by poets, painters and architects.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Colour encodes the system | On the Elea 9003 mainframe (1959, Ettore Sottsass with Andries Van Onck, under engineer Mario Tchou), coloured strips marked what each cabinet contained and the console showed status as a mosaic of coloured, engraved cubes. Mario Bellini's Divisumma 18 calculator (1973) used colour and soft rubber keys to separate functions. | **Product:** colour assigned by function family, consistent across the product line. **UI:** semantic accent tokens — one hue per function family (input = blue, operators = amber, commit = red, system = grey) — used identically on keys, icons, chart series and documentation; a status board of small coloured tiles rather than text logs. |
| G2 · Human-scale technology | The Elea racks were held to about 150 cm so engineers could see each other across the room; cables ran overhead in channels rather than under the floor. Technology was sized to people and maintenance. | **Product:** cap object heights to sight lines; service access designed in. **UI:** dashboards that never fill the full viewport with chrome — keep sight lines to collaborators (presence avatars, shared cursors visible); "maintenance" views (logs, debug) one click away rather than buried. |
| G3 · Sculpted shell | Marcello Nizzoli's Lexikon 80 (1948) and Lettera 22 portable (1950) wrapped typewriters in continuous, softly sculpted die-cast or sheet shells with organic transitions. | **Product:** one continuous sculpted skin with soft transitions between planes. **UI:** containers with continuous-curvature corners and soft elevation (two-layer shadows, 0 1px 2px + 0 8px 24px at low opacity), surfaces that feel moulded rather than boxed. |
| G4 · The personal machine as companion | Sottsass and Perry King's Valentine portable typewriter (1969) in red ABS with its own case was marketed as a casual, anti-office object. | **Product:** a bold single body colour turns equipment into a personal possession. **UI:** a personal-mode skin — one saturated brand colour (#D2232A) as full background on the app icon, widget and splash — framing a tool as a companion rather than corporate software. |
| G5 · The first desktop computer, keyboard-first | Bellini's housing for the Programma 101 (1965, engineering by Pier Giorgio Perotto) made a programmable calculator a desk object with a clear keyboard, magnetic card slot and printed tape. | **Product:** turn a new category into a familiar desk tool through layout clarity. **UI:** keyboard-first design with a visible command strip, printed-tape style history (a running, scrollable log of operations), and record/replay of sequences. |
| G6 · Brand as a cultural programme | Under Adriano Olivetti (president from 1938), the company commissioned Giovanni Pintori's posters, showrooms by BBPR (New York, 1954) and Carlo Scarpa (Venice, 1957–58), plus housing and social services in Ivrea. | **Product:** brand expressed through architecture, publishing and social programmes, not only ads. **UI:** a brand that ships editorial content, open tools and documentation with the same care as the product; marketing pages designed as exhibitions. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Olivetti soft greys and greens (#A9B3A5), Lettera pale blue-green (#9DB8B0), Valentine red (#D2232A), Divisumma yellow (#F2C200), Elea mosaic accents (red, green, yellow, white on dark grey).
- **Type:** Pintori's spaced lower-case sans logotype (1947); later Walter Ballmer's Olivetti logotype (1970). Free equivalents: Work Sans or DM Sans lowercase with +4% tracking.
- **Form:** sculpted shells, wedge profiles (Bellini), rubber skins over keys, coloured caps.
- **Composition:** machines composed as landscapes of keys; posters of numerals and abstract shapes.

## Attitude

Adriano Olivetti believed an industrial company had cultural and social duties, and that beautiful, humane machines were part of a better society. Design was strategy, not decoration, and artists were partners in the company's thinking.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `ettore-sottsass`, `richard-sapper`, `achille-castiglioni`.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams` — Braun's grey restraint versus Olivetti's warm colour; together they give semantic colour inside calm systems.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism` — Olivetti's humane, sensual surfaces oppose raw hostility.

## Don't copy

The Lettera 22, Valentine and Programma 101 forms, the Elea console mosaic layout, Pintori's posters, the Olivetti logotypes. Take semantic colour and human-scale technology.
