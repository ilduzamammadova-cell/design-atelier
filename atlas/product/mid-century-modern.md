# Mid-Century Modern

**Essence:** Post-war modernism made domestic and cheerful — new materials, organic curves and a manufacturer-led idea of the good, open-plan life.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Design director inside the company | At Herman Miller, George Nelson served as design director (1945–72) and brought in the Eameses, Isamu Noguchi and Alexander Girard; at Knoll, Florence Knoll's Planning Unit shaped interiors and products together. Taste was institutionalised through one curator. | **Product:** a single design director curates a roster of outside authors under one brand voice. **UI:** a design-system "editor" role who approves contributions from many teams; contributed components must pass a voice checklist (radius, motion, copy tone) before entering the library. |
| G2 · Organic meets machine | Moulded plywood, fibreglass and wire produced biomorphic shapes (Saarinen's Womb chair, 1948, and Tulip chair, 1957; Bertoia's Diamond chair, 1952) on industrial lines. | **Product:** one organic gesture per object on a rational base. **UI:** a strict 8px grid and system type, interrupted by a single organic element per view — a blob-shaped avatar mask, a curved section edge, or a hand-drawn illustration. |
| G3 · Atomic and molecular motifs | The Ball Clock (Nelson office, c. 1948–49, often credited to Irving Harper) and the Marshmallow sofa (1956) build objects from repeated circular modules joined by thin rods. | **Product:** compose from repeated identical discs or nodes. **UI:** node-and-link visual motif for status, navigation or loaders; dot-cluster icons; repeated circular chips in a radial or lattice arrangement. |
| G4 · The open plan and the system office | Robert Propst's Action Office for Herman Miller (1964, revised 1968) proposed reconfigurable work surfaces and panels for changing tasks — a progressive idea that later degraded into the cubicle. | **Product:** reconfigurable modular furniture; watch how systems decay when cost-cutting removes flexibility. **UI:** user-arrangeable workspaces (panels, docks, split views) with sensible defaults; guard against the "cubicle" failure — rigid templates that users cannot reshape. |
| G5 · Folk colour in modern frames | Alexander Girard's textiles and interiors (e.g. La Fonda del Sol restaurant, New York, 1960) brought saturated folk-art palettes into modern spaces. | **Product:** a modern form with one rich, celebratory textile. **UI:** neutral component chrome + one illustrative pattern band per brand moment (onboarding, celebration screen), with saturated warm accents (#E4572E, #F2A541, #3A7D44). |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** walnut and teak (#5E3B22, #A0623A), olive (#7A7D3A), mustard (#D9A32B), burnt orange (#D2602A), turquoise (#2FA4A9), white fibreglass, black wire.
- **Type:** confident grotesques and quirky display faces. Free equivalents: Work Sans, Archivo, Josefin Sans; for display, Fraunces (soft) or Righteous used sparingly.
- **Form:** tapered dowel legs, boomerang and kidney shapes, moulded shells, wire cages, spheres on rods, floating cabinets.
- **Composition:** open plans, low horizontals, floor-to-ceiling glass (Case Study Houses, 1945–66), indoor-outdoor continuity.

## Attitude

The movement believed modern design could make ordinary life sunnier, more sociable and more efficient, and that manufacturers could deliver that vision at scale. The label itself came later (Cara Greenberg's 1984 book popularised it), and the 2000s revival risks turning it into nostalgia décor.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `charles-and-ray-eames`, `isamu-noguchi`, `scandinavian-modern`.
- **Productive tension with:** `teenage-engineering` — mid-century warmth with TE's precise digital-instrument graphics yields playful, crafted tech UI.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism` — raw mass and hostility against the movement's sunny domesticity.

## Don't copy

The Ball Clock, Marshmallow sofa, Coconut chair, Tulip and Womb chair outlines, Bertoia's wire-grid shapes, Girard's specific textile patterns. "Mad Men" pastiche — teak, starbursts, atomic boomerangs all at once — reads as costume. Take one gene at a time.
