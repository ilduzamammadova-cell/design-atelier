# Charles and Ray Eames

**Essence:** Serious play — learning a material, a constraint or an idea so thoroughly that the result feels inevitable and joyful at once.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Powers of Ten | The film *Powers of Ten* (1977, from a 1968 sketch version) moves from a picnic blanket to the edge of the universe and into a carbon atom, one order of magnitude per step. Scale becomes a navigable dimension. | **Product:** design the object at every scale (part, product, room, city). **UI:** semantic zoom — every level of a dataset has its own designed view (overview tile → summary card → detail → raw record), with a continuous zoom transition and a scale indicator (×10 steps) always visible. |
| G2 · The guest-host relationship | The Eameses framed the designer as a good host who anticipates the guest's needs. | **Product:** design for the arrival moment and the comfort of others. **UI:** onboarding that prepares the space before the user arrives (sensible defaults, sample data, pre-filled templates); empty states written as invitations with one clear first action. |
| G3 · Constraint as the material | Moulded plywood (leg splint for the US Navy, 1942; LCW chair, 1945–46) and fibreglass shells (from 1950) came from long experiments with what each process could do cheaply. Constraints were embraced, not escaped. | **Product:** list constraints (cost, process, time) and design from them. **UI:** before visual design, write the constraint sheet — bandwidth, devices, content volume, a11y — and treat each as a generator (e.g. 3G budget → text-first cards with typographic hierarchy instead of images). |
| G4 · Colour panels in a frame | The Eames Storage Units (ESU, 1950) combine a steel frame with interchangeable panels in primary colours, birch, laminate and perforated metal. | **Product:** fixed frame, user-chosen infills. **UI:** a grid of modules where each tile takes one fill from a small, mixed palette (two primaries, one neutral, one textured/patterned) — rhythm through colour placement, not decoration. |
| G5 · The collection as composition | Ray Eames' arrangements of objects, toys, folk art and photographs — and the multi-screen show *Glimpses of the USA* (Moscow, 1959) — treat abundance as a designed field. | **Product:** curated "functioning decoration" displayed with care. **UI:** media-rich collection views (masonry or multi-screen grids) where many small images form one composition; consistent gutters (8px) let density read as order. |
| G6 · Toys as research | House of Cards (1952), the Toy, kites and films are prototypes for ideas, treated as seriously as furniture. | **Product:** build playful prototypes to explore principles. **UI:** a sandbox/playground mode in complex tools where users can experiment without consequences; design-system docs with interactive toys for every token. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** ESU primaries — red (#C8372D), yellow (#F2B705), blue (#2A5DA8) — with birch (#E2C79F), walnut (#5C3A21), black (#1B1B1B), off-white fibreglass (#ECE6D8).
- **Type:** mid-century grotesques and slab serifs in exhibitions and films; Ray's hand lettering. Free equivalents: Work Sans, Archivo, Roboto Slab, plus a loose hand font like Caveat used sparingly.
- **Form:** compound-curved plywood, moulded shells, wire bases, exposed steel cross-bracing (Eames House, 1949).
- **Composition:** grids filled with varied panels; dense, rhythmic collections; diagrammatic exhibitions (*Mathematica*, 1961).

## Attitude

The Eameses believed design is problem-solving within constraints, and that the best solutions come from deep curiosity and play. They extended design into film, exhibitions and education because they saw communication of ideas as the same craft as making a chair.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `mid-century-modern`, `isamu-noguchi`, `scandinavian-modern` — organic modern warmth.
- **Productive tension with:** `ulm-school` — Ulm's operational method with the Eames' curiosity yields systems that are rigorous and delightful.
- **Clashes with:** `kenya-hara` — the Eames abundance (collections, colour, toys) fights Hara's emptiness.

## Don't copy

The Eames Lounge Chair and Ottoman (1956), LCW/DCW profiles, the moulded plastic shell chair outlines, the Hang-It-All, the Eames House façade composition, and *Powers of Ten* imagery. Trade dress is actively protected. Take semantic zoom, guest-host and constraint thinking.
