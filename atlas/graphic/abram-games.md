# Abram Games (1914 to 1996)

**Essence:** Maximum meaning, minimum means, two ideas fused into one image that springs open in the viewer's mind.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Two ideas, one silhouette | His wartime blood-donor poster places a soldier inside the outline of a blood bottle; cause and consequence share one form. | Hero illustration fuses product and benefit into a single silhouette (e.g. a lock shaped like a house); no second image on the screen. Test: describe it in one sentence with "is also". |
| G2 · The wound spring | He described the designer as constructing a mechanism that releases instantly when the eye catches it, rational construction, emotional effect. | Every campaign image is reviewed for a 1-second read and a 5-second second read; if either fails, simplify. Copy ≤ 7 words on image. |
| G3 · Spiral that steers the eye | *Your Talk May Kill Your Comrades* (1942) uses a spiral of "speech" running from a face to three bayoneted soldiers. | A single spiral or curved path leads from headline to call-to-action; motion on entry follows the same curve (≤ 600ms). |
| G4 · Airbrush softness, hard silhouette | Soft airbrushed gradients and modelling sit behind hard-edged flat figures and lettering. | One soft gradient background per screen with crisp, flat foreground shapes and type on top; never gradient on gradient. |
| G5 · Objects with faces | Later posters (*Top People Read The Times*, 1950s) give everyday objects a gentle anthropomorphic humour on a plain colour field. | Mascots built from the product itself plus minimal facial cues, on one flat background colour; humour gentle, never sarcastic. |
| G6 · Event emblem as kit | His Festival of Britain emblem (1951) combined a compass star and Britannia head with bunting, a mark designed for many applications. | Event or launch identity built as modular parts (star, head, pennants) that can separate into badges, patterns and stickers. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, deep blue, cream, teal ~#1E7F7A, black; airbrushed tonal transitions.
- **Type:** condensed sans capitals and slab or Egyptian display letters, often hand-drawn → Oswald, Alfa Slab One, Libre Franklin.
- **Form:** silhouettes, spirals, stars, airbrushed volumes, anthropomorphic objects.
- **Composition:** one central image, strong diagonal or spiral movement, short headline integrated into the picture.

## Attitude

Largely self-taught, Games served as an official War Office poster artist during the Second World War, producing designs that had to be understood instantly by soldiers and civilians. He believed reason should discipline expression: the designer constructs, the viewer feels. His later work softened modernism with a gentle British humour.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `polish-poster-school`, `paul-rand`, one-idea metaphors and humane wit.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, his fused metaphors placed inside Swiss structure: public-service campaigns that are both clear and emotional.
- **Clashes with:** `postmodern-new-wave`, layered, multiple readings dissolve his single compressed idea.

## Don't copy

*Your Talk May Kill Your Comrades* (1942), the blood-donor recruitment poster (c. 1942), the Festival of Britain emblem (1951), the BBC television symbol (1950s), *Top People Read The Times*.
