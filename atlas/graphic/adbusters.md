# Adbusters and Culture Jamming

**Essence:** The advertisement turned inside out: flawless copies of advertising grammar carrying anti-consumer messages.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Subvertising | Spoof ads reproduce a famous campaign's photography, copy line and typography so exactly that the twisted message lands before the viewer notices. | "Honest" variants of persuasive patterns: a banner built in the familiar sale-banner layout that shows real cost, impact or terms instead of urgency. Only your own patterns, never another brand's marks. |
| G2 · The uncommercial | TV spots and campaigns that sell abstention rather than products, such as Buy Nothing Day. | Give the "not now" path equal visual weight to the purchase path (same size, same contrast); offer "remind me in 30 days" instead of countdown pressure. |
| G3 · Readers as reporters | The ad-free, reader-supported magazine regularly prints readers' photos of altered billboards and street interventions. | A moderated contribution gallery where users submit photographs with location and date; contributions displayed in a strict grid with credit lines. |
| G4 · Glossy dissent | High production values are the weapon: critique looks as expensive as the thing it attacks (critics call this its contradiction). | Civic or critical products use premium polish — the same type, image and print standards as luxury brands — so credibility is never lost on craft. |
| G5 · One image, one date, one call | The 2011 #OCCUPYWALLSTREET poster: a dancer balanced on the Charging Bull, a date, a hashtag. | Campaign surfaces carry one surreal image, one date and one action (hashtag, link or button). Nothing else above the fold. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** whatever the parodied genre uses (perfume monochrome, detergent-box primaries); the magazine itself favours black, white and saturated photo spreads.
- **Type:** borrowed from the target genre; editorial pages mix serif and grotesque text with layered headlines → free: Libre Caslon, Archivo, IBM Plex Mono.
- **Form:** photographic pastiche, mock product shots, the "black spot", reader-submitted snapshots.
- **Composition:** single provocations; magazine spreads juxtapose text and image for maximum friction.

## Attitude

Founded in 1989 by Kalle Lasn and Bill Schmalz after broadcasters refused to air Lasn's film answering a logging-industry ad, Adbusters treats the media environment as an ecology polluted by advertising. It draws on the Situationists' détournement, John Heartfield's photomontage and Barbara Kruger's text-image work, and has been accused by the left of using the very language it attacks.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `punk-graphics`, `jan-van-toorn`, `tibor-kalman` — détournement, critical montage and socially driven editorial.
- **Productive tension with:** `corporate-identity` — studying identity manuals precisely is what makes subversion convincing; fluency in the corporate language sharpens the critique.
- **Clashes with:** `designers-republic` — irony on irony: tDR already turns corporate pastiche into pleasure, and the activism becomes merchandise.

## Don't copy

Specific subvertisements (the "Absolut" and Calvin Klein parodies, the Prozac detergent box), the Blackspot logo, the 2011 Occupy poster. Never reproduce real trademarks inside product UI.
