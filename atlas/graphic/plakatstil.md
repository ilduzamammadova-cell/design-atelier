# Plakatstil

**Essence:** Sell one thing by showing one thing, a single object and a single name, enlarged on a flat field until nothing else can compete.

Key people: Lucian Bernhard (1883 to 1972), Ludwig Hohlwein (1874 to 1949), Hans Rudi Erdt, Julius Klinger, Julius Gipkens. The Berlin printer Hollerbaum & Schmidt held exclusive contracts with several of them, which made the "Berlin poster" a recognisable house sound. Its Swiss cousin is the *Sachplakat* (object poster).

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Object + name, nothing else | Bernhard's Priester matches poster (c. 1905) left only two matches and the brand word on a bare ground; the passer-by gets it in one glance | Product hero = one isolated product render + the brand wordmark on a flat background. No tagline, badge or carousel above the fold; the CTA arrives on first scroll |
| G2 · Subtract until it breaks | Bernhard's working story is one of painting things *out*, table, cigar, ashtray, until only the indispensable sign remained | Review ritual: hide each element of a screen in the prototype; restore it only if a 5-second test fails without it. Splash, empty and paywall screens carry at most two elements |
| G3 · The name as a second image | Bernhard's dense, brush-built letters carry as much visual mass as the picture; word and object are equals | Wordmark/display set at weight 800 to 900, 64 to 120px, tracking −2%, in the ink colour; the name must survive as the only element in a grayscale thumbnail |
| G4 · Flat field, few inks | Lithographic economy: a mid-tone ground, a dark silhouette, one hot accent on a single detail (the red lining of a shoe) | Palette of 4 tokens: `ground`, `ink`, `paper`, `accent`. The accent appears on exactly one detail per screen (price, "add" button). No gradients, no drop shadows |
| G5 · Texture as a second voice | Hohlwein kept the flat-shape structure but filled planes with checks, stripes and dappled light | Allow one patterned surface per screen (e.g. a check or grain fill behind the product card) while all UI chrome stays flat and untextured |
| G6 · Legible at walking speed | Posters were judged at distance and in motion; scale contrast does the work | QA rule: brand + product must be identifiable at 25% zoom or under a Gaussian blur of 8px in Figma; if not, enlarge or remove |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** saturated flat grounds (mustard, olive, brick, ultramarine) with black silhouettes and one red/orange accent, approx. `#C8A24A`, `#2B3A55`, `#B23A2E`, `#1C1C1C`, `#F4EEDC`.
- **Type:** heavy hand-lettered romans and grotesques; Bernhard's lettering was cut as type (Bernhard Antiqua, Flinsch, 1913; later Bernhard Gothic and Bernhard Fashion in the US). Free stand-ins: Ultra, Alfa Slab One, Fraunces Black.
- **Form:** closed silhouettes with almost no interior detail; Hohlwein adds pattern and patches of light.
- **Composition:** object centred or slightly low, name across the top or bottom, generous empty colour around both.

## Attitude

Advertising is a signal, not a story: the designer's job is to find the one sign that carries the brand and remove everything else. The style is also a neutral instrument, Hohlwein later produced posters for the Nazi regime, a reminder that the formula amplifies any message equally.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `a-m-cassandre`, `paul-rand`, both push reduction further, adding geometry or wit to the single-sign formula.
- **Productive tension with:** `new-typography`, a centred pictorial object hero combined with asymmetric, grid-led information below gives a product detail page that is both iconic and functional.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, ornamental borders and whiplash curves smother the lone object the style depends on.

## Don't copy

Bernhard's Priester matches poster, his Stiller shoe poster and Manoli "M" trademark; Hohlwein's PKZ and Riquet Mammut-Kakao posters (and his Nazi-era posters); the drawn outlines of the Bernhard typefaces.
