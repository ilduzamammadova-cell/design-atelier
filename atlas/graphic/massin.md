# Massin (1925 to 2020)

**Essence:** The book as a stage, typography that performs voices, volume, time and space so a text can be heard while it is read.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · One typeface per voice | In his edition of Ionesco's *La Cantatrice chauve* (Gallimard, 1964) each character speaks in a distinct type style and is identified by a small photographic portrait rather than a name. | Multi-person interfaces (chat, transcripts, comments, scripts) assign each speaker a typographic voice, weight, width, slope or size, instead of coloured bubbles; a small portrait marks each voice. |
| G2 · Volume as size | Lines grow, shrink, tilt and pile up as the exchanges get louder; simultaneous speech overlaps on the page. | Audio and meeting transcripts map loudness to font weight/size within a capped range (e.g. 14 to 28px); overlapping speech is shown as overlapping text lines rather than sequential rows. |
| G3 · Time as space | Pacing across spreads follows the rhythm of the play, like film editing; silence becomes white space. | Scroll-driven narratives in which vertical spacing encodes duration (1s pause = 1 line of space); long silences become blank viewports. |
| G4 · Elastic letters | He printed type onto rubber sheets, stretched and photographed them to make figurative typography before computers made it easy. | Kinetic text that stretches with drag or scroll using spring physics (stiffness ~200, damping ~20), snapping back to the set form; for expressive moments only. |
| G5 · One text, many styles | His design of Queneau's *Exercices de style* (1963) set the same story in dozens of typographic manners. | Brand-voice exploration: render one key screen in 20+ typographic treatments before choosing; keep the variants as a reference sheet. |
| G6 · Ink and calligraphy | For *Délire à deux* (1966) handwriting, blots and splashes became the voice of the text. | Ink-blot masks and hand-drawn letters as a display layer for literary or cultural content; never over functional text. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** severe black on white, high-contrast photographs; occasional red titling in Gallimard's tradition.
- **Type:** many faces at once, grotesques, Didones, italics, wood types, handwriting → Bodoni Moda, Libre Caslon, Bricolage Grotesque, Space Mono; hand-drawn ink for display.
- **Form:** speech bubbles, distorted letters, ink blots, photographic figures.
- **Composition:** cinematic sequences across spreads; letters at angles, overlapping, varying wildly in size.

## Attitude

Self-taught in graphic design (he learned under Pierre Faucheux), Massin was a writer as well as an art director at Gallimard for over twenty years. He saw typography as an interpretive art akin to poetry, aiming to express the passage of time and space in book form; his study *La Lettre et l'image* (1970) traced letters as images through history.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `futurism`, `dada`, `herb-lubalin`, type that performs meaning.
- **Productive tension with:** `emil-ruder`, Ruder's disciplined texture as the ground from which Massin's typographic voices erupt.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, fixed type hierarchies cannot perform different voices.

## Don't copy

The spreads of *La Cantatrice chauve* (1964), *Délire à deux* (1966), the *Exercices de style* cover (1963), Henry Cohen's photographs used in the Ionesco book.
