# Grapus (1970–1990)

**Essence:** Graphic guerrillas — handwriting, scrawls and colliding universal symbols put to work for social, political and cultural causes instead of markets.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Handwriting as honesty | Grapus preferred scrawled headlines and graffiti-like lettering to polished typesetting; the hand signals a real person speaking. | In civic, activist or community products, key messages are hand-written SVG (drawn per message, not a script font), with a typeset accessible equivalent in the DOM. |
| G2 · Graphic slang | A vocabulary of universal symbols — hands, heads, globes, sun, moon, flags, blood, fireworks — layered so they contradict and provoke. | Opinion and editorial modules may collage 2–4 universal symbols in tension (a globe with a bandage, a hand holding a ballot); symbol set curated and reviewed for misreadings. |
| G3 · Collective authorship | Work was discussed at length and signed by the collective; individual credit was refused. | Credits presented as team or community; tools that let multiple contributors' marks coexist visibly on one canvas (annotation layers per person). |
| G4 · Primaries over photography | Primary colours and scribbled marks drawn directly over photographs and found images. | Campaign images: documentary photo with red, blue and yellow scribble overlays (marker-like strokes 4–8px) highlighting what matters. |
| G5 · Content debate first | Every project began with analysis and argument about the message; form came only after agreeing on the core. | Kick-off is a message workshop producing one sentence and one symbol; no visual work until both are agreed and written down. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, blue, yellow, black and white; occasional photographic greys and pastels in later work.
- **Type:** handwriting, brushed and scrawled letters; stencils; minimal typeset text → hand-drawn display; Karla or DM Sans for information.
- **Form:** drawn symbols, collage of pop icons, found images, graffiti marks.
- **Composition:** dynamic, informal, seemingly spontaneous; elements burst across the sheet.

## Attitude

Founded by Pierre Bernard, François Miehe and Gérard Paris-Clavel after May 1968 (two of them had studied with Henryk Tomaszewski in Warsaw), Grapus believed advertising manufactured false needs and redirected design toward political, social and cultural ends. The designer was to be both artist and citizen. After 1990 its members continued in separate studios.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `henryk-tomaszewski`, `polish-poster-school`, `punk-graphics` — hand-made, urgent, anti-commercial graphics.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — handwritten urgency set on a strict grid: credible yet human civic campaigns.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity` — anonymous uniformity versus collective, spontaneous, political voice.

## Don't copy

The 1982 Grapus exhibition poster (smiley/Mickey/Hitler collage), *Apartheid: le cancer du monde* (1986), the Louvre identity (1989), the peace-movement posters.
