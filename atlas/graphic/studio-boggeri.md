# Studio Boggeri (founded 1933)

**Essence:** The studio as a curator of talent, a Milan office that imported Swiss-German rigour and the new photography into Italian industry and gave a stage to a generation of designers.

Antonio Boggeri (1900 to 1989), formerly at the printers Alfieri & Lacroix, founded the studio in 1933. It worked as an intermediary between client and printer and drew on a changing roster: Xanti Schawinsky (1933 to 36, fresh from the Bauhaus), Marcello Nizzoli, Max Huber (from 1940), Albe Steiner, Bruno Munari, Enzo Mari, Carlo Vivarelli, Heinz Waibl and later `bruno-monguzzi`. Clients included `olivetti`, Pirelli, Motta, La Rinascente and the publisher Einaudi. The 1981 Milan Triennale held a retrospective.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Studio as system plus roster | A stable method and client relationship with rotating specialist designers | Design org model: one documented system + rotating specialists; contractor onboarding pack (principles, tokens, templates) that gets a newcomer shipping on brand in a day |
| G2 · New photography on a grid | Product photos treated as objective modules in a strict grid (Nizzoli's Montecatini brochure, 1933) | Product grids with identical photographic treatment (angle, light, background) on a strict 4 to 6 column grid; any photo that breaks the treatment is reshot |
| G3 · Explain the machine in close-ups | Schawinsky's Olivetti brochure (1934) breaks a typewriter into detail shots and diagrams | Feature pages that decompose a product into close-up modules, each with one label and one spec; the whole product shown once, last |
| G4 · Tools of the trade as image | Huber's 1942 studio brochure shows gouache tubes, dividers, clips and type | Self-promotion, docs headers and careers pages built from the team's real tools and process artefacts, photographed objectively |
| G5 · Lower-case logotype + montage | Schawinsky brought a lower-case logotype and photomontage to Olivetti advertising | Consumer-tech identity: lower-case geometric wordmark paired with photo-collage campaign imagery |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black-and-white photography with primaries and red; clean white grounds, approx. `#111111`, `#FFFFFF`, `#D52B1E`, `#1F4FA0`.
- **Type:** grotesques and geometric sans in lower case. Free: Jost, Familjen Grotesk, Work Sans.
- **Form:** photographs, photograms, diagrams, photomontage, geometric marks.
- **Composition:** grid-based asymmetry, modular image fields.

## Attitude

Graphic design as an industrial practice, systematic, collaborative, rooted in rationalist architecture's rigour, at a time when Italian print was split between typesetters and poster painters. The stress fell on systems rather than single brilliant pieces.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `swiss-international`, `new-typography`, shared rational base.
- **Productive tension with:** `fortunato-depero`, Futurist Italian exuberance against rational studio method gives playful yet systematic Italian brands.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic`, dissolving forms undo the objective photography at the studio's core.

## Don't copy

The Studio Boggeri "B" mark (first conceived by Bruno Munari); Schawinsky's Olivetti calendar and M40 brochure; Huber's 1942 brochure cover; Nizzoli's Montecatini brochure.
