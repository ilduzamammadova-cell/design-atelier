# Peter Behrens

**Essence:** The first corporate designer. For AEG he gave one industrial company a single visual language across trademark, typeface, catalogue, kettle and factory, built on geometric grids and classical clarity.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · One company, one language | Appointed artistic adviser to AEG in 1907, he designed the trademark, typography, catalogues, advertising, electrical products and buildings. It is often called the first comprehensive corporate identity. | A single token source powers brand, product UI, hardware surfaces, packaging and environments. Logo, type, colour and grid are specified once and consumed everywhere; no surface gets local exceptions. |
| G2 · Grid from square and circle | After J. L. M. Lauweriks joined his Düsseldorf school (1904), Behrens built layouts on grids derived from a square drawn around a circle, often in 1:1.5 or 1:2 rectangles. | Layout grid from a square-and-circle module: card aspect ratios 1:1, 2:3, 1:2 only; circular elements (avatars, dials, badges) inscribed exactly in square cells; poster and hero layouts subdivided by halving. |
| G3 · Classical capitals for an industrial brand | Behrens-Antiqua (1908) sought the authority of Roman inscriptions and gave AEG material a clean, sober voice. In 1900 he had already set book text in sans serif. | Pair inscriptional capitals (Cinzel or a similar Trajan-derived face) for the brand headline with a sober grotesque (Archivo, Work Sans) for UI. Caps are tracked +8% and used only for titles and wordmark. |
| G4 · Families of products from shared parts | His AEG electric kettles combined a few body shapes, materials and finishes into many models. | Variant systems built combinatorially: shape × size × tone tokens generate a component family (e.g. 3 shapes × 3 sizes × 3 tones). Document the matrix, not individual one-off designs. |
| G5 · Energy as geometric radiation | The 1907–08 Metallfadenlampe advertisement shows a bulb at the centre of concentric dotted arcs, with light expressed as a geometric pattern. | Radial dot-matrix patterns for power, progress and connectivity states (concentric dotted rings expanding at 1.2s intervals), replacing glow effects and lens flares. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black #121212, warm white #EDE6D6, gold/ochre #C9A227, oxide red #9C3B2B, deep blue-green #24433F.
- **Type:** Behrens-Schrift (1902), Behrens-Kursiv (1907), Behrens-Antiqua (1908), all with Klingspor; AEG lettering derived from inscriptional capitals. Free stand-ins: Cinzel (capitals), Archivo or Work Sans for supporting text.
- **Form:** honeycomb-hexagon AEG trademark (1907–08), geometric borders, dotted radiating patterns, strict symmetry.
- **Composition:** axial symmetry; rectangles in square-based ratios; generous margins; strong black-and-white contrast.

## Attitude

Behrens moved from Jugendstil painter to architect of industrial culture. He held that typography expresses the character of an age, and that art and industry must unite to give machine production dignity. That belief helped found the Deutsche Werkbund (1907). Gropius and Mies van der Rohe both worked in his office.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `corporate-identity`, `otl-aicher`: the lineage of total identity systems.
- **Productive tension with:** `william-morris`: Morris's hidden-structure patterning used within Behrens's industrial grid gives warmth in an engineered brand.
- **Clashes with:** `jules-cheret`: sober industrial symmetry against carefree gesture.

## Don't copy

- The AEG trademarks Behrens drew (hexagonal honeycomb mark and lettered versions), AEG posters and catalogues.
- Behrens-Antiqua and Behrens-Schrift outlines; the Werkbund 1914 poster.
