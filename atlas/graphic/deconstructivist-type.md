# Deconstructivist Typography (Cranbrook and CalArts)

**Essence:** Typography as discourse: text and image layered so the reader must interpret, exposing how meaning is put together.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Oppositions as structure | Katherine McCoy's Cranbrook graduate poster (1989) overlaid a communication diagram with paired terms from the curriculum (see/read, image/text, art/science). | Comparison and decision pages built as mirrored columns of paired concepts; the central axis line between them is the main graphic element and labels each trade-off. |
| G2 · The diagram as image | Communication-theory diagrams and semiotic models became the composition of posters. | Use the product's real model (flow, data model, process) as the hero illustration; its nodes are clickable entry points into the content. |
| G3 · Marginalia and entropy | Edward Fella's flyers added asides, notes and invented letters to the main message, using copier decay, stencils, clip art and hand lettering. | Margin annotations: secondary commentary in a handwritten-style face in the side column, joined by thin leader lines to the text it comments on; collapses into footnotes on mobile. |
| G4 · Reader as co-author | Multiple readings were intended; the viewer constructs meaning instead of receiving it. | Layered content in one spatial layout: summary, detail and commentary layers the reader toggles; the layout does not jump when a layer is switched. |
| G5 · Vernacular as source | Students looked past universal sans-serifs to shop signs, catalogues and everyday America (after Venturi and Scott Brown). | One vernacular reference per project (a local sign, a form, a receipt) documented with a photo, and translated into one token: a face, a rule style or a label format. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** duotone gradients (red to blue), collage colour, printed photographs with flat process tints.
- **Type:** everything at once, Univers, Franklin Gothic, Bodoni, typewriter, hand lettering, and new faces such as Keedy Sans and Template Gothic → free: Libre Franklin, Bodoni Moda, Courier Prime, Caveat for annotation.
- **Form:** diagrams, arrows, brackets, collaged student work, architectural photographs.
- **Composition:** dense layers, multiple reading paths, text broken into fields.
- **Key names:** Katherine and Michael McCoy (co-chairs at Cranbrook 1971 to 95), Edward Fella, Allen Hori, Andrew Blauvelt, Jeffery Keedy, P. Scott and Laurie Haycock Makela, Martin Venezky.

## Attitude

The "Cranbrook discourse" drew on semiotics and French post-structuralism (Barthes, Derrida) and on architecture's postmodern turn. The McCoys rejected a single method: students were expected to find their own philosophy. Communication was no longer seen as a one-way solution to a problem but as an exchange in which meaning is negotiated.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `emigre`, `jan-van-toorn`, the magazine that published the work and the Dutch critic of neutral design.
- **Productive tension with:** `muriel-cooper`, her depth-as-hierarchy turns layered discourse into navigable layers.
- **Clashes with:** `bruno-monguzzi`, enforced silence and layered discourse cannot share a page.

## Don't copy

Katherine McCoy's Cranbrook graduate programme poster (1989), Allen Hori's "Typography as Discourse" poster (1989), Andrew Blauvelt's printmaking poster (1987), Edward Fella's Detroit Focus Gallery flyers.
