# Fortunato Depero (1892 to 1960)

**Essence:** Futurism made friendly, mechanical toys, puppet geometry and a whole brand world built from interlocking angular figures.

Depero (1892 to 1960) joined the Futurists in 1914, opened his Casa d'Arte Futurista in Rovereto (1919), worked in New York from 1928 to 1930, and spent about a decade (c. 1926 to 36) building Campari's visual world.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · A brand is a world, not a logo | For Campari he produced posters, press ads, a futurist "single issue" booklet (1931) and the 1932 Campari Soda bottle, a recognisable universe rather than one mark | Build a kit of 10 to 20 modular figures and shapes in the brand palette and use it across onboarding, empty states, 404s, packaging and merch, instead of a single mascot |
| G2 · Robot-puppet figures | People assembled from cones, cylinders and wedges like wooden toys (his *Balli Plastici* marionettes, 1918) | Illustration rule: figures built only from primitives on a 45° grid, max 8 parts each; motion = rigid parts rotating at joints, no squash-and-stretch |
| G3 · Letters as architecture | His Book Pavilion at the 1927 Monza Biennale used giant letters as the building's walls | Section headers whose letterforms act as containers: SVG/`clip-path` masks cut from the wordmark frame photos or become card shapes |
| G4 · The container is the ad | The Campari Soda bottle carries no paper label: an inverted cone of frosted glass with the name moulded in | Make the product's container shape distinctive enough to work without text, an app-icon silhouette or button shape recognisable in grayscale at 24px |
| G5 · Self-published object | *Depero Futurista* (1927), his portfolio bound with two industrial bolts | Portfolio or case-study sites treated as an object: hardware details (bolts, rivets) as fasteners on cards; page turns with physical weight |
| G6 · Diagonal stage, flat colour | Posters split on steep diagonals with vibrant flat planes and repeated angular forms | Hero layouts split on a 30°/60° diagonal; flat saturated fills only; repeat one angular motif 3 to 5 times for rhythm |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** flat red, yellow, black, white and cobalt, approx. `#E2231A`, `#FFC20E`, `#111111`, `#F5F0E6`, `#1F4E9E`.
- **Type:** hand-built block capitals, stencil-like, often set on diagonals. Free: Big Shoulders Display, Bungee, Archivo Black; stencil: Stardos Stencil.
- **Form:** interlocking planes, toy-like figures, cones, gears.
- **Composition:** theatrical staging, diagonal thrust, densely packed.

## Attitude

The Futurist "reconstruction of the universe" meant art for the bar, the shop window and the toy box; commerce was a legitimate stage, not a betrayal. He worked within Fascist Italy's cultural apparatus, take the playfulness, not the politics.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `memphis`, `push-pin-studios`, playful geometry and saturated colour with a wink.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams`, rigorously restrained hardware plus one Depero-style toy figure in setup and packaging gives friendly product onboarding.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, organic whiplash curves fight his rigid joints and produce visual mush.

## Don't copy

The Campari Soda bottle form; the bolted *Depero Futurista* book; his Campari advertising figures; the Book Pavilion; the *Balli Plastici* puppets; his *Vanity Fair* covers.
