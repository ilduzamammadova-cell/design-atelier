# Emigre (Rudy VanderLans & Zuzana Licko)

**Essence:** Accept the machine's limits and make them the aesthetic: bitmap fonts, a magazine that changed shape every issue, and a foundry for uncomfortable new letters.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Native resolution | Licko's first fonts (Emigre, Oakland, Matrix, 1985 to 86) were drawn *for* the coarse Macintosh screen and printer instead of imitating metal type. | One brand display face built on the real pixel grid (integer stems, e.g. 12px cap height), rendered crisp without smoothing at its native sizes; never scaled to fractional sizes. |
| G2 · Legibility is learned | Licko's position that people read best what they read most, which started the 1990s "legibility wars". | Introduce an unusual face gradually: first in short labels, then headlines, measuring comprehension at each step. Never in forms, errors or legal text. |
| G3 · Format follows the issue | No two issues looked alike; guest editors, sizes and structures changed (letter size from no. 33, books with Princeton Architectural Press at the end). | Each season of an editorial channel gets a new layout template while reading controls, navigation and type sizes for body stay fixed. |
| G4 · Discourse as product | The magazine published criticism, interviews and debate alongside work too experimental for other journals. | A public design journal or changelog where designers explain decisions; design notes are first-class content with their own URL and typography. |
| G5 · Revival with one deliberate twist | Mrs Eaves (1996) reworked Baskerville with a small x-height and generous ligatures; Filosofia rethought Bodoni for legibility. | Pick a historical text face and change one parameter on purpose (x-height, spacing, contrast) for the product's reading context; record the deviation in the type spec. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** mostly black on white or newsprint; covers vary freely; layered greys from early laser output.
- **Type:** Emigre, Oakland (later Lo-Res), Matrix, Modula, Citizen; outside contributors such as Template Gothic (Barry Deck), Dead History (P. Scott Makela), Keedy Sans, Exocet (Jonathan Barnbrook); Mrs Eaves, Filosofia, Base → free: Silkscreen or Pixelify Sans (bitmap), Libre Baskerville, Bodoni Moda.
- **Form:** stair-stepped pixels, layered type, typewriter and copier images in the first issue.
- **Composition:** different every issue; often dense, layered, deliberately unconventional sequencing.

## Attitude

VanderLans found Dutch modernism too restrictive; Licko, trained partly in programming, approached type as something to build within the computer's constraints. Calling themselves "new primitives", they chose to work with the Mac rather than force it into old standards. Massimo Vignelli publicly dismissed their work; the feud made the magazine famous. It ended with issue 69, "The End", in 2005.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `april-greiman`, `deconstructivist-type`, early digital pioneers and the academic laboratory that fed the magazine.
- **Productive tension with:** `massimo-vignelli`, a strict canonical system with one Emigre-style display face keeps order and gains a voice.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, fonts that change with every issue undermine the fixed standards a corporate system needs.

## Don't copy

The *Emigre* covers and spreads; the outlines of Emigre-licensed fonts (licence them instead); Mrs Eaves' signature ligature set.
