# Bruno Munari (1907 to 1998)

**Essence:** Play as a method of knowledge, useless machines, books without words, and simplicity reached through serious curiosity.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Useless machines | From the 1930s he hung light, balanced abstract constructions that move only to delight, kinetic art without function. | One purposeless, delightful object per product (footer mobile, 404 page, idle state): shapes that sway with cursor or device tilt. It must never block a task and must respect reduced motion. |
| G2 · Unreadable books | The *libri illeggibili* (from 1949) tell stories through paper colour, cut holes, textures and page sequence, with no words. | A wordless onboarding or story sequence: coloured panels, cut-out masks and layered reveals advanced by swipe; meaning carried by sequence and shape alone, with an accessible text alternative. |
| G3 · Variations on one name | His Campari poster (mid-1960s) repeats the brand name in dozens of different lettering styles, turning one word into a crowd. | Campaign pattern: the wordmark rendered in 20+ type treatments in a dense mosaic; the name is constant, the style varies. Use for celebratory or launch moments only. |
| G4 · Transparent layers | He composed images by superimposing transparent sheets so word and image interact as you turn pages. | Stacked translucent layers (opacity 40 to 60%, multiply) that slide independently to assemble an image on scroll; each layer meaningful alone. |
| G5 · Learning by doing | His children's books and workshops (e.g. at the Brera gallery) invited touch, discovery and making instead of instruction. | Tutorials as sandboxes: the user manipulates real objects with guardrails; no blocking modal instructions; hints appear only after inactivity. |
| G6 · Simplification as hard work | Munari insisted that removing is harder than adding; the simple result hides great effort. | Every design review records what was removed; a component may not ship until one element has been taken away from its first version. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** clear primaries, black and white, translucent overlaps; playful but not saturated to excess.
- **Type:** grotesques and classical romans, freely varied; hand lettering → Space Grotesk, Libre Caslon, Bricolage Grotesque for playful display.
- **Form:** mobiles, cut paper, holes, transparencies, forks that gesture like hands, cubes and simple objects.
- **Composition:** open, airy, sequential; the page as a space to explore.

## Attitude

Munari held that the designer is today's artist, not as a genius, but as someone who restores contact between art and the public. He refused boundaries between art, design, writing and teaching, and treated children as the most serious audience of all. His book *Arte come mestiere* (1966; English *Design as Art*) explains the method.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `paul-rand`, `corita-kent`, designers who made play and children's directness central.
- **Productive tension with:** `max-bill`, fellow concrete-art founder; exact proportion plus kinetic play gives systematic yet joyful interfaces.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, locked, closed systems leave no room for the useless machine.

## Don't copy

The Campari poster, *Macchine inutili*, *Libri illeggibili*, *Le forchette di Munari*, his children's books (e.g. *Nella notte buia*), Danese objects (Cubo ashtray 1957, Falkland lamp).

## Study

- *Design as Art* (*Arte come mestiere*, 1966); Corraini Edizioni reissues of his books.
- Movimento Arte Concreta (co-founded 1948); Compasso d'Oro awards (1954, 1955, 1979).
- Jeremy Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design* (2001), Munari entry; Corraini Edizioni (Mantua), his principal publisher today.
