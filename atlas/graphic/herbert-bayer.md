# Herbert Bayer (1900–1985)

**Essence:** Turned Bauhaus experiment into usable systems — a single-case alphabet, a hierarchy you can read at a glance, and exhibitions designed around the visitor's eye.

Bauhaus student in Weimar (1921–23), master of the new typography and printing workshop in Dessau (1925–28), art director at the Dorland agency in Berlin and of *die neue linie* (1928–38), emigrant to the US in 1938 (MoMA's *Bauhaus 1919–1928* show), later design consultant to the Container Corporation of America and editor-designer of the *World Geo-Graphic Atlas* (1953).

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · One alphabet, not two | His *universal* alphabet (1925) dropped capitals as redundant; the Bauhaus print shop went lower-case | Lower-case for all UI labels, navigation, buttons and headings; capitals only for proper nouns and acronyms; geometric sans to keep it calm |
| G2 · Hierarchy by measured contrast | Size and weight steps set by an objective ranking of content; bars lead the eye (Kandinsky 60th-birthday poster, 1926) | Write a content priority list before layout; type scale of exactly 4 steps derived from it (e.g. 64/32/18/12); one heavy bar links title to the key fact or CTA |
| G3 · Design for the field of vision | His diagram for exhibitions (Paris *Section Allemande*, 1930) tilted panels above and below eye level toward the viewer | Place content by the user's real perceptual field: primary actions in the thumb zone on mobile; on kiosks/TV/AR, panels sized and angled to the viewing position |
| G4 · Surreal montage for commerce | Photomontage covers for *die neue linie* made modernism palatable to a mass readership | Marketing imagery: one surreal juxtaposition (scale or material swap) inside an otherwise clean, grid-locked layout |
| G5 · Information as a world | The *World Geo-Graphic Atlas* unified maps, charts, pictographs and colour codes into one visual language | Data-dense products use one pictogram set and one colour code across all charts; each screen addresses one theme |
| G6 · Type as architecture | Kiosk, newsstand and cinema-facade projects (1924–25) made lettering structural | Wayfinding where type is built into the environment: large lower-case labels on walls and floors instead of separate signs |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black and white with one primary, often red; later softer airbrushed tones — approx. `#111111`, `#F5F3EE`, `#D7261E`, `#2C5DAA`.
- **Type:** universal (concept), Futura, Venus Grotesk. Free: Jost, Josefin Sans, Poppins.
- **Form:** circles, bars, pointing hands, photomontage, pictographs.
- **Composition:** asymmetric grid; sometimes the whole aligned block rotated as one unit.

## Attitude

Communication as service and design as total environment. His pragmatism had a dark chapter: in Berlin he produced exhibition material for the Nazi regime before emigrating — a reminder that systems serve whoever commissions them.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `new-typography`, `swiss-international` — shared asymmetric, sans-serif rationality.
- **Productive tension with:** `alexey-brodovitch` — Bayer's measured hierarchy plus Brodovitch's intuitive pacing gives magazine apps that are both scannable and alive.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type` — deliberate illegibility undoes the measured hierarchy that is his core.

## Don't copy

The universal alphabet drawings; the Kandinsky birthday poster (1926); the Thuringian emergency banknotes (1923); *die neue linie* covers; the *Section Allemande* poster and catalogue (1930); *World Geo-Graphic Atlas* spreads; the photomontage *Lonely Metropolitan* (1932).
