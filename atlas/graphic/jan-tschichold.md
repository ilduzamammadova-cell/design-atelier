# Jan Tschichold (1902 to 1974)

**Essence:** The great explainer and then the great apostate, he codified asymmetric functional typography, later returned to classical symmetry, and in both phases served the text with obsessive precision.

A calligrapher trained in Leipzig, he saw the 1923 Bauhaus exhibition, published "elementare typographie" (1925) and *Die neue Typographie* (1928), designed cinema posters for the Phoebus-Palast in Munich (1926 to 27), was arrested by the Nazis in 1933 and moved to Basel. At Penguin Books (1947 to 49) he reformed hundreds of titles with written house rules; his Sabon typeface followed in 1967.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Rules written for the compositor | He taught printers through demonstration pages and, at Penguin, a short set of composition rules that standardised spacing, punctuation, capitals and numerals | A 1 to 2 page "composition rules" document (dashes, quotes, numerals, abbreviations, spacing) enforced by CMS/content linting, typographic quality as a checkable rule set, not taste |
| G2 · Hang everything from one axis | Asymmetric balance: elements hang from a strong vertical (or diagonal) axis with white space as counterweight | Hero layout with one vertical axis at ⅓ width; every text element aligns to it; a single shape (e.g. a circle) on the opposite side balances the mass |
| G3 · Small type, placed with care | His posters set tiny, exactly spaced text beside one dominant element (*Konstruktivisten*, 1937: a list of names under a single sand-coloured disc) | Small text (11 to 12px) gets the same care as headlines: line-height 1.5, tracking +1 to 2%, aligned to a key edge of the dominant visual; secondary is not sloppier |
| G4 · Method follows the text | After 1933 he argued that asymmetric sans suits commerce and technique, while literature deserves classical symmetry | Explicit content-type → layout mapping: reading views centred with a serif at 60 to 70 characters; dashboards and transactional screens asymmetric sans |
| G5 · Page proportion as canon | His late research on book proportions sought harmonious relations between page and text block | Reading-view margins from a fixed ratio (e.g. inner : top : outer : bottom = 2 : 3 : 4 : 6, scaled to viewport); measure capped at ~66ch |
| G6 · Film still + diagonal band | The Phoebus posters combine film stills, diagonal planes and lower-case sans | Media title cards: still image, one diagonal colour band, lower-case title, date in small caps-sized text |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black and red on white early; sand/gold discs and spectral type later; quiet classical inks after 1935, `#111111`, `#E2231A`, `#C9A86A`, `#FFFFFF`.
- **Type:** early grotesques and Futura-era sans; later Sabon, Bembo, Garamond. Free: Archivo or Hanken Grotesk for the early work; EB Garamond or Crimson Pro for the later.
- **Form:** rules, circles, photographs, negative photographic portraits.
- **Composition:** asymmetric axis with large white areas (1925 to 38); centred classical book pages (1940s on).

## Attitude

Clarity is the aim, and good typography disappears into the service of reading. Having been jailed by a regime obsessed with order, he came to distrust absolutism in design and defended freedom to use any historical tool that fits the content.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `new-typography`, `swiss-international`, he is the bridge between them.
- **Productive tension with:** `arts-and-crafts`, classical book craft against systematised modernism gives elegant, rule-driven reading apps.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type`, it attacks the legibility he spent a life defending.

## Don't copy

The Phoebus-Palast posters (*Die Hose*, *Die Frau ohne Namen*, 1927); *Konstruktivisten* (1937) and *Der Berufsphotograph* (1938) posters; the "elementare typographie" pages; Penguin Shakespeare covers; Sabon's letterforms.
