# Willem Sandberg (1897 to 1984)

**Essence:** Modernism with its hands dirty, crisp type set against torn paper, red over blue, cheap kraft beside glossy plates, all arranged as if thinking aloud.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Torn edge against machine edge | Large letters and shapes torn from coloured paper sit beside sharp sans-serif type; the contrast of edges carries the energy. The habit began in wartime scarcity and stayed. | One irregular, scanned element per view (a torn-paper shape or letter) carrying the key word or number; everything else is crisp vector type and hard rectangles. Never two torn elements on one screen. |
| G2 · Lines broken by sense | His texts are unjustified and broken into sentence fragments placed freely on the page, so each line holds one thought. | Hero, intro and campaign copy is authored as phrase lines (2 to 6 words, one idea per line) in the CMS; flush left, ragged right, no hyphenation, no automatic balancing. Body text below keeps normal flow. |
| G3 · Overprint makes a third colour | Red printed over blue yields a third, darker colour; he mixed primaries rather than keeping them apart as De Stijl did. | Two accent inks (≈ #E3262B, ≈ #1F4FA0) with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` where they overlap; the resulting violet appears only at overlaps, never as a token of its own. |
| G4 · Rough paper for words, smooth for art | Catalogue text ran on coarse brown or recycled paper; artworks were reproduced on smooth white stock tipped in; tracing-paper sheets veiled images until the page turned. | Two surfaces with fixed roles: text on a warm, faintly textured ground (≈ #D9CBB0); images on clean white cards with a 1px edge. Progressive reveal: an image first appears under a 60% translucent veil that lifts on scroll or tap. |
| G5 · The letter as a vessel | In *experimenta typographica* the counters and spaces between letters become pictures, a u turned into a jug filled with smaller letters. | Once per page, one oversized letter of the headline (or the product initial) becomes a container: its counter holds a photo, a number or a short list. The rest of the typography stays plain. |
| G6 · Clash of voices for emphasis | Bold slab or ultrabold sans meets a delicate script for the accented word; emphasis by changing voice, not by bolding. | Inside a sans paragraph, the single emphasised word switches to a script or slab at 1.25 to 1.5× size in the accent red. One emphasised word per block; no bold or italic elsewhere. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** signal red ≈ #E3262B, primary blue ≈ #1F4FA0, overprint violet ≈ #3A1F4A, kraft ≈ #B9976B, newsprint off-white ≈ #EFEAE0, black.
- **Type:** grotesk sans and bold Egyptians with occasional script → free: Archivo or Schibsted Grotesk for text, Alfa Slab One for heavy accents, Pinyon Script for the delicate counter-voice.
- **Form:** torn paper letters and bars, hard-edged colour blocks, enlarged single letters, mixed paper stocks.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, airy, apparently casual but carefully balanced; information revealed in sequence across pages.

## Attitude

Sandberg directed the Stedelijk Museum from 1945 until 1962 and designed nearly all its printed matter himself, treating catalogues as a conversation with the public rather than a record. During the occupation he forged documents for the resistance and made *experimenta typographica* in hiding. He trusted chance, rough materials and plain speech over polish and symmetry.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `hendrik-werkman`, `kurt-schwitters`, found material, chance and the poetry of print.
- **Productive tension with:** `massimo-vignelli`, Sandberg's torn accents inside a disciplined Vignelli grid give a museum identity that is both systematic and alive.
- **Clashes with:** `dieter-rams`, Rams removes every trace of the hand; Sandberg's work is the trace of the hand.

## Don't copy

The *experimenta typographica* pages (1943 to 44; published 1956), the Stedelijk catalogue and poster covers including the torn *bibliotheek* (1957), *Museum journaal* covers, the *nu* pages (1959), and the enlarged SM letters used for the museum.
