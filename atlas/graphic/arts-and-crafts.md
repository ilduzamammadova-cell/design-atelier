# Arts and Crafts

**Essence:** A moral revolt against cheap industrial goods. Make useful things well, from honest materials, with every part planned as one whole, and believe that good design improves life for the people who make and the people who use.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The book as architecture | Emery Walker argued (1888) that paper, ink, type, spacing, margins, illustration and ornament must be planned together like a building. Ornament had to belong to the page scheme. | Design whole screens and flows before components: a single spec page per view fixing type, spacing, imagery and decoration together. No component ships without being shown in at least one complete composition. |
| G2 · The spread is the unit | Private-press designers composed the two facing pages as one image, with margins proportioned so the pair sits together. | On tablet and desktop, design two-pane layouts (list + detail, text + notes) as a single composition: shared top line, mirrored margins (inner narrower than outer), one baseline grid across both panes. |
| G3 · Truth to materials | Work should show what it is made of and how. Ruskin and Morris attacked imitation (fake marble, machined "carving"). | Digital honesty: no fake paper, leather or stitching. Screens are light: depth from light and shadow, texture only from real content (photos of real material). Interactions reflect actual system state (no fake progress). |
| G4 · The pure-type counter-strain | The Doves Press (Cobden-Sanderson and Walker, from 1900) rejected illustration and ornament altogether: only fine type, spacing, paper and presswork, as in the 1903 Doves Bible. | A "reader" mode with type only: one text face, one size plus headings, red for initials or section marks, no images or chrome. Polish goes into spacing, measure and paragraph rhythm. |
| G5 · Designer and maker together | Guilds and workshops (Morris & Co., Century Guild, Guild of Handicraft) united design with making and treated workers as craftspeople, not machine parts. | Organize as design-engineers: the person designing a component also builds or pairs on it. Craft reviews happen in the running product, not only in mockups. |
| G6 · Beauty as a public good | The movement held that ordinary people deserved beautiful, durable things, though its own products were often costly. | Spend polish on public, unglamorous surfaces (forms, receipts, settings, error states, transit and civic flows) to the same standard as the marketing page. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** vegetable-dye palette: indigo #2C3E6B, madder #A23B2A, weld yellow #C9A640, leaf green #5B7A3A, undyed linen #EAE2CF; books in black and red on handmade paper #EFE7D3.
- **Type:** revived incunabula and Venetian romans (Morris's Golden), Doves Type, Ashendene; black letter (Troy, Chaucer). Free stand-ins: Goudy Bookletter 1911, EB Garamond, UnifrakturMaguntia for black-letter accents.
- **Form:** woodcut borders and initials, stylized plants, birds and vines, hand-printed texture.
- **Composition:** dense text blocks on classically margined pages; spreads designed as units; decoration framing text, never floating.

## Attitude

Led by John Ruskin's writing and William Morris's practice, the movement saw industrial production as degrading both object and worker. Its answer was fitness for purpose, honest materials, joy in labour and the unity of all the arts. Its contradiction (handmade beauty only the rich could afford) pushed its heirs toward reconciling design with industry.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `william-morris`, `illuminated-manuscripts`: sources and centre of the movement.
- **Productive tension with:** `peter-behrens`: Arts and Crafts ethics without the anti-machine stance becomes the Werkbund; honest craft scaled by industry.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`: ironic surface play against sincerity and truth to materials.

## Don't copy

- Kelmscott and Doves Press pages; Morris & Co. patterns; Century Guild *Hobby Horse* designs.
- Recognisable Roycroft and Essex House marks and layouts.
