# Jan van Toorn

**Essence:** Design that shows its seams, so the viewer starts questioning the message instead of swallowing it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Dialogic, unfinished montage | Catalogues and calendars assembled from fragments, typed and handwritten notes and press photos, left visibly unresolved so the viewer completes the meaning. | Report and editorial pages show sources as fragments: quote cards with raw crops, original document snippets, captions stating who said what and when. No seamless composite hero. |
| G2 · Circle the hidden actor | In a 1972 calendar a crowd of everyday faces contains a ringed figure of power. | Annotation layer: one circle or underline drawn on an image to point out the detail that matters, always paired with a caption explaining why. |
| G3 · Same image, altered reproduction | A series of posters reused one TV still, each with different colour separations and hand colouring, exposing how media make images. | A campaign or issue series reuses one image across all items, changing only crop and colour separation; the argument appears in the grid view of the whole set. |
| G4 · Each publication answers its subject | Against a fixed house style, every catalogue and exhibition got its own typographic response. | Shared components, but type pairing and accent colour are chosen per publication from a written content brief; the brief is stored next to the theme. |
| G5 · Format that performs | A fake-fur catalogue cover, a book whose binding must be destroyed to be read, a magazine cover that doubles as the postal wrapper. | One meaningful physical-style action in onboarding or packaging (tear, unfold, reveal) that relates directly to content; never repeated as a gimmick. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** process colours as signals; red or blue tints over news photographs; office-paper whites.
- **Type:** idiosyncratic choices per job, typewriter faces, handwriting, plain grotesques → free: IBM Plex Mono, Courier Prime, Archivo.
- **Form:** photomontage, press photos, stamps, circles and arrows of annotation.
- **Composition:** additive, crowded, collage-like; the act of assembling stays visible.

## Attitude

Van Toorn held that graphic design is never neutral: left unexamined, it serves the status quo. He wanted the audience to stay able to judge the motives behind a message against their own experience. In the 1972 debate with Wim Crouwel he argued for subjectivity, chaos and the everyday against cool professionalism; later he led the Jan van Eyck Academie and taught widely.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `adbusters`, `metahaven` — critique of media and power through their own visual codes.
- **Productive tension with:** `wim-crouwel` — the classic Dutch opposition; an objective grid carrying deliberately unfinished montage.
- **Clashes with:** `peter-saville` — Saville aestheticises borrowed images; Van Toorn exposes them. Combined, critique turns into a stylish surface.

## Don't copy

The Mart. Spruijt calendars (1960s–70s), the Van Abbemuseum catalogues, the *Mens en Omgeving* posters (1982), the Willem Drees stamp (1986), the *Dutch Art + Architecture Today* covers.
