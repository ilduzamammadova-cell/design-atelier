# Isotype

**Essence:** Count with pictures, repeat one standard pictogram to show quantity, so anyone can compare without reading numbers.

Begun as the "Vienna Method" at Otto Neurath's Social and Economic Museum (1925), with Marie Reidemeister (later Neurath, 1898 to 1986) as chief "transformer" and Gerd Arntz (1900 to 1988) designing most pictograms from 1928. Renamed Isotype in 1935, it moved to The Hague (1934) and England (1940); Marie continued the work until 1971. Futura was adopted soon after its release.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · More = more symbols, never bigger symbols | Quantity shown by repeating a fixed-size unit; enlarging a pictogram to mean "more" was forbidden as misleading | Unit charts for public-facing data: 1 icon = N units, rows of 10, a cut icon for the remainder; area-scaled icons banned in the chart library |
| G2 · The transformer | Before any drawing, a "transformer" decides what comparison the chart must make and turns raw data into a visual argument | A required step before chart design: one written sentence stating the comparison, approved by data owner and designer; one message per chart |
| G3 · Pictogram grammar | A consistent visual syntax: base figures combine with modifiers (a man + a factory = worker), colour codes categories, silhouettes legible at 5mm | Icon system with compositional grammar: base glyphs + modifier badges on a 24px grid; solid silhouettes, no outlines; legible at 16px |
| G4 · Few flat colours with fixed meaning | A small palette of flat colours, each tied to a category across every chart | Max 5 categorical colours, flat, no gradients; a colour means the same category on every chart in the product |
| G5 · Read it like a line of text | Rows aligned on a common baseline, labels at the left, read left to right | Horizontal unit-chart rows with left-aligned direct labels and equal row height; no legends when labels fit |
| G6 · Data for citizens | Charts made for workers and schoolchildren to understand housing, health and the economy | Public dashboards carry a plain-language caption (reading age ~12) under each chart stating what to notice |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** muted flat red, blue, black, ochre and green on off-white, approx. `#C0392B`, `#2F5D8A`, `#1A1A1A`, `#D9A441`, `#4E7D4A`, `#F4EFE6`.
- **Type:** Futura for labels and titles. Free: Jost.
- **Form:** solid geometric silhouettes (originally linocut), simple maps, repeated rows.
- **Composition:** aligned rows and columns, consistent spacing, pictograms and maps combined.

## Attitude

Visual education as a democratic tool: if facts are visible, citizens can judge for themselves. Neurath's motto summed it up: "Words divide, pictures unite." The method's rigour lies in honest counting, not in style.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `harry-beck`, `ladislav-sutnar`, `otl-aicher`, clarity systems for public information.
- **Productive tension with:** `photomontage`, neutral counting beside emotive montage gives data journalism that is both honest and moving.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic`, distortion and colour flux destroy the countable unit.

## Don't copy

Gerd Arntz's individual pictograms; plates from *Gesellschaft und Wirtschaft* (1930); specific Isotype charts and children's books.
