# László Moholy-Nagy (1895–1946)

**Essence:** Light, photography and type as one medium — a "new vision" in which the designer programs perception instead of decorating surfaces.

He replaced Itten as head of the Bauhaus preliminary course in 1923, designed most of the Bauhausbücher, left for Berlin in 1928, and after Amsterdam and London founded the New Bauhaus in Chicago (1937) and then the School of Design (1939, later Institute of Design).

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Typophoto | The photograph treated as part of the typography, not an inset picture — word and image as one objective message (*Malerei Fotografie Film*, 1925) | Components where text and image share one box: headlines knock out of or run into photos, captions sit on image edges; no default "image, then caption below" stack |
| G2 · Light as material | Photograms and the *Light-Space Modulator* (1930) made transparency, overlap and glow the subject | Layered translucent planes (60–80% opacity, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`) to show stacked states; one global light direction for every gradient and highlight in the UI |
| G3 · Specify, don't hand-make | His "telephone pictures" (1923) were defined on graph paper with an enamel colour chart and fabricated by a sign company | Design as parametric spec: every screen must be rebuildable from tokens, grid coordinates and a written spec alone — test by having someone build it without the file |
| G4 · The new camera eye | Worm's-eye, bird's-eye, raking light and extreme close-up defamiliarise ordinary things | Product imagery from top-down flat-lays and macro crops; avoid mid-distance eye-level shots |
| G5 · Show the medium | The Bauhausbücher prospectus (1929) is a photograph of the metal type that printed it | Brand moments built from the product's real material: documentation that reveals grid and tokens on hover; marketing images made of actual components |
| G6 · Every direction allowed, clarity first | He argued for using all linear directions and contrasts, while insisting communication must never suffer for a preconceived aesthetic | Vertical labels (`writing-mode: vertical-rl`) and rotated section markers allowed on wide screens; body text always horizontal, 16px+ |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, greys and transparent overlays with red and yellow accents — approx. `#111111`, `#EDEDED`, `#9C9C9C`, `#E03C31`, `#F2C14E`.
- **Type:** grotesques and geometric sans, heavy bars, stripes run through words. Free: Schibsted Grotesk, Archivo, Jost.
- **Form:** transparent planes, discs of light, photograms, metal, glass, celluloid.
- **Composition:** right-angle rhythms crossed by diagonal planes; open space; photo and type interlocked.

## Attitude

A technological optimist: he predicted that people unable to use a camera would be the illiterates of the future, and saw design education as the training of perception for an industrial society. *Vision in Motion* (1947) carried the Bauhaus method to American schools.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `bauhaus`, `herbert-bayer` — shared typophoto and workshop logic.
- **Productive tension with:** `hendrik-werkman` — machine light against hand-inked grain gives luminous yet tactile interfaces.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic` — both obsessed with light and colour, but swirl versus clarity produces visual mud.

## Don't copy

His photograms; the *Light-Space Modulator*; the telephone pictures (EM 1–3); Bauhausbücher covers and the 1929 type prospectus; the *Pneumatik* poster; the *Malerei Fotografie Film* jacket; photographs such as *From the Radio Tower Berlin* (1928).
