# Edward Johnston (1872–1944)

**Essence:** The scribe who gave a city its voice — letters reasoned out from the pen and from Roman stone, then stripped to plain strokes so a hurrying public could read them.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Roman proportions, plain stroke | For Frank Pick's London Underground (commissioned 1913, in use from 1916) he drew a sans whose capitals keep the varied widths of Roman inscriptional letters — a near-circular O, wide C and D, narrow E and S — but with an even, unmodulated stroke. Modern without being mechanical. | For wayfinding and navigation labels choose a humanist sans whose capitals vary in width (round O, narrow E/S), not a uniform-width grotesk. All-caps labels get +5% tracking; sentence case for anything longer than three words. |
| G2 · A typeface as infrastructure | One exclusive letterform carried the station names, posters, timetables and later the redrawn bar-and-circle symbol. The type *was* the identity long before logos did that job. | One family is both brand and UI font; the wordmark is simply the product name set in it. No separate "marketing font". Commission or customise two or three signature glyphs (R, a, g) so the face is ownable. |
| G3 · One signature micro-detail | The dots on i and j are small diamonds — a 45° square that became the face's fingerprint (later carried into the punctuation of New Johnston). | Pick one tiny geometric mark and give it every dot job in the system: list bullets, status dots, breadcrumb separators, unread markers, focus caret. Nothing else may use that shape. |
| G4 · Form follows the tool | His teaching derived every letter from a broad-edged pen held at a constant angle; the "foundational hand" grew from close study of a tenth-century English psalter (the Ramsey Psalter, BL Harley MS 2904). | Draw the icon and illustration set with one declared virtual tool: a calligraphic nib at a fixed 30°, thick-to-thin about 2:1, flat terminals. Any glyph that breaks the nib angle is redrawn. |
| G5 · Legible for the moving reader | Letters reduced to their simplest distinct skeletons for people reading signs while walking; the lowercase l borrows the curl of the t so it never reads as I. | Before adopting a face for transit, data or codes, set `Il1 O0 rn/m` at 12px and at 40% blur; reject it if any pair collides. Platform, seat and booking codes always use the disambiguated forms. |
| G6 · Versals and red | His manual *Writing & Illuminating, & Lettering* (1906) teaches built-up versal capitals and red writing to open sections — hierarchy by ink and capital, not by size. | Two-ink hierarchy in long reading: body in near-black, section openings marked by one red versal or red run-in heading at body size. No bold, no size jumps inside running text. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Underground red ≈ #DC241F, deep blue ≈ #0019A8, black ink #1A1A1A, vellum ≈ #F3ECDC; manuscripts in black and vermilion with occasional gold.
- **Type:** Johnston Sans (1916), New Johnston (Eiichi Kono at Banks & Miles, 1979), Johnston100 (Monotype, 2016) — all proprietary to Transport for London → free: Cabin (a humanist sans its designer describes as inspired by Johnston and Gill); for pen-born text, Alegreya.
- **Form:** monoline circles and squares tempered by pen logic; diamond dots; broad-nib minuscules with slanted stress.
- **Composition:** centred, generously margined book pages; on signs, a single name on a single band.

## Attitude

Johnston believed letters should be made, not imitated: study a great historical model until its rules are in your hand, then write freely and plainly. Clarity was a moral and civic duty. Through pupils such as Eric Gill, Graily Hewitt and Alfred Fairbank, and the scribes' society his students founded in 1921, his method shaped a century of British lettering.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `harry-beck`, `eric-gill` — the same civic clarity, from map to sign to book.
- **Productive tension with:** `otl-aicher` — Aicher's rationalist pictogram grid wearing a pen-reasoned humanist voice gives wayfinding that is systematic yet warm.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type` — Johnston's whole project is the instant, unambiguous read that deconstruction deliberately withholds.

## Don't copy

The Johnston Sans, New Johnston and Johnston100 outlines; the Underground roundel and its name bar (TfL trademarks); his signed manuscript pieces and the Ramsey Psalter's script as a font. Take proportion, the single detail and the tool-logic, not the letters.
