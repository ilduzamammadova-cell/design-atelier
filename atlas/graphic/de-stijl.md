# De Stijl

**Essence:** Reduce everything to horizontal, vertical, three primaries and three neutrals — then balance unequal parts until they feel equal.

Founded in 1917 around Theo van Doesburg's journal *De Stijl* (published until 1932), with Piet Mondrian, Bart van der Leck, Vilmos Huszár, J. J. P. Oud and later Gerrit Rietveld. Its graphic face: Huszár's block-built logo (1917), Van Doesburg's square-module letters and horizontal journal format (1921), Rietveld's Schröder House (1924) as built proof.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Asymmetric equilibrium | Unequal areas held in balance by the weight and placement of colour; never mirror symmetry | Dashboards with unequal panels (5/12 + 7/12, 2/3 + 1/3); the strongest colour goes in the *smallest* panel to balance the large neutral one |
| G2 · Six-colour system | Red, yellow, blue + black, white, grey — nothing else | Exactly six tokens (`#D52B1E`, `#F5C400`, `#1D4E9E`, `#111111`, `#FFFFFF`, `#BDBDBD`); the three primaries encode three categories and are never decorative |
| G3 · Bars as structure, colour as plane | Black lines divide space; colour fills whole planes; no floating objects | Thick rules (4–8px, black) form an irregular rectilinear grid; panels are fills, not cards; no radii, no shadows |
| G4 · Letters from the square | Huszár and Van Doesburg built letters on a square module, sacrificing curves | Display lettering and icons drawn on a 5×5 or 7×7 module grid; never below 32px; text stays in a neutral sans |
| G5 · Open composition | Planes run to the edge and imply continuation beyond the frame (as Rietveld's planes extend past the house's volume) | Rules and colour panels bleed off the viewport edge rather than stopping at the container; content stays within the grid |
| G6 · One language from painting to chair | Art absorbed into everyday objects, interiors and buildings | A single token set governs app, packaging, signage and space; no medium gets its own visual language |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** as G2; red often the only second colour in print because it could compete with black.
- **Type:** sans-serif, set in tight rectangular blocks; constructed square alphabets for logos. Free: Archivo Black, Space Grotesk; module-based display: Silkscreen or Pixelify Sans as a starting point.
- **Form:** rectangles and straight lines only (diagonals later disputed).
- **Composition:** orthogonal, asymmetric, open-ended; type balanced at the corners of an implied rectangle.

## Attitude

After the war the group sought universal laws of harmony as a prototype for a new social order; the Dutch *schoon* means both pure and beautiful. Subjective expression was banned. The movement split over Van Doesburg's diagonal "elementarism" (c. 1924–25) and effectively ended with his death in 1931.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `bauhaus`, `swiss-international` — De Stijl ideas reached Weimar through Van Doesburg's 1921–22 stay.
- **Productive tension with:** `dada` — Van Doesburg's own pairing; a strict primary grid with one randomised collage element gives order with a pulse.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — curves and ornament contradict the orthogonal axiom.

## Don't copy

Mondrian's compositions (e.g. *Composition with Red, Yellow and Blue*); Rietveld's Red Blue Chair and Schröder House; Huszár's *De Stijl* logo; Van Doesburg's alphabet and journal covers; Van der Leck's Batavier Line poster.
