# Postmodern New Wave Typography

**Essence:** The Swiss grid learning to dance: the International Style's vocabulary kept, its grammar deliberately broken.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Grid shown, then broken | Basel students set up an obvious structure (rules, bars, modules) so that a departure from it reads as intent, not accident. | Draw the 12-column grid as 1px rules at 8% ink in the hero. Exactly one element per view (headline or image) crosses it by at least one full column; everything else snaps. |
| G2 · Stair-step rules | Heavy bars and stepped lines punctuate space and lead the eye down the page like a staircase. | Section dividers are 4px bars that step down in three 24px increments, ending where the next heading starts. Maximum one stepped divider per screen. |
| G3 · Weight shift inside a word | Italic or bold switches mid-word to stress a syllable or a meaning, something the Swiss canon forbade. | In display headings of 40px or more, allow one weight change inside a single word. Never in body, labels or navigation. |
| G4 · Wide-tracked small sans | Letterspacing, abandoned during the phototype era, comes back as texture and tone. | Eyebrow labels in sans caps, 11–12px, tracking +0.18em, three words at most. Paragraph text stays at 0 tracking. |
| G5 · Reversed key word | A single word knocked out in white from a chunky black block becomes the emphasis device. | Emphasis component: text inset in a solid ink block, 0.15em horizontal padding, 0 radius. One per content block. |
| G6 · Sunny postmodern palette | San Francisco designers paired warm greys with lavender, turquoise and peach, plus confetti-like gestures. | Neutral surfaces in warm grey; three pastel accents used only as flat fills behind type, never as text colour (they fail contrast). |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Basel work mostly black on white with process red and blue; Bay Area pastels: lavender `#B7A6D8`, turquoise `#5DC6C0`, peach `#F5B699`, warm grey `#9C968E`.
- **Type:** Akzidenz-Grotesk and Univers, letterspaced, mixed weights; later eccentric revived 1920s–30s display faces (the New York "retro" strand) → free: Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk; for retro display, Limelight or Poiret One sparingly.
- **Form:** stepped rules, dots and dot screens, arrows, diagonals, confetti dashes, overlapping translucent planes.
- **Composition:** structure implied, then violated; diagonal type; dense clusters balanced against open fields.

## Attitude

A generation trained in Swiss rigour found it had become an academic style and decided to test every rule rather than obey it. The pioneers refused the word "style"; they saw their work as research into how typography could carry emotion and complexity. The movement's weakness was speed: within a decade its moves became decoration in the hands of imitators.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `wolfgang-weingart`, `memphis` — the first supplies typographic method, the second the decorative colour and pattern of the 1980s strand.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — keep a real Swiss grid as spine and let only one or two new-wave genes break it; the friction gives controlled liveliness.
- **Clashes with:** `kenya-hara` — layered bars and tracked labels fill exactly the emptiness that carries Hara's meaning; the mix looks merely unfinished.

## Don't copy

Weingart's Kunstkredit and Das Schweizer Plakat posters, April Greiman's China Club and CalArts pieces, Michael Vanderbyl's California Public Radio poster, William Longhauser's Michael Graves poster, Willi Kunz's McLuhan interpretations. Borrow the operations, not the compositions.
