# Blue Note Records (Reid Miles & Francis Wolff)

**Essence:** A consistent formula, tinted session photograph, one extra ink, type sized to the music, recombined differently for every record.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Tinted photo + one ink | Francis Wolff's session photographs were toned in a single colour and paired with one additional ink for names and title, often for budget reasons. | Images rendered as monotone duotone (e.g. deep blue ~#0D3B66 or ochre) plus exactly one accent ink for title and key metadata; no full-colour photography in collection views. |
| G2 · Type size as tempo | Reid Miles made titles huge and condensed, or tiny and lowercase, letting scale and weight carry the mood of the session (e.g. *Midnight Blue*). | Collection and item headers choose a scale token by mood ("whisper" 18px → "shout" 160px) and width (condensed for intensity); the scale is part of the content metadata. |
| G3 · Tight crop inside hard geometry | A musician cropped to a sliver, seen through vertical black bars (*Hub-Tones*, 1962), turns a portrait into rhythm. | Hero photos masked by rigid geometric bars or slots (e.g. 7 vertical bars, one offset); the person is recognisable through a single gap. |
| G4 · Formula, not template | Every cover uses the same ingredients, photo, bands, type, label number, but proportions change per release, so the catalogue reads as a family without repetition. | Card component with fixed slots (image, band, title, number) whose proportions vary per item from a small set of allowed ratios (1:1, 2:1, 1:3); the catalogue grid stays uniform. |
| G5 · Mixed faces, strict contrast | Miles mixed condensed grotesques, slab serifs and typewriter type freely, held together by stark black/white contrast. | Per-item display faces drawn from a curated set of 4 to 6, all set in the same two colours, with one neutral text face for metadata. |
| G6 · Authority on the back | The reverse carried detailed liner notes and session data, personnel, dates, engineer. | Detail views include a long-form notes section and a precise metadata table (credits, dates, versions) in a monospaced or serif face. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, one tint per cover (cyan-blue, ochre, green, magenta); duotone photography.
- **Type:** condensed grotesques, Franklin Gothic, Clarendon-style slabs, typewriter faces, often lowercase → Antonio or Oswald (condensed), Libre Franklin, Zilla Slab, Space Mono.
- **Form:** cropped photographs, bars, bands, label numbers as graphic elements.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, grid-like, high contrast between type mass and photo; text often bleeding off the edges.

## Attitude

Blue Note's founder Alfred Lion and photographer Francis Wolff, both emigrés from Germany, treated modern jazz as serious art deserving serious design. Miles, who worked for the label from the mid-1950s until about 1967 and designed hundreds of covers, turned a tight budget and a constant formula into one of design's most coherent catalogues.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `swiss-international`, `new-york-school`, modernist type and photography in a cultural catalogue.
- **Productive tension with:** `push-pin-studios`, cool photographic formula against warm illustrated eclecticism; the two coexisted in 1960s record design.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, flowing ornament dissolves the stark bands and crops.

## Don't copy

*Blue Train* (1957), *Hub-Tones* (1962), *Midnight Blue*, *Cool Struttin'*, *Somethin' Else* covers; Francis Wolff's photographs; the Blue Note logo and label design.
