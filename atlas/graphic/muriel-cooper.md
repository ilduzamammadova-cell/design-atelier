# Muriel Cooper

**Essence:** Print discipline carried into the screen's depth: information as a landscape you fly through, sharp in front and fading behind.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Depth as hierarchy | In the Visible Language Workshop's three-dimensional type, the layer in focus was sharp and opaque while layers behind grew lighter, so x, y and z all carried meaning. | Active panel at 100% opacity; parent context behind at 60% opacity, scale 0.96, 2px blur; grandparent at 30%. Moving between levels zooms along z instead of sliding sideways. |
| G2 · Information landscape | *Information Landscapes* (TED5, 1994) let viewers travel through continuous typographic space instead of turning pages. | Semantic zoom: overview shows titles only, zooming in reveals summaries, then full text; the URL encodes position so any view can be shared. |
| G3 · Mark from the smallest unit | The MIT Press logo is a row of vertical bars that reads as books on a shelf and as the letters "mitp". | Build the brand mark from the product's most basic UI primitive (bars, cells, rules) so the logo can animate out of real interface elements. |
| G4 · Grid marshals complexity | Her *Bauhaus* book (1969) ordered a huge archive of documents, drawings and photographs on a rigorous grid in Helvetica, with an overprinted spine-like cover. | Archival or reference content in a strict 6-column grid with fixed image ratios (1:1, 3:4) and captions always in the same column, whatever the source material. |
| G5 · Designer and programmer at one table | The VLW was an open studio where designers pushed engineers for tools: transparency, colour, then moving and pulsing type. | Pair one designer and one engineer from day one; tokens and motion are defined in code first and mirrored into design files, not the other way round. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black or dark-blue grounds with luminous red, blue, green and white type; overprinted red/yellow/blue for the *Bauhaus* cover; MIT greys in print.
- **Type:** Helvetica and Univers in books; anti-aliased sans type in 3D space on screen → free: IBM Plex Sans, Hanken Grotesk.
- **Form:** translucent planes of text, grids, bars; typewriter-set text in *Learning from Las Vegas* (1972).
- **Composition:** layered, perspectival on screen; rigorous grid in print.

## Attitude

Cooper saw the information revolution as the Bauhaus saw the industrial one: a reason to rethink everything. She predicted that electronic media would blur the lines between tool and design, designer and artist, author and designer, professional and amateur. Her aim was to move graphic design from form towards content and to make complex information graspable.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `april-greiman`, `bauhaus` — digital depth and a pedagogy of experiment.
- **Productive tension with:** `irma-boom` — physical depth of the book against dematerialised depth of the screen; borrow book-like edges for a zoomable interface.
- **Clashes with:** `punk-graphics` — degraded photocopy textures destroy the legibility that layered depth depends on.

## Don't copy

The MIT Press logo, the *Bauhaus* (1969) cover, the *Learning from Las Vegas* layout, the *Information Landscapes* screens.
