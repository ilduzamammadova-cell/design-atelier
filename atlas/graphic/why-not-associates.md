# Why Not Associates

**Essence:** Typography as entertainment: experimental layering carried to the high street, the TV ident and finally the pavement.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Walkable typography | The *Comedy Carpet* in Blackpool (2011, artwork by Gordon Young, typography by Why Not) sets over a thousand jokes and catchphrases into some 2,200 m² of granite and concrete. | A "floor" layer of dense quotation text behind a landing page, sized by importance and readable when zoomed; it doubles as texture and as an archive of real user words. |
| G2 · Read at two distances | The carpet's letter sizes work up close and from the top of Blackpool Tower. | Hero type in two layers: one giant word legible across a room (≥15vw) built from small readable text (text fill or clipped text) for close reading; both carry real content. |
| G3 · Dimensional, moving letters | Light-drawn letters, three-dimensional type and revolving billboards (Smirnoff, 1989 to 90) brought motion and depth to type. | Intro motion moves letters through z-space with perspective transforms for ≤1.2s, then settles to flat type; static version for reduced-motion users. |
| G4 · Avant-garde in the mail-order catalogue | The *Next Directory* (late 1980s) put layered new-wave typography and computer imaging in front of millions of shoppers. | E-commerce: each catalogue section opener gets one experimental layout; product grids and checkout stay standard and predictable. |
| G5 · Showbill exuberance | Music-hall poster logic: many faces, sizes and weights in celebration. | Mixed showbill typography (four or five families) allowed only at celebratory moments, launches, milestones, year-in-review, never in everyday screens. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** saturated reds, blues and blacks of showbills; light-painting blues and whites; photographic gloss.
- **Type:** showbill mixtures of fat faces, slabs and grotesques; refined classical serifs beside digital distortion → free: Abril Fatface, Alfa Slab One, Bricolage Grotesque.
- **Form:** extruded letters, light trails, layered photography (often with Rocco Redondo), letters in stone and concrete.
- **Composition:** layered and dynamic on page and screen; rectangular panels on the ground.

## Attitude

Founded by Andy Altmann, David Ellis and Howard Greenhalgh after the Royal College of Art, where Gert Dumbar encouraged them, the studio stayed pragmatic rather than theoretical. Humour, football and popular culture sit beside experiment; they wanted multiple readings and an active audience but also to delight commercial clients.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `paula-scher`, `studio-dumbar`, type at architectural scale and the Dutch mentor's joyful layering.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, a strict modular field (the carpet's panels) holding showbill chaos.
- **Clashes with:** `metahaven`, light entertainment inside earnest political critique trivialises both.

## Don't copy

The *Comedy Carpet* layout, the *Sensation* exhibition poster (1997), the *Next Directory* pages, the light-drawn question mark of their 1998 monograph, the Smirnoff billboards.
