# Constructivism

**Essence:** The designer as engineer of a new society — art leaves the easel for the poster, the book, the kiosk and the factory.

In 1921 a group around Vladimir Tatlin and Alexander Rodchenko renounced "art for art's sake" for production; Aleksei Gan's brochure *Konstruktivizm* (1922) named three principles — tectonics (ideology fused with form), faktura (the nature of materials) and construction (the process of organising). Key designers: `el-lissitzky`, `alexander-rodchenko`, `stenberg-brothers`, Gustav Klutsis, Varvara Stepanova, Lyubov Popova. Socialist Realism ended the experiment in the early 1930s.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The diagonal as force | Compositions tilted 15–45° read as movement and struggle rather than rest | One "agitational" layout variant per product: the headline block and its local grid rotate −12° to −30°; navigation, forms and body text always stay axis-aligned |
| G2 · Red, black, paper | A severely limited palette in which red is the only active colour | Tokens: `--ink #111`, `--paper #F2EDE4`, `--signal #D7261E`; red reserved for primary action, active state and the single most important number — nothing else |
| G3 · Show the construction | Faktura and construction meant visible process: structure, joints, material | Expose the scaffolding: hairline construction lines, measurement ticks and grid coordinates behind hero type; spec annotations as a visual layer in docs and marketing |
| G4 · Bars carry hierarchy | Heavy rules, bars and blocky sans lettering do the work that ornament once did | Hierarchy via 6–16px bars and rules instead of cards and shadows; section labels sit reversed on red bars; no rounded corners |
| G5 · Modular agitprop units | Kiosks, stands and clubs (Klutsis's kiosk designs 1922, Rodchenko's Workers' Club 1925) were standardised, transportable, multi-function | Content units designed once to work as poster, screen card and stand panel — one component with print, mobile and signage variants |
| G6 · Photography replaces drawing | Montage and the camera were the modern illustration tools | Imagery = photography and montage only; illustration reserved for diagrams. See `photomontage` |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** `#D7261E`, `#111111`, `#F2EDE4`, occasional `#2350A0` and `#F5C400`.
- **Type:** geometric and condensed sans-serif hand-lettering (Cyrillic block capitals), letters built from rectangles. Free (with Cyrillic): Oswald, Bebas Neue, Rubik, Unbounded.
- **Form:** circles, wedges, bars, arrows, photo cut-outs, overprinted planes.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, diagonal axes, extreme scale contrast, tight registration of overprinted colour.

## Attitude

Art must be useful to the collective: design as a citizen's duty, the poster as a tool for building a new life. The idealism was real and so was its instrumentalisation — many protagonists were silenced or killed under Stalin.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `new-typography`, `swiss-international` — its direct descendants.
- **Productive tension with:** `japanese-modernism` — the same red-black-white and geometry, but calm instead of agitational: serene yet decisive interfaces.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — vegetal ornament contradicts the engineering stance at every point.

## Don't copy

Lissitzky's *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge*; Rodchenko's Lengiz "Books" poster with Lilya Brik; the Stenbergs' film posters; Tatlin's *Monument to the Third International* model; Klutsis's posters.
