# Polish Poster School

**Essence:** The poster as an auteur's metaphor, hand-made image and hand-made letters interpreting a film or play instead of advertising it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Interpret, don't illustrate | With no studio stills or marketing copy supplied, designers invented a single poetic image that commented on the work rather than showing its stars. | Cover art for each item (film, course, release) is a commissioned interpretation, not a screenshot or still; one metaphor per item. Metadata lives elsewhere. |
| G2 · Lettering by the same hand | Titles were drawn or painted by the artist as part of the image, never typeset in a neutral face. | Campaign and cover titles are hand-lettered SVGs that share the illustration's tool and texture; the UI around them stays typeset. |
| G3 · Separate information strip | Circus posters carried only the word *cyrk*; dates and venues were printed on strips pasted beneath the image. | Card layout in two zones: an artwork zone with no text overlay, and a detachable info strip (date, place, price, action) in a plain sans below it. |
| G4 · Cut paper and flat silkscreen | Tomaszewski and others built images from torn and cut coloured paper and printed them flat. | Illustrations from torn-paper shapes with visible fibre edges, 3 to 5 flat inks, no gradients; scan real paper for textures. |
| G5 · Dark surreal turn | From the 1960s Starowieyski, Lenica and Cieślewicz pushed toward unsettling, metaphysical and sometimes macabre images. | Brand guidelines may permit uncanny, enigmatic imagery for culture and editorial contexts; define where it is off-limits (health, children, finance). |
| G6 · Signature over house style | Each artist kept a personal voice; the national style was plurality, not uniformity. | Guest-artist slots inside a fixed frame (monthly cover, seasonal splash); the frame stays constant while authorship rotates and is credited. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** saturated flats, vermilion, ultramarine, ochre, pink, black, often on off-white or a single strong field.
- **Type:** hand-drawn, brushed, cut or painted lettering; minimal set type for credits → hand-lettering per project; DM Sans or Karla for information strips.
- **Form:** torn paper, painterly gesture, collage, metamorphic figures, enlarged halftone.
- **Composition:** one dominant image, title integrated, information subordinate or separated.

## Attitude

In a state-run economy with little commercial competition, cultural posters became a space for artistic freedom and subtle commentary. Designers trained at the Warsaw and Kraków academies treated the poster as public art trading in ideas rather than commodities; the Warsaw International Poster Biennale (from 1966) and the Poster Museum at Wilanów (1968) made this a national institution.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `henryk-tomaszewski`, `grapus`, `cuban-posters`, hand-made, metaphorical, culturally engaged posters.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, a poetic hand-made image locked into a strict grid with a Swiss information strip.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, uniform brand manuals erase the auteur voice that defines the school.

## Don't copy

Trepkowski's *Nie!* (1953), Tomaszewski's *Moore* (1959), Cieślewicz's *Vertigo* (1963), Lenica's *Wozzeck* (1964), Świerzy's *Jimi Hendrix* (1974), Starowieyski's theatre posters, the *Solidarność* logo (c. 1980).
