# A. M. Cassandre (1901 to 1968)

**Essence:** The poster as a machine for announcing, monumental geometric images in which word and picture are built on one structure.

From his first poster for the furniture store Au Bûcheron (1923) through railway and liner posters (*Étoile du Nord* and *Nord Express*, 1927; *L'Atlantique*, 1931; *Normandie*, 1935), he revitalised French advertising. For Deberny & Peignot he drew Bifur (1929), Acier Noir (1936) and Peignot (1937). MoMA gave him a solo show in 1936; he worked for *Harper's Bazaar* and the Container Corporation in the late 1930s before turning to theatre and painting.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Geometry first, subject fitted | Compositions start from a ratio rectangle and its diagonal; the subject is then fitted to that armature | Heroes built from one ratio (e.g. 2:3) and its diagonal: key image aligned to the diagonal, headline on a baseline parallel to the frame; the armature is drawn first in Figma |
| G2 · Scale exaggerated for meaning | A liner towers over a tiny tug; only the funnels of another ship are shown | Key object fills ≥ 70% of the frame and is cropped by the edges, with one tiny contextual element for scale |
| G3 · Word and image interlocked | Lettering runs through and around the image; a newspaper's name cropped to its familiar short form | Campaign wordmark allowed to crop at the viewport edge when brand recognition is high; letters knock out of or overlap the image rather than sitting in a text box |
| G4 · Three-frame story | Dubonnet (1932): the same figure fills with colour as the word grows letter by letter | Three-step progressive reveal for onboarding or ads: one figure, colour fills in, the word builds across three frames |
| G5 · Volume by airbrush | Smooth tonal ramps model forms inside hard geometry | Single-hue two-stop linear gradients to model volume on flat geometric shapes; never multi-hue gradients |
| G6 · The typeface as poster | Bifur strips letters to essential strokes; Acier Noir is half solid, half outline; Peignot mixes small capitals into lower case | One distinctive unicase or stencil display face for headings only; body stays in a neutral sans |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** deep blues and black with cream, red accents and metallic greys, approx. `#0D1B3D`, `#1E3F8F`, `#E8DFC8`, `#C1272D`, `#8A8F98`.
- **Type:** Bifur, Acier Noir, Peignot; geometric hand-lettered capitals, often in a border band. Free: Big Shoulders Stencil Display, Poiret One, Cormorant Unicase.
- **Form:** hard-edged planes, airbrushed gradients, pictographic silhouettes, converging lines.
- **Composition:** one monumental image, strong diagonals or vanishing points, lettering framing the edges.

## Attitude

He saw the poster artist as a transmitter of messages, not a painter expressing himself; clarity and impact for the passer-by came first. Like Le Corbusier and Léger he admired machine beauty, and later admitted neoclassical and surrealist touches. He also drew the Yves Saint Laurent monogram (1961).

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-deco`, `streamline-moderne`, shared machine-age glamour.
- **Productive tension with:** `hendrik-werkman`, airbrushed precision meets ink grain: warm, tactile luxury travel brands.
- **Clashes with:** `dada`, chance scatter destroys the single geometric armature his images depend on.

## Don't copy

*Étoile du Nord*, *Nord Express*, *L'Atlantique*, *Normandie*, *Statendam*, *Dubonnet*, *L'Intransigeant* and *Au Bûcheron* posters; the outlines of Bifur, Acier Noir and Peignot; the YSL monogram.
