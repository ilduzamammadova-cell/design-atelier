# Will H. Bradley

**Essence:** A self-taught printer who treated type as a shape to be squeezed and stretched. He turned Beardsley's flat, contoured forms into American magazine design and later invented the art director's job.

## Visual facts

- **Color:** flat orange #E4572E, black #1A1A1A, ultramarine #2D5DA8, olive green #6E7F3A on cream #F2E8D2; muted pastel two-colour printing in chapbook work.
- **Type:** Caslon, Bradley's own hand-lettered display (cut as the Bradley typeface in the mid-1890s), and his ATF designs. Free stand-ins: Libre Caslon Text and Display; a blackletter accent such as Grenze Gotisch.
- **Form:** sinuous contours, peacock and floral motifs, silhouettes, woodcut-like spot cuts, plain rules.
- **Composition:** asymmetric curvilinear posters; symmetrical, letterspaced title blocks in the chapbook manner.

## Attitude

Bradley learned design in print shops and libraries, not schools. He absorbed Morris's craft ideals and Beardsley's line, then answered them with American pragmatism: designing for magazines, advertisers and type founders, running his own Wayside Press, and later art-directing mass magazines such as *Collier's*. For him design served print production.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `william-caslon`: the chapbook style is a love letter to Caslon.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: block-justified display lines and flat poster colour inside a modular grid suit expressive yet orderly editorial design.
- **Clashes with:** `alphonse-mucha`: crisp, flat American poster language against ornate European idealization.

## Don't copy

- *The Chap-Book* posters (*Thanksgiving*, *The Twins*, 1894–95), *Inland Printer* covers, *Bradley: His Book* poster.
- Bradley type and his ATF ornaments as exact outlines.
