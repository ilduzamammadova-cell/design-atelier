# Herbert Matter (1907 to 1984)

**Essence:** The designer-photographer, montage, violent scale jumps and flat graphic shapes that make photographs feel like sculpture in space.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Extreme scale jump | His Swiss tourism posters (mid-1930s) put a giant face or cobbled road in the foreground and a tiny skier or distant peak behind; the gap in scale creates space and drama. | Hero compositions with two photographic elements at ≥ 1:10 scale ratio, e.g. an oversized cropped product detail bleeding off the viewport and a tiny full product or person in the distance. |
| G2 · Three-plane montage | Foreground, middle ground and background are separate photographs, hard-cut and stacked to build impossible depth. | Parallax made of 3 hard-edged photographic cut-outs (no blur, no feathering), each moving at a different scroll rate (1.0 / 0.6 / 0.3). |
| G3 · Photo object + flat shadow | For Knoll (from 1946) he paired photographed chairs with flat yellow or black shapes acting as graphic shadows, echoing Calder. | Product cards: cut-out product photo over a flat, offset colour shape that acts as its "shadow"; no CSS drop shadows anywhere. |
| G4 · Veiled reveal | A Knoll brochure hid a chair under a translucent sheet; turning the page revealed the object. | A vellum layer over a product that lifts away on scroll or click: a flat paper-tone sheet at partial coverage (or a printed screen pattern) slides or folds back to reveal the object, no backdrop blur; use once per page. |
| G5 · Letters as hanging objects | His Calder catalogue cover suspends the letters of the name like parts of a mobile. | Wordmark letters animated individually, hanging from an invisible line with slight rotation and inertia that responds to scroll or cursor. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** photographic black and white plus one or two flat colours (signal red, yellow ~#F2C200, sky blue); colour printed as clean areas over gravure-like photos.
- **Type:** hand-drawn condensed capitals in 1930s posters; later Akzidenz/Futura-like sans → Oswald (condensed caps), Jost.
- **Form:** photograms, cut-out photographs, biomorphic shapes, sweeping perspective.
- **Composition:** diagonals and vanishing points, montage across planes, type as a solid block at the base.

## Attitude

Trained as a painter under Léger and Ozenfant, Matter brought modern art's freedom into commercial photography and design. He believed the camera could be as inventive as the brush and that business communication could carry the energy of the avant-garde.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `paul-rand`, `charles-and-ray-eames`, modern art made useful for business; Knoll-era organic modernism.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, objective photography pushed into montage and scale drama: dynamic but orderly product storytelling.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic`, all-over ornament erases the single dramatic leap in scale that drives his images.

## Don't copy

The Swiss National Tourist Office posters (including the "All roads lead to Switzerland" and Pontresina designs, mid-1930s), Knoll advertisements (1948 and later, including the "Chimney Sweeper"), the New Haven Railroad "NH" mark (1954), the Calder catalogue cover (1964).

## Study

- MoMA collection: https://www.moma.org ; Museum für Gestaltung Zürich: https://www.emuseum.ch
- Meggs' History of Graphic Design, chs. 16 to 17 and 20; Jeremy Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design* (2001).
- Matter taught photography at Yale (1952 to 76) and consulted for the Guggenheim and the Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (1958 to 68).
