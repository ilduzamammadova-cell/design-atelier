# Aubrey Beardsley

**Essence:** Pure black against pure white: one massive black shape, a few hair-fine lines and a bloom of dense pattern, drawn with wicked wit and nothing in between.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The black spot | Compositions built around one dominant solid black mass (a cloak, hair, a background) came to be called "the black spot". The rest of the page balances against it. | One large solid black (#0B0B0B) shape per screen, covering 25 to 40% of the area (a side panel, hero block or image mask), with everything else on white in fine detail. In dark themes, invert it: one large white field. |
| G2 · Emptiness next to density | Wide fields of untouched white sit beside small zones of obsessive pattern (feathers, roses, dots). | Pattern quota: dense decorative texture limited to ≤ 10% of the canvas, placed against large empty areas. Never spread pattern evenly. Contrast of density is the point. |
| G3 · Line made for reproduction | He drew for photomechanical line blocks, which reproduced his pen line faithfully: no tones, only line and solid. | Vector-only illustration: two values (black and white, or ink and paper tokens), uniform hairlines at 0.75 to 1px, solids, dotted lines for tone. No gradients, no opacity. It scales crisply and themes cleanly. |
| G4 · Off-centre balance | From Japanese prints he took asymmetric balance: figures pressed to an edge, a long thin element crossing the empty middle. | Asymmetric hero layouts: focal element anchored to the left or right third, the centre left empty or crossed by one thin line (a rule or stem) that connects heading and image. |
| G5 · A single signal colour | *The Yellow Book* (1894 to 97), with Beardsley as its first art editor, made a loud yellow cover a public symbol of the new and outrageous. | A black-and-white system with exactly one signal colour (e.g. chrome yellow #F2C200) used for the brand surface and one CTA. Its scarcity makes it the identity. |
| G6 · Grotesque wit | His drawings slip satire, caricature and the grotesque into elegant frames and spoil any piety. | Copy and easter eggs with a dry, ironic voice inside an otherwise elegant interface (a sly 404, a mischievous empty state), on the margins only, never in critical flows. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black #0B0B0B and paper white #FAF8F2; occasional chrome yellow #F2C200 (*Yellow Book*), sometimes red.
- **Type:** books and magazines set in Caslon Old Face, with hand-drawn titles. Free stand-in: Libre Caslon (Text and Display).
- **Form:** hairline contours, solid black masses, dotted lines, peacock feathers, roses, elongated figures, satyrs and grotesques.
- **Composition:** tall formats, asymmetry, large empty fields, borders turned from Kelmscott foliage into flat stylized pattern.

## Attitude

Beardsley was famous at twenty and dead of tuberculosis at twenty-five, and worked in a feverish hurry. He took the Kelmscott border and the Japanese print and made them modern, ironic and erotic, deliberately offending Victorian taste. Morris was furious. For Beardsley, style was a provocation and a pleasure.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `giambattista-bodoni`: shared extreme contrast; Didone headlines over Beardsley-like black masses read as luxury decadence.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: the black spot rebuilt as a geometric block inside a modular grid gives severe yet sensual editorial work.
- **Clashes with:** `chromolithography`: his black-and-white economy dies in a flood of colour.

## Don't copy

- Illustrations for *Salome* (1894), *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1893 to 94), *The Rape of the Lock* (1896), *Lysistrata* (1896).
- *The Studio* cover (1893) and *Yellow Book* covers; his characters and grotesques.
