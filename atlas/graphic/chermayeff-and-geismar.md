# Chermayeff & Geismar (founded 1957)

**Essence:** Each identity grows from its problem, not from a house style, the simplest form that is memorable and has a small hook in it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · One inflected letter | The Mobil logotype (1964) is a plain geometric word in which only the red "o" differs, a single accent that separates the name from everything around it. | Wordmark in a neutral geometric sans with exactly one character carrying the accent colour or a modified form; that character alone becomes the app icon and favicon. |
| G2 · Problem before style | Ivan Chermayeff and Tom Geismar rejected a recognisable studio look; solutions came from the client's needs and the constraints of each brief. | Every project starts with a written problem statement and constraints; each visual decision in the review deck cites which constraint it answers. No reuse of previous project aesthetics. |
| G3 · Simple with a barb | Geismar's test: a symbol must be memorable and carry some small barb that makes it stick, yet stay appropriate and pleasant. | Logo acceptance checklist: drawable from memory after 5 seconds; one unexpected detail; works in one colour at 16px; no gradients needed. |
| G4 · Abstract geometry for big institutions | The Chase Manhattan octagon (1960), four wedges around a square, proved a large organisation could be identified by a pure abstract form. | Brand symbol built from 2 to 4 identical rotated parts; reuse the rotation as a motion principle (loader spins the parts, transitions rotate 90°). |
| G5 · Mark extended into objects | Mobil service stations echoed the logotype's circles in cylindrical pumps and canopies. | The brand shape becomes physical UI: circular buttons and avatar frames echoing a round mark; the logo's angle reused in card corners or chart styling. |
| G6 · Collage and scribble in the poster work | Chermayeff's posters and book covers use torn paper, found objects and a quick handwritten line for informality. | Marketing layer (campaigns, posters, social) uses cut-paper collage and a single handwritten line, while the product UI stays strictly systematic. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** one strong corporate colour per client (Chase blue ~#0F4C9E, Mobil red ~#E1251B and blue); black and white.
- **Type:** custom geometric and extended sans logotypes; Helvetica and classical serifs for text → Jost, Instrument Sans, Archivo Expanded.
- **Form:** circles, wedges, rotated modules, cut paper and found objects in posters.
- **Composition:** symbol-centred, clean, generous space; posters built around one image.

## Attitude

Founded as Brownjohn, Chermayeff & Geismar, the studio called itself a design office, not an art studio. Its partners grounded wit and modern-art knowledge in problem-solving and produced over a hundred identity programmes; the guiding belief is that durable marks come from clear thinking rather than fashionable styling.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `paul-rand`, `corporate-identity`, reduced, durable marks for large organisations.
- **Productive tension with:** `henryk-tomaszewski`, cut-paper poster informality next to strict identity systems; the C&G posters already hint at this.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`, decorative pattern overwhelms the single-accent idea.

## Don't copy

Chase Manhattan octagon (1960), Mobil (1964), NBC peacock (1986), PBS, Smithsonian, Time Warner marks; Ivan Chermayeff's posters (e.g. Churchill: *The Wilderness Years*, 1983).
