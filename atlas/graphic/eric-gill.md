# Eric Gill

**Essence:** A letter-cutter's typography: classical proportions carried into a humanist sans, unjustified text set with even word spaces, and initials and images cut so that they interlock with the words.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Even spaces, ragged right | In *An Essay on Typography* (1931) he argued for equal word spacing with unequal line lengths, ahead of most printers. | Body text is always flush-left/ragged-right with default word-spacing; `text-wrap: pretty` to avoid short last lines; no `text-align: justify` in UI or on mobile. Rag tested at the narrowest breakpoint. |
| G2 · Sans with roman bones | Gill Sans (Monotype, 1928), following his teacher Edward Johnston's Underground type, kept classical Roman proportions in a sans serif: wide round letters, narrow E and S. | For warm UI text, choose a humanist sans with classical proportions (Cabin, Alegreya Sans) rather than a geometric or neo-grotesque default. Check that the *O* is round and that *E*, *F*, *S* are narrow. |
| G3 · Letters cut, not drawn | A stone carver and inscription cutter first, he shaped letters through the chisel, with crisp V-cut terminals and permanent, monumental proportions (later the Perpetua type, designed from 1925). | Wayfinding and permanent signage use inscriptional capitals: tracked caps +10%, generous line spacing, centred or flush-left only, carved/engraved finish for physical signs. Reserve them for fixed, durable messages. |
| G4 · Image and initial interlock | In *The Four Gospels* (Golden Cockerel Press, 1931) wood-engraved figures climb and curl through the initial letters, and the opening lines step down in size. | Illustrated drop caps where the figure and letter share outline (single SVG, 4 lines tall), followed by an opening line in caps and a stepped size reduction into body text. Use once per chapter. |
| G5 · Public press, not private press | At Hague & Gill (from 1928) he hand-set and printed, but said a public press prints what customers need, unlike a private press. | Craft in service of real briefs: hand-finished details (custom lettering, illustrations) applied to everyday deliverables (forms, timetables, receipts), not only to showcase pieces. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black #111111 on warm white #F5F0E6; red #A8322A for initials and headings in books; stone grey #8E8A82 for carved work.
- **Type:** Perpetua (designed from 1925), Gill Sans (1928), Joanna (1930 to 31), Golden Cockerel type. Free stand-ins: Cabin or Alegreya Sans for Gill Sans warmth; Cormorant or Crimson Pro for a Perpetua-like text roman.
- **Form:** wood engraving with clean white line, simplified figures with elongated faces and flowing drapery, V-cut inscriptional letters.
- **Composition:** classical book pages; unjustified text; descending type sizes on opening lines; illustrations integrated into initials.

## Attitude

Gill fused Arts and Crafts belief in work as spiritual and moral activity with a severe classical taste. He wanted letters to be useful, durable and plain-spoken. His diaries, made public in Fiona MacCarthy's 1989 biography, record sexual abuse of his daughters; museums now present his work with that context. Use the typographic ideas; don't romanticize the man.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `edward-johnston`: teacher and source of the humanist sans.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: roman-proportioned sans and ragged-right text within a modular grid gives a warmer International Style.
- **Clashes with:** `postmodern-new-wave`: chiselled permanence against layered, deliberately unstable type.

## Don't copy

- Gill Sans, Perpetua and Joanna outlines (Monotype); his engravings for *The Four Gospels* and Golden Cockerel books.
- Specific inscriptions and memorials.
