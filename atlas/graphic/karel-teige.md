# Karel Teige (1900–1951)

**Essence:** Poetism — constructivist structure in the service of play, lyricism and everyday pleasure; the book cover as a picture-poem.

Theorist and designer of the Czech avant-garde group Devětsil (founded 1920), editor of *Disk* and *ReD* (1927–31), he designed well over a hundred books and periodicals, mostly with what the letterpress case offered. With poet Vítězslav Nezval and dancer Milča Mayerová he made *Abeceda* (1926). He exhibited in the Ring's circle and lectured at the Bauhaus; after 1948 the Communist authorities silenced him.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The picture-poem | Photos, postcards, tickets and type composed as a poem without verse lines | Story cards composed as visual poems: 3–5 found images + 1–3 words, no paragraphs; for travel, culture and memory/recap features |
| G2 · Alphabet as performance | *Abeceda* pairs geometric letter constructions with photos of a dancer forming each letter | A brand alphabet where each letter has a gesture or photo partner; used for onboarding, loading sequences or a kids' learning mode |
| G3 · Work from the type case | Rules, blocks and sans types available at the printer were his palette | Generate covers, share images and thumbnails from text + rules + one colour block only — a rule-based template that never needs a designer per item |
| G4 · Each task its own typography | He held that a billboard, a scientific book and a poetry volume need different typographic approaches | Explicit design-system modes: marketing (expressive), documentation (neutral), reading (literary) — each with its own scale and spacing presets |
| G5 · Grid slots, free collage inside | His poetry covers lock surreal collage into an orthogonal frame of bars and blocks | Strict grid of image slots; inside each slot content may be free collage, rotation or cut-out — the frame never moves |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, black, blue and cream — approx. `#D62828`, `#111111`, `#2B4C9B`, `#F4EBDC`.
- **Type:** grotesques, geometric sans, heavy rules; full diacritic support matters. Free: Archivo, Space Grotesk, Jost.
- **Form:** photomontage, circles, bars, stars, globes, dance photography.
- **Composition:** orthogonal grid, overlapping planes, collage elements in boxes.

## Attitude

Constructivism builds the world and poetism makes it joyful — design should bring art into daily life, sport, cinema and travel. A social idealist who distrusted "decorativism" and snobbery in book design, he was too cosmopolitan for the post-1948 regime.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `ladislav-sutnar`, `el-lissitzky` — shared Czech and Russian constructive book culture.
- **Productive tension with:** `isotype` — poetic picture against informational picture gives playful educational content that still informs.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity` — rigid brand manuals squeeze out the lyric play that is his point.

## Don't copy

The *Abeceda* letter spreads; *ReD* and *Disk* covers; his Odeon poetry covers; his collages; the covers of *Stavba a báseň* (1927) and *Nejmenší byt* (1932).
