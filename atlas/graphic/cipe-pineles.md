# Cipe Pineles (1908–1991)

**Essence:** Editorial design as empathy — serious form for audiences that others talked down to, with fine artists invited onto the page.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Painters, not illustrators | At *Seventeen* (1947–50) she commissioned fine artists — Ben Shahn, Richard Lindner, Ad Reinhardt among them — to illustrate fiction, dissolving the wall between art and commerce. | Commission different artists per section or season; hold their varied styles together with a fixed editorial frame (grid, type, captions). Credit the artist visibly on every image. |
| G2 · Respect the reader | *Seventeen* and *Charm* addressed teenage girls and working women as intelligent adults; content and layout were sophisticated, not sugary. | For audiences usually simplified (teens, older users, first-time buyers), keep information density and typographic quality of a premium product; ban infantilising pastels and baby-talk microcopy. |
| G3 · Reflection and repetition | Mirrored photographs and repeating stripes (a 1949 *Seventeen* cover) give a page rhythm and wit. | A hero that mirrors its image or headline across an axis, or repeats one stripe motif from photo into type; one mirrored element per page. |
| G4 · The subject's world as structure | *Charm*'s "women at work" spreads used the tools of the office — typewriter keys, running text — as the graphic backdrop. | Section backgrounds built from the subject's own tools at low contrast (keyboard grid for a writing app, ruled ledger for finance, recipe card lines for food). |
| G5 · Hand-lettered warmth | Her own painted lettering and illustration (collected later in her recipe book) bring a personal voice next to classical type. | Hand-lettered section titles (SVG) paired with a classical serif body (EB Garamond, Bodoni Moda); handwriting never used for body copy. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** fashion-magazine palettes: red-and-white stripes, black and white with one strong accent, warm painterly tones.
- **Type:** Didone mastheads and classical serifs, condensed sans for decks, hand lettering → Bodoni Moda, EB Garamond, Oswald.
- **Form:** fashion photography, commissioned paintings, painted lettering, stripes.
- **Composition:** clear editorial grid with generous, well-defined space for images; playful repetition and reflection.

## Attitude

Pineles drew on her own experience to decide what her readers deserved: intelligence, wit and real art. The first woman admitted to the New York Art Directors Club (1943), she proved that editorial design could change who is taken seriously.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `bradbury-thompson`, `push-pin-studios` — illustrated, warm, art-literate editorial design.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — painterly commissions inside a strict grid give culture platforms both rigour and personality.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity` — closed, manual-driven uniformity smothers the issue-by-issue editorial voice she relied on.

## Don't copy

Specific *Seventeen* (1947–50) and *Charm* (1950–59) covers and spreads, the artists' commissioned illustrations, the illustrations in *Leave Me Alone with the Recipes*.
