# Art Deco

**Essence:** Machine-age geometry worn as luxury — stepped, zigzag, sunburst and streamlined ornament applied with couture finish.

Named after the 1925 Paris *Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes* (the label itself came into use in the 1960s, popularised by Bevis Hillier's 1968 book). In graphics: posters by `a-m-cassandre`, Jean Carlu, Paul Colin and Charles Loupot, Erté's *Harper's Bazaar* covers, Georges Lepape's *Vogue* covers, Joseph Binder's Viennese posters; in the US it fed into `streamline-moderne`.

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, gold, ivory, jade, coral, deep blue — approx. `#0E0E10`, `#C8A951`, `#F2E8D5`, `#1F6F5C`, `#E8735A`, `#1B2A4A`.
- **Type:** Bifur, Broadway, Peignot, Parisian; Didone mastheads in fashion. Free: Poiret One, Limelight, Bodoni Moda, Federo.
- **Form:** zigzags, sunbursts, fountains, speed lines, motifs borrowed from Egyptian and Mesoamerican art after the 1922 Tutankhamun discovery.
- **Composition:** symmetry, verticality, framed and stepped; or diagonal airbrushed posters.

## Attitude

Modernity as pleasure and status: it reconciled machine geometry with French luxury craft and spread through department stores, cinemas, liners and fashion. Modernists dismissed it as superficial. Its habit of mining non-Western motifs as exotic decoration deserves care — borrow geometry, not other cultures' sacred forms.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `a-m-cassandre`, `streamline-moderne` — machine-age glamour and speed.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — deco ornament confined to frames and premium moments while content stays grid-clean gives luxury fintech or hospitality apps.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism` — raw anti-finish and gilded polish cancel each other out.

## Don't copy

The Chrysler Building crown; Erté's and Lepape's covers; Carlu's Gellé Frères poster; Schulz-Neudamm's *Metropolis* poster; Cassandre's posters; the Broadway and Bifur letterforms.

## Study

- Charlotte Benton, Tim Benton & Ghislaine Wood (eds.), *Art Deco 1910–1939* (V&A, 2003); Bevis Hillier, *Art Deco of the 20s and 30s* (1968).
- Musée des Arts Décoratifs, Paris (madparis.fr); V&A (collections.vam.ac.uk); Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design*, pp. 80–83.
