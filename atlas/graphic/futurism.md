# Futurism

**Essence:** Typography as noise and velocity — words thrown across the page at different speeds and volumes, the page treated as a battlefield rather than a column.

Filippo Tommaso Marinetti (1876–1944) launched the movement with his manifesto in *Le Figaro* (20 February 1909). The 1913 manifesto on the destruction of syntax and "words-in-freedom" (*parole in libertà*), published in Florence's *Lacerba*, turned typography into the front line: many inks, many faces, no grammar.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Volume = size | Words-in-freedom sized a word by how loud or fast it is, not by its grammatical rank | A `--intensity` custom property (0–1) drives `font-size: clamp()` and weight for feedback text: a "+1" renders at 14/400, a 100-day streak at 56/900 |
| G2 · Many faces, one page | Marinetti asked for three or four inks and up to twenty typefaces on a page — italic for quick sensations, bold for violent sounds | Inside one fenced zone (launch hero, campaign page) allow 3–5 faces as semantic roles: condensed italic = motion, heavy grotesque = impact, mono = data. Everywhere else the product uses one family |
| G3 · Lines of force | Diagonal rules and radiating vectors push the eye along trajectories | 1–3 diagonal rules (12–18°) connect sections or throw the eye to the CTA; entrance animations travel along that same vector, 250–400ms, ease-out |
| G4 · Simultaneity | Several moments or viewpoints shown at once instead of in sequence | Live views (sports, trading, events) show concurrent streams as overlapping translucent layers on one canvas rather than hidden behind tabs |
| G5 · Sound words as headlines | Onomatopoeia (*Zang Tumb Tuuum*, 1914) replaces description | In playful products, a feedback "stamp" word (e.g. CLACK!, VROOM) appears for a key action — one per interaction, 600ms, never on error states |
| G6 · Publication as event | Manifestos as stunts; leaflets dropped on crowds; a book lithographed on tin (1932) | Launches staged as one-time takeovers: a kinetic manifesto screen shown once per user, then archived; physical metaphors in unboxing and packaging |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** print mostly black and red on cream; paintings saturated — `#111111`, `#D7261E`, `#F1E9D2`, accent `#1F4BFF`.
- **Type:** a riot of letterpress woods and metal types, Bodoni-like romans against grotesques. Free: Bodoni Moda, Anton, Archivo Black, Libre Caslon Italic, Space Mono.
- **Form:** fragmentation, repeated motifs, radiating lines, onomatopoeic letter clusters.
- **Composition:** no single reading direction; words arc, spiral and collide; high density.

## Attitude

Speed, machines and the city were the new beauty, and an art that obeyed old syntax could not describe them. The movement also glorified war and fed into Fascism — take the kinetic grammar, leave the ideology.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `dada`, `deconstructivist-type` — shared typographic anarchy and type-as-performance.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a calm grid that hosts exactly one exploding futurist headline per article gives editorial sites a signature moment without chaos.
- **Clashes with:** `scandinavian-modern` — soft domestic warmth neutralises the aggression, leaving only noise.

## Don't copy

Marinetti's *Zang Tumb Tuuum* cover and his words-in-freedom plates (e.g. *Montagne + Vallate + Strade x Joffre*); Soffici's *BÏF§ZF+18* pages; Boccioni's *Unique Forms of Continuity in Space*; Balla's *Dynamism of a Dog on a Leash*; the tin book *Parole in libertà futuriste* (1932).
