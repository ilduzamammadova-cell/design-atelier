# Herb Lubalin (1918 to 1981)

**Essence:** The word is the picture, letters squeezed, joined and swashed until their shape says what the text says.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Typogram | A brief visual poem in type: a letter is altered so the word depicts its meaning (an ampersand cradling "child", joined letters for marriage). | One typogram per brand or campaign: a logotype or hero word in which a single glyph is modified to depict the concept. Never in UI labels or navigation. |
| G2 · Spacing to the point of touching | Exploiting phototype, he compressed letterspacing until letters kissed and overlapped, forming ligatures. | Display headings at −4% to −6% tracking, line-height 0.85 to 0.95; custom ligatures for recurring letter pairs in the brand name (OpenType `dlig`). Body text stays normal. |
| G3 · Everything in one rectangle | Big headline, dense text and image locked into a single tight rectangle with almost no air (the *Avant Garde* "No More War!" poster-competition ad, late 1960s). | Hero block where a 120px headline and 14px justified body share one rectangle and one column edge; no free space between headline and copy; the block itself sits in generous margins. |
| G4 · Letters as vessels | Capital O's and counters become frames for photographs; image becomes letter. | Image masks shaped by brand letters (avatar inside an "o", product shot inside a counter); one per screen. |
| G5 · Ligature-rich geometric sans | The *Avant Garde* masthead grew into a geometric family (with Tom Carnase, 1970) full of slanted capital ligatures. | A geometric sans (Poppins / URW Gothic) with a small set of custom alternate ligatures used only in brand moments. |
| G6 · Type specimen as content | *U&lc* (from 1973) turned a type promotion journal into a dense tabloid of mixed faces, rules and giant numerals. | Changelogs, release notes and "what's new" pages styled like a type tabloid: big numerals, mixed weights, heavy and hairline rules, packed columns. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** mostly black and white; one hot accent (red ~#E10600) for headlines.
- **Type:** Didones, Caslon and Times for editorial; ITC Avant Garde Gothic, Lubalin Graph, swash capitals → Bodoni Moda, Libre Caslon, Poppins/URW Gothic, Zilla Slab.
- **Form:** letterforms as objects, ligatures, swashes, cropped photography at extreme scales.
- **Composition:** compressed blocks, square formats (*Avant Garde*), big-small contrasts, grids violated on purpose.

## Attitude

Lubalin used type as a means of giving form to a concept, not as an art for its own sake. He accepted that impact sometimes outranks legibility and pushed phototype's new elasticity further than anyone. With Aaron Burns and Ed Rondthaler he co-founded ITC (1970) to license type fairly to designers.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `push-pin-studios`, `new-york-school`, conceptual, expressive American typography.
- **Productive tension with:** `emil-ruder`, Lubalin's expressive display over Ruder's disciplined reading texture.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic`, both bend legibility, from opposite directions; together they produce unreadable noise.

## Don't copy

Mother & Child logo (1960s), *Families*, the *Avant Garde* logo and ITC Avant Garde Gothic outlines, *Eros* (1962) and *Fact* layouts, *U&lc* masthead, the "No More War!" poster.
