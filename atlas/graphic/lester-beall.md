# Lester Beall (1903 to 1969)

**Essence:** Avant-garde montage put to work for ordinary people, arrows, stripes and photographs that inform before they persuade.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Signs for low literacy | His Rural Electrification Administration posters (1937 to 41) explained the benefits of electricity through arrows, flat bands and a single photograph, readable by people with limited reading skills. | Critical flows (setup, safety, payment) must be understandable as icon + one-word label + arrow. Test each step with text hidden; if users fail, the visual layer is not doing its job. |
| G2 · A cultural code as pattern | The REA series built fields of red-and-white stripes and dot grids from the American flag behind documentary photos. | Derive one background pattern from an existing shared code (stripes, dots, ruled lines) and run it as a field behind photographic content; photos sit in hard-edged rectangles on top. |
| G3 · Wood type meets modern layout | He paired bold 19th-century American wood types with diagonal, asymmetric modernist composition. | One vintage display face (wood-type slab such as Alfa Slab One or Ultra) for H1 only; all other text in a modern grotesque; H1 may run on a diagonal (≤ 15°). |
| G4 · Layered technical montage | Later brochures overlay formulas, diagrams, giant letters and tinted photographs in transparent layers. | Technical product pages: monochrome photo tinted in one ink, with data annotations, formulas or schematic lines overprinted (multiply blend) in a second ink; huge single-letter or numeral for section starts. |
| G5 · The identity manual | His corporate programmes (International Paper, 1960, among others) came with manuals specifying permitted uses and forbidden abuses, down to a sign painter's proportions. | Every brand and component doc has paired "do / don't" examples and exact proportional specs, written so a non-designer contractor could apply them correctly. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** flag red ~#C8102E, blue ~#1C3F94, white, black; bold primaries; tinted monochrome photos.
- **Type:** 19th-century wood types and Egyptians, Futura and other geometric sans, typewriter faces → Alfa Slab One, Ultra, Jost.
- **Form:** arrows, stripes, dot grids, photomontage, giant letters, diagrams.
- **Composition:** diagonals, horizontal bands against verticals, layered transparencies, strong scale contrasts.

## Attitude

Largely self-taught through European journals, Beall translated the Bauhaus, Dada and constructivism into a direct American language for public agencies and later for corporations. He believed visual contrast and high information content could serve both democracy and business.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `constructivism`, `corporate-identity`, elemental signs and the manual-driven identity he helped pioneer.
- **Productive tension with:** `push-pin-studios`, vernacular revival type handled with Beall's informational discipline: nostalgic but useful.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, whiplash curves fight his arrows, bands and stripes.

## Don't copy

The Rural Electrification Administration posters (1937 to 41), *Scope* covers for Upjohn, the International Paper tree mark (1960), the Connecticut General identity.
