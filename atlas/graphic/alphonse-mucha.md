# Alphonse Mucha

**Essence:** A serene central figure enthroned in ornament: a halo behind the head, a tall frame around the body and a decorative plinth beneath the words, all in soft, harmonious colour.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The halo | His figures sit against a circle, arch or mosaic disc behind the head, which marks the focal point and lifts the subject from the ground. | A halo element behind the primary subject: a circle 1.3–1.6× the head or product width, in a tint of the accent colour, optionally with a thin ring. Use for profile headers, product hero shots and testimonials. |
| G2 · Tall panel with three zones | *Gismonda* (1894–95) stretched the poster into a tall, narrow format, and many of his works divide into a title zone at top, the figure in the middle and a lettered base panel. | A vertical 9:16 story/card template with three fixed zones: title band (12%), image zone (68%), text plinth (20%) with its own framed background. Content swaps, zones stay. |
| G3 · Lettering inside the frame | Titles live in bands of the ornamental frame, often in mosaic-backed panels, so type is part of the architecture. | Headline containers styled as frame bands (inset panel with 1px rule and patterned or tinted ground) that belong to the card border, not floating text. |
| G4 · Contrast through line, not colour | His palettes are muted and harmonious, and liveliness comes from fluid contour and pattern rather than saturated colour. | Low-chroma palette (ochre, dusty rose, sage, gold, slate) at similar values; hierarchy comes from line weight, pattern density and scale. Pairs well with accessible-but-soft themes; verify text contrast separately. |
| G5 · Radiating flourishes | Hair, drapery and plant tendrils spiral out from the focal figure and fill the frame with rhythm. | Decorative line motifs radiate from the focal point toward the edges (SVG strokes at 1–1.5px, 30–40% opacity) to guide the eye inward. Background only, never behind body text. |
| G6 · Teach the system | His *Documents décoratifs* (1902) gave artisans a manual of motifs and compositions, a design system in plates. | Pair the component library with composition plates: full example layouts showing how frames, halos and patterns combine, not just individual parts. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** ochre #D8B36A, dusty rose #D7A6A1, sage #A7B08A, gold #BFA15A, slate blue #8FA6B2, ivory #F3EAD7; dark contour #4A3B2F.
- **Type:** custom drawn lettering, often rounded and slightly Byzantine. Free stand-ins: Federant for headlines; Cormorant Garamond for supporting text.
- **Form:** idealized female figures, stylized hair, Byzantine mosaics, Slavic folk patterns, flowers and stars.
- **Composition:** symmetry around a vertical axis, framed panels, a circular halo, a plinth for text.

## Attitude

Mucha saw art as eternal rather than "new" and resisted the Art Nouveau label. He wanted beauty to reach ordinary people through posters, packaging and calendars. Later he devoted himself to the *Slav Epic*, art in the service of a people's history. Idealism and craft over novelty.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-nouveau`: he became its most popular face; "le style Mucha" was a synonym around 1900.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: the halo-and-plinth composition rendered with photography and a grotesque gives warm structure for beauty brands.
- **Clashes with:** `punk-graphics`: serene idealization meets deliberate ugliness and rage.

## Don't copy

- *Gismonda* (1894–95), *Job* (1896, 1898), *Monaco Monte-Carlo* (1897), *The Seasons* and *Zodiac* panels, *Slav Epic*.
- His idealized woman as a character type with signature hair swirls.
