# Piet Zwart (1885 to 1977)

**Essence:** The "typotekt", builds pages from the printer's stock of letters and rules the way an architect builds from steel, with Dada's wit and De Stijl's clarity.

Trained in architecture and furniture, never formally in typography, Zwart began graphic work in his mid-thirties for the flooring firm Laga and from 1923 for the cable maker NKF (Nederlandsche Kabelfabriek), producing hundreds of ads and the celebrated 1928 catalogue. He also worked for the Dutch PTT and designed the Bruynzeel kitchen (1938). He sympathised with De Stijl but found it too dogmatic to join.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Compose with stock material | He ordered words, rules and symbols from the compositor and arranged the pieces physically, no custom drawing | "Kit-only" constraint for marketing pages: build exclusively from existing design-system components, glyphs and rules; the restriction forces inventive combinations and keeps everything shippable |
| G2 · Field of tension | The page as a charged field: extreme jumps in size and direction, overlaps, type interacting with the ground | Hero with one oversized word (≈ 30vw) against 14px supporting copy rotated 90°; size ratio ≥ 1 : 15; one overlap with a halftone photo |
| G3 · Copy and form born together | As his own copywriter he wrote short slogans and repeated key words, stepping them in size | Designer-owned microcopy: headlines ≤ 5 words; one keyword repeated 2 to 3 times in rising size (e.g. "fast / faster / fastest") |
| G4 · Respect the reader's time | He assumed readers were flooded with print: big words for the gist, secondary matter clearly separated | Two explicit layers per page: a scannable top layer (big words, big numbers) and a visibly quieter detail layer (smaller, grey, expandable) |
| G5 · Overprint to make a third colour | In the NKF catalogue a red wedge over a blue halftone yields purple; transparency is structure | Translucent primary-colour planes over product photos with `multiply`, producing a third tone; restrict to two primaries per composition |
| G6 · Pun as identity | His personal logo plays on *zwart* = black | Derive a wordmark or symbol from the literal meaning of the brand name, one visual pun, executed with typographic means only |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, blue, yellow and black, often overprinted, `#E1251B`, `#1E4FA3`, `#F7C600`, `#111111`.
- **Type:** grotesques and slabs from the case, bold against light, lower-case. Free: Archivo, Roboto Slab, Space Grotesk.
- **Form:** rules, circles, arrows, halftone photos used as shapes, letters as images.
- **Composition:** orthogonal and diagonal mixed, overlap, repetition, dynamic asymmetry.

## Attitude

Typography must belong to its own time and its own production methods, and it carries social responsibility because mass print shapes everyday life. Lacking typographic training freed him from the trade's rules.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `paul-schuitema`, `kurt-schwitters`, fellow Ring members with the same construct-from-material logic.
- **Productive tension with:** `isotype`, Zwart's witty typographic scale jumps applied to neutral pictogram counts give lively but honest infographics.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco`, ornamental polish dulls his blunt economy.

## Don't copy

NKF advertisements and catalogue spreads (1926 to 28); the ITF film exhibition poster (The Hague, 1928); Laga ads; his personal logo (1927); his PTT designs and the Trio printing-house specimen.
