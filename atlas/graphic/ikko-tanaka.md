# Ikko Tanaka

**Essence:** Tradition rebuilt from flat geometric planes: a dancer's face, a landscape or a flower made of grid squares and warm colour.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Portrait from grid planes | The *Nihon Buyo* poster (1981) builds a traditional performer's face from squares, circles and triangles placed on a visible grid. | Illustrations and avatars generated on a fixed 3×4 cell grid; each cell holds one primitive (square, quarter-circle, triangle) from a six-colour palette. No outlines, no gradients except one per figure. |
| G2 · Close-valued planes | Warm/cool contrast, analogous ranges and neighbouring fields of nearly equal value, with one small saturated accent. | Adjacent surface tokens differ by ΔL ≤ 8 (perceptual lightness); one saturated accent (often a circle) covers ≤5% of the screen. Text colour is tested separately for contrast. |
| G3 · Landscape as bands | Mountains and waves of woodblock prints become rhythmic stacked bands of blue and green under a graded sky. | Section backgrounds as 4 to 6 horizontal bands of a stepped scale; the band under the current section anchor is the darkest, giving a quiet sense of place while scrolling. |
| G4 · The no-brand brand | As Muji's first art director (from 1980) he let kraft paper, plain type and the goods themselves carry the identity. | Brand mark appears once per screen at most; identity is carried by material tokens (unbleached paper tone, plain sans, generous margins) rather than logo repetition. |
| G5 · Four scripts as material | Kanji, kana, Latin letters and symbols composed together as equal visual elements (the 1959 arrows poster). | Multilingual layouts give each script native settings, CJK line height ~1.7, Latin ~1.5, and compose them as equals instead of treating translation as overflow. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** vermilion `#E03C31`, indigo `#2B3A67`, ochre gold `#C8A24B`, sky blue `#3E8FD6`, kraft `#C9A97A`, black and warm greys.
- **Type:** Japanese Mincho and Gothic with Helvetica-style grotesques → free: Noto Serif JP / Shippori Mincho, Noto Sans JP, Schibsted Grotesk.
- **Form:** circles, squares, quarter-circles, bands; frontal, emblem-like figures descended from family crests.
- **Composition:** frontal and often symmetric, on an explicit grid, with large calm fields.

## Attitude

Tanaka joined Edo-period woodblock prints, Noh theatre and the tea ceremony to Bauhaus geometry, asking what abstraction can mean when simplicity no longer automatically signals spirituality. He helped professionalise Japanese graphic design (Nippon Design Center) and art-directed for Seibu and Muji, believing beauty and plainness could serve everyday life.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `kenya-hara`, `japanese-modernism`, Hara continued the Muji line he started.
- **Productive tension with:** `memphis`, both build from flat geometric colour, but Tanaka's calm tempers Memphis' clamour into serene pattern.
- **Clashes with:** `punk-graphics`, torn, defaced collage cannot coexist with planes whose power is their composure.

## Don't copy

The *Nihon Buyo* poster (1981), the Sapporo '72 poster, the Muji launch advertising, the 1959 *World Graphic Design* exhibition poster. Do not use kabuki or Noh faces as decoration; take the grid-of-planes method, not the characters.
