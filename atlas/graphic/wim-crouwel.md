# Wim Crouwel

**Essence:** The grid as generator: letters, posters and whole institutions built from one module, as if designed for a computer that did not yet exist.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Letters from the module | His *New Alphabet* (1967) drew every character from rectangular units with no diagonals or curves, so text could be shown on early screens without distortion. | Build the logotype or a display face on the layout's base unit (e.g. 8px cells, horizontal and vertical strokes only). Use it for headings ≥48px; never for running text. |
| G2 · Expose the matrix | In the *Vormgevers* poster (1968) the grid that generated the letters is printed as part of the image. | Show the construction grid behind hero content as 0.5px lines at 8% ink, and align every edge to it. The grid is content, not a developer overlay. |
| G3 · House style for an institution | Two decades of Stedelijk Museum catalogues and posters shared formats, grids and type, so the museum, not each artist, became recognisable. | Content templates lock grid, type sizes and format; variety comes only from imagery and one accent colour per item. No one-off page layouts. |
| G4 · How little is enough | He tested how much of a letter is needed for recognition, accepting some discomfort for the sake of the system. | Icon reduction test: draw icons on a 16px orthogonal grid with no diagonals; add detail only if five-second recognition tests fail. |
| G5 · Transport-grade consistency | Signage for Schiphol airport (with Benno Wissing, 1960s) became a model of rigorous wayfinding. | Navigation elements (breadcrumbs, step indicators, back links) occupy one fixed position and colour across the whole product, on every screen, without exception. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** flat primaries and black; pale greys; tonal gradations on the 1976 Dutch definitive stamps.
- **Type:** *New Alphabet*, *Fodor*, the Stedelijk alphabet and later digital revivals (e.g. Gridnik); Univers and Helvetica in house styles → free: Space Grotesk for grotesque text, Major Mono Display or a custom grid font for display.
- **Form:** square-cornered strokes, visible graph-paper grids, stacked bars.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, exact, ruled by modules; text often set in lowercase only.

## Attitude

Crouwel saw the designer as an objective problem solver who simplifies both message and means, convinced that typography must be visually orderly to be readable. He embraced technology and the future with genuine excitement. In a famous 1972 debate with Jan van Toorn he defended neutrality and craft against subjective expression.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `experimental-jetset`, `swiss-international` — his heirs and his school.
- **Productive tension with:** `jan-van-toorn` — the 1972 debate as a recipe: an objective grid carrying subjective montage.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — whiplash curves cannot live on a square module; one of the two must lose.

## Don't copy

The *New Alphabet* outlines, the *Vormgevers* poster, specific Stedelijk Museum posters and catalogues, the 1976 PTT stamps, the Fodor alphabet.
