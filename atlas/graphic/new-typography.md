# New Typography

**Essence:** Typography derived from the function of the text — asymmetric, sans-serif, standardised, with white space doing structural work.

Its lineage runs from Lissitzky's "Topography of Typography" (1923) and Moholy-Nagy's Bauhaus statements to Tschichold's insert "elementare typographie" (October 1925) and his handbook *Die neue Typographie* (1928). The *Ring neuer Werbegestalter* (Circle of New Advertising Designers), launched by Kurt Schwitters in 1927–28 with Willi Baumeister, Max Burchartz, Walter Dexel, César Domela, Robert Michel, Georg Trump, Jan Tschichold and Friedrich Vordemberge-Gildewart, later joined by Piet Zwart, Paul Schuitema and others, promoted it through travelling exhibitions and the book *Gefesselter Blick* (1930).

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Asymmetry as function | Reading order dictates placement; a central axis was rejected as decoration imposed on words | Left-aligned by default; headings flush-left, ragged-right text; centring allowed only for single-word badges and icon buttons |
| G2 · Standard formats | DIN 476 paper sizes (1922), standard letterheads with fixed address windows and reference lines | Fixed document templates with standard zones (logo, address window, reference line, date) matching A-series and envelope windows; in product UI a fixed set of modal and sheet sizes |
| G3 · Contrast instead of ornament | Emphasis by weight and size steps, rules, bars, dots and arrows — never decoration | Transactional screens carry no decorative illustration; emphasis only through 3 weights, 4 sizes and rule bars |
| G4 · White space is structure | Empty zones are part of the build, not leftovers | Intentional empty column (e.g. 3 of 12 columns) as a layout token on desktop; negative space specified in the grid, not as padding afterthought |
| G5 · Economy of sorts | The Ring argued for few typefaces and even one case to cut printers' costs | One family, 2–3 weights, variable font where possible; performance budget enforced on font files |
| G6 · Objective photography | Photographs preferred to drawings as precise, modern illustration | Product and editorial images neutral, frontal, uncropped by decoration; no illustrated embellishment around photos |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black and red on white, light grey tints — `#111111`, `#E2231A`, `#FFFFFF`, `#E6E6E6`.
- **Type:** Akzidenz-type grotesques, Venus, Futura (1927), Kabel (1927). Free: Hanken Grotesk, Archivo, Jost, Work Sans.
- **Form:** bars, rules, dots, arrows, photographs, sometimes a single circle.
- **Composition:** asymmetric open grid, flush-left blocks, occasional diagonal; a few large elements against small precise text.

## Attitude

Typography should express its own machine age — clear, efficient, international. German critics called the abandonment of black letter "un-German", and in 1933 the Nazis arrested Tschichold. He later judged the movement's absolutism uncomfortably close to authoritarian thinking — a useful warning against turning a method into dogma.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `swiss-international`, `jan-tschichold` — the postwar Swiss style is its direct continuation.
- **Productive tension with:** `hendrik-werkman` — functional system plus hand-inked texture gives warmth without losing clarity.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — centred ornamental pages are exactly what it was invented to abolish.

## Don't copy

Tschichold's "elementare typographie" pages and Phoebus-Palast posters; the *Gefesselter Blick* cover; Ring members' signature works (Zwart's NKF pages, Burchartz's Bochumer Verein catalogue, Schuitema's Berkel brochures).
