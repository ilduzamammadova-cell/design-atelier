# Roman Cieślewicz (1930 to 1996)

**Essence:** Mass-media images turned against themselves, enlarged halftone, body fragments and a central circle that stares back.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Halftone made visible | He enlarged collage and photographs until the screen dots became texture, creating two levels of reading: the image and the dots that build it. | Images rendered as coarse halftone (dot pitch 6 to 10px) in one ink on white; on hover or zoom the halftone resolves to the full photo. Use for editorial and campaign imagery, not product shots. |
| G2 · The staring centre | Many posters and layouts focus on a circle, often an eye, target or lens, with hands, jaws or legs as fragments around it. | Hero compositions centred on a circular crop (avatar, lens, target); surrounding fragments cropped hard at the edges; one central gaze per page. |
| G3 · Black, white and red | Extreme value contrast, thresholded photographs and a single red. | Palette of #000000, #FFFFFF and one red (~#E30613); photographs thresholded or high-contrast; no mid-tone greys in brand imagery. |
| G4 · Constructivism revived | For the Centre Pompidou's *Paris-Moscou* catalogue (1979) and similar work he reused diagonal bars and bold sans of the 1920s without pastiche. | Section dividers as 45° bars; one rotated headline per campaign; heavy grotesque in black and red on a light ground. |
| G5 · Magazine as visual laboratory | As art director of *Ty i Ja*, then in Paris for *Elle* and his own visual essays, he built image-only sequences. | An editorial "visual essay" mode: full-screen image sequences with no captions until the end; scroll-snapped, one image per viewport. |
| G6 · Image as critique | Posters such as *Zoom contre la pollution de l'œil* (1971) questioned the flood of mediated images. | Content that deliberately asks interpretation: images without explanatory captions, placed to provoke thought, reserve for cultural, not transactional, contexts. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, red; sometimes a single acid accent; high-contrast photography.
- **Type:** bold grotesques, stencils and Cyrillic/Latin constructivist letters → Archivo Black, Big Shoulders Display, Space Grotesk.
- **Form:** halftone collage, eyes, hands, skulls, targets, montage of body fragments.
- **Composition:** central, iconic, symmetrical or diagonal; image dominates text.

## Attitude

Trained at the Kraków Academy and shaped by the Polish poster, Cieślewicz moved to Paris in 1963 and took part in the critique of consumer culture around May 1968. He crossed freely between "serious" and "popular" design and believed images could carry ideas too complex for words.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `polish-poster-school`, `grapus`, `constructivism`, engaged, image-led, political design.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, his disquieting halftone imagery inside rigorous Swiss order: serious cultural institutions with an edge.
- **Clashes with:** `mid-century-modern`, cosy organic optimism softens the disquiet that is the point.

## Don't copy

*Vertigo* (*Zawrót głowy*, 1963), *Zoom contre la pollution de l'œil* (1971), *Che Si* (1967), the *Paris-Moscou* catalogue cover (1979), his Odéon theatre posters.
