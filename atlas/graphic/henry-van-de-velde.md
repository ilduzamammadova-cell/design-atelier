# Henry van de Velde

**Essence:** Line as force rather than flower: abstract curves that express energy and shape both positive and negative space, set in a lifelong argument for the artist's individuality within modern production.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Abstract line as meaning | For Tropon food concentrate (c. 1898) he made no picture of the product or its users. Swelling abstract curves and colour suggested vitality, an early abstract advertisement. | Brand imagery without literal depiction: a set of 3 to 5 abstract line forms whose tension expresses the product's quality (speed, nourishment, calm). Used in heroes, motion intros and packaging instead of stock photos. |
| G2 · Line shapes both figure and ground | His ornament defines negative space as actively as positive, so the white between the lines is shaped too. | Draw illustrations and patterns so the background shapes are designed: test by inverting colours, where both versions should read well. Use for logos and spot illustrations. |
| G3 · Ornament as expression, not decoration | He argued ornament should express structure and force, earning a place as art rather than applied decoration. | Each decorative element states its job in the spec: "shows direction", "marks section", "signals energy". Elements without a stated expressive job are removed. |
| G4 · Honest machine form | Machine-made objects, he argued, should look like what they are and not pretend to be handmade. | Digital-native styling: no fake brush strokes, simulated paper or stitched leather. Texture appears only where it comes from real input (a user's drawing, a photograph). |
| G5 · The artist inside the standard | In the 1914 Werkbund debate he defended individual artistic freedom against Hermann Muthesius's call for standardization. | Design systems with declared expression slots: tokens and components are standard, while specific zones (campaign heroes, editorial openers, seasonal art) are explicitly free for authored work, documented as such. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Tropon ochre yellow #E7C14A, brown #8A5A2B, deep red #A3312C, dark green #3E5B3A; book work in black with red or gold.
- **Type:** custom lettering integrated into ornament; for the Nietzsche editions (Insel Verlag, 1908) he designed ornamental panels, initials and title lettering. Free stand-in: Federant for display; a robust serif such as Cormorant for text.
- **Form:** abstract, swelling and tapering curves; symmetrical linear frames; no naturalistic flowers.
- **Composition:** centred symmetrical frames in books; dynamic, interlocking curve masses in the poster.

## Attitude

Painter turned designer, inspired by Morris, Van de Velde wrote and taught as much as he made. His essay "Déblaiement d'art" (Clearing the Way for Art) argued for the unity of the arts. In Weimar he directed the School of Arts and Crafts, the institution that preceded the Bauhaus. He believed all arts share one language of form and that ornament, rightly used, is structure made visible.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-nouveau`: the abstract, theoretical wing of the movement.
- **Productive tension with:** `peter-behrens`: his individual expressive line inside Behrens's rational corporate system is the Werkbund argument turned into a working style.
- **Clashes with:** `josef-muller-brockmann`: expressive abstract ornament against objective neutrality.

## Don't copy

- The Tropon poster and packaging; the *Ecce Homo* and *Also sprach Zarathustra* title ornaments (Insel, 1908).
- His interior and stained-glass designs (e.g. for Maison Otlet).
