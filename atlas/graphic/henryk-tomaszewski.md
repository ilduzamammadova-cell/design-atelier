# Henryk Tomaszewski (1914–2005)

**Essence:** A scissor cut, a brush scrawl and a joke — the smallest gesture carrying the whole idea on an open field.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Rough-cut letters | For the Henry Moore exhibition poster (1959) he cut the name from paper, uneven and lively, on one deep blue field, with a stone-like form in the "O". | Display wordmarks built from cut-paper letter SVGs: baselines wobble ±3px, widths irregular, set on one flat colour field; one letter may host an image. |
| G2 · Tiny gesture, huge field | Many posters rely on one small drawn sign — a hand, a mark — in a large, almost empty space. | Empty states and confirmation screens with one small drawn gesture (≤ 10% of area) in a calm flat field; no extra illustration or copy. |
| G3 · The brush as authority | Late theatre posters (e.g. *Pralnia*, 1981) put loose brushed letters and figures in charge of the page. | Marketing headlines may be brushed (drawn per campaign); always paired with clean typeset metadata for accessibility and translation. |
| G4 · Letter as joke | Letters double as images and puns; the wit is inside the lettering, not added to it. | One visual pun per campaign hidden in the title lettering (a letter that is also an object); never in navigation or legal text. |
| G5 · No product, no slogan | Free of marketing copy and stills, his posters interpret rather than sell; viewers complete the meaning. | Culture and event pages lead with interpretive art and the title; prices and schedules move to a secondary, clearly structured strip. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** single bold fields (ultramarine, red, ochre, black) with white cut forms; painterly greys and earth tones in late work.
- **Type:** cut, torn and brushed letters drawn by hand → hand-lettering only; Karla or Work Sans for any supporting information.
- **Form:** paper cut-outs, quick brush marks, simple hands and faces, collage.
- **Composition:** sparse, off-centre, generous emptiness, lettering as the main image.

## Attitude

Professor of poster design at the Warsaw Academy of Fine Arts from 1952 until 1985, Tomaszewski taught that a designer is both artist and citizen. He resisted socialist-realist propaganda by turning to the School of Paris and to play; his students included the future founders of `grapus`.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `polish-poster-school`, `grapus` — hand-made, witty, idea-led posters.
- **Productive tension with:** `otl-aicher` — a strictly gridded pictogram system redrawn in cut paper: humane yet consistent icon sets.
- **Clashes with:** `massimo-vignelli` — a five-typeface canon has no room for scissor-cut letters.

## Don't copy

*Moore* (1959), *Hadrian VII* (1969), *Marie and Napoleon* (1964), *Pralnia* (1981), the 1948 London Olympics football poster.
