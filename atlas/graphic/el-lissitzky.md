# El Lissitzky (1890 to 1941)

**Essence:** The book and the exhibition as constructed spaces, page elements behave like architectural bodies floating in a programmed space.

Trained as an architectural engineer in Darmstadt, he met Malevich in Vitebsk (1919) and developed the *Proun*, which he described as "an interchange station between painting and architecture". In Berlin (1921 to 25) he became the main channel carrying Russian ideas to De Stijl, the Bauhaus and Merz. He called himself a "constructor", not an artist.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The book as a built object | *For the Voice* (Mayakovsky, 1923): set entirely from the type case, with a die-cut thumb index of symbols so each poem can be found by touch | Persistent edge tab rail (right edge on desktop, bottom on mobile) with one pictogram per section; each tab's glyph and colour reappear in that section's header |
| G2 · Grid as program | *The Isms of Art* (with Arp, 1925): three-column structure for text in three languages; images 1, 2 or 3 columns wide; big bold numerals link pictures to captions | Image grid where every image spans exactly 1, 2 or 3 of 3 columns; 48px+ bold index numerals tie each image to a caption list elsewhere on the page |
| G3 · Proun space | Bodies float in an ambiguous axonometric space, neither flat nor in perspective, advancing and receding from the picture plane | Isometric (30°) diagram and illustration system; planes float with a fixed offset, no realistic shadows; hover moves an element 8px along the isometric axis |
| G4 · Story told by shapes | *About Two Squares* (made 1920, published 1922): a children's tale whose heroes are a red and a black square | Explainers and onboarding acted out by 2 to 3 geometric actors (one per concept, colour = role) instead of illustrated people |
| G5 · Exhibition as medium | Soviet pavilions at *Pressa*, Cologne (1928) and *Film und Foto* (1929): moving belts, photo-murals, a guided visitor path | Scroll narratives laid out as a spatial path: full-bleed photo walls alternate with dense information modules; progress shown as a route, not a percentage |
| G6 · Light, photo and type fused | Photogram ad for Pelikan ink (1924); the self-portrait *The Constructor* (1924) overlays hand, compass and grid paper | Type and image fused with blend modes (multiply/screen) and shared edges instead of image-plus-caption stacks |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, black, white, grey, sometimes yellow, approx. `#E0311B`, `#121212`, `#F4F1EA`, `#9A9A9A`.
- **Type:** letters constructed with drafting instruments, grotesques from the case, heavy rules. Free: Archivo Black, Rubik, Unbounded.
- **Form:** circles, wedges, axonometric bars and planes, photograms.
- **Composition:** dynamic diagonal axis with weight high on the page, or rigid right angles animated by one counterweight; layouts drafted on graph paper.

## Attitude

Design was construction of a new world of objects for a new society. His 1923 text "Topography of Typography" (*Merz 4*) argued that printed words are taken in by the eye, that the sequence of pages is cinematic, and that new media would replace the old book. In the 1930s, under Stalin, his talent went into state propaganda volumes such as *USSR in Construction*.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `new-typography`, `jan-tschichold`, Tschichold's "Elementare Typographie" (1925) used his work as a key example.
- **Productive tension with:** `arts-and-crafts`, two opposite ideas of the book as a total object; together they suggest tactile, crafted yet programmatic digital books.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco`, luxury ornament dissolves his constructive logic into decoration.

## Don't copy

*Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* (1919 to 20); *About Two Squares*; the *For the Voice* spreads; *The Constructor* self-portrait; the Pelikan photogram; the Russian Exhibition poster (Zurich, 1929); the Proun paintings; *The Isms of Art* layout.
