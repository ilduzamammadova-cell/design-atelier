# Punk Graphics (DIY, cut-and-paste)

**Essence:** Make it tonight with what is on the table: ransom-note letters, photocopies and stolen icons turned against their owners.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Ransom-note setting | Each letter cut from a different newspaper or magazine; inconsistency becomes the voice. Jamie Reid made it the Sex Pistols' signature. | A display component where each glyph draws from 4 to 6 font families, seeded per user so it stays stable. One word or short title only; the accessible name holds the clean string. |
| G2 · Photocopy generation loss | Copier contrast destroys midtones and adds grain; the image looks used and urgent. | Image token `img.xerox`: SVG threshold filter (~55%) plus noise, output in ink and paper only. Applied to editorial imagery, never to product photos users must evaluate. |
| G3 · One cheap loud ink | Black-and-white artwork lifted by a single fluorescent spot colour that printers hated. | Palette: ink, newsprint and one fluorescent accent (yellow `#F4E900` or pink `#F2509B`) covering ≤10% of any screen, tags, highlights, the primary action. |
| G4 · Détournement | An official image or format (a royal portrait, a flag, a bus destination board) is kept recognisable and twisted in one place. | Campaign template reuses the grammar of a familiar civic genre (notice, receipt, ticket, timetable) with a new message; only the user's own institutional formats, never third-party trademarks. |
| G5 · Visible fastening | Collages held together by tape, staples and safety pins that stay in view. | Moodboard or scrapbook views: images carry tape-strip pseudo-elements at two corners, rotated ±3°, and sit ±2px off the grid on purpose. |
| G6 · Zine economy | Folded, stapled, typewritten fanzines (Sniffin' Glue, 1976) proved anyone could publish. | Long-form pages in one column, typewriter monospace body at 16/24, hand-lettered SVG headers, no hero image above the fold. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black `#111111`, newsprint `#F1EEE4`, fluorescent yellow and hot pink, torn red and blue from flags.
- **Type:** cut-out newspaper letters, Letraset rub-downs, typewriter, stencil, marker scrawl → free: Special Elite, Courier Prime, Big Shoulders Stencil; any mix for ransom setting.
- **Form:** torn edges, high-contrast halftone, safety pins, defaced portraits, slogans on stickers.
- **Composition:** collage with no grid, text crammed into leftover space, deliberately crooked.
- **Key makers:** Jamie Reid (Sex Pistols; earlier the Suburban Press, 1970 to 75), Barney Bubbles (Stiff Records), Malcolm Garrett with Linder Sterling (Buzzcocks), Mark Perry (Sniffin' Glue), Gee Vaucher (Crass), Raymond Pettibon (Black Flag/SST).

## Attitude

Punk refused to beautify. It borrowed the Situationist and Dada habit of hijacking mass-media images, and it trusted the bedroom and the photocopier more than the studio. Ugliness was a political position: polish meant complicity with the establishment it was mocking.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `adbusters`, `jan-van-toorn`, détournement and visible montage as critique.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, punk letters on a Swiss grid is exactly the friction from which post-punk (`peter-saville`, `neville-brody`) emerged.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, a fixed, approved, endlessly reproduced mark is the opposite of a one-off rupture; punk moves inside brand guidelines become costume.

## Don't copy

Jamie Reid's *God Save the Queen* sleeve and the safety-pinned Queen portrait, the *Never Mind the Bollocks* cover lettering, the torn flag of *Anarchy in the UK*, the *Pretty Vacant* buses; the Black Flag bars; the Crass symbol.
