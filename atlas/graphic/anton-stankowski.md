# Anton Stankowski (1906 to 1998)

**Essence:** Making the invisible visible, forces, signals and processes translated into exact, energetic geometry.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Diagram the invisible | He drew what cannot be photographed, transmission, heat transfer, a computer's inner workings, as abstract line and colour systems, after first studying the subject. His book was titled *Visual Presentation of Invisible Processes* (1967). | System status, data pipelines and sync processes shown as abstract line fields: parallel lines that change colour or density as they pass a processing node. Research the real process first; each visual change must map to a real state change. |
| G2 · Structural element instead of a logo | For Berlin (from 1968) he gave the city not a trademark but a constant layout element, a long horizontal line with a short vertical, with the name always placed in fixed relation to it. | Identity carried by a layout motif: e.g. a 2px rule with a 12px vertical tick before every section title, the product name always right of the tick. The logo becomes optional; the motif appears on every screen. |
| G3 · Dynamic equilibrium in a square | Marks such as his SEL trademark (1953) and the Deutsche Bank logo (1970s) set one asymmetric, energetic stroke inside a calm square frame. | App icons, avatars and status badges: a square frame plus one diagonal or offset energy element; the frame never changes, the inner element encodes state. |
| G4 · Radiating configurations | Radial and fanning structures express transmission and broadcast. | Signal, upload and broadcast states as radial line bursts (8 to 16 rays) growing outward; progress expressed by ray length rather than a bar. |
| G5 · Studio research feeds commissions | His painting and design fed each other; formal experiments became a bank of solutions. | Maintain a "free study" library of generative patterns (Figma page or code sandbox) from which product illustrations and loaders are selected. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** saturated blue (~#1D4FA0), red, yellow and green on white; black line; colour used to mark change of state.
- **Type:** Akzidenz-Grotesk medium, set small and subordinate → Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk.
- **Form:** parallel lines, diagonals, stepped bars, squares, radial bursts; early photographic close-ups turned abstract.
- **Composition:** constructivist, asymmetric balance in a square, type kept minimal beside the image.

## Attitude

Stankowski combined constructivist form with curiosity about science and engineering; he believed a designer could only invent a convincing symbol after understanding how the thing actually works. He moved freely between painting and commissioned work and saw no hierarchy between them.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `karl-gerstner`, `otl-aicher`, systematic construction for industry and civic identity.
- **Productive tension with:** `japanese-modernism`, Japanese central, emblematic calm plus Stankowski's process energy gives memorable status and data visuals.
- **Clashes with:** `push-pin-studios`, narrative, historical illustration fights his abstract process diagrams.

## Don't copy

The Deutsche Bank logo, the SEL trademark (1953), the Berlin layout element, the Viessmann and SEL calendar images, his paintings.
