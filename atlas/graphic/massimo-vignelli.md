# Massimo Vignelli (1931–2014)

**Essence:** A small canon applied with total discipline — few typefaces, red, black and white, grids, and diagrams that choose clarity over literal truth.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · A short canon of typefaces | He argued that a handful of classics (Helvetica, Bodoni, Garamond, Century, Futura, Times) are enough for almost any job; novelty fonts are noise. | Max two families per product, chosen from a short documented canon (e.g. Hanken Grotesk + Bodoni Moda). New fonts require a written reason. Sizes limited to a 5-step scale. |
| G2 · Black title band | The National Park Service *Unigrid* (1977) gives every folder a black band with the park name in white as a logotype, above a strict grid. | Page header as a full-width black band (min 64px), title in white in one size; all content below on a fixed grid; no hero images inside the band. |
| G3 · Diagram over geography | The 1972 New York subway map used 45° and 90° lines, even station spacing and one colour per route — clarity over scale. | Maps, flows and pipelines drawn as octilinear diagrams; equal spacing between nodes; colour encodes route/category only. Offer a geographic view separately if accuracy matters. |
| G4 · Modular message panels | The subway signs (with Bob Noorda, from 1966) combine square coloured route discs with text strips, assembled like sentences. | Chip/tag system: fixed-size coloured square or circle for the category + a text strip; tags compose in a row to form readable "sentences" (e.g. [A] [C] Downtown & Brooklyn). |
| G5 · Red, black, white | A dominant palette of black and white with a single red, plus greys for content. | Tokens: #000000, #FFFFFF, one red (~#E2231A), 4 neutral greys. Red for emphasis and brand only; status colours handled by shape and icon where possible. |
| G6 · Semantics, syntactics, pragmatics | He reviewed design through meaning, structure and use — a semiotic checklist. | Component review template with three questions: What does it mean? Is it structurally consistent with the system? Does it work in use (data, edge cases, accessibility)? |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, red ~#E2231A; Knoll posters in transparent primaries.
- **Type:** Helvetica, Bodoni, Garamond, Century Expanded, Futura, Times → Hanken Grotesk, Bodoni Moda, EB Garamond, TeX Gyre Schola, Jost, Tinos.
- **Form:** bands, squares, circles, heavy rules, flat colour; strong typographic masses.
- **Composition:** rigorous modular grids, generous margins, few elements at strong contrast of scale.

## Attitude

Vignelli believed design is one discipline from a spoon to a city, and that it should be appropriate, disciplined and timeless rather than fashionable. With Lella Vignelli he took Italian modernism to New York (Unimark, 1965; Vignelli Associates, 1971) and later defended modernism against what he saw as postmodern frivolity.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `swiss-international`, `corporate-identity`, `otl-aicher` — systematic, public-scale information design.
- **Productive tension with:** `herb-lubalin` — expressive Didone or geometric display inside Vignelli's canon; Vignelli's own Bodoni work hints at this luxury edge.
- **Clashes with:** `push-pin-studios` — decorative historical pastiche contradicts his canon and he said so.

## Don't copy

The 1972 NYC subway map, the 1966–70 subway signage system, the American Airlines logo (1967), Knoll posters, Bloomingdale's bags, exact Unigrid formats.
