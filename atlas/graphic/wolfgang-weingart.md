# Wolfgang Weingart

**Essence:** Typography treated as a physical material to push, layer and over-expose, by a teacher who took the Swiss style apart from inside the type shop.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Stay in the process | He insisted the designer control every step from typesetting to film to plate, so the effects grow out of the medium itself. | Build every visual effect from live platform primitives (CSS blend modes, SVG filters, real text). No baked text-in-image in heroes; every texture must be code-generated and every word selectable. |
| G2 · Film sandwich | From the mid-1970s he stacked film positives (type, halftone, photo) and exposed them as one dense, registered surface. | Hero built from at most three stacked layers: dot-screen field, photo in `multiply`, live type on top. Layer offsets are grid units (8px multiples), never arbitrary nudges. |
| G3 · The dot as image | Enlarged halftone dots and the moiré of two screens shifted against each other became pictures in their own right. | A `texture.dot` token (radial-gradient, 6 to 8px pitch, ≤20% ink) used only on empty fields or behind display type; switched off in high-contrast mode. |
| G4 · Numbered reading path | Numbers and arrows replaced left-to-right, top-to-bottom order, freeing the layout while keeping sequence. | For non-linear layouts, attach large step numerals (01, 02, 03 at 2× body size) to blocks. Visual scatter is allowed only if DOM and tab order follow the numbers. |
| G5 · Spacing as a variable | Letter, word and line spacing were explored as expressive parameters rather than fixed correctness. | Define a tracking scale (−0.02, 0, +0.08, +0.2em) and vary headline tracking by section to shift tone. Body tracking never changes. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** early letterpress work in black on white; later two- and three-colour overprints, signal red, sky blue, grey, ochre, building illusionistic layers.
- **Type:** Akzidenz-Grotesk and Univers, often letterspaced, cut, stepped or reversed out of bars → free: Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk.
- **Form:** halftone dots, moiré, film edges, step rules, arrows, small symbols and pictograms registering on ambiguous colour fields.
- **Composition:** type pushed to the poster edges, layered depth, the grid set up then contradicted.

## Attitude

Weingart believed the International Style had become sterile and anonymous and that typography could carry intensity, humour and chance. He refused to call his work a style; he wanted students to question premises, and he grew frustrated when his experiments were copied as mannerisms. Teaching was the real work: the classroom was a laboratory.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `april-greiman`, `deconstructivist-type`, his student and his American heirs extend the same method into colour, video and theory.
- **Productive tension with:** `wim-crouwel`, same Swiss training, opposite temperament; a Crouwel module holding Weingart's layered textures gives density without chaos.
- **Clashes with:** `kenya-hara`, dot screens and stacked film fill the emptiness Hara's work depends on.

## Don't copy

The Kunstkredit 1976/77 poster, Das Schweizer Plakat 1900 to 1984 poster, his Typografische Monatsblätter covers and student-exercise compositions.
