# Jules Chéret

**Essence:** The poster as celebration: a figure caught mid-leap in a cloud of bright colour, with lettering that dances to the same gesture.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · One figure in motion leads the words | His typical poster has a central figure in an animated pose (twisting, reaching, laughing) whose gesture flows into lettering that echoes its curve. | Hero composition: one illustrated or photographed figure whose direction of movement points into the headline. Set the headline on a slight arc or angle (−6° to −12°) that continues the figure's line. One figure, one gesture. |
| G2 · Three colours and black, overprinted | From the 1880s he worked with a black drawing line plus red, yellow and blue, overprinting them to get a wide range of colours and textures. | A four-ink palette: black key + red #E03C31, yellow #F6C343, blue #2B59C3. Every other colour comes from overlap (`mix-blend-mode: multiply`) or tints of those three, so the brand gets many colours from one rule. |
| G3 · Joy as the message | His *Chérettes* were confident, carefree women, a new public image of pleasure in modern life. The product is the mood. | A celebratory motion personality for success and launch moments: springy easing (`cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)`), upward movement, confetti-like colour bursts lasting under 800ms. Reserved for genuine wins, never on routine saves. |
| G4 · Figures floating on washes of light | His backgrounds are luminous gradients and vignettes rather than settings, and figures seem lifted off the ground. | Backgrounds as soft radial washes (two of the palette colours, centre-weighted behind the focal figure) fading to paper colour at the edges. No hard background shapes behind the hero. |
| G5 · Built for the street | He printed posters in sections up to about two metres tall and designed them to be read from across a boulevard. | A distance test for every promo surface: at a 10% thumbnail, the key word and the figure's gesture must still read. If not, cut words or enlarge the figure. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** primaries red #E03C31, yellow #F6C343, blue #2B59C3 over a black line #1A1A1A; sky washes of pale yellow #FBE7A1 to blue #8FB3E8; stipple and spatter textures.
- **Type:** hand-drawn, swelling display lettering on the stone. Free stand-ins: Abril Fatface for bold headlines; Yesteryear for a drawn script accent.
- **Form:** fluid crayon line, scraped and spattered colour, twisting bodies, flying fabric.
- **Composition:** diagonal and spiral movement; figure dominant in the centre; lettering above and below echoing the pose.

## Attitude

A lithographer from childhood who drew directly on the stone, Chéret believed the street could be an art gallery and that advertising could carry beauty and pleasure to everyone. France awarded him the Légion d'honneur in 1890 for creating a new art that served commerce. Optimism was his method.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `henri-de-toulouse-lautrec`: same Paris nightlife, sharpened into flat shapes.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: Chéret's single joyful gesture as the one illustrated element in an otherwise rational, typographic layout.
- **Clashes with:** `firmin-didot`: buoyant chaos against frozen neoclassical perfection.

## Don't copy

- Specific posters: *Bal du Moulin Rouge* (1889), *Palais de Glace* (1893), *Saxoléine* series, *Folies-Bergère* posters.
- The *Chérette* figure type as a character.
