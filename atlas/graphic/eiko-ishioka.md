# Eiko Ishioka

**Essence:** An art director who staged images instead of showing products: bodies, costume and vast empty space arranged into unsettling cross-cultural scenes.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Sell the idea, not the merchandise | Her Parco department-store campaigns of the 1970s rarely showed goods; they staged a provocation about gender, beauty or nationality with a short slogan. | Marketing pages open with one staged image and one provocative line; product specifics appear only after the first scroll or interaction. |
| G2 · One figure, immense emptiness | A single costumed body, often seen from behind or in an odd pose, stands in a field of space or nature. | Hero subject occupies ≤25% of the frame, off-centre; the rest is a flat or natural field. No secondary images in the hero. |
| G3 · The vertical column | Japanese text runs as a slender vertical column beside the image, acting as structure. | Allow one vertical text column on an edge of hero compositions (`writing-mode: vertical-rl` for CJK; 90° rotation for short Latin captions), never for interactive labels. |
| G4 · One blood colour | Deep red costumes and objects burn against bone-white body paint, black or neutral ground. | Palette of neutral field (bone, black, sand) plus one intense red (`#B5121B`) reserved exclusively for the single focal subject on each screen. |
| G5 · The question as headline | Campaign titles asked or ordered things that unsettled assumptions about women, East and West. | Headlines framed as open questions or challenges to the reader; no feature claims in the hero copy. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** blood red, bone white, black, gold, deep cobalt (the stretched-fabric canvas poster, 1973).
- **Type:** vertical Japanese text, classical Latin serifs set small → free: Shippori Mincho, Noto Serif JP, Cormorant.
- **Form:** sculptural costume, stretched fabric, painted bodies, tropical leaves, theatrical props.
- **Composition:** single subject, monumental negative space, frontal or rear views.

## Attitude

Ishioka refused to be packaged as "Japanese style". She treated the art director as the author of a whole world, moving from posters to film, opera, Broadway and Olympic ceremonies. Her standard was work that would be timeless, original and revolutionary, and her images engaged sexual politics not to be correct but to challenge.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `m-m-paris`, `vaughan-oliver` — fashion-world art direction and uncanny, staged imagery.
- **Productive tension with:** `ikko-tanaka` — both reinvent Japanese tradition, one through calm flat planes, the other through disturbing bodies; planes plus one staged figure is a strong pairing.
- **Clashes with:** `pentagram` — a visual pun resolves meaning instantly; Ishioka withholds it. Together the wit deflates the mystery.

## Don't copy

The Parco posters (including the Faye Dunaway campaign), the Miles Davis *Tutu* cover (photograph by Irving Penn), the *Bram Stoker's Dracula* costumes, the *Mishima* sets, the Beijing 2008 ceremony costumes.
