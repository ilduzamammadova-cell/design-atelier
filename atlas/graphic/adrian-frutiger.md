# Adrian Frutiger (1928 to 2015)

**Essence:** Type as an invisible public service, whole families engineered as systems, and letters drawn to be read at a glance, at an angle, on the move.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Numbered type matrix | Univers (released 1957) was planned as 21 fonts on a grid of weight × width, named by two-digit codes (55 = regular) instead of adjectives. | Name typography tokens by coordinates, not adjectives: `type-55` (text regular), `type-65` (bold), `type-57` (condensed). The table of allowed coordinates is the type system; designers pick cells. |
| G2 · Shared vertical metrics | Every Univers member shares x-height, ascender and descender, so weights and widths mix on one line without jumps. | Choose families/settings so all UI text shares one x-height; inline emphasis changes weight or width only, never size; buttons, inputs and labels align on a common baseline. |
| G3 · Signage-born humanist sans | The airport signage at Roissy-Charles de Gaulle produced the face released as Frutiger (1976): open apertures and distinct shapes that survive distance, angle and speed. | Wayfinding, dashboards and small labels use an open-aperture humanist sans (Source Sans 3, Fira Sans). Test labels at 11 to 12px and under 2px gaussian blur; they must stay identifiable. |
| G4 · Readable by machine and human | OCR-B (1968) was drawn so scanners and people could both read it, unambiguous forms without losing humanity. | Codes, IDs and amounts in a monospaced face with tabular figures and clearly distinguished 0/O, 1/l/I; copy-paste friendly, never letterspaced. |
| G5 · Reader in motion | His forms were tested against how people actually see, peripheral vision, glare, distance. | Define minimum sizes per viewing distance (phone 14px, kiosk 24px, wall display 48px). Check contrast in glare (simulate 30% white overlay). |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black on white; signage colour pairs (yellow on dark grey/black at CDG); colour is not his medium.
- **Type:** Univers, Méridien, Frutiger, OCR-B, Avenir (1988) → Source Sans 3, Fira Sans (Frutiger-like); Hanken Grotesk (Univers-like); OCR-B digital versions; Nunito Sans (Avenir-like).
- **Form:** letterforms with even colour, open counters, controlled stroke modulation.
- **Composition:** charts and matrices; signage panels with generous margins and short messages.

## Attitude

Frutiger saw the type designer's highest achievement as not being noticed: a good letter disappears into reading. He worked with craft patience, years on one family, and thought about signs and symbols as a universal human inheritance, not a branding device.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `emil-ruder`, `otl-aicher`, systematic families feeding systematic layouts and signage.
- **Productive tension with:** `hermann-zapf`, Frutiger's rational clarity with Zapf's calligraphic warmth yields humane, institutional type systems.
- **Clashes with:** `herb-lubalin`, compressing spacing to extinction undoes the open spacing that makes Frutiger's letters work.

## Don't copy

The outlines of Univers, Frutiger, Avenir, Méridien, OCR-B, license them or use open alternatives; never trace or redraw them.
