# Alexey Brodovitch (1898 to 1971)

**Essence:** Editorial design as music and film, pacing, cropping and white space choreograph photographs into a rhythm across pages.

After Paris in the 1920s (sets for the Ballets Russes, layouts for *Arts et Métiers Graphiques*) he taught advertising design in Philadelphia from 1930 and ran his peripatetic Design Laboratory. Carmel Snow made him art director of *Harper's Bazaar* (1934 to 58), where he brought in Cassandre, Man Ray, Munkácsi and Cartier-Bresson and launched Richard Avedon and Irving Penn. He also designed the short-lived *Portfolio* (1950 to 51).

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Pace the whole issue | Spreads sequenced like film cuts, quiet, loud, quiet, with deliberate shifts of scale | Long-form stories storyboarded as a rhythm before any screen is drawn (e.g. A-B-A-C: dense, empty, dense, full-bleed); each section declares its tempo |
| G2 · Crop for energy | Decisive enlargement and cropping; from contact sheets he chose the frame with movement over the posed one | Image guidelines: extreme ratios (1:3, 3:1) and off-centre focal points; select frames with gesture or blur; never the static catalogue pose |
| G3 · Type echoes image | Initials and headlines mirror the shape of the photo, a condensed letter beside a tall figure | Headline layout responds to the image's dominant axis: vertical photo → condensed, stacked type; diagonal photo → type on the same diagonal |
| G4 · White space as luxury | Open pages with sharp type on clear ground give the pictures room | Editorial templates with ≥ 40% empty area in heroes; one image, one headline, Didone display serif |
| G5 · Repetition as rhythm | Repeated forms across a cover or masthead (round wings echoing eyes; a repeated title) bind a page | Repeat one key motif 3 to 5 times at different scales through a page or feed for cohesion |
| G6 · Surprise every issue | He insisted on constant novelty and avoided rigid modular grids | Template system with one deliberately unpredictable slot per issue/release (a format that may change), so the publication never fully repeats |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black-and-white photography dominates, with sparing accents, approx. `#111111`, `#FFFFFF`, `#E9E4DD`, accent `#D94F70`.
- **Type:** Didot/Bodoni-style mastheads, fine modern serifs, telegram-style lettering. Free: Bodoni Moda, Playfair Display, Libre Bodoni.
- **Form:** silhouettes, bleeding photographs, torn edges and hand-drawn headlines in the 1930s.
- **Composition:** spread-based, asymmetrical, film-strip sequences, big scale contrasts.

## Attitude

Make it new, every time; respect the photographer and give the image the stage. Teaching was central, his Design Laboratory trained a generation of photographers and art directors.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `new-york-school`, `a-m-cassandre`, editorial modernism with pictorial wit.
- **Productive tension with:** `josef-muller-brockmann`, an exact modular grid with Brodovitch's intuitive crops and pacing gives editorial systems that stay alive.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis`, pattern-on-pattern clutter kills the white-space pacing his pages depend on.

## Don't copy

Specific *Harper's Bazaar* covers and spreads (e.g. the June 1940 cover with Herbert Matter); *Portfolio* covers and spreads; images from his photobook *Ballet* (1945); the Bazaar masthead.
