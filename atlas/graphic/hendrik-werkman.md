# H. N. Werkman (1882–1945)

**Essence:** The press as a paintbrush — wood type, rollers and stencils used for their dents, grain and ink, producing one-off prints with modernist bones.

After his large Groningen printing business failed in 1923, Werkman worked in a small attic print shop. He made *druksels* (prints) and *tiksels* (typewriter compositions), published the experimental magazine *The Next Call* (nine issues, 1923–26), and during the occupation produced about forty clandestine publications as De Blauwe Schuit (1940–44). He was shot by the Nazis on 10 April 1945, days before Groningen's liberation.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Imperfection is information | Worn, nicked wood type and uneven inking make each impression unique | 3–5 scanned ink/grain textures applied as masks to large display type; a seeded random picks the texture per render so repeat visits feel printed, never identical |
| G2 · Letters as shapes | Type turned sideways, repeated in columns or blocks so O's and M's become figures | Header patterns built from the brand's own glyphs repeated and rotated (e.g. rows of "o" as dots); never placed under readable text |
| G3 · Ink straight to paper | Later he rolled ink and used stencils directly on the sheet, building images in transparent layers | Illustrations as 2–3 overlapping flat transparent shapes (`multiply`), edges slightly irregular (subtle SVG turbulence), no outlines |
| G4 · Small, periodic, personal | *The Next Call* and De Blauwe Schuit were short-run, intimate publications | A low-frequency, crafted newsletter or zine as a brand channel; each issue has a unique generated cover; limited editions over constant feeds |
| G5 · Typewriter as drawing tool | *Tiksels* arranged typewriter characters into abstract compositions, prefiguring concrete poetry | Monospace character-art compositions for developer tools, terminal splash screens and loading states, built from the product's own mono font |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** warm saturated inks on off-white — approx. `#D9481E`, `#E3A72F`, `#2A3F8F`, `#1A1A1A`, `#F3EBDD`.
- **Type:** worn wood display type, slabs and sans; typewriter characters. Free: Alfa Slab One and Bebas Neue (with texture), Courier Prime for tiksels.
- **Form:** rectangles, stencilled shapes, letter blocks, roller marks.
- **Composition:** open, asymmetric, rhythmic repetition, generous paper.

## Attitude

Printing as personal freedom and joy in material — paper, wood, ink — rather than commerce. His wartime press showed that beauty itself can be resistance. His influence reached Willem Sandberg and, through him, postwar Dutch design.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `kurt-schwitters`, `willem-sandberg` — found material, chance and the poetry of print.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a precise grid carrying printed textures yields warm yet rigorous products.
- **Clashes with:** `streamline-moderne` — chrome airbrush finish and ink grain cancel each other out.

## Don't copy

Pages of *The Next Call*; specific druksels (e.g. the De Ploeg exhibition posters); the *Chassidische legenden* prints (1941–43); his calendar pages; De Blauwe Schuit covers.
