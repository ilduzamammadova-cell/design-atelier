# Paula Scher

**Essence:** Type as a crowd: words packed, scaled and shouted until they become the picture, then the building.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The typographic crowd | Nineteenth-century wood-type playbills reborn: many sizes and weights packed edge to edge with no room to breathe. | Announcement surfaces set in a condensed grotesque, 3–5 sizes, leading 0.85–0.9, every line justified to full container width. No image inside the same block. |
| G2 · Type at architectural scale | Words painted across facades, floors, doors and ceilings (the New 42nd Street Studios, the NJPAC arts school). | On wide screens, one section word sized to the viewport (`clamp(6rem, 18vw, 22rem)`) bleeding off one edge; it doubles as a wayfinding anchor ("LEARN", "SHOP"). |
| G3 · Borrow a grammar, change its weight | Russian constructivist diagonals given the heft of wood type; sources that contradict each other combined into something new. | Pick one historical layout grammar (e.g. a 30° constructivist diagonal) and render it in a deliberately non-period typeface; record both as tokens (`composition.diagonal: 30deg`, `font.display`). |
| G4 · Identity as type behaviour | The Public Theater identity (1994) is not a fixed lockup but a way words behave: mixed weights, cropped, stacked, tumbling. | Brand component takes content props (season, event name) and arranges them by fixed rules — weight mixing within words, left anchor, baseline stacking — instead of shipping static logo files for campaigns. |
| G5 · Information as painted texture | Her map paintings turn place names into dense, emotional colour fields. | Decorative "map" or overview views may use hand-lettered labels at varied sizes as texture, with a toggle that reveals an accurate, accessible version of the same data. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, signal red `#E02020`, strong yellow `#FFD100`, occasional pastel or pink; flat, poster-printed.
- **Type:** condensed and extra-bold wood-type grotesques, mixed with slabs and didones; hand-painted letters in later work → free: Anton, Big Shoulders Display, Archivo Black, Alfa Slab One.
- **Form:** letters as solid masses, cropped by edges; diagonals; photography cut out and dropped into the type.
- **Composition:** dense, top-to-bottom stacks; architecture used as the page.

## Attitude

Scher believes type can be image, voice and space at once, and that public institutions deserve the energy of the street. She has said she prefers "more" to "less". Her history-hopping is not nostalgia: she mixes periods to provoke a new form.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `constructivism`, `why-not-associates` — diagonal energy and type at the scale of the city.
- **Productive tension with:** `wim-crouwel` — a strict modular type system pushed to crowd density gives order that still shouts.
- **Clashes with:** `kenya-hara` — quiet emptiness and typographic crowd neutralise each other.

## Don't copy

The Public Theater identity and posters (e.g. *Bring in 'da Noise, Bring in 'da Funk*, 1995), the Citi logo, the 1985 Swatch parody of Herbert Matter, the Windows logo (2012), her map paintings, her CBS record covers.
