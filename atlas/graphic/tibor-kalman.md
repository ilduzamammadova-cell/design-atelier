# Tibor Kalman (M&Co.)

**Essence:** Design as a way to make people think: humble vernacular forms, jokes and shocking images put to work for a social argument.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Vernacular honesty | For Restaurant Florent and others, M&Co. borrowed the look of undesigned things — diner menus, stock photos, matchbooks, anatomy charts — instead of slick "designer decade" style. | A humble brand may deliberately use system fonts, default-looking controls and plain-language microcopy as its style; polish goes into the content and the joke, not the chrome. |
| G2 · What if? | In *Colors* the faces of world-famous people were digitally altered to change their race, turning prejudice into a visible question. | "Counterfactual" views: let users toggle a swapped variable (place, income, gender) to reveal bias in their own data; altered states are always clearly labelled. |
| G3 · One issue, one image | Each *Colors* issue tackled a single theme (racism, AIDS, ecology) and argued it through a single cover image, in two languages. | Editorial template: one topic per issue or page; the cover is one image plus one line, set in the reader's language and a second language side by side. |
| G4 · The wordless issue | One issue told its story almost entirely in pictures. | A wordless mode for universal content: sequences understandable through images and numerals alone, with text available on demand rather than by default. |
| G5 · Twisted everyday object | M&Co. clocks and watches displaced or scrambled their numerals while still telling time. | Rethink one non-critical default control (a clock, an empty state, a progress counter) with a witty twist that still works; never touch controls where errors cost money or safety. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** bold flat colour on black or white for *Colors*; the muted, slightly off colours of stock photography and cheap print.
- **Type:** plain grotesques, typewriter faces, the language of signage and forms → free: Libre Franklin, Courier Prime, Public Sans.
- **Form:** straight documentary photographs, manipulated portraits, found diagrams, everyday objects.
- **Composition:** simple and direct; a single provocation per page.

## Attitude

Kalman demanded that designers take responsibility for what they help sell, and he distrusted design used to make bad products look good. He saw *Colors*, created with Benetton's Oliviero Toscani, as a magazine for a global village of young people closer to each other than to their parents. Optimism and provocation were the same project.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `adbusters`, `jan-van-toorn` — design as social argument.
- **Productive tension with:** `pentagram` — the same visual wit, but aimed at causes rather than clients; Pentagram polish plus Kalman content is persuasive.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco` — luxury glamour and vernacular humility contradict each other.

## Don't copy

The *Colors* covers and logotype, the altered portrait of Queen Elizabeth II, the Restaurant Florent advertising, M&Co. clocks and watches, the Talking Heads work.
