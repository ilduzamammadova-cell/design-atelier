# Ahn Sang-soo

**Essence:** A writing system reconsidered: Hangul's letter parts freed from the uniform square and set like playful modular pieces.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Break the box | His typeface *Ahn Sang-soo* (1985) kept each Hangul letter part at a fixed shape instead of squeezing it to fill a uniform square, so syllables have uneven outlines, a path of experiment for Korean type. | Let elements keep their natural size: chips, tabs and tags sized by content with a shared top alignment and ragged bottoms, instead of forcing equal boxes. |
| G2 · Letters from primitives | Hangul is built from a few geometric strokes and a circle; his lettering exposes that construction. | Logo, display letters and icon set constructed from the same three primitives (circle, vertical bar, horizontal bar); new icons must be derivable from them. |
| G3 · The letter that becomes a face | Posters and lettering play with letter parts as eyes, mouths and figures. | One glyph of the logotype may become a character (an "o" as an eye in empty states), once per flow at most, never in navigation. |
| G4 · Script-native typography | He translated Tschichold and Ruder into Korean but built Korean typography from Hangul's own logic rather than copying Latin rules. | For each script, define native typographic tokens (Hangul: line height ~1.6 to 1.7, tighter tracking, its own size steps) rather than inheriting Latin defaults with a fallback font. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** primaries and black on white; bright, poster-like contrasts.
- **Type:** *Ahn Sang-soo* (1985) and *Madang*; modular, geometric Hangul → free: Noto Sans KR, Nanum Square, Gowun Dodum.
- **Form:** circles, bars, letter parts as figures, grids of syllables.
- **Composition:** modular and gridded, with playful rotations and scale jumps.

## Attitude

Ahn treats Hangul as a designed, almost scientific system and sees typography as a continuation of that invention. He founded Ahn Graphics (1985), launched the alternative culture magazine *bogoseo/bogoseo* (1988), served as ICOGRADA vice-president (1997 to 2001) and later founded the Paju Typography Institute (PaTI) as an alternative school. He received Leipzig's Gutenberg Prize in 2007.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `wim-crouwel`, `karel-martens`, modular letters and geometric play.
- **Productive tension with:** `emigre`, another script's experiment with constraint; bitmap logic and jamo logic can share a modular grid.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, organic whiplash curves break the modular logic that gives the letters their character.

## Don't copy

The outlines of the *Ahn Sang-soo* and *Madang* typefaces, his Hangul posters, the *bogoseo/bogoseo* covers. Do not use Hangul (or any living script) as decoration without real, correct text.
