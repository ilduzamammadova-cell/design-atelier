# Peter Saville

**Essence:** The cover as a found object: images and codes borrowed from science, art history and industry, set with icy modernist restraint and almost no words.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Recontextualised image | A radio-astronomy plot of a pulsar for *Unknown Pleasures* (1979), a Fantin-Latour flower painting for *Power, Corruption & Lies* (1983): images moved, nearly untouched, from one genre to another. | Campaign imagery sourced from public-domain scientific, archival or technical material, credited, minimally treated. The strangeness must come from context, not from filters. |
| G2 · Name withheld | Many Factory sleeves carried no band name or title on the front; identification moved to the spine, back or sticker. | Hero carries only the image; the brand name lives in a small persistent mark or the URL. Useful for launches and editions, not for onboarding. |
| G3 · Code instead of words | A colour-coded alphabet on *Power, Corruption & Lies* replaced the title with a key the audience had to crack. | An encoded element (colour bars, dot pattern) spells a release or edition name; the key is published in docs or revealed on hover. Never used for critical information. |
| G4 · The catalogue number as identity | Factory Records numbered everything (records, posters, even the club) with FAC numbers. | Every artefact gets a real, sequential serial shown in monospace ("N° 0042") as a brand element on receipts, releases, invites. |
| G5 · Industrial object as format | The *Blue Monday* sleeve (1983) was die-cut to look like a floppy disk. | One component borrows the form language of a real industrial object relevant to the product (ticket stub, filing tab, disk label), with its notches and codes, used consistently. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** black, white, cream, pale silver, floral pinks and greens; tints rather than primaries.
- **Type:** small, carefully spaced classical capitals or modernist grotesques, often almost invisible → free: Cormorant (caps), Instrument Sans, IBM Plex Mono for codes.
- **Form:** found plates, data plots, art reproductions, die-cuts, colour keys.
- **Composition:** centred or strictly modular, vast margins, single image.

## Attitude

Saville found in Tschichold's New Typography a parallel to the new wave coming out of punk. He works like an editor or curator of existing culture, lifting images from one context into another with deliberate irreverence. Taste and timing matter more to him than invention from scratch; later he applied the same eye to fashion houses and to Manchester as the city's creative director.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `experimental-jetset`, `new-typography`, cool modernist restraint used as attitude.
- **Productive tension with:** `neville-brody`, one hot emblem inside a cool, nameless layout.
- **Clashes with:** `paula-scher`, shouting typographic crowds and nameless covers cancel each other.

## Don't copy

The *Unknown Pleasures* pulsar plot, the *Power, Corruption & Lies* roses and colour code, the *Blue Monday* floppy-disk sleeve, *Closer*, the Burberry logo (2018), the Manchester Metrolink livery.
