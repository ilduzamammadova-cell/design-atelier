# Alexander Rodchenko (1891–1956)

**Essence:** Maximum signal from minimum parts — block letters, bars, a photograph shot from below, and one word shouted.

After non-objective painting and spatial constructions, Rodchenko abandoned easel painting for production around 1921 (the shift Nikolai Tarabukin's 1923 essay called "from the easel to the machine"), taught at the Vkhutemas metalwork faculty, designed *LEF* (1923–25) and *Novyi LEF* (1927–28), film posters for Dziga Vertov and Eisenstein, and — with the poet Vladimir Mayakovsky — a stream of advertisements for state enterprises (1923–25), the pair working as a two-person advertising-constructor team.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The radical viewpoint | His late-1920s photographs shoot from directly below or above, with steep diagonal horizons, to make the familiar strange | Photo direction rule: no eye-level shots. People and products photographed from ≥45° above or below; crop so the dominant line runs diagonally |
| G2 · Copy and form conceived together | Mayakovsky wrote short, punchy slogans; Rodchenko built the visual around them as one unit | Content design tokens: each component declares a character budget (e.g. hero ≤ 4 words, caps, in a red bar); writer and designer sign off the slot together |
| G3 · Serial frame, variable core | Ten covers for the "Mess Mend" adventure series (1924) share a fixed geometric frame; title, number, second colour and montage change per volume | Series template for episodes, reports or drops: fixed frame + 3 variables (number, accent from a 6-colour rotation, one image) |
| G4 · Shouted hierarchy | Heavy underlines, bars, arrows and exclamation marks steer the eye straight to the point | One heavy directional element per screen — a thick red rule or arrow running from headline to CTA; never two competing pointers |
| G5 · Split-colour logotype | The *LEF* masthead is printed half red, half black in tight registration | Two-tone wordmark via a hard-stop gradient (`linear-gradient(#D52B1E 50%, #111 50%)` clipped to text); crisp split, no blend |
| G6 · Photomontage as modern illustration | Montages for Mayakovsky's *Pro Eto* (1923) and magazine covers replaced drawn pictures | Editorial illustrations as photo cut-outs with flat colour planes and arrows; never mixed with drawn illustration in one view |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red, black and cream with occasional blue or yellow — approx. `#D52B1E`, `#111111`, `#F2ECDF`, `#1D3E8A`, `#F5C400`.
- **Type:** heavy geometric sans hand-lettering that set the template for Soviet display type. Free (with Cyrillic): Oswald, Bebas Neue, Rubik Mono One.
- **Form:** bars, circles, arrows, photographic close-ups, eye motifs.
- **Composition:** static right-angle structures charged by one diagonal; image plus one bold word.

## Attitude

Art should produce useful things for a new society; the camera could retrain how people see. When Socialist Realism took over he was sidelined, worked on the propaganda magazine *USSR in Construction*, and went back to painting.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `constructivism`, `stenberg-brothers` — shared Moscow grammar of bars, montage and cinema.
- **Productive tension with:** `alexey-brodovitch` — radical camera angles inside a paced, white-space-rich editorial flow give dramatic yet elegant magazine apps.
- **Clashes with:** `arts-and-crafts` — handicraft nostalgia against machine agitation reads as costume.

## Don't copy

The Lengiz "Books" poster with Lilya Brik (1924); the *Kino-Glaz* poster (1924); the *Battleship Potemkin* poster; *LEF* / *Novyi LEF* covers; the "Mess Mend" covers; photographs such as *Pioneer Trumpeter* and *Girl with a Leica*.
