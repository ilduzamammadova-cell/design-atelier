# Cuban Revolutionary and Film Posters (ICAIC, OSPAAAL)

**Essence:** Silkscreened flats in unexpected saturated pairs and one emblematic image — an eclectic poster language for audiences across languages.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Silkscreen flats, surprising pairs | Film posters for ICAIC (the national film institute, founded 1959) were hand-pulled silkscreens in bright, unconventional colour pairs. | Palette of 4–6 high-chroma flats (magenta ~#E5007D, orange ~#F58220, violet ~#5B2A86, cyan ~#00A0C6, black) with no gradients; overlapping shapes keep a 1px misregistration offset as a deliberate print trace. |
| G2 · Icon, ideograph, telegram | OSPAAAL solidarity posters (from 1966) used one elemental symbol and a few words, printed in Spanish, English, French and Arabic for distribution across continents. | Shareable cards with one symbol and ≤ 5 words, stacked in up to four languages with equal typographic weight; layout tested for right-to-left scripts. |
| G3 · Reinterpret the film | Designers such as Eduardo Muñoz Bachs, Antonio Reboiro and René Azcuy made original metaphors for foreign and local films rather than reusing studio art. | Each item's cover art is a fresh interpretation in a consistent format size; a fixed info band carries title, year and credits. |
| G4 · Radiating frames | Elena Serrano's *Day of the Heroic Guerrilla* (1968) radiates a portrait outward through nested frames over a map. | Emphasis and "live" states shown by nested concentric frames radiating from the subject; 3–5 rings, alternating two hues. |
| G5 · Eclectic assimilation | With no single doctrine, designers absorbed pop art, psychedelia, Polish posters and Swiss geometry while avoiding Soviet-style heroic realism. | A brand allows several illustration styles per campaign as long as format, palette and info band stay constant; ban literal heroic stock photography. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** saturated magenta, orange, violet, red, green, yellow, cyan on black or white; flat silkscreen ink.
- **Type:** hand-drawn display lettering, geometric sans, psychedelic and pop letterforms → hand-lettering for titles; Space Grotesk or DM Sans for multilingual information.
- **Form:** emblems (star, sun, raised fist, camera), flat figures, op-art patterns, cartoon drawing.
- **Composition:** central emblem, strong symmetry or radial structure, clear information band.

## Attitude

After 1959 the poster became a central medium of public address in Cuba, used for cinema, cultural events and state campaigns and, through OSPAAAL, for international solidarity. Designers worked inside a political framework, yet the cultural posters in particular are remarkable for formal freedom, joy and invention.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `polish-poster-school`, `psychedelic`, `pop-art` — artist-made, high-colour cultural posters.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — Félix Beltrán's geometric work already shows it: emblematic colour within strict geometry.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity` — single-mark uniformity contradicts the per-poster reinvention that defines the genre.

## Don't copy

The Korda-derived Che Guevara image and Serrano's *Day of the Heroic Guerrilla* (1968), Raúl Martínez's *26 Fidel* (1968), Beltrán's star posters, specific ICAIC film posters by Muñoz Bachs, Azcuy, Reboiro, Rostgaard. Revolutionary and political iconography carries real histories of struggle and violence; do not use it as decorative "edge".
