# Paul Rand (1914–1996)

**Essence:** Modern art's shapes, a child's directness and a corporation's seriousness, resolved into marks that are simple enough to last and loose enough to play.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Elastic elementary mark | He reduced trademarks to elementary, universal shapes (IBM 1956, Westinghouse 1960, ABC 1962) that survive any size — and then allowed them to be played with when a concept demanded it. | Define a core mark that works at 16px and in one colour, plus a documented set of sanctioned playful variants (seasonal, rebus, animated). The core is never distorted; the variants are explicitly listed. |
| G2 · Rebus | A word replaced by a picture (*Eye-Bee-M*, 1981): reading becomes a small game the audience solves. | Use a one-line rebus in an onboarding or 404 screen; icons may pun on product names. Limit: one rebus per product surface. |
| G3 · Contrast pairs | Red against green, organic shape against geometric type, photographic tone against flat colour, torn edge against sharp edge. | Palette built on two complementary hues of equal lightness plus black; illustrations combine one photographic cut-out with flat shapes; at least one torn or irregular edge per composition. |
| G4 · Handwriting beside crisp type | A scrawled tag or signature next to mechanical stencil or geometric lettering gives warmth and authorship. | Personal annotation layer: short handwritten notes (SVG, drawn) over rigid cards or charts for tips and highlights; maximum one per view. |
| G5 · Stripes as mnemonic | Horizontal bars unify the three IBM letters and evoke scan lines; a pattern becomes the memory hook. | A stripe motif (e.g. 8 horizontal bars with equal gaps) used to mask hero headings, as loader animation, and as section dividers — one consistent bar ratio. |
| G6 · Primitive-shape characters | His children's books (with Ann Rand, 1950s) build figures from a few flat shapes. | Characters and mascots built from 3–5 primitives (circle, rectangle, triangle, blob), flat colour, no outlines, no gradients. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red ~#E1251B, green ~#3A8F3A, IBM-style blue ~#1F70C1, yellow, black on white; flat, saturated, few at a time.
- **Type:** Bodoni, Futura, City (basis of the IBM logo), Akzidenz and his own handwriting → Bodoni Moda, Jost, Zilla Slab.
- **Form:** cut paper, collage, photograms, dots, stripes, simple pictographs.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, elements floating in white space, a few large shapes with one surprise.

## Attitude

Rand defined design as the integration of form and function, and the designer's cultural role as raising public taste rather than serving its lowest level. He believed a trademark suggests rather than depicts, and that play and beauty are serious business. Late in life he openly opposed what he saw as style-for-style's-sake.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `saul-bass`, `bruno-munari` — reduced symbols with personality; design for children and adults alike.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — Rand's play inside Swiss order is the midpoint of the atlas axes: rational yet warm identity systems.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type` — he rejected it explicitly; mixed with his elemental marks, the result loses both clarity and edge.

## Don't copy

IBM (1956; striped 1972), *Eye-Bee-M* (1981), Westinghouse (1960), ABC (1962), UPS (1961), NeXT (1986); *Direction* covers (1938–45); the children's-book illustrations.
