# New York School (American modernist graphic design)

**Essence:** European modernism made pragmatic and funny, one big idea, where word and picture complete each other.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The single big idea | Designers such as Rand, Bass and the Doyle Dane Bernbach teams reduced a message to one concept, a pun, a fused symbol, a surprising juxtaposition, and let it carry everything. | Every landing section has a written one-line concept before any layout. If headline and image can be understood separately, redesign until each needs the other. |
| G2 · Word-image fusion (art/copy team) | Writer and art director worked as one creative team, so headlines and pictures were conceived together rather than layout following copy. | Microcopy and illustration are specced as a pair in the component (`headline + image` slot); no placeholder copy in design reviews; writers in design critiques. |
| G3 · Modern art as a business tool | Collage, torn edges, free shapes and flat colour borrowed from Klee, Arp, Matisse and the cubists entered ads, book jackets and annual reports. | Illustration system of cut and torn shapes in 3 to 4 flat colours, with photographic cut-outs mixed in; used for marketing and empty states, not for data UI. |
| G4 · Honest restraint | The "new advertising" replaced superlatives with factual, self-aware headlines, a small product picture and wide white space. | Hero with a small product image (≤ 25% of width) in open space, a modest, witty headline and plain factual body copy; no badges, no exclamation marks. |
| G5 · Figurative typography | Letters became objects and objects became letters, a round "o" as a wheel, a word that sags like a mattress. | Allow one glyph per headline to be replaced by an icon or object of identical optical size; reserve for brand moments, never navigation. |
| G6 · Intuitive asymmetry | Space was organized by eye and tension rather than a published grid; balance is felt, not calculated. | A soft 12-column grid with one sanctioned "break" per screen: an image or headline that crosses the gutter or bleeds off the edge. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** primaries and complementaries in flat areas (red ~#D7262E, green ~#2E8B57, ochre ~#E3A72F, blue ~#1F4E9A) with black and lots of white.
- **Type:** Futura, Bodoni, Franklin Gothic, Clarendon and 19th-century wood types mixed with handwriting → Jost, Bodoni Moda, Libre Franklin, Zilla Slab.
- **Form:** cut paper, collage, photograms, silhouettes, pictographic glyphs.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, one dominant image, generous white space, type integrated into the picture.

## Attitude

American designers borrowed the avant-garde's forms but answered to a competitive, commercial, egalitarian culture: a design had to be clear, memorable and personal at once. Wit was a form of respect, it assumes the audience is intelligent.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `push-pin-studios`, `paul-rand`, `saul-bass`, the same conceptual, illustrative American line.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, American wit placed on Swiss structure is the template for much later studio work.
- **Clashes with:** `ulm-school`, semiotic method and anonymous system leave no room for the personal pun.

## Don't copy

Doyle Dane Bernbach's Volkswagen campaign (from 1959), Rand's IBM/ABC/Westinghouse marks, Bass's film titles and posters, George Lois's *Esquire* covers (1960s).
