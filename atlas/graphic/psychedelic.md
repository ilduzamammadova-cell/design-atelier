# Psychedelic Posters

**Essence:** Lettering melted into ornament and colours set to vibrate, a poster to be deciphered by insiders, not read by everyone.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Letters that fill a shape | Wes Wilson, reviving Vienna Secession lettering, swelled and bent letters until the words filled a silhouette or the whole sheet. | For event heroes only: display words fitted into a shape via variable-font width/weight axes or envelope warp; always paired with a plain, accessible text version of the same information. |
| G2 · Equal-value complementaries | Victor Moscoso, who studied colour with Josef Albers at Yale, placed complementary hues of equal lightness side by side so edges shimmer. | Poster moments use two complementary hues with the same OKLCH lightness (e.g. L 0.65 orange vs blue) for a deliberate vibration. Never for body text or controls; enforce contrast checks on all functional UI. |
| G3 · Decipher, don't read | Promoters' posters spoke to a community that "got it"; businessmen famously could not read them. | Layered easter eggs for the community: hidden messages revealed on hover, focus or long-press; core information always available plainly. |
| G4 · Whiplash borders | Art Nouveau curves returned as frames, dividers and hair-like flows. | Ornamental dividers and frames from flowing SVG curves for music, festival or fashion contexts; one ornamental layer per screen. |
| G5 · Light-show liquidity | Concert light shows dissolved rooms into throbbing, oily colour; posters echoed that motion. | Slow fluid shader or gradient-mesh backgrounds (≥ 20s loops) behind event content; stop entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| G6 · Appropriated images recoloured | Moscoso and others recycled engravings, Victorian photographs and paintings, recoloured in clashing inks. | Public-domain imagery duotoned in neon pairs (magenta/acid green, orange/blue) as campaign art; always credit sources. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** fluorescent pinks, acid greens, electric blues, oranges, purple; Day-Glo and metallic inks in London.
- **Type:** hand-drawn, swelling Art Nouveau and Secession-derived lettering → hand-drawn only for display; Bricolage Grotesque or Fraunces for any readable supporting text.
- **Form:** whiplash curves, concentric circles, collage of old engravings, sensual figures.
- **Composition:** all-over, edge-to-edge, symmetrical, no empty space.

## Attitude

The San Francisco artists, Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Rick Griffin, Stanley Mouse and Alton Kelley, made posters for dance concerts at the Fillmore and Avalon ballrooms, mostly self-taught and outside the profession. The work celebrated altered perception, community and rejection of corporate modernism; in London, Martin Sharp and Hapshash and the Coloured Coat carried it into the underground press.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-nouveau`, `op-art`, `push-pin-studios`, ornament, optical colour and illustration.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, vibrating colour and warped type locked into a strict grid gives contemporary club and festival identities.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, uniform, legible brand systems and tribal decipherment negate each other.

## Don't copy

Specific Fillmore and Avalon posters (e.g. Wilson's 1966 Grateful Dead and Association posters, Moscoso's 1967 Chambers Brothers poster), Griffin's *Aoxomoxoa* cover (1969), Mouse and Kelley's skull-and-roses, Martin Sharp's *Mister Tambourine Man* (1967), Hapshash's UFO posters.
