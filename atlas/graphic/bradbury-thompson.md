# Bradbury Thompson (1911–1995)

**Essence:** The print shop as laboratory — process colours pulled apart, halftone dots enlarged, old engravings re-staged, then all of it disciplined into lucid pages.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Separations as palette | In *Westvaco Inspirations* (61 issues, 1938–62) he took four-colour process plates apart and overprinted them to create new colours and rhythms. | Palette = cyan ~#00A3E0, magenta ~#E4007C, yellow ~#FFE600, black, plus their overprint mixes. Overlapping shapes use `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; colour is generated by overlap, not by adding new swatches. |
| G2 · Printing artifacts as ornament | Halftone dots and letterforms enlarged until they become pattern and movement. | Section textures generated as coarse halftone (dot pitch 6–10px) from real product imagery; giant single letters cropped as background shapes. |
| G3 · Old engravings, new stage | With little budget for new art, he mined 18th- and 19th-century engravings and recombined them with modern type and colour. | Illustration library built from public-domain archives (e.g. museum open-access collections), recoloured in one process ink each and cropped dramatically; always credit the source. |
| G4 · Monoalphabet | *Alphabet 26* (1950) proposed a single form per letter, eliminating the capital/lowercase duplication. | Single-case typography for navigation and labels (all small caps or a unicase display face); sentence case only in long text. |
| G5 · Two modes: experiment and reading | Later he designed classical, highly readable formats (*Smithsonian*, the *Washburn College Bible*, 1979) with Old Style type. | Products get two declared modes: an expressive "cover" mode (colour, overprint, scale) and a calm "reading" mode (classical serif, wide margins, no effects) for long-form content. |
| G6 · Multiple exposure | A saxophonist repeated in offset, overprinted primaries shows motion and sound. | Hover or "playing" states duplicate an element as 2–3 offset ghosts in process colours at 40% opacity, multiply-blended. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** CMYK primaries and their overprints on white; black for engravings and type.
- **Type:** Caslon, Baskerville and other Old Style faces; Futura and grotesques for display; enlarged single letters → Libre Caslon, Libre Baskerville, Jost.
- **Form:** halftone dots, engravings, giant letters, stripes, flat geometric shapes.
- **Composition:** complex but legible flow across spreads; dramatic scale contrasts; strong left edges.

## Attitude

Thompson saw production limits as invitations: the typecase and press were his canvas. He combined deep historical knowledge with experiment, then turned toward quiet classical order when the content was meant to be read for hours. Teaching at Yale (1956–95), he passed on both halves.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `cipe-pineles`, `herb-lubalin` — editorial invention and typographic play.
- **Productive tension with:** `armin-hofmann` — Hofmann's black-and-white discipline gives Thompson's chroma a backbone.
- **Clashes with:** `minimalism-art` — overprint exuberance turns timid when forced into reductive silence.

## Don't copy

Specific *Westvaco Inspirations* spreads (e.g. issues 151, 186, 210, 216), the *Washburn College Bible* pages, his U.S. postage stamps, the *Alphabet 26* specimen.
