# Kurt Schwitters (1887–1948)

**Essence:** A collagist who could also run a city's stationery — proof that play and system can live in the same hand.

Schwitters named his one-man movement *Merz* (a fragment of *Kommerz* found in a collage, 1919). He published the magazine *Merz* (1923–32), ran an advertising office, the Merz-Werbezentrale (from 1924), with Pelikan inks as a major client, worked as typographic consultant to the city of Hanover, and founded the designers' circle *Der Ring neuer Werbegestalter* (1927–28). See `new-typography`.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Everything is material | Tram tickets, wrappers, string and labels — washed, sorted by size and colour, then composed colour against colour, texture against texture | Keep a material library: scanned real ephemera reduced to 2–3 tones, tagged with colour and size tokens so headers can pull matching scraps programmatically |
| G2 · Type plays the characters | In *Die Scheuche* (1925, with Käte Steinitz and Theo van Doesburg) letters, bars and rules from the type case act out a fairy tale | Onboarding or explainer animations made only from the UI's own glyphs, brackets and rules — no separate illustration style to maintain |
| G3 · Loose look, strict measure | His 1920s ad work (e.g. the Pelikan pages in *Merz 11*, 1924) keeps collage energy but aligns everything to constructivist axes | Collage layer allows ±3° rotation, but every element snaps its baseline or edge to the 8px grid; it looks casual and measures exact |
| G4 · Civic system | For the city of Hanover he standardised forms and stationery: rule bars, sans-serif, fixed zones | Document system: letter, invoice, notice and receipt generated from one component set with locked rule bars, type sizes and address zones |
| G5 · Sound as layout | The *Ursonate* (1922–32) scores spoken syllables like music; his *Systemschrift* (1927) tried to make letters reflect sounds | Loading or audio states that spell a word with letter-spacing that stretches and contracts with the sound/progress |
| G6 · One absurd detail inside rigor | Nonsense placed in a functional frame keeps a system human | One humorous micro-detail per functional screen (copy, easter egg), never on a critical path or error state |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** muted papers (ochre, brown, grey-blue, rust) plus printer's red and black — approx. `#B9A27A`, `#6E5B45`, `#8FA3B0`, `#C0392B`, `#111111`.
- **Type:** grotesques from the case, heavy rules, pointing-hand dingbats, his experimental phonetic alphabet. Free: Work Sans, Archivo, Space Grotesk; rules as 4–8px bars.
- **Form:** torn and cut paper, overlapping rectangles, stamped letters, circles.
- **Composition:** constructivist asymmetry with collage residue; text blocks hung on strong verticals.

## Attitude

Art and commerce are both raw material; the designer composes rather than confesses. The Berlin Dadaists refused him as too bourgeois, and he answered by building bridges — to Lissitzky, Van Doesburg and the advertising world. Driven out by the Nazis, he went to Norway (1937) and Britain (1940).

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `dada`, `hendrik-werkman` — found material and the joy of print.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a rigorous grid product whose marketing layer is collage made from product scraps.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco` — lacquer-and-gold glamour cancels the humility of scrap paper.

## Don't copy

Specific Merz collages (e.g. *Merzbild 25A*, *Das Undbild*), the *Merzbau*, the *Ursonate* score layout, the *Die Scheuche* spreads, his Pelikan advertisements and the Merz triangle mark.

## Study

- *Merz* magazine (1923–32), esp. *Merz 11* "Typoreklame" (1924) and *Merz 8/9* "Nasci" (1924, with Lissitzky); *Die Scheuche* (1925); "Thesen über Typographie" (1924).
- Sprengel Museum Hannover and its Kurt Schwitters Archive (sprengel-museum.de); John Elderfield, *Kurt Schwitters* (1985).
- Monoskop (monoskop.org) for digitised *Merz* issues; Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design*, "The Ring".
