# Bauhaus

**Essence:** Start from elements, point, line, plane, colour, material, and build every object from them, for the machine.

Walter Gropius opened the Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar in 1919; it moved to Dessau (1925 to 32) and briefly Berlin before closing under Nazi pressure in 1933. Its 1923 exhibition marked the turn from craft romanticism ("art and craft") to "art and technology, a new unity". Graphic leaders: `laszlo-moholy-nagy`, `herbert-bayer`, Joost Schmidt. Furniture and metalwork are covered in `marcel-breuer` and `marianne-brandt`.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Foundation course first | Every student, whatever the discipline, began with the *Vorkurs*: elemental studies of material, form and colour (Itten, then Moholy-Nagy, then Albers) | Bootstrap a design system from primitives before components: one shape language (radius scale), one surface/elevation logic, one colour logic. Any component that needs a new primitive triggers a system review |
| G2 · Shape ↔ colour correspondence | Kandinsky's 1923 workshop questionnaire paired triangle, yellow, square, red, circle-blue as elemental equivalents | Bind shape and colour semantically and keep it fixed product-wide: e.g. warning = triangle + yellow, stop = square + red, info = circle + blue; never reuse a shape for another meaning |
| G3 · Prototypes for industry | Workshops produced models (lamps, textiles, wallpaper) to be licensed for mass production | Every design deliverable ships as a production asset, a component with props, variants and tokens, never a one-off mock |
| G4 · Standardised formats | The print workshop used DIN paper sizes, sans-serif and lower-case as norms | Exports, documents and share cards derived from √2 (A-series) proportions; a fixed set of formats rather than per-project sizes |
| G5 · Total work, one workshop | Architecture, furniture, typography, theatre and textiles under one roof and one set of principles | One cross-discipline crit: product, brand, motion and engineering review the same artefact against the same principles; designers commit tokens directly to code |
| G6 · Publish the school | Fourteen Bauhausbücher (1925 to 30), the *bauhaus* magazine and exhibitions spread its ideas | Treat the design system's documentation as a published series, each volume one principle with worked examples |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** primaries on white and grey with black, approx. `#D52B1E`, `#F6C000`, `#1F4FA0`, `#1A1A1A`, `#F2F0EB`.
- **Type:** grotesques (e.g. Venus Grotesk in the print shop), Bayer's single-case universal alphabet as an experiment; Futura was contemporary but not a Bauhaus product. Free: Jost, Josefin Sans, Work Sans.
- **Form:** circle, square, triangle; tubular steel; glass curtain walls; photography.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, grid-based, bars and rules, type and photo integrated.

## Attitude

Gropius wanted a new guild of artists and craftsmen that would give the machine-made object quality and dignity; he claimed not to want a "Bauhaus style", yet one emerged. The Nazi closure scattered its people worldwide (New Bauhaus Chicago 1937, Black Mountain, Harvard), which made the school a global template. Today the circle-square-triangle-primaries surface is a cliché; the method is what transplants.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `swiss-international`, `ulm-school`, its direct educational heirs.
- **Productive tension with:** `arts-and-crafts`, its own origin; reviving the craft half produces digital products with a made-by-hand finish on a systematic base.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco`, luxury ornament contradicts elemental economy; mixes tend toward hotel-lobby kitsch.

## Don't copy

The Bauhaus seals (1919 and Schlemmer's 1922 profile); Joost Schmidt's 1923 exhibition poster; Bayer's universal alphabet drawings; the Dessau building's lettering; Bauhausbücher covers; the Wagenfeld lamp, Brandt teapot and Breuer chairs.
