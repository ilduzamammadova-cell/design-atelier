# UX / HCI — sources for study

The strongest resources behind the `atlas/ux/` cards, for a designer building an interaction attitude.

1. Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (1988/2002; revised and expanded 2013) — the vocabulary every UX conversation still uses: discoverability, mapping, constraints, feedback, the gulfs and seven stages of action, slips versus mistakes; the 2013 edition adds signifiers, the Double Diamond and activity-centred design.
2. Jef Raskin, *The Humane Interface* (2000) — the rigorous case for modelessness, undo over warnings and habit-safe design.
3. Nielsen Norman Group article library, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ — decades of evidence-based guidance, including the 10 heuristics.
4. Ben Shneiderman et al., *Designing the User Interface* (6th ed., 2016) — the standard HCI textbook; direct manipulation and the Eight Golden Rules.
5. Alan Cooper et al., *About Face* (4th ed., 2014) — goal-directed design, personas, posture and excise in one practical volume.
6. Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (2nd ed., 2001) — the foundation of honest, dense data display.
7. Bill Moggridge, *Designing Interactions* (2006) — first-hand interviews with the people who invented the mouse, GUI, PalmPilot and more.
8. Doug Engelbart Institute, https://dougengelbart.org — the 1968 demo, the 1962 framework and the augmentation philosophy.
9. Bret Victor, https://worrydream.com — essays and talks (*Magic Ink*, *Inventing on Principle*, *Learnable Programming*) that redefine what tools for thought could be.
10. Alan Kay, "The Early History of Smalltalk" (ACM HOPL, 1993) — how PARC's interface ideas were actually conceived.
11. Weiser & Brown, "Designing Calm Technology" (1995), https://calmtech.com — plus Amber Case, *Calm Technology* (2015) for the eight principles.
12. Harry Brignull, https://www.deceptive.design and *Deceptive Patterns* (2023) — the definitive catalogue of what never to do.
13. Kat Holmes, *Mismatch* (2018) and the Microsoft Inclusive Design toolkit, https://inclusive.microsoft.design — inclusion as a source of innovation.
14. W3C WCAG 2.2, https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ — the testable floor for accessible interfaces.
15. Jon Yablonski, https://lawsofux.com and *Laws of UX* (2nd ed., 2024) — psychology findings condensed into usable heuristics.
16. Jeff Johnson, *Designing with the Mind in Mind* (3rd ed., 2020) — the perception and cognition science behind the rules.
17. Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think, Revisited* (2014) and *Rocket Surgery Made Easy* (2010) — plain, funny, practical usability and DIY testing.
18. Dan Saffer, *Microinteractions* (2013) — anatomy of small moments; trigger, rules, feedback, loops.
19. Brad Frost, *Atomic Design*, https://atomicdesign.bradfrost.com — free book on building component systems.
20. Amershi et al., "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction" (CHI 2019) — 18 tested guidelines, the best starting point for AI UX.
21. Google PAIR, *People + AI Guidebook*, https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook — practical worksheets for AI product design.
22. Emily Campbell, Shape of AI, https://www.shapeof.ai — a living pattern library for generative and agentic interfaces.
23. Marc Stickdorn et al., *This Is Service Design Doing* (2018) and the GOV.UK Service Manual, https://www.gov.uk/service-manual — designing whole services, not screens.
24. B. J. Fogg, *Tiny Habits* (2019), read alongside Natasha Dow Schüll, *Addiction by Design* (2012) — behaviour design and its ethical shadow.
25. Richard Saul Wurman, *Information Anxiety* (1989) — LATCH and the origins of information architecture.
26. Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013), https://www.momtestbook.com — a short, practical rulebook for interviews that yield facts instead of polite lies (past behaviour over opinions, deflect compliments, judge by commitment); use it before product research and when interviewing a designer about their own taste.
