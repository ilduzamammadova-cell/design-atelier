# Sources: graphic design from writing systems to c. 1910 (agent: graphic-early)

The strongest places to study this period, one line each.

1. **Philip B. Meggs & Alston W. Purvis, *Meggs' History of Graphic Design* (5th ed., 2012), chs. 1–12.** The standard survey from cuneiform to Behrens; best single map of the period with dense figure captions.
2. **Jeremy Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design* (2001), "A New Profession".** Short, well-illustrated entries on Behrens, Van de Velde, Bradley, Gill, Mackintosh and the Wiener Werkstätte, framed by the rise of the profession.
3. **British Library, Medieval and Earlier Manuscripts (bl.uk).** Lindisfarne Gospels, Diamond Sutra (868 CE) and hundreds of digitized illuminated books to study page structure up close.
4. **Trinity College Dublin Digital Collections (digitalcollections.tcd.ie).** Full Book of Kells facsimile, the best way to see diminuendo initials and carpet pages at zoom.
5. **Gallica, Bibliothèque nationale de France (gallica.bnf.fr).** Tory's *Champ Fleury*, Fournier's *Manuel typographique*, Didot editions and thousands of French posters, all free to view.
6. **Museum Plantin-Moretus, Antwerp (museumplantinmoretus.be).** UNESCO-listed printing house with the world's oldest surviving presses and Garamond-era punches and matrices.
7. **Gutenberg Museum, Mainz (gutenberg-museum.de).** Gutenberg Bibles, reconstructed press and type-casting demonstrations, for understanding the original "component system".
8. **St Bride Library, London (sbf.org.uk).** Leading printing library: Caslon and Victorian type specimens, wood type and printing ephemera.
9. **Hamilton Wood Type & Printing Museum, Two Rivers, Wisconsin (woodtype.org).** Largest wood-type collection; specimens and workshops on display typography's industrial roots.
10. **Rob Roy Kelly, *American Wood Type 1828–1900* (1969).** The classic study of wood-type manufacture and letterform categories.
11. **Michael Twyman, *A History of Chromolithography: Printed Colour for All* (2013).** Authoritative account of colour printing's technique and visual language.
12. **Owen Jones, *The Grammar of Ornament* (1856), digitized at Internet Archive.** The 37 principles plus 100 plates, the first systematic "pattern library".
13. **Victoria and Albert Museum (vam.ac.uk).** Morris & Co., Owen Jones, Beardsley, Arts and Crafts and Art Nouveau holdings with good online records.
14. **William Morris Gallery, Walthamstow (wmgallery.org.uk).** Morris's patterns, Kelmscott books and process material in his family home.
15. **William S. Peterson, *The Kelmscott Press* (1991).** Best study of how Morris actually designed type, borders and books.
16. **Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983), with Playfair, ed. Wainer & Spence (2005).** Playfair's inventions and the modern rules they led to.
17. **Musée d'Orsay and Musée des Arts Décoratifs, Paris (musee-orsay.fr, madparis.fr).** French Art Nouveau objects and the great poster collections (Chéret, Grasset, Lautrec, Mucha).
18. **Musée Toulouse-Lautrec, Albi, and the Mucha Museum / Mucha Foundation, Prague.** Single-artist collections with full poster runs and preparatory work.
19. **The Hunterian, University of Glasgow, and Glasgow School of Art Archives.** Mackintosh's drawings, lettering, the reassembled Mackintosh House and Glasgow Four material.
20. **MAK – Museum of Applied Arts, Vienna (mak.at), with ÖNB ANNO for *Ver Sacrum*.** Wiener Werkstätte archive and the digitized Secession magazine, a model of a design-laboratory publication.
21. **Tilmann Buddensieg, *Industriekultur: Peter Behrens and the AEG* (1984).** The definitive record of the first corporate identity, from trademark to turbine hall.
22. **Paul Greenhalgh (ed.), *Art Nouveau 1890–1914* (V&A, 2000).** Broadest international survey of the style across media and cities.
23. **Ditchling Museum of Art + Craft (ditchlingmuseumartcraft.org.uk).** Gill, Johnston and the Ditchling guild in context, including how the museum handles Gill's abuse history.
24. **Palace Museum, Beijing, and National Palace Museum, Taipei (dpm.org.cn, npm.gov.tw).** Canonical Chinese calligraphy and seal collections, for learning structure from originals, not fonts.
