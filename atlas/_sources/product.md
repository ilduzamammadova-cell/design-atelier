# Sources, product and industrial design (agent: product)

The strongest resources for studying industrial and product design from the 1850s to 2026, chosen for depth and for how well they show *why* forms were chosen.

1. **Vitra Design Museum, Weil am Rhein**: https://www.design-museum.de, the deepest furniture and industrial design collection in Europe; its monographs (Eames, Panton, Colombo, Noguchi, Grcic, Memphis) are the best single-designer references.
2. **Die Neue Sammlung, The Design Museum, Munich**: https://dnstdm.de, one of the oldest design collections, strong on German industrial design, Braun and Sapper.
3. **Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum**: https://collection.cooperhewitt.org, searchable online collection; holds the Henry Dreyfuss archive, the key source for human-factors design.
4. **MoMA Architecture and Design collection**: https://www.moma.org/collection, canonical modern objects with dates and makers; its 1972 catalogue *Italy: The New Domestic Landscape* is essential for Italian design.
5. **Design Museum, London**: https://designmuseum.org, exhibitions on contemporary product practice (e.g. Jongerius' *Breathing Colour*) and a good online design-history archive.
6. **HfG-Archiv / Museum Ulm**: https://hfg-archiv.museumulm.de, primary documents of the Ulm School: curricula, student projects, Braun and Lufthansa work; the root of systematic product design.
7. **Associazione Archivio Storico Olivetti, Ivrea**: https://www.archiviostoricolivetti.it, the full record of a company where design, architecture, graphics and social programmes were one strategy.
8. **Vitsœ, "Good design" (Dieter Rams' ten principles)**: https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design, the clearest primary statement of reductive product ethics, with context from the company that still makes his shelving.
9. **Klaus Klemp and Keiko Ueki-Polet (eds.), *Less and More: The Design Ethos of Dieter Rams* (2009)**, the most complete visual catalogue of Braun-era work, with control layouts that translate directly to UI.
10. **Henry Dreyfuss, *Designing for People* (1955) and *The Measure of Man* (1960)**, the founding texts of ergonomics and user-centred design; still the best argument for designing for the range of human bodies.
11. **Jeffrey L. Meikle, *Twentieth Century Limited: Industrial Design in America, 1925 to 1939* (1979)**, the standard history of streamlining and the birth of the design consultancy.
12. **Eames Office**: https://www.eamesoffice.com, films, essays and project histories; *Powers of Ten* and the Eames' constraint-led method in their own material.
13. **Library of Congress, Charles and Ray Eames papers and Raymond Loewy drawings**: https://www.loc.gov, primary sketches and correspondence; shows process rather than finished icons.
14. **Barbara Radice, *Memphis: Research, Experiences, Results, Failures and Successes of New Design* (1984)**, the insider account of Memphis by its critic, explaining intent behind the patterns.
15. **Andrea Branzi, *The Hot House: Italian New Wave Design* (1984)**, a participant's theory of Italian radical and postmodern design.
16. **Enzo Mari, *Autoprogettazione?* (Corraini edition of the 1974 project)**, plans and argument for open, self-built design; a direct ancestor of open-source design systems.
17. **Fondazione Achille Castiglioni, Milan**: https://www.fondazioneachillecastiglioni.it, studio-museum with his collection of anonymous objects; the best place to understand design by observation.
18. **Kenya Hara, *Designing Design* (Lars Müller, 2007) and *White* (2009/2010)**, the clearest writing on emptiness, sensory design and Muji's philosophy.
19. **Naoto Fukasawa and Jasper Morrison, *Super Normal: Sensations of the Ordinary* (Lars Müller, 2007)**, 200 ordinary objects with commentary; a training manual for quiet, archetype-led design.
20. **Hella Jongerius, *I Don't Have a Favourite Colour* (Gestalten, 2016)**, practical colour research from building Vitra's colour library; directly useful for building colour tokens.
21. **IEEE Spectrum feature on the Olivetti Elea 9003**: https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-italian-computer-olivettis-elea-9003-was-a-study-in-elegant-ergonomic-design, rare detailed account of human-scale, colour-coded computer design (150 cm racks, mosaic console).
22. **Snow White design language documentation and Hartmut Esslinger, *Keep It Simple* (2013)**, measurable rules (groove pitch, radii) of frog's Apple language; the clearest pre-digital model of design tokens.
23. **Teenage Engineering guides**: https://teenage.engineering/guides, official manuals showing how hardware colour-codes map to screen graphics; a live case of product-to-UI translation.
24. **Gary Hustwit, *Objectified* (2009) and *Rams* (2018)**, documentary interviews with Rams, Ive, Fukasawa, Morrison and the Bouroullecs, among others, in their own words.
