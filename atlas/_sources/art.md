# Sources, art (painters, sculptors, fine-art movements)

The strongest resources for studying how fine art's visual thinking transfers to design. One line each: what it is and why it is worth the time.

1. **Josef Albers, Interaction of Color** (Yale University Press, 1963; 50th-anniversary edition and app, 2013), the single most useful color book for interface designers: color judged in context, by experiment.
2. **Josef and Anni Albers Foundation** (albersfoundation.org), archives, teaching material and images for both Alberses; the closest thing to their classroom.
3. **Anni Albers, On Weaving** (1965; expanded Princeton edition, 2017), structure-born pattern and the grid as a humane system, written by a maker.
4. **Paul Klee, Pedagogical Sketchbook** (1925) and **The Thinking Eye** (ed. Spiller, 1961), an artist's complete course in line, motion, rhythm and color; directly usable for motion and icon design.
5. **Wassily Kandinsky, Point and Line to Plane** (1926), a taxonomy of visual primitives; useful for diagram and illustration vocabularies.
6. **Meggs' History of Graphic Design**, ch. 13 "The Influence of Modern Art" (and ch. 11 on ukiyo-e, ch. 15 on suprematism and De Stijl), the clearest map of how cubism, futurism, Dada, surrealism and expressionism fed graphic design.
7. **MoMA collection online** (moma.org/collection), high-quality records and essays for Matisse, Mondrian, Malevich, Kelly, Martin, LeWitt, Warhol and more; also archive pages for "The Responsive Eye" (1965).
8. **Tate collection and Tate Papers** (tate.org.uk), strong texts on Rothko's Seagram Murals, Riley, Hockney, Delaunay, Kusama's Infinity Rooms, Albers retrospectives.
9. **Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum** (guggenheim.org), Kandinsky holdings and the catalogues of Hilma af Klint (2018 to 19), Agnes Martin (2016) and James Turrell (2013).
10. **The Met, Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History** (metmuseum.org/toah), concise, reliable essays on ukiyo-e, cubism and modern movements; plus the Leonard A. Lauder Cubist Collection.
11. **Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, Japanese prints** (collections.mfa.org), one of the largest ukiyo-e collections outside Japan, zoomable images for studying cropping and keyline.
12. **Centre Pompidou** (centrepompidou.fr), Matisse cut-outs, Delaunay, Kandinsky (Nina Kandinsky bequest); key for Paris-based modernism.
13. **Hilma af Klint Foundation** (hilmaafklint.se), the authoritative source on the Paintings for the Temple and the catalogue raisonné.
14. **Kunstmuseum Den Haag and the RKD Mondrian Edition Project** (kunstmuseum.nl, rkd.nl), the largest Mondrian collection and his writings and letters.
15. **Chinati Foundation and Judd Foundation** (chinati.org, juddfoundation.org), minimalism as permanent installation, with Judd's writings on art, furniture and space.
16. **Sol LeWitt, "Paragraphs on Conceptual Art"** (Artforum, 1967) and **MASS MoCA wall-drawing installation** (massmoca.org), the founding text of rule-based art, effectively a manifesto for design systems.
17. **Calder Foundation** (calder.org), chronology, photographs and a works database; the best way to study balance and motion.
18. **gerhard-richter.com**: the artist's official online catalogue raisonné, searchable by series (Colour Charts, Strips, Abstracts).
19. **Rothko Chapel** (rothkochapel.org) and **National Gallery of Art, Washington** (nga.gov), color as environment; NGA also holds major Rothko, Calder and Escher collections.
20. **Bridget Riley, The Eye's Mind: Collected Writings** (1999), a working painter's precise account of perception and pattern.
21. **haring.com** (Keith Haring Foundation), archive of the subway drawings, Pop Shop and public projects; a model of art as accessible communication.
22. **Escher in Het Paleis** (escherinhetpaleis.nl) and Doris Schattschneider, **Visions of Symmetry** (1990), the mathematics and method behind tessellation and metamorphosis.
23. **Zentrum Paul Klee, Bern** (zpk.org), the largest Klee collection, with a digital guide to his teaching and work.
24. **Agnes Martin, Writings** (Hatje Cantz, 1992), short texts on emptiness, perfection and humility; the attitude behind quiet interfaces.
25. **Roden Crater and jamesturrell.com**: documentation of Skyspaces and light works; essential for anyone designing with light, gradients and time.
