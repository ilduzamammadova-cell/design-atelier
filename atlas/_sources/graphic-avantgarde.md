# Sources, graphic design c. 1905 to 1945 (pictorial modernism and the avant-garde)

Agent key: graphic-avantgarde. Resources for the designer to study, strongest first.

1. Philip B. Meggs & Alston W. Purvis, *Meggs' History of Graphic Design*, 5th ed. (2011), ch. 12 to 17, the standard narrative of the period with dense captions; best single overview of who did what and when.
2. Jeremy Aynsley, *A Century of Graphic Design* (2001), section "The New Design Experiment" (pp. 42 to 92), short, image-led entries on each figure, including the Ring and Studio Boggeri, which most surveys skip.
3. Jan Tschichold, *The New Typography* (1928; Eng. trans. Ruari McLean, University of California Press, 1995), the movement's own handbook; read it to get the rules first-hand.
4. Christopher Burke, *Active Literature: Jan Tschichold and New Typography* (Hyphen Press, 2007), careful, well-illustrated analysis of how the New Typography was actually practised.
5. Johanna Drucker, *The Visible Word: Experimental Typography and Modern Art, 1909 to 1923* (1994), the best account of Futurist, Dada and Russian typography as language.
6. Dawn Ades, *Photomontage* (Thames & Hudson, 1976; rev. 1986), concise history from Berlin Dada to Soviet propaganda, with the key images.
7. Merrill C. Berman Collection (mcbcollection.com), the largest private collection of avant-garde graphic design, searchable online with high-quality images and essays.
8. MoMA collection and exhibition archive (moma.org), Lissitzky, Rodchenko, Stenberg (1997 retrospective), Bauhaus (1938, 2009), Tschichold Collection; press releases and catalogues online.
9. Van Abbemuseum, Eindhoven (vanabbemuseum.nl), one of the largest El Lissitzky holdings in the world.
10. Bauhaus-Archiv / Museum für Gestaltung, Berlin (bauhaus.de) and Stiftung Bauhaus Dessau (bauhaus-dessau.de), primary archives for Bauhaus print, teaching material and the Bauhausbücher.
11. Ellen Lupton & J. Abbott Miller, *The ABCs of ▲■●: The Bauhaus and Design Theory* (1991), sharp short essays on Bauhaus form theory and its afterlife in design.
12. Harvard Art Museums, Bauhaus collections and the essay "The New Typography of the Ring" (harvardartmuseums.org), strong online access to Bauhaus and Ring material.
13. Monoskop (monoskop.org), digitised avant-garde periodicals and books (*Merz*, *De Stijl*, *LEF*, Czech avant-garde) to study whole publications, not single images.
14. Sprengel Museum Hannover, Kurt Schwitters Archive (sprengel-museum.de), Merz, Schwitters' advertising and municipal work.
15. RKD, Netherlands Institute for Art History (rkd.nl), Van Doesburg archive and Dutch avant-garde documentation; pair with Alston W. Purvis, *Dutch Graphic Design 1918 to 1945* (1992).
16. Christina Lodder, *Russian Constructivism* (Yale, 1983), the scholarly foundation on constructivist theory and practice.
17. Margarita Tupitsyn, *Gustav Klutsis and Valentina Kulagina* (ICP, 2004), Soviet propaganda photomontage in depth.
18. Iva Janáková (ed.), *Ladislav Sutnar: Prague-New York, Design in Action* (2003), the bridge from Czech modernism to American information design.
19. Eric Dluhosch & Rostislav Švácha (eds.), *Karel Teige 1900 to 1951* (MIT, 1999), Teige's theory, poetism and book design.
20. Marie Neurath & Robin Kinross, *The Transformer: Principles of Making Isotype Charts* (Hyphen Press, 2009), with the Otto and Marie Neurath Isotype Collection, University of Reading, the working method behind pictorial statistics.
21. London Transport Museum (ltmuseum.co.uk), Frank Pick's patronage: Kauffer's posters, Johnston's lettering, Beck's diagram; pair with Ken Garland, *Mr Beck's Underground Map* (1994).
22. Mark Haworth-Booth, *E. McKnight Kauffer: A Designer and His Public* (1979; rev. 2005), how a modernist sustained a career through enlightened clients.
23. Henri Mouron, *A. M. Cassandre* (1985) and the Musée des Arts Décoratifs, Paris (madparis.fr), Cassandre's posters, type and method.
24. Kerry William Purcell, *Alexey Brodovitch* (Phaidon, 2002), editorial pacing and art direction, with full spreads.
25. Jeremy Aynsley, *Graphic Design in Germany 1890 to 1945* (2000), Plakatstil, the Werkbund, Weimar modernism and Nazi-era design in one context.
