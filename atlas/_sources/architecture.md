# Sources, architecture

The strongest resources for studying the architecture section of the atlas. One line each, with why it is worth your time.

- **MoMA Architecture & Design collection**: https://www.moma.org, holds the Mies van der Rohe Archive and, with Columbia's Avery Library, the Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation Archives; drawings show how the ideas were developed, not just the finished photos.
- **Canadian Centre for Architecture (CCA)**: https://www.cca.qc.ca, research archive and exhibition catalogues (including Carlo Scarpa's work); good essays on architecture as a way of thinking.
- **Pritzker Architecture Prize**: https://www.pritzkerprize.com, reliable biographies, jury citations and acceptance speeches for Barragán, Ando, Hadid, Zumthor, SANAA and Ban.
- **Fondation Le Corbusier**: https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr, primary archive for the Modulor, the polychromy and the buildings.
- **Arthur Rüegg, *Le Corbusier: Polychromie architecturale*** (Birkhäuser, 1997; 3rd ed. 2016), the color keyboards with the reasoning behind them; a model for building a palette as chords.
- **Fundació Mies van der Rohe**: https://miesbcn.com, history and reconstruction of the Barcelona Pavilion; the best place to study the free-standing plane.
- **Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation**: https://franklloydwright.org, buildings, drawings and decorative designs; useful for compression-and-release and total design.
- **Alvar Aalto Foundation**: https://www.alvaraalto.fi, Paimio, Viipuri and furniture archives; with **Artek** (https://www.artek.fi) for the L-leg furniture system.
- **Louis I. Kahn Collection, Architectural Archives, University of Pennsylvania**: https://www.design.upenn.edu/architectural-archives, sketches of served/servant space and light studies.
- **Barragan Foundation**: https://www.barragan-foundation.org, guardian of Barragán's archive and image rights; read it to understand both the work and the usage restrictions.
- **Casa Luis Barragán**: https://www.casaluisbarragan.org, the house and studio (UNESCO 2004); the most direct record of his color and light.
- **MASP**: https://masp.org.br, Lina Bo Bardi's museum, with the glass easels back in use since 2015; documentation on her display philosophy.
- **Zeuler R. M. de A. Lima, *Lina Bo Bardi*** (Yale University Press, 2013), the standard critical biography.
- **Richard Murphy, *Carlo Scarpa and Castelvecchio Revisited*** (2017), detailed analysis of Scarpa's joints, mounts and layering of old and new.
- **Fondazione Querini Stampalia**: https://www.querinistampalia.org, a small, visitable Scarpa masterpiece of water, thresholds and seams.
- **Peter Zumthor, *Thinking Architecture* (1998) and *Atmospheres* (2006)**, short books that teach a sensory design method directly transferable to product work.
- **Benesse Art Site Naoshima**: https://benesse-artsite.jp, several Ando buildings in one place; the clearest lesson in approach paths and light.
- **Vitra Campus**: https://www.vitra.com, buildings by Hadid (Fire Station, 1993), Ando (Conference Pavilion, 1993) and others side by side; also the Vitra Design Museum collections.
- **Shigeru Ban Architects**: https://shigerubanarchitects.com, project documentation for paper-tube structures and disaster-relief kits.
- **SANAA**: https://www.sanaa.co.jp, official project archive; study the plans more than the photos.
- **Christopher Alexander, *A Pattern Language* (1977) and *The Nature of Order* (2002 to 05)**, the bridge between architecture and design systems; read alongside the Center for Environmental Structure site, https://www.patternlanguage.com.
- **Reyner Banham, *The New Brutalism: Ethic or Aesthetic?*** (1966) and the **SOS Brutalism database** (https://www.sosbrutalism.org), the movement's founding argument plus a global inventory of buildings.
- **UNESCO World Heritage listings**: https://whc.unesco.org, official descriptions for Le Corbusier (2016), Wright (2019), Gaudí (1984/2005) and Casa Barragán (2004).
- **Film: *My Architect*** (Nathaniel Kahn, 2003), a personal way into Kahn's buildings and the light inside them.
