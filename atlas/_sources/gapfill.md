# Gap-fill, sources for study

Resources behind the gap-fill cards (edward-johnston, beggarstaffs, willem-sandberg, ivan-sutherland, vannevar-bush, seymour-papert, constantin-brancusi, expressionism, gio-ponti, alexander-girard, louis-sullivan). One line each: what it is and why it is worth the time.

1. **Edward Johnston, *Writing & Illuminating, & Lettering*** (1906, still in print): the manual that founded modern Western calligraphy. Letterforms reasoned out from the tool and a historical model.
2. **Justin Howes, *Johnston's Underground Type*** (2000): how a scribe's letters became a city's infrastructure, with the drawings and revisions.
3. **London Transport Museum collections** (ltmuseum.co.uk): posters, signage and type material from Frank Pick's Underground. The best case study of an identity carried by a typeface.
4. **Colin Campbell, *The Beggarstaff Posters*** (1990): the one monograph on Pryde and Nicholson's partnership, covering the printed, unprinted and lost designs.
5. **V&A prints and posters collection** (collections.vam.ac.uk): Beggarstaff and 1890s poster holdings for studying flat colour and cut-paper reduction.
6. **Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam collection online** (stedelijk.nl/en/collection): Sandberg's catalogues, posters and *experimenta typographica*, catalogued and imaged.
7. ***Eye* magazine, "Willem Sandberg: warm printing"** (Summer 1997, eyemagazine.com): a clear account of his materials, overprinting and sequence-of-revelation catalogues.
8. **Ad Petersen (ed.), *Sandberg: Designer and Director of the Stedelijk*** (010 Publishers, 2004): the standard monograph on his museum print work.
9. **Ivan Sutherland, *Sketchpad* thesis** (1963; Cambridge Computer Laboratory TR-574, 2003, free PDF): the origin of constraints, instances and direct graphical manipulation, readable today.
10. **Vannevar Bush, "As We May Think"** (*The Atlantic*, July 1945): the founding text of associative trails and personal knowledge tools. Short enough to reread every year.
11. **Nyce and Kahn (eds.), *From Memex to Hypertext*** (1991): Bush's memex drafts with essays tracing the line to Engelbart, Nelson and the web.
12. **Seymour Papert, *Mindstorms*** (1980; free at mindstorms.media.mit.edu): turtles, body-syntonic learning and debugging-as-learning. Essential for anyone designing creative or learning tools.
13. **Logo Foundation, MIT Media Lab** (el.media.mit.edu/logo-foundation): history, papers and the design goals of Logo from its makers.
14. **Atelier Brancusi, Centre Pompidou** (centrepompidou.fr): the artist's own reconstructed studio, with sculptures, bases and his photographs. The arrangement is the lesson.
15. **Philadelphia Museum of Art and MoMA Brâncuși holdings** (philamuseum.org, moma.org): *The Kiss*, *Bird in Space* and *Endless Column* in high-quality records.
16. **Meggs' History of Graphic Design, ch. 13** "The Influence of Modern Art": the clearest short account of how expressionism, its woodcut and its posters fed graphic design.
17. **Brücke-Museum, Berlin** (bruecke-museum.de) and **Lenbachhaus, Munich** (lenbachhaus.de): the core collections of Die Brücke and Der Blaue Reiter respectively.
18. **MoMA, 1910 KG Brücke catalogue** (moma.org/collection): a direct look at carved lettering set beside plain grotesk type.
19. **Gio Ponti Archives** (gioponti.org) and **Domus archive** (domusweb.it): drawings, buildings and the magazine he edited for decades. Design as editorial culture.
20. **Lisa Licitra Ponti, *Gio Ponti: The Complete Work 1923 to 1978*** (1990): the full catalogue, from Richard-Ginori porcelain to the Pirelli Tower.
21. **Vitra Design Museum, Alexander Girard Estate** (design-museum.de) and *Alexander Girard: A Designer's Universe* (2016): the archive and the definitive exhibition catalogue.
22. **Museum of International Folk Art, Santa Fe** (internationalfolkart.org): the Girard collection and *Multiple Visions*. A lesson in abundance, colour and display.
23. **Art Institute of Chicago, Sullivan's *System of Architectural Ornament* drawings** (artic.edu): the 1922 to 23 plates that grow ornament from geometry, viewable online.
24. **Louis Sullivan, "The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered"** (1896): the source of "form follows function", read in full rather than as a slogan.
