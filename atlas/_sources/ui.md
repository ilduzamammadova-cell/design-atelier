# Sources — UI section (history of graphical and screen interfaces)

The strongest resources for studying interface languages as visual systems. One line each, with why it is worth your time.

1. **GUIdebook gallery** (guidebookgallery.org) — screenshot archive of nearly every desktop OS version (Alto/Star to Mac OS X, Windows, NeXTSTEP), plus reprinted period articles such as "Designing the Star User Interface."
2. **Folklore.org** (Andy Hertzfeld) — first-hand stories from the original Macintosh team; explains *why* round rects, the Command glyph and other decisions happened.
3. **Computer History Museum** (computerhistory.org) — Alto, Star, NeXT and Palm artifacts, oral histories and the Susan Kare profile; the authoritative dating source.
4. **Smith, Irby, Kimball, Verplank, Harslem, "Designing the Star User Interface," *Byte*, April 1982** — the eight principles behind the desktop metaphor; still the clearest statement of a consistent UI model.
5. **Stanford "Making the Macintosh" project** (web.stanford.edu/dept/SUL/sites/mac) — primary interviews, including Susan Kare on pixels, mosaics and road signs.
6. **Infinite Mac** (infinitemac.org) and the **Previous** NeXT emulator — run classic Mac OS and NeXTSTEP yourself; touching the real thing beats screenshots.
7. **98.css** (Jordan Scales, GitHub) — open-source CSS recreation of Windows 98 chrome; the best way to study bevel tokens in code.
8. **Web Design Museum** (webdesignmuseum.org) — curated captures of websites 1991–2006, strong on Flash-era studios and Web 2.0.
9. **Olia Lialina, "A Vernacular Web" (2005)** and **One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age** (with Dragan Espenschied) — the key critical writing and archive on amateur GeoCities-era design.
10. **GifCities** (gifcities.org, Internet Archive) — searchable GeoCities GIFs; a primary source for vernacular web ornament.
11. **Apple developer archive: iOS 7 UI Transition Guide and older iPhone/Aqua HIGs** (via developer.apple.com archive and the Wayback Machine) — official statements of each Apple design era.
12. **Material Design 3** (m3.material.io), the archived 2014 spec (material.io/archive), and **Google Design's "Expressive Material Design" research article** (design.google, 2025) — the most fully documented design system in public.
13. **Fluent 2** (fluent2.microsoft.design) and **Microsoft Learn: Mica and Acrylic** pages — official rationale for functional materials.
14. **Mike Kruzeniski, "From Transportation to Pixels" (2011)** and the **MIX10 "Windows Phone UI and Design Language"** session — Metro's origins in signage and Swiss typography, told by its designers.
15. **Nielsen Norman Group** (nngroup.com) — research-based critiques of skeuomorphism and flat design; balances the aesthetic history with usability evidence.
16. **Kent Sullivan, "The Windows 95 User Interface: A Case Study in Usability Engineering," CHI 1996** — how testing shaped the Start button and taskbar.
17. **Game UI Database** (gameuidatabase.com, Edd Coates) — thousands of searchable game screens filterable by genre, layout and element.
18. **Fagerholt & Lorentzon, *Beyond the HUD* (Chalmers, 2009)** — the diegetic / non-diegetic / spatial / meta framework for game interfaces.
19. **Tech at Bloomberg, "Designing the Terminal for Color Accessibility" (2021)** and *IEEE Spectrum*'s Bloomberg Terminal history — rare inside views of a density-first professional UI.
20. **pouet.net, Demozoo and scene.org** — catalogues and archives of demoscene productions; **demoscene-the-art-of-coding.net** for the heritage campaign.
21. **16colo.rs** and **textfiles.com** — ANSI/ASCII art packs and BBS-era text culture; **teletextart.co.uk** for living Teletext art.
22. **Winamp Skin Museum** (skins.webamp.org) and **Webamp** (Jordan Eldredge) — tens of thousands of user-made skins, browsable and playable in the browser.
23. **Mark Weiser & John Seely Brown, "Designing Calm Technology" (1995)** and **Amber Case, *Calm Technology* (2015)** — the theory behind ambient and e-ink interfaces.
24. **brutalistwebsites.com** (Pascal Deville) and **brutalist-web.design** (David Bryant Copeland) — the collection that named brutalist web design, and a set of usable principles distilled from it.
25. **Meggs' History of Graphic Design, ch. 24 "The Digital Revolution—and Beyond"** — places Kare's bitmap fonts, early desktop publishing and 1990s interactive media within graphic-design history.
