# Sources: serif and character typefaces (agent: type-serif)

The strongest places to study the typefaces in `atlas/type/` written by this agent (Garamond to Tiempos), one line each.

1. **Fonts In Use (fontsinuse.com).** Real-world uses indexed by typeface: the Dunlap broadside in Caslon, the Higgs announcement in Comic Sans, Vignelli's "few basic typefaces". The best way to see what a face *signals*.
2. **Letterform Archive, San Francisco (letterformarchive.org; online archive at oa.letterformarchive.org).** Type specimens, foundry catalogues and lettering originals, searchable at high resolution.
3. **Luc Devroye, Type Design Information pages (luc.devroye.org).** An exhaustive, cited record of who drew which revival or clone, invaluable for untangling Garamond, Clarendon and Rockwell lineages.
4. **Google Fonts specimen "About" pages and Google Fonts Knowledge (fonts.google.com).** Designer notes and licences for the open alternatives: Libre Baskerville, Gelasio, Playfair, Fraunces, Cinzel, Besley, Bodoni Moda.
5. **Microsoft Typography font list (learn.microsoft.com/typography/font-list).** Versions, designers and script coverage of Georgia, Times New Roman, Courier New and Comic Sans: essential for system font stacks.
6. **Museum Plantin-Moretus, Antwerp (museumplantinmoretus.be).** Surviving 16th-century punches and matrices, the physical source for Garamond revivals.
7. **Museo Bodoniano, Parma (museobodoniano.com).** Bodoni's punches and *Manuale*, plus pages comparing Bauer, ITC and other revivals.
8. **St Bride Library, London (sbf.org.uk).** Caslon specimens, Fann Street Foundry material and Victorian display type.
9. **MoMA architecture and design collection (moma.org).** Digital typefaces held as design objects, including HTF Didot, with context on why they matter.
10. **Paul Shaw, *Revival Type: Digital Typefaces Inspired by the Past* (2017).** Traces each revival back to its specific source, the antidote to "a Garamond is a Garamond".
11. **Alexander Lawson, *Anatomy of a Typeface* (1990), and alexanderslawsonarchive.com.** Chapter-length histories of Century, Baskerville, Caslon, Bodoni and more.
12. **Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*.** The standard reference for text setting, figures, italics and classification.
13. **Beatrice Warde ("Paul Beaujon"), "The 'Garamond' Types", *The Fleuron* 5 (1926).** The classic piece of type detective work separating Jannon from Garamond.
14. **Edward M. Catich, *The Origin of the Serif* (1968).** How brush and chisel produced Roman capitals: the theory behind Trajan and inscriptional faces.
15. **Hermann Zapf, *Manuale Typographicum* (1954; 1968) and *About Alphabets* (1960).** A calligrapher's reasoning behind Palatino and Optima, in his own layouts.
16. **Matthew Butterick, *Practical Typography* (practicaltypography.com).** Screen-era rules for body text, system fonts and free text faces (Charter).
17. **Bill Hill on making Georgia and Verdana (W3C www-font list, 1998).** A short first-hand account of designing type from the pixel up.
18. **Sofie Beier, *Reading Letters: Designing for Legibility* (2012).** Research-grounded legibility, for judging claims like Century Schoolbook's or Comic Sans's.
19. **Gerard Unger, *While You're Reading* (English ed. 2007).** A type designer explains what readers actually perceive, the basis for sensible UI type decisions.
20. **Indra Kupferschmid, "Type classifications are useful, but the common ones are not" (kupferschrift.de).** A practical, modern way to describe faces by construction and form.
21. **Klim Type Foundry design notes (klim.co.nz).** How a contemporary foundry updates historical function (Tiempos) without imitation.
22. **indestructible type (github.com/indestructible-type).** Open, researched revivals (Bodoni Moda, Besley*, Cooper*) with full source files to study.
23. **Errol Morris, "Hear, All Ye People; Hearken, O Earth" (*NYT* Opinionator, 2012; Pentagram Papers 44).** The well-known experiment on typeface and credibility (Baskerville).
24. **Eye Magazine, "The Nebiolo legacy" (Autumn 2021).** Italian foundry history behind Microgramma and Eurostile.
