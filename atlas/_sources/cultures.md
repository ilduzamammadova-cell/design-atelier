# Sources: world pattern, craft and design traditions (cultures)

The strongest resources for studying the traditions in `atlas/cultures/`. Use them to learn principles and context; follow each card's Respect section before borrowing anything.

1. **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage lists** (ich.unesco.org), authoritative entries for Indonesian batik (2009), Taquile textile art (2008), Romanian-Moldovan ia with altiță (2022), Kente craftsmanship (2024), Día de Muertos (2008); explains meaning, transmission and safeguarding.
2. **V&A, London** (collections.vam.ac.uk), Jameel Gallery of Islamic art, Iznik, Indian textiles (*The Fabric of India*, 2015), Japanese textiles, Maqdala 1868 material.
3. **British Museum** (britishmuseum.org/collection), Asante kente, Kuba textiles, Pacific barkcloth, Greek vases, Iznik (Godman Collection), Chinese ceramics (Percival David Collection).
4. **Metropolitan Museum of Art** (metmuseum.org), Islamic galleries, Ottoman tughra, Andean textiles, Astor Court Ming garden; Heilbrunn Timeline essays for quick context.
5. **Smithsonian NMAI and NMAfA** (americanindian.si.edu, africa.si.edu), Navajo weaving, Guna molas, bògòlan, kente; increasingly community-voiced interpretation.
6. **Te Papa Tongarewa** (collections.tepapa.govt.nz), Māori taonga and the largest public collection of Pacific barkcloth; models of iwi-led interpretation.
7. **Tokyo National Museum** (tnm.jp) and **National Museum of Korea** (museum.go.kr), kamon-bearing lacquer and textiles; bojagi, dancheong and Hangul material.
8. **Museu Nacional do Azulejo, Lisbon** and the **SOS Azulejo** project, the definitive tile collection, and the ethics of tile provenance.
9. **Eric Broug, *Islamic Geometric Patterns* (2008)** and **Keith Critchlow, *Islamic Patterns* (1976)**, learn to construct patterns with compass and straightedge.
10. **Lu & Steinhardt, "Decagonal and Quasi-Crystalline Tilings in Medieval Islamic Architecture", *Science* 315 (2007)**, the girih tile method explained.
11. **Sheila S. Blair, *Islamic Calligraphy* (2006)** and **Huda Smitshuijzen AbiFarès, *Arabic Typography* (2001)**, script history and the practical rules of Arabic type.
12. **Daniel Sheets Dye, *A Grammar of Chinese Lattice* (1937; Dover, *Chinese Lattice Designs*)**, over a thousand lattices classified by rule; a generative design textbook.
13. **Leonard Koren, *Wabi-Sabi for Artists, Designers, Poets & Philosophers* (1994)** and **Jun'ichirō Tanizaki, *In Praise of Shadows* (1933)**, the aesthetics of imperfection and shadow.
14. **John W. Dower, *The Elements of Japanese Design* (1971)**, family crests as a constructed system.
15. **Bill Holm, *Northwest Coast Indian Art: An Analysis of Form* (1965)**, the canonical structural analysis; read it to understand a closed system, not to imitate it.
16. **Anni Albers, *On Weaving* (1965)**, weave structure as the root of image; draws deeply on Andean textiles.
17. **Doran H. Ross, *Wrapped in Pride* (Fowler Museum, 1998)**, kente in Ghana and the diaspora.
18. **Victoria L. Rovine, *Bogolan* (2001)**, bògòlan from ritual cloth to contemporary art and fashion in Mali.
19. **Indigenous Art Code, Arts Law Centre "Artists in the Black", and Terri Janke's *True Tracks* (2021)**, practical protocols for Indigenous cultural and intellectual property in Australia, useful as a model everywhere.
20. **Productivity Commission, *Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Visual Arts and Crafts* (2022)**, evidence on fake Indigenous-style products and proposed remedies.
21. **WIPO Traditional Knowledge resources and WIPO Lex**: texts of Panama's Law 20 (2000), Mexico's 2022 cultural heritage law and other folklore protections.
22. **Waitangi Tribunal, *Ko Aotearoa Tēnei* (Wai 262 report, 2011)** and **Toi Iho**, Māori rights over taonga works and the Māori-made mark.
23. **Scottish Register of Tartans** (tartanregister.gov.uk), threadcounts, registration and usage notes; tartan as pattern-as-data.
24. **Walters Art Museum, Baltimore**: one of the largest collections of Ethiopian manuscripts and crosses outside Ethiopia.
25. **Owen Jones, *The Grammar of Ornament* (1856)**, historic survey of world ornament; valuable for its plates, but read critically, as a product of its colonial era.
