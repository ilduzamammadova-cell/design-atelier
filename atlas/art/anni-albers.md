# Anni Albers

**Essence:** Pattern that grows out of structure, the grid of warp and weft, with one free thread wandering through it.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Structure is the surface | In weaving, pattern comes from how threads interlace, not from applied decoration | Let the data structure make the visual pattern: calendars, tables or heatmaps where the rhythm of filled and empty cells is the only ornament; no decorative backgrounds |
| G2 · Grid plus a wandering thread | Pictorial weavings such as Ancient Writing (1936) set floating supplementary threads against a strict ground | A strict baseline/column grid with exactly one free element per view crossing it (a hand-drawn underline, a meandering connector) |
| G3 · Material chosen for function | Her Bauhaus diploma piece (1929) was a wall covering of cotton and cellophane that absorbed sound and reflected light | Surfaces chosen by job: matte where people read, gloss or sheen only where feedback is needed; texture signals affordance |
| G4 · Dense parallel strands, one knot | Fine parallel lines and knot drawings (1940s) | Data visualisation as bundles of 1px parallel strands (sparklines, stream lines) with one "knot" marking the key event |
| G5 · Triangle grammar | Prints from the 1960s to 70s build fields of repeated triangles | Pattern and icon systems from triangles on a square grid, alternating fill and outline to encode state |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** undyed linen #D9CDB4, natural white #F2EEE3, black #1C1C1C, slate #6F7377, rust #A0522D, ochre #C99A3B, indigo #2F3D5C.
- **Type:** no typography of her own; a grid-born face suits, IBM Plex Mono or Space Grotesk.
- **Form:** warp/weft grids, stepped blocks, triangles, knots, meandering threads.
- **Composition:** allover and modular; shallow space; edges as selvages.

## Attitude

Albers raised weaving to the level of painting while also designing fabrics for industrial production. She wrote clearly about making (On Designing, 1959; On Weaving, 1965) and saw the loom's constraints as a discipline that frees invention. In 1949 she was the first textile artist given a solo exhibition at MoMA.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `josef-albers`, `agnes-martin`, `bauhaus`, the grid as a quiet, humane structure.
- **Productive tension with:** `hilma-af-klint`, esoteric symbols set inside a woven-grid logic.
- **Clashes with:** `pop-art`, glossy mass-media imagery overwhelms tactile, structural quiet.

## Don't copy

Six Prayers (1966 to 67), Ancient Writing (1936), With Verticals (1946), her Bauhaus wall hangings (1926 to 27), the Intersecting prints, and her fabric designs.
