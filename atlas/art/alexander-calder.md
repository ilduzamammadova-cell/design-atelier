# Alexander Calder

**Essence:** Balance made visible — flat colored shapes on wire arms that move with the air, an engineer's precision serving delight.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Counterweight balance | Mobiles balance unequal masses at different distances along a lever | Asymmetric layouts balanced like a mobile: heavy elements near the axis, light ones far out, weight × distance roughly equal on both sides |
| G2 · Motion as composition | The work changes with air currents; no single fixed view | Ambient micro-motion in illustration (±3–6° rotation, 6–12s periods, phases offset) via springs, triggered by scroll or pointer; off under prefers-reduced-motion |
| G3 · Drawing in space | Wire sculptures and the Circus (1926–31) treat wire as a line in three dimensions | Illustrations in one wire-like stroke (2px, round caps) that can rotate in pseudo-3D during transitions |
| G4 · Primaries on paddles | Flat painted sheet-metal shapes in red, black, yellow, blue and white | Organic paddle and leaf shapes, each one flat color, linked by thin lines, as accent graphics or flow-diagram nodes |
| G5 · Mobile vs stabile | Duchamp named the moving works mobiles (1931); Arp named the static ones stabiles | Separate a stable frame (navigation, structure — never animated) from a mobile layer (cards, accents that respond) |
| G6 · Hanging hierarchy | Branching rods form a nested tree in balance | Visualise hierarchies and budget splits as mobiles: a parent bar with children hanging, size proportional to value |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Calder red #D62718, black #111111, yellow #F2B70D, blue #1D4EA3, white #FAFAF7, galvanized grey #9EA3A6.
- **Type:** his own wire-like handwriting on prints and posters → a rounded monoline sans (Quicksand, Nunito) for display.
- **Form:** discs, paddles, petals, wire, rods, bolted sheet steel.
- **Composition:** cantilevered and branching, open, asymmetric, suspended in space.

## Attitude

Trained as a mechanical engineer, Calder turned to abstraction after a 1930 visit to Mondrian's studio and then made Mondrian's color move. His work is serious engineering in the service of play — a good model for interaction designers who want motion to feel natural and generous.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `piet-mondrian`, `surrealism` (Miró's shapes), `henri-matisse` — floating primaries and organic forms.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a static typographic grid with one kinetic, balanced element.
- **Clashes with:** `mark-rothko` — solemn immersion and whimsy undercut each other.

## Don't copy

Calder's Circus (1926–31), Lobster Trap and Fish Tail (1939), Flamingo (Chicago, 1974), the National Gallery of Art East Building mobile, the Braniff "Flying Colors" aircraft (1973), and specific mobile configurations.
