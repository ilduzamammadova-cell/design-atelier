# Surrealism (Magritte, Ernst, Miró, Arp, Dalí)

**Essence:** The ordinary made strange — calm, plainly rendered images that contradict logic, plus soft biomorphic forms drawn from the unconscious.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Calm paradox | Magritte painted familiar objects in impossible relations with flat, matter-of-fact clarity | Campaign and hero images that fuse two familiar things into one impossible image, rendered plainly; one idea per image, never surreal clutter |
| G2 · Word against image | The Treachery of Images (1929) captions a pipe by denying it is one | Captions that playfully question their image, reserved for brand moments (404, empty states, launch pages); never on functional controls |
| G3 · Seamless engraving collage | Max Ernst's collage novels (Une semaine de bonté, 1934) recombined 19th-century engravings so the joins vanish | An illustration style built from public-domain engravings recombined, printed in one ink color, cut edges hidden |
| G4 · Biomorphic forms | Arp and Miró grew soft, amoeba-like shapes by intuition; 1950s furniture and textiles absorbed them | Freeform blob containers, avatars or background shapes with one flat fill and no fixed corner radius; curves drawn, not generated |
| G5 · Deep empty space | De Chirico's and Dalí's long shadows and vast plazas dwarf small figures | Illustrated or 3D heroes with a low horizon, long raking shadows and one tiny focal object, for contemplative scale |
| G6 · Automatism | Drawing without conscious control to release images | Personal marks from a user's own gesture (a scribble that becomes an avatar or signature pattern) |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Magritte sky blue #7FA6CF, cloud white #F4F4F2, bowler black #1A1A1A; Miró primaries red #D7302A, yellow #F2C12E, blue #1E4C9A, green #2E7D4F; desert ochre #C9A063.
- **Type:** Magritte's neat schoolbook script in paintings → a modest serif (Libre Caslon Text) or careful script used sparingly.
- **Form:** clouds, eyes, stars, hybrid objects, blobs, engraving hatching.
- **Composition:** single centered object on a plain ground, or deep perspective with a tiny figure.

## Attitude

André Breton's 1924 manifesto aimed to free imagination from reason and convention. The painters, more than the poets, reached the public; advertising has borrowed their methods ever since, often trivially. Use the method — displacement, disrupted expectation — not the props.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `dada`, `paul-klee`, `push-pin-studios` — wit, metaphor and visual puns.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — objective type and grid around one impossible image makes a powerful poster or landing page.
- **Clashes with:** `minimalism-art` — literalism denies the hidden meaning surrealism depends on.

## Don't copy

Magritte's The Treachery of Images (1929), The Son of Man (1964), Golconda (1953); Dalí's The Persistence of Memory (1931), Lobster Telephone, Mae West Lips Sofa, the Chupa Chups logo (1969); Meret Oppenheim's fur-lined teacup (Object, 1936). No melting clocks, bowler hats or floating apples.
