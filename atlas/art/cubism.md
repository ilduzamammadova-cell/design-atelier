# Cubism (Picasso, Braque, Gris, Léger)

**Essence:** A picture is a built object assembled from fragments and viewpoints, not a window onto a scene.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Many viewpoints at once | Picasso and Braque showed a subject from several angles in one image, as the eye actually scans and assembles | Show one object as interlocking facets in a single composition (front, side, spec, in use) instead of a carousel; dashboard tiles designed as facets of one entity, sharing edges |
| G2 · Shallow layered space | Planes slide over each other in a few millimetres of depth; no vanishing point | Overlapping panels on 2 to 4 fixed z-levels with 8 to 24px offsets and flat fills; no deep perspective, no large blurred shadows; planes may overlap one another's edges |
| G3 · Real material as sign | Papier collé (Braque, 1912) glued wallpaper, newsprint and printed wood grain into drawings; a texture stands for a thing | Insert flat cut rectangles of scanned material (paper, receipt, fabric, photo texture) into a vector layout; each material consistently denotes one category |
| G4 · Letters as fragments | Stencilled words and newspaper mastheads cut mid-word sit inside the composition | Display type cropped by containers to show partial words, used as a background layer behind content; readable text stays whole |
| G5 · Structure first, subject laid on | Juan Gris planned a proportional grid first, then placed the subject onto it | Define the modular grid (rational or golden-section divisions) before content; image crops snap to grid divisions even when that cuts the subject |
| G6 · Léger's machine flats | Léger turned figures and cities into tubes, cones and flat primaries with black contours, a language posters adopted | Icon and illustration sets built only from cylinders, cones and discs, heavy black outline, flat primaries |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** analytical phase: ochre #B08D57, umber #6B5236, grey-green #7D8370, dusty black #2A2722, paper #D9CBB0. Synthetic phase and Léger: red #C8362B, blue #1F4E9A, yellow #E8B923, black.
- **Type:** stencil letters, newspaper grotesques, Didone mastheads → Big Shoulders Stencil, Stardos Stencil, Playfair Display for masthead fragments.
- **Form:** faceted planes, edges that dissolve between planes, oval canvases, faux wood grain, pictographic figures.
- **Composition:** analytical works dense at center and thinning toward the edges; synthetic works flat interlocking shapes on a hidden grid.

## Attitude

Cubists rejected four centuries of single-point illusion. Painting became construction: shapes, textures and values arranged as relationships. Poster artists of the 1920s (Cassandre, McKnight Kauffer) took the flat planes and geometric letterforms straight into advertising. For a designer it licenses showing structure and process openly.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-deco` (poster artists absorbed cubist planes), `futurism` (faceted motion), `david-hockney` (his photo joiners extend multi-view seeing).
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, a rational grid holds the fragments so faceted compositions stay legible.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, whiplash curves and planar facets cancel each other out.

## Don't copy

Picasso's Les Demoiselles d'Avignon (1907) and Guitar constructions (1912 to 14), Braque's Fruit Dish and Glass (1912), Léger's The City (1919), Picasso's signature and late line portraits.

## Study

- Juan Gris, The Sunblind (1914, Tate); Fernand Léger, The City (1919, Philadelphia Museum of Art)
