# Myriad (1992)

**Essence:** A digital-native humanist sans, smooth, open and adjustable, that became the quiet voice of Apple's product era.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Designed as a variable system | Adobe released Myriad in 1992 as a Multiple Master font: users could interpolate weight and width themselves, a direct ancestor of today's variable fonts. | Ship one variable font file and expose `wght` and `wdth` as tokens; derive in-between weights for specific jobs (e.g. 450 for dark-mode body, 550 for small buttons) instead of loading four static files. |
| G2 · Humanist warmth without quirks | Slimbach and Twombly kept humanist structure (open apertures, slight modulation, a true italic) but smoothed away idiosyncratic details, so it fits any product. | For a friendly but neutral product voice choose a humanist sans with no signature quirks; then put personality in one controlled place (an illustration style, a colour) rather than in the type. |
| G3 · Corporate typeface as product language | Apple used Myriad in marketing and packaging from 2002 (replacing Apple Garamond) until San Francisco took over around 2015; type, product photography and white space worked as one language. | Align marketing and product type: the face on the box, the website and the device UI should be one family (or two with fixed roles). Audit three touchpoints, packaging, web, app, for type consistency each release. |
| G4 · Width flexibility for packaging | Condensed and semi-extended widths fit long names on small labels without changing voice. | Use a semi-condensed width for product names and SKUs in cards and tables; keep body at normal width; never fake condensing with `transform: scaleX`. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** humanist sans.
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Robert Slimbach and Carol Twombly (with Fred Brady and Christopher Slye), Adobe, 1992, as a Multiple Master font (credits for the wider team vary by source).
- **Digital versions & licences:** Myriad Pro (OpenType, c. 2000; Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, multiple widths); Myriad Set/Apple variants (custom, Apple). Commercial; included with some Adobe software and available via Adobe Fonts.
- **Proportions:** medium x-height, open apertures, slight stroke modulation, flared-feeling terminals, true italic with cursive details, wide range of widths.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** PT Sans (ParaType, 2009, OFL, humanist, with caption and narrow styles; Latin + Cyrillic); Source Sans 3 (Adobe's own open humanist-gothic, OFL, variable).

## Attitude

Myriad shows Adobe's belief that type for the digital age should be both humane and engineered, designed for interpolation and screen rendering, without losing the reading comfort of the humanist tradition.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** Apple marketing 2002 to 2015, Adobe software documents and PDFs, packaging; reads "clean consumer tech of the 2000s". Saturation: medium-low today, feels slightly dated but calm.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 8/10, open apertures, gentle modulation and a true cursive italic; its smoothness keeps it polite rather than personal.
- **UI fitness:** body text, UI labels and moderate display; 12px labels hold up. Myriad Pro covers Latin, Greek, Cyrillic; check web-licence scope.
- **Pairs with:** Minion or Garamond for long documents (Adobe-era humanist pairing); Futura for a crisper display voice; Source Code Pro for code.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `jonathan-ive`, `mac-os-x-aqua`, the clean, white-space Apple era.
- **Productive tension with:** `editorial-web`, a neutral humanist body under expressive serif headlines.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalist-web`, politeness and polish undermine raw honesty.

## Don't copy

Myriad outlines; Apple's custom Myriad variants and packaging layouts.
