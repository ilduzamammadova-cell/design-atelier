# Clarendon

**Essence:** Strength with a handshake. Heavy slab serifs curve into the stems through soft brackets, so a bold, authoritative letter still feels approachable.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Bracketed slab: warmth inside strength | Earlier "Egyptian" slabs were blunt and mechanical. Clarendon's slabs flow into the stems through curved brackets, with moderate contrast, so weight reads as reliability, not aggression. | Brand and headline face for products that need authority without coldness (outdoor, utilities, food, civic): weight 600–800 at ≥ 24px. Long text goes to a gentler text serif or the family's regular weight. |
| G2 · Big x-height, short extenders | A tall lowercase and compact ascenders and descenders pack strong words into little vertical space and read at distance. That made it a poster and signage face. | Signage, wayfinding and mobile headings: leading 1.05–1.15 at display sizes, sentence case, ≥ 20px on signs. Cards and list titles stay compact without losing legibility. |
| G3 · Friendly terminals | Ball terminals on *a* and *c* and a curled leg on *R* add charm to a sturdy body. | Translate into components: 2–4px corner radius (never 0, never pill), thick 2px icon strokes with rounded caps. The details soften; the mass stays solid. |
| G4 · A genre, specified by traits | Besley's design protection lasted three years. Copies followed at once, and "Clarendon" became a whole category of type. | Define the brand slab by traits rather than by font name (bracketed slab, large x-height, ball terminals, moderate contrast), so licensed and open implementations (Sentinel, Besley*, Clarendon) can be swapped per platform. |
| G5 · One poster moment | In wood type and "French Clarendon" variants it shouted from circus bills and wanted posters. | Allow one poster-scale moment per flow (empty state, onboarding, campaign) with heavy Clarendon at 12–20vw. Everywhere else it stays at normal sizes, so the Old-West cliché never takes over. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** Clarendon / bracketed slab serif (the "Ionic" branch of 19th-century Egyptians).
- **Origin:** Robert Besley, a partner in Thorowgood & Besley (the Fann Street Foundry), London, 1845. Punchcutter Benjamin Fox may have shaped the design. It was registered under the Ornamental Designs Act 1842 and is often called the first registered typeface.
- **Main revivals:** Haas/Stempel Clarendon (Hermann Eidenbenz, 1953), ATF Craw Clarendon (1955), Monotype New Clarendon (1960), Superclarendon (2007), Sentinel (Hoefler & Frere-Jones, 2009). All commercial.
- **Anatomy:** large x-height, short extenders, vertical stress, moderate contrast, bracketed slab serifs, ball terminals, curled *R* leg.
- **Free / open alternatives:** Besley* (Owen Earl / indestructible type, OFL; raised x-height, new italic, variable weight, Cyrillic since v3) and Montagu Slab (OFL, Google Fonts).

## Attitude

Clarendon was a commercial display invention that became the furniture of public life on posters, signs and nameplates. It believes strong communication should still be friendly, and that weight is a form of clarity, not of power.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** wanted posters and the American West, circus bills, US National Park Service signs (Craw Clarendon, since replaced), the *Wheel of Fortune* wheel; Sentinel on Barack Obama's 2012 campaign site. Saturation is moderate: slabs had a heritage-craft wave in the 2010s, and Clarendon itself now feels under-used.
- **Robotic ↔ humane: 7/10.** Curved brackets, ball terminals, vertical but gentle stress and a tall friendly lowercase. The firm, regular construction keeps it from feeling handmade.
- **UI fitness:** headlines and short body (≤ 3 lines) at ≥ 16px; labels workable in medium weight ≥ 14px thanks to the x-height; long body only in the regular weight. Low contrast renders robustly on 1× screens. Coverage: Besley* has Cyrillic; check Greek.
- **Pairs with:** an American gothic sans for UI (Franklin Gothic, or open Libre Franklin). A Century-style text serif for long reading (TeX Gyre Schola). Courier Prime for receipts and tickets.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `victorian-wood-type`: the same 19th-century public voice at poster scale.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: Eidenbenz's 1953 Haas Clarendon shows a Victorian slab rebuilt with modernist discipline, warmth on a grid.
- **Clashes with:** `didot`: hairline formality next to sturdy slabs looks like two brands arguing.

## Don't copy

Sentinel's and Haas Clarendon's outlines without licence; NPS sign layouts and the *Wheel of Fortune* lettering; Western "wanted" poster pastiche as a brand.
