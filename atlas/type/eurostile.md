# Eurostile

**Essence:** Letters shaped like television screens. Rounded rectangles, wide stances and flat terminals turned the 1960s idea of the future into type, and they now read as "yesterday's tomorrow".

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Superellipse counters | Rounds become squarish superellipses, like the cathode-ray screens of the 1950s–60s. | Use one superellipse for type and containers: buttons, cards, app icons and video frames with continuous ("squircle") corners at roughly 25–30% of their height, so shapes and letters share one geometry. |
| G2 · Extended width means speed | Microgramma began as wide titling caps. The horizontal stance reads as fast, low and aerodynamic. | Extended caps only for short readouts (≤ 3 words): mode names, speed or power values, HUD labels, tracked +5–10%. Body and descriptions never use the extended width. |
| G3 · Width as a family axis | Eurostile came in extended, normal and condensed versions; Eurostile Next has 5 weights × 3 widths. | Width tokens on a variable or multi-width family: condensed for dense tables and tabs, normal for labels, extended for hero and brand moments. The type stays in one family across every density. |
| G4 · Caps first, lowercase added later | Microgramma (1952) had capitals only; Eurostile (1962) added lowercase to the same skeleton. | Let capitals carry the brand voice (HUD labels, headings) and set running text in a humanist sans, since squared-off lowercase is tiring to read. |
| G5 · Retro-futurism has an expiry date | HAL 9000's displays in *2001: A Space Odyssey*, *Star Trek* titles and *Back to the Future* fixed it as a cinematic future. | Use it deliberately for retro-future or motorsport heritage. For a genuinely contemporary future, keep the superellipse geometry (G1) but drop the face. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** geometric (square) sans-serif.
- **Origin:** Microgramma (Alessandro Butti with Aldo Novarese, Nebiolo, Turin, 1952; capitals only). Eurostile (Novarese, Nebiolo, 1962) added lowercase, a bold condensed and the narrow Eurostile Compact.
- **Main digital versions:** Eurostile LT (Linotype). Eurostile Next (Akira Kobayashi, Linotype, 2008; redrawn from metal specimens; 15 fonts, 5 weights × 3 widths, no italics). URW versions. All commercial. Phototype-era copies circulated as Aldostyle, Eurogothic, Eurostyle and others.
- **Anatomy:** squared rounds, large x-height, low contrast, horizontal terminals, tight apertures on *c, e, s*, wide extended styles.
- **Free / open alternatives:** Michroma (Vernon Adams, OFL; a reworking of the rounded-square extended genre, one weight).

## Attitude

Novarese gave postwar Italy a letter for television, cars and electronics: the confident future of industrial design. The face believes technology is sleek and controllable. Its charm today is that this optimism has become a period style.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** *2001: A Space Odyssey*, *Star Trek*, *Doctor Who*, *Back to the Future*, *Total Recall*; logos for Toshiba, Casio and GEICO. Saturation is high in gaming, automotive and "tech HUD" clichés.
- **Robotic ↔ humane: 2/10.** Squared rounds, flat horizontal terminals, closed apertures and uniform strokes. Nothing in it recalls a hand.
- **UI fitness:** display and short HUD labels in caps (≥ 12px, tracked). Not body text: closed apertures and identical rectangles make words blur. Renders crisply on screens. Coverage: check Cyrillic and Greek per version; Michroma covers extended Latin.
- **Pairs with:** a humanist sans for reading and controls (Frutiger, or open Source Sans 3) for warmth and legibility. A mono for numeric readouts (IBM Plex Mono or JetBrains Mono). A wide grotesk (open Archivo, expanded widths) when the brand needs extended text beyond caps.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `game-ui`: HUDs, telemetry and mode screens already speak its language.
- **Productive tension with:** `scandinavian-modern`: squircle tech type in wood, felt and soft colour gives warm electronics.
- **Clashes with:** `william-morris`: tech rectangles beside dense botanical pattern share nothing but confusion.

## Don't copy

HAL 9000 screen graphics and *Star Trek* title cards; Toshiba, Casio and GEICO wordmarks; Eurostile Next outlines without licence.
