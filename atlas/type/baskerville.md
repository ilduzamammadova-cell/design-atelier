# Baskerville

**Essence:** The Enlightenment's clear voice. Upright stress, round open bowls and crisp serifs sit halfway between the scribe's pen and the engraver's burin.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Transitional upright axis | Stress turns nearly vertical and contrast increases, but serifs stay bracketed. The result is exact without being icy, a middle path between old-style and Didone. | The serif for products that must feel precise but not cold (finance statements, legal summaries, research). Body 17 to 18px / 1.55; headings in the same family at semibold, not a separate display face. |
| G2 · Wide, round letters | Broad *O*, open bowls and generous counters give a calm, stately line, at the cost of horizontal space. | Shorter measure (55 to 65ch). On mobile, check long words in narrow columns: below a 320px content width, fall back to a more compact text face. |
| G3 · Contrast is a surface property | Baskerville's sharpness relied on smooth hot-pressed paper and dense ink. On screens the pixel density is the paper. | Two cuts by device: Libre Baskerville (lower contrast, taller x-height) on 1× screens and for body ≤ 16px; a high-fidelity revival on 2×+ and ≥ 24px. Text colour at least #1C1C1C on light grounds, so hairlines don't grey out. |
| G4 · The credible face | In Errol Morris's 2012 online experiment with about 45,000 *New York Times* readers, a claim set in Baskerville drew slightly more agreement than the same claim in five other faces. | Use it for statements that must be believed (pricing terms, policy, methodology notes), knowing the effect is small. Type supports evidence; it never replaces it. |
| G5 · One signature glyph | Baskerville's characterful italic and some distinctive capitals and ligatures give a recognisable accent inside a sober text. | Pick one glyph from the licensed/open family (ampersand, *Q*, italic *&*) as a recurring brand device at large size; everything else stays neutral. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** Transitional serif.
- **Origin:** John Baskerville, Birmingham; punches cut by John Handy; first used in his Virgil of 1757. Isaac Moore cut a simplified imitation for Joseph Fry around 1768.
- **Main digital versions:** Monotype Baskerville (1923, under Stanley Morison; commercial). ITC New Baskerville (1982, from Linotype's New Baskerville; commercial). Baskerville ships with macOS. Mrs Eaves (Zuzana Licko, Emigre, 1996) is a low-contrast, low-x-height, loosely spaced reinterpretation for display; Mrs Eaves XL handles text.
- **Anatomy:** moderate x-height, near-vertical stress, sharp bracketed serifs, round wide bowls, higher contrast than old-styles; a delicate italic.
- **Free / open alternatives:** Libre Baskerville (Pablo Impallari, OFL), based on ATF Baskerville (1941) with a taller x-height, wider counters and less contrast for 16px web text. Baskervville (ANRT students, Nancy, from 2017, OFL) revives Claude Jacob's Strasbourg version of about 1815, which leans towards Didot in its italic.

## Attitude

Baskerville believed refinement of material and craft was itself respect for the reader. The type is part of a whole system of ink, paper, press and margin. Borrow the system thinking, not only the letters.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** British and American academic and literary publishing, institutional trust. The Morris experiment made it a minor folk symbol of credibility. Saturation is moderate: it reads as serious without being stuffy.
- **Robotic ↔ humane: 6/10.** The rational vertical axis and regular construction are cooler than Caslon. Open round bowls and a calligraphic italic keep it warm.
- **UI fitness:** body text yes (≥ 17px; Libre Baskerville from 15 to 16px); display good from 24 to 64px; labels marginal (semibold ≥ 14px only). Many free Baskervilles are Latin-only, so confirm Cyrillic and Greek before committing.
- **Pairs with:** Gill Sans or another British humanist sans for headings and UI (open: Source Sans 3), a historical British pairing. Futura (or open Jost) for mid-century editorial contrast. IBM Plex Mono for figures and code.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `massimo-vignelli`: he listed Baskerville among his "extra" faces, with economy of type as a discipline.
- **Productive tension with:** `wolfgang-weingart`: a precise transitional serif inside layered, experimental structure keeps the chaos legible and credible.
- **Clashes with:** `y2k-chrome-and-acid`: Enlightenment calm under chrome and acid gradients reads as confused.

## Don't copy

Mrs Eaves' drawings (an Emigre licence is required); Monotype Baskerville outlines; Baskerville's 1757 Virgil title page reproduced as a layout.
