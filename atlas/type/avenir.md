# Avenir (1988)

**Essence:** Futura's geometric ideal, humanised — circles made slightly oval, verticals thickened, ascenders shortened, so a geometric sans finally reads comfortably in text.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Geometry corrected for reading | Frutiger kept the geometric skeleton but gave verticals more weight than horizontals and drew the o as a slight oval, not a circle; text no longer flickers. | When you want a geometric brand feel in body copy, pick a geometric with optical corrections (stroke contrast ~10%, double-storey a); body 16/26, never pure-circle geometrics below 16px. |
| G2 · Shorter ascenders, larger x-height | Compared with Futura, ascenders are cut down and the x-height raised, so lines can be set closer and small sizes stay legible. | Tighter leading is allowed: body line-height 1.45–1.5 instead of 1.6; labels at 13px remain readable. Pair a large-x-height geometric with 4px-based spacing for compact mobile UI. |
| G3 · Soft authority | It reads clean and modern but not cold — the weight it carries is gentle, which is why civic and service brands adopt it. | For service, health and public products, use the medium (500) weight for headlines rather than bold (700): authority through clarity, not volume. Buttons in 500–600, sentence case. |
| G4 · Systematised weights | Frutiger originally numbered the weights (35 to 95, like Univers); Avenir Next (rework with Akira Kobayashi, mid-2000s) switched to named weights and added condensed widths, small caps and old-style figures. | Use the extended family's typographic features in UI: `font-variant-caps: all-small-caps` for acronyms in running text, `onum` in long reading, `lnum tnum` in tables. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** geometric sans with humanist corrections.
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Adrian Frutiger, Linotype, designed 1987, released 1988 (the name is French for "future", nodding to Futura).
- **Digital versions & licences:** Avenir (Linotype); Avenir Next (Frutiger with Akira Kobayashi, reworked 2004–07; condensed widths, small caps, Cyrillic and Greek, plus a later "World" extension); bundled with macOS and iOS since 2012. Commercial (Monotype).
- **Proportions:** medium-large x-height, open round counters, near-monoline with heavier verticals, double-storey a, shortened ascenders; italics are obliques with mild correction.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** Nunito Sans (Jacques Le Bailly, OFL — non-rounded companion to Nunito; variable with width and optical-size axes; widely suggested as an Avenir stand-in); Figtree (Erik D. Kennedy, OFL — "clean yet friendly" geometric for apps).

## Attitude

Frutiger thought Futura's idealism had cost it readability. Avenir is a correction made with respect — keep the clarity of construction, but let human perception have the final word.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** Amsterdam's city identity, Apple's bundled fonts, wellness, travel and healthcare brands; reads "friendly-modern, trustworthy, slightly corporate". Saturation: medium-high in consumer brands.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 6/10 — geometric skeleton, but thicker verticals, oval o and double-storey a remove the drafting-table chill. Oblique italics hold it back from higher.
- **UI fitness:** body text, UI labels and display — one of the few geometric sans that works at 13–16px. Avenir Next covers Latin, Greek, Cyrillic; World edition extends further. Light weights (300 and below) thin out on 1× screens.
- **Pairs with:** Georgia or Source Serif 4 for editorial warmth; Source Sans 3 when text columns must be narrower; SF Mono or IBM Plex Mono for tabular data.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `scandinavian-modern`, `inclusive-design` — friendly clarity for everyday services.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — a strict grid with a softer geometric voice yields approachable rigour.
- **Clashes with:** `punk-graphics` — its politeness drains any raw energy.

## Don't copy

Avenir / Avenir Next outlines; Amsterdam's identity artwork.
