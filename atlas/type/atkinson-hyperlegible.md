# Atkinson Hyperlegible (2019)

**Essence:** A typeface designed with and for low-vision readers, every easily confused character pushed apart, so recognition beats elegance and the quirks become warmth.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Differentiate the confusables | Characters that blur into each other (I/l/1, O/0, B/8, p/q, E/F) are given distinct shapes, a serifed I, a tailed l, a slashed or dotted zero, asymmetric bowls. | For inputs, codes, prices, medication names and verification screens, use a face where every confusable pair differs. Add a test page to the design system: `Il1 O0 B8 S5 Z2 rn m` at 14px and 2× blur. |
| G2 · Co-design with the affected readers | Applied Design Works worked with a Braille Institute low-vision specialist and a panel of people with low vision during a rebrand; the typeface was named after founder J. Robert Atkinson. | Recruit 5 to 8 users with the relevant need (low vision, dyslexia, older adults) to test type choices before launch; record which glyphs caused errors and fix by alternates or face changes. |
| G3 · Open counters, angled spurs, longer tails | Large inner spaces and exaggerated details (spurs, tails) increase the difference between shapes at the cost of conventional smoothness. | Accept visible quirks in body text when the audience needs legibility; balance by keeping layout calm: generous line-height (1.6), left-aligned text, 60 to 70ch measure, no justified text. |
| G4 · Free for reach | Released free (SIL OFL on Google Fonts); Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (2025) expanded to 7 weights, a variable font and 150+ languages, with a Mono companion. | Specify accessibility-first fonts by default in public-sector and health templates; include the Mono version for data tables and codes so numbers share the same disambiguation. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** grotesque core with humanist and differentiated details (accessibility sans).
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Braille Institute of America with Applied Design Works (Elliott Scott, Megan Eiswerth, Linus Boman, Theodore Petrosky), released 2019. Next and Mono announced February 2025.
- **Digital versions & licences:** Atkinson Hyperlegible (2 weights + italics, 27 languages); Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (ExtraLight-ExtraBold, variable, 150+ languages); Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono. OFL via Google Fonts; Braille Institute also distributes under its own free EULA.
- **Proportions:** medium-large x-height, very open counters, low-to-moderate contrast, exaggerated spurs and tails, circular dots (a nod to Braille).
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** Luciole (CTRDV with typographies.fr, 2019, CC BY 4.0, designed for visually impaired readers); Source Sans 3 with its tailed l for a calmer, partial version of the gene.

## Attitude

Atkinson Hyperlegible starts from the reader who struggles most and treats that as the design brief, not an edge case. Its premise is that clarity for low-vision readers improves reading for everyone.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** accessibility-minded public services, healthcare, nonprofits, education; award recognition (Fast Company Innovation by Design, 2019). Reads "inclusive, candid". Saturation: rising, still distinctive.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 6/10, the distinct, slightly irregular forms feel personal and frank; the grotesque core and functional exaggerations keep it from being lyrical.
- **UI fitness:** body text, labels, forms, data (with Mono); serviceable display in bold. Minimum 14px for body on mobile still recommended. Original covers 27 Latin languages; use Next for broader Latin coverage, check Cyrillic/Greek needs.
- **Pairs with:** Georgia or another large-x-height serif for long reading (keeps the accessibility logic); Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for numbers and codes; a single bold display face for brand moments.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `inclusive-design`, `universal-design`, `don-norman`, people-first legibility.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, rigorous grids carry its quirky letters calmly.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type`, illegibility as aesthetic is the opposite brief.

## Don't copy

Not an artifact problem (free licence), but don't claim accessibility by font alone; contrast, size, spacing and testing still decide.
