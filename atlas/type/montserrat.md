# Montserrat (2011)

**Essence:** A neighbourhood's painted signs and shop canopies, preserved as a free typeface, wide, round and sociable, and now so common it has become a template default.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Preserving local lettering | Ulanovsky documented 1920s to 50s signs, posters and windows in Buenos Aires's Monserrat district because urban renewal was erasing them, and released the result openly. | Before choosing a brand face, collect lettering from the place or trade your product serves; if a free face exists that preserves it, prefer it, and credit the source in the brand guide. |
| G2 · Wide caps for street presence | Broad, geometric capitals made for reading on façades and awnings give short headlines strong presence. | All-caps H1/H2 in a wide geometric at 600 to 700 weight, +2 to 4% tracking, max 4 words; body and UI in a narrower face to save width. |
| G3 · Open licence as distribution | Kickstarter-funded and released under the SIL OFL via Google Fonts, then expanded (Latin redraw by Jacques Le Bailly and Cyrillic in 2017, plus Alternates and Subrayada). | Prefer OFL faces when a product must ship in apps, PDFs, emails and partner sites without licence audits; record the version and source repository in the design system. |
| G4 · Weight redistribution for text | The 2017 revision made the Regular lighter so it works better in longer text. | Choose weights by role: Medium (500) for headings, Regular (400) only for short paragraphs; if body text looks heavy, step down to 300 at 18px+ rather than shrinking. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** geometric sans (vernacular-based).
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Julieta Ulanovsky, begun 2010 at FADU/University of Buenos Aires, released 2011 on Google Fonts; 2017 revision with Jacques Le Bailly (Latin) and Sol Matas & Juan Pablo del Peral (Cyrillic).
- **Digital versions & licences:** Montserrat (variable), Montserrat Alternates, Montserrat Subrayada; SIL Open Font License.
- **Proportions:** wide, large x-height, circular rounds, low contrast, double-storey a, flat terminals; true italics with modest cursive adjustments.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** Red Hat Display (OFL, American-signage geometric, tighter); Figtree (OFL, narrower, friendlier for body text). Commercial kin: Gotham.

## Attitude

Montserrat's founding idea is conservation, keeping a city's common, unsigned lettering alive by making it free. Its later ubiquity is the opposite outcome: a local voice turned into a global default.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** Canva and website-builder templates, event posters, small-business sites; reads "template, friendly, 2015 to 2020". Saturation: very high, among the most used Google Fonts families.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 5/10, wide circular geometry and even strokes are mechanical, but the street-sign origin, generous widths and true italics give it an outgoing, sociable feel.
- **UI fitness:** display and short labels; body text tiring at length (wide). Body ≥ 16px, labels ≥ 13px. Covers Latin (incl. Vietnamese) and Cyrillic.
- **Pairs with:** Source Sans 3 or Fira Sans for UI text; Bodoni or a Didone for editorial headlines if you invert roles; JetBrains Mono for code. Never the Montserrat + Open Sans or Lato template duo.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `gotham`, `web-1-vernacular`, found, unauthored lettering as voice.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, wide vernacular caps inside a strict modular grid.
- **Clashes with:** `centered-hero-three-cards-template`, together they are the generic landing page.

## Don't copy

Not an artifact problem (OFL), the risk is sameness. Don't present Montserrat as an exclusive brand asset; customise or choose differently if distinctiveness matters.
