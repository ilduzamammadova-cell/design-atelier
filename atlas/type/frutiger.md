# Frutiger (1976)

**Essence:** A sign face built for people walking fast past backlit panels — open shapes that stay identifiable at a glance, at an angle and out of focus.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Open apertures for reading at speed | Drawn for the airport at Roissy (completed early 1970s, installed 1975): the openings of c, e, a, s are wide, so letters don't close up under blur, glare or distance. Frutiger checked forms by viewing them blurred. | Choose UI text faces with open apertures; test labels at 12px and through a 2px Gaussian blur — c/e/o and a/s must remain distinct. Use this test as a gate in the type-selection checklist. |
| G2 · Humanist skeleton, sign-ready weight | Less geometric than Univers: slight stroke modulation and pen-like terminals give letters individual silhouettes; the airport weights were bolder for light boxes. | Wayfinding and dark-mode UI need heavier text than print: use 500 for labels on dark backgrounds, 400 on light; minimum 14px for nav labels, 24px for kiosks, 48px+ for wall signage viewed at 3–5 m. |
| G3 · Distance-legible proportions | Tall ascenders and a moderate x-height keep word shapes distinct (the "bouma" of a word) at signage distance. | For signs and map labels prefer sentence case over all caps (word shape aids recognition); all-caps only for ≤ 2-word codes with +8% tracking. |
| G4 · National reuse | Switzerland adopted a variant (ASTRA-Frutiger) for road signs in 2003; the UK NHS uses Frutiger in its identity. Legibility, once proven, becomes public infrastructure. | For public-service products choose a face with documented legibility use (road, health, transit) and say so in the design rationale; set a minimum contrast of 7:1 for critical instructions. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** humanist sans.
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Adrian Frutiger; developed from the Roissy (Charles de Gaulle Airport) signage alphabet; released 1976 by D. Stempel AG with Linotype (added to Linotype's library 1977).
- **Digital versions & licences:** Frutiger (Linotype); Frutiger Next (1999–2000, larger x-height and a calligraphic true italic); Neue Frutiger (Akira Kobayashi with Frutiger, 2009 — returns to the original design, more weights, Paneuropean and Cyrillic versions; Frutiger Arabic). ASTRA-Frutiger (Swiss roads). Commercial (Monotype).
- **Proportions:** medium x-height, wide open apertures, slight stroke modulation, terminals cut perpendicular to the stroke; original italic is a sloped roman, Frutiger Next has a true italic.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** Source Sans 3 (Adobe, OFL — open apertures, humanist-gothic, true italic); Fira Sans (Mozilla/Carrois, OFL — humanist, screen-tuned, wide language coverage via FiraGO).

## Attitude

Frutiger thought type should serve the reader's eye before the designer's taste — his ideal letter is noticed only when it is missing. Frutiger the typeface is that conviction in its most practical form.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** airports, hospitals, the NHS, Swiss roads, banks; also the source of the "Frutiger Aero" nostalgia label for 2000s glossy UI. Reads "public service, reassuring". Saturation: medium; feels institutional rather than trendy.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 7/10 — open apertures, gentle modulation and pen-logic terminals feel human and calm; the original sloped-roman italic and institutional evenness keep it from being warmer.
- **UI fitness:** body text, UI labels and signage alike; one of the safest faces for 12–14px. Neue Frutiger covers Latin, Greek, Cyrillic; Arabic as a companion. Check licence scope for apps/web.
- **Pairs with:** Source Serif 4 (open) or Frutiger's own Méridien logic for reports; Rockwell or Roboto Slab for technical headings; OCR-B or IBM Plex Mono for codes and numbers on tickets.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `otl-aicher`, `universal-design`, `inclusive-design` — legibility as a public right.
- **Productive tension with:** `bauhaus` — strict primary geometry and colour in the layout, humane letters for reading.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type` — the face exists to remove the ambiguity that deconstructivism manufactures.

## Don't copy

Frutiger outlines (all versions), ASTRA-Frutiger, NHS identity artwork; Charles de Gaulle signage designs.
