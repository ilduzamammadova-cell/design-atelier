# Rockwell

**Essence:** A slab serif built with compass and ruler. The strokes are almost even, the *O* is nearly a circle and the slabs are square, so it reads as engineered, sturdy and slightly retro.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Monoline geometric slab | The strokes keep nearly one width, rounds approach perfect circles, and slab serifs meet stems without brackets. It is the slab version of 1930s geometric modernism. | Headline and brand face ≥ 20px, weights 600 to 800. Match icons to it: 2px uniform strokes, square line caps, 90° joins. Type and pictograms come from one geometry. |
| G2 · One odd detail | A serif sits on the apex of the capital *A*, an unexpected flag in an otherwise rational system. | Allow exactly one quirky detail in a geometric brand system: one glyph feature echoed in the logo or one icon terminal. Everything else stays strictly regular. |
| G3 · Even texture, flat paragraphs | Monoline weight gives an even, strong colour in a line, but little stroke variation to guide the eye through long text. | Rockwell runs at most 3 lines: headlines, labels, prices, scoreboards. Paragraphs switch to a text serif or humanist sans. |
| G4 · Numbers as hardware | Square slabs and even strokes make figures that look stamped or stencilled: robust at a glance. | Timers, scores, prices and counters in Rockwell bold at 32 to 96px with tabular spacing (or fixed-width boxes if the font lacks `tnum`). Units and labels sit beside them in a lighter sans. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** geometric slab serif (Egyptian).
- **Origin:** Litho Antique (William Schraubstadter, Inland Type Foundry, 1910) was reissued by ATF as Rockwell Antique (Morris Fuller Benton, 1931). Monotype's Rockwell (1934) was produced under Frank Hinman Pierpont, partly in response to Stempel's Memphis.
- **Main digital versions:** Monotype Rockwell (commercial; via Monotype and Adobe Fonts; preinstalled on macOS). Bitstream's Geometric Slabserif 712 is a lookalike.
- **Anatomy:** monoline strokes, large x-height, unbracketed square slabs, serif at the apex of *A*, two-storey *a*, near-circular *O*, low contrast.
- **Free / open alternatives:** Rokkitt (begun by Vernon Adams, completed by Kalapi Gajjar; OFL; nine weights, variable, italics) and Arvo (Anton Koovit, OFL; geometric slab with slight contrast, four styles).

## Attitude

Rockwell belongs to the machine-age belief that letters could be constructed like parts, from circles, lines and squares. It promises solidity and plain dealing. In a product it says "built to last", sometimes with a wink at mid-century industry.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** Arby's advertising, *Challengers* (2024) film titles, Expo 86 signage, the Docklands Light Railway in the late 1980s, early-1990s *Guinness World Records*. Saturation is moderate: easy to reach in system font menus, so amateur "rugged" uses are common.
- **Robotic ↔ humane: 4/10.** Compass-drawn rounds, monoline strokes and unbracketed slabs are mechanical. The two-storey *a* and chunky slabs add a friendly, workshop warmth.
- **UI fitness:** display, short labels (≥ 14px in medium) and numerals. Not body text. Renders robustly on 1× thanks to low contrast. Coverage: check Cyrillic and Greek in the licensed version; Rokkitt covers extended Latin.
- **Pairs with:** a geometric sans for UI (Futura, or open Jost), keeping the 1930s geometry consistent. Century Schoolbook or TeX Gyre Schola for longer reading, giving the slab a sturdy text partner. A humanist sans (Source Sans 3) when the interface needs softening.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `streamline-moderne`: 1930s machine optimism; rounded speed lines with geometric slabs.
- **Productive tension with:** `scandinavian-modern`: workshop slab type in soft wood and textile tones gives warm utility.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`: compass geometry against whiplash curves, with no shared logic.

## Don't copy

Monotype outlines without licence; the Arby's and *Challengers* typographic identities.
