# Cooper Black

**Essence:** A heavyweight that hugs. Old-style letters are inflated into soft, rounded masses that nestle together, the most cheerful bold in type history.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Soft serif | The serifs and terminals are rounded and blobby. Old-style forms are puffed up rather than sharpened, so the heaviest weight still feels gentle. | Friendly headline face at weight 800 to 900, 28 to 72px. Give the UI the same softness: corner radii 12 to 16px, round avatars, warm palette. Never use it for errors, security or money confirmations. |
| G2 · Letters that nest | Tight fit lets shapes lock together, as on the *Pet Sounds* cover (1966), where the words read as a single block. | In logotypes and hero headlines: tracking −2% to −4%, leading 0.95 to 1.0, and kern so bowls tuck under overhangs. Check ascender-descender collisions at every breakpoint. |
| G3 · Even, heavy colour for far viewing | Low contrast and a big x-height read at distance and survive rough printing. The foundry pitched it "for far-sighted printers with near-sighted customers". | Stickers, badges, app-store art, packaging and signage at small physical sizes: Cooper-style display holds at 10 to 12px bold where hairline faces fail. |
| G4 · A time machine with a cycle | It was big in 1920s advertising, faded in the 1960s, returned with 1966 to 70s rock and pop, came back as 1990s irony and again in 2010s retro. | Treat it as a time-signal token. To cite the 1970s, pair it with an era palette (orange #E3732B, mustard #E8B23A, brown #6B4226). To avoid pastiche, set it in a cold modern palette with strict grids. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** soft-serif display (heavy old-style).
- **Origin:** Oswald Bruce Cooper, released by Barnhart Brothers & Spindler, Chicago, in 1922 as the black weight of his Cooper Old Style; American Type Founders took over the rights.
- **Main digital versions:** URW, Bitstream and Adobe/Linotype digitizations (commercial); a URW version ships with many Microsoft products. Cooper Hilite added white highlights.
- **Anatomy:** large x-height, small counters in the black weight, rounded serifs, low contrast, slightly diagonal old-style stress, short extenders, chunky italic.
- **Free / open alternatives:** Cooper* (Owen Earl / indestructible type, OFL): a researched revival of the whole Cooper series, light to black, with italics. Fraunces (Undercase Type, OFL): variable "Softness" and "Wonk" axes, drawing on Windsor, Souvenir and the Cooper series.

## Attitude

Cooper was a lettering man who drew type to sell things warmly to ordinary people. Purists sneered; printers and customers loved it. Its lesson is that friendliness can be built into weight itself.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** *Pet Sounds* (The Beach Boys, 1966), The Doors' *L.A. Woman*, Odd Future and Tyler, the Creator; widely attributed to the *Garfield* and easyJet logos. A recurring retro favourite, now feeding the wider "soft serif" revival via Fraunces and its relatives.
- **Robotic ↔ humane: 9/10.** Rounded terminals, soft blobby serifs, uneven old-style stress and a bouncing italic. Nothing in it is sharp or mechanical.
- **UI fitness:** display only for the black weight (≥ 24px); short labels on stickers and badges. Never body. The lighter Cooper weights (Cooper*) can carry 1 to 3 lines at ≥ 16px. Renders sturdily everywhere. Coverage: check non-Latin scripts in Cooper* and Fraunces before committing.
- **Pairs with:** a crisp geometric sans for UI (Futura, or open Jost) to sober it up. A typewriter mono (Courier Prime) for menus, receipts and tickets. A neutral grotesk (open Archivo) when the content must stay serious next to a cheerful header.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `push-pin-studios`: 1960s to 70s revival of vernacular letterforms with wit and warmth.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`: soft fat letters placed with modernist rigour and asymmetric white space give friendly authority.
- **Clashes with:** `didot`: hairline haughtiness beside soft bulk makes both look like parodies.

## Don't copy

The *Pet Sounds* cover lettering; the *Garfield* and easyJet logos; commercial Cooper Black outlines without licence.
