# Century Gothic (1990)

**Essence:** A wide, round, open geometric shipped free with Office, the friendliest-looking default, and therefore the face of a million school handouts and small-business logos.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Width as openness | Monotype redrew Twentieth Century to match ITC Avant Garde's widths: very round bowls, wide letters, a large x-height. The page looks bright and unhurried. | Use a wide geometric only for short display lines (≤ 6 words) where you want air: hero lines, signage, badges. Body text in a wide face costs ~15% more line length, budget the layout for it or switch faces under 18px. |
| G2 · Light colour on the page | Thin, even strokes and big counters make text light, the University of Wisconsin-Green Bay switched its default email font to it in 2010 to save printer ink (critics noted the extra paper its width uses). | Light-coloured faces need contrast elsewhere: text at #1A1A1A or darker, ≥ 400 weight, never on mid-tone backgrounds; check WCAG contrast with the actual weight, not just the colour pair. |
| G3 · Single-storey a, circle o | Schoolbook-like shapes echo how children learn to write, which reads as approachable, and, if unchecked, as childish. | If you borrow the single-storey "a" for friendliness, offset it with mature layout cues: restrained colour (≤ 2 accents), strict grid, sentence case. Never combine round geometric type + rounded corners ≥ 16px + pastels in one screen. |
| G4 · Metric-compatible substitute | Built so documents set in ITC Avant Garde reflow unchanged, a licensing workaround that became a typeface of its own. | Keep a metric twin in your fallback stack: URW Gothic or TeX Gyre Adventor share Avant Garde's (and so Century Gothic's) widths, so swapping fonts doesn't break layouts. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** geometric sans.
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Monotype, 1990 to 91; a redrawing of Monotype's Twentieth Century (Sol Hess, Lanston Monotype, 1937 to 47, itself Futura-inspired), widths matched to ITC Avant Garde Gothic. Digital-only from the start.
- **Digital versions & licences:** Century Gothic (Monotype), widely bundled with Microsoft Office and Windows. Commercial; bundled licences limit redistribution.
- **Proportions:** very wide, large x-height, short ascenders/descenders, circular bowls, single-storey a and g, low contrast, wide open apertures.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** TeX Gyre Adventor (GUST licence) and URW Gothic (URW base 35, free licences), Avant Garde-model geometrics with matching widths.

## Attitude

Century Gothic is not the product of a manifesto but of distribution: it became popular because it was there and looked cheerful. Its lesson is that friendliness from round shapes is cheap, real warmth needs restraint around it.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** school worksheets, PowerPoint decks, local-business logos, early-2000s web; reads "DIY, cheerful, a bit dated". Saturation: high in amateur contexts, choose it only knowingly.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 4/10, the round, open forms feel friendly, but it is pure geometry with no stroke modulation and no true italic (oblique); its warmth is the warmth of a toy, not of a hand.
- **UI fitness:** display only for most products; body text tiring beyond a paragraph (wide, similar circular letters). UI labels ≥ 14px. Latin-focused, check extended Latin and Cyrillic needs.
- **Pairs with:** Source Sans 3 or Fira Sans for UI text (absorbs the width); Georgia to add maturity; never another round geometric.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `web-2-gloss`, `corporate-memphis-illustration`, the friendly-default look of the 2000s and 2010s.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, set inside a severe grid, the roundness reads intentional.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism`, raw concrete honesty against cheerful geometric reads as a parody of both.

## Don't copy

Century Gothic and Twentieth Century outlines; don't redistribute bundled Office font files.
