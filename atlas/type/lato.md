# Lato (2010)

**Essence:** Friendly without being childish, classical proportions with softly rounded details, transparent in paragraphs and quietly characterful at headline size.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Semi-rounded details for warmth | Dziedzic gave stroke ends and joins a slight rounding, enough to feel warm, not enough to look like a rounded "kids" font; a firm structure keeps it serious. | To soften a product without infantilising it: semi-rounded type (not fully rounded terminals), 6 to 8px component radii, mid-saturation colour; avoid adding rounded corners ≥ 16px plus pastel plus rounded type together. |
| G2 · Transparent small, characterful large | He aimed for a face that disappears in body text but shows its shapes at large sizes. | Use one family for both roles: body 16/26 at 400; headlines at 700 to 900 and 32px+ with −1% tracking, the same face gains personality with scale, so no second display face is needed. |
| G3 · Classical proportions in a sans | Capitals follow classical models, giving harmony and a slightly elegant, grown-up tone. | For services aimed at adults with anxiety (health, finance, legal), choose a sans with classical capital proportions; use sentence case and 400 to 600 weights to sound calm. |
| G4 · Corporate brief turned public good | Planned as a corporate face for a client who chose another direction, it was released under the OFL; later extended (2013 to 14) to 3000+ glyphs with Cyrillic, Greek and IPA. | When a custom face is shelved, consider releasing or reusing it; and always check which version you ship, the extended Lato 2.0 (latofonts.com) covers far more languages than the long-standing Google Fonts copy. |

## Visual facts

- **Classification:** humanist sans with semi-rounded details (sometimes described as "humanist-geometric").
- **Designer · year · foundry:** Łukasz Dziedzic, tyPoland, Warsaw; begun summer 2010 ("lato" is Polish for summer), published December 2010 under the OFL with Google's support. Extended 2013 to 14 with Adam Twardoch and Botio Nikoltchev.
- **Digital versions & licences:** Lato 1.x (Google Fonts, Latin), Lato 2.0 (latofonts.com: nine weights with italics, Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, IPA); SIL OFL. Carlito (OFL) is a Lato-based font metric-compatible with Calibri.
- **Proportions:** medium-large x-height, open apertures, low contrast with gentle modulation, semi-rounded terminals, true italic; nine weights from Hairline to Black.
- **Free / open alternatives (keep the gene):** it is itself OFL. For the same warm-but-adult tone: Nunito Sans (OFL, non-rounded with friendly rounds); Carlito where Calibri-compatible metrics are required.

## Attitude

Lato's design brief is emotional balance: serious and friendly at once. It suggests that warmth in type comes from small softenings of an otherwise disciplined structure, not from cartoonish rounding.

## Using it today

- **Signals:** 2010s websites, healthcare and nonprofits, university templates; reads "friendly, trustworthy, a little default". Saturation: very high on the web; still credible, rarely distinctive.
- **Robotic ↔ humane:** 7/10, semi-rounded details, classical proportions and a true italic feel warm and adult; even colour and familiarity keep it from feeling personal.
- **UI fitness:** body text and UI labels; display works in heavy weights. Minimum 13px for labels. Check the version for Cyrillic/Greek (Lato 2.0 vs Google Fonts 1.x).
- **Pairs with:** Rockwell, Roboto Slab or Garamond for headings, a warm slab or old-style adds distinctiveness; Source Code Pro for data; avoid the Lato + Open Sans + Montserrat trio.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `scandinavian-modern`, `calm-technology`, gentle, humane clarity.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, friendly letters inside strict structure feel approachable and competent.
- **Clashes with:** `neo-brutalism-web`, politeness muffles the trend's harshness.

## Don't copy

Not an artifact problem (OFL), the risk is sameness; don't treat it as a distinctive brand face without customisation.
