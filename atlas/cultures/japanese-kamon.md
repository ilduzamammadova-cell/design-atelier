# Kamon

**Essence:** Identity distilled into a single-colour emblem drawn with compass and ruler, legible from a battlefield banner down to a sleeve.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Circle-bounded mark | Most mon sit inside an implied or drawn circle (maru), so every crest shares one silhouette and reads at a glance on cloth, lacquer or roof tile. | All icons and avatars drawn inside one fixed circle (e.g. 20px live area on a 24px canvas); equal optical diameter across the set; app and team marks as one-colour glyph in a circle. |
| G2 · Compass construction | Crests were drafted with compass and straightedge; curves share a small set of radii, which gives the family its calm. | Build icons from a limited arc set (radii 4/8/12 on a 24 grid) and publish the construction lines in the icon spec so new marks can be drawn by rule. |
| G3 · Counted symmetry | Crests use 3-, 5-, 6-, 8- or 16-part radial symmetry; the count itself is identifying. | A badge or tier system where segment count encodes level (3-fold basic, 6-fold pro, 8-fold enterprise) while shape language stays constant. |
| G4 · Family variants from one motif | One plant or object (wisteria, hollyhock, wood sorrel) is varied by enclosure, count, inversion or rotation to mark branch families. | Generate a sub-brand set from one base glyph plus modifiers: ring, square frame, inverted fill, rotation. Document modifiers like tokens. |
| G5 · Solid, outline and reversed | Mon are woven white on black formal kimono, dyed dark on light noren, and also drawn as outline-only "shadow" versions. | Every mark ships in three states: solid, outline (2px stroke on 24), and reversed; states map to default, disabled and inverse surfaces. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** monochrome by nature: white on black (formal montsuki), white on indigo #1F2F4A, black on undyed cloth #EFE9DD; occasional vermilion #C0392B on lacquer.
- **Type:** crests are pictorial; pair with a geometric gothic such as Zen Kaku Gothic New or M PLUS 1p, or a disciplined Latin grotesk.
- **Form:** plants (paulownia, chrysanthemum, wisteria, ginkgo), objects (fans, arrow feathers, wheels), geometric lozenges, stylised characters.
- **Composition:** one centred emblem, generous margin, absolute symmetry or controlled rotational balance.

## Attitude

Kamon carry a lineage across centuries with no colour heraldry at all, only shape. They were meant to be reproduced by many hands (dyers, lacquerers, tile makers) without drift, so the drawing had to be rule-based. That is the transferable lesson: identity as a constructible system, not a picture.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `otl-aicher`, `susan-kare`, `japanese-modernism` — constructed pictograms and single-colour clarity.
- **Productive tension with:** `art-nouveau` — organic plant drawing forced into a strict circle and counted symmetry.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic` — melting forms and colour floods destroy the emblem's constancy.

## Don't copy

The sixteen-petal imperial chrysanthemum (barred from trademark registration under Japan's Trademark Act, Art. 4(1)(i)); the Go-shichi no kiri paulownia used by the Japanese government; the Tokugawa triple hollyhock; any living family's or company's registered crest (for example marks derived from historic crests, such as Mitsubishi's three diamonds).

## Respect

Mon are still family property, used on formal kimono, graves and shop curtains. Do not lift a real family's crest as decoration; draw new marks with the grammar. The imperial chrysanthemum and government paulownia are state symbols. Credit the source as "constructed in the manner of Japanese kamon". Caricature looks like a random crest slapped on a sushi brand, samurai and ninja imagery, or brush-style fake kanji. Avoid the sayagata/manji motifs in Western products; they are Buddhist in origin but read as hate symbols to many viewers.
