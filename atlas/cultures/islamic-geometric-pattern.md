# Islamic geometric pattern

**Essence:** Infinite order unfolded from a single circle with compass and straightedge, so that every part implies the whole and the pattern continues beyond the wall.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Everything from one circle | Patterns are constructed by dividing a circle into 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 parts; all lines derive from that first measure. | Derive layout and icon proportions from one module: choose a base circle (e.g. 96px) and a fold (6 or 8); spacing steps follow its ratios (√2 for 4/8-fold, √3 for 6/12-fold). |
| G2 · The pattern continues | Repeat units on square or hexagonal cells tile the plane; the visible panel is a window onto an endless field. | Background patterns built as true tiles and cropped hard at the container edge, never shrunk to fit; resizing reveals more pattern, not a bigger one. |
| G3 · Star hubs and strapwork | Focal stars (8, 10, 12, 16 points) are surrounded by secondary polygons; interlaced straps pass over and under. | Navigation or concept maps as star hubs with secondary nodes; connectors drawn as two-tone straps with 2px gaps at crossings to show over/under. |
| G4 · Decorated tiles that always join (girih) | A set of five equal-edged tiles (decagon, pentagon, hexagon, bowtie, rhombus) carries lines that meet edge midpoints, so any arrangement yields continuous strapwork; by 1453 at the Darb-i Imam shrine (Isfahan) designers achieved near quasi-periodic patterns (Lu & Steinhardt, 2007). | Components whose border lines exit at fixed edge midpoints, so any adjacency of cards produces a continuous line across the grid; a "girih" card set where layout freedom never breaks the pattern. |
| G5 · Handmade variance (zellige) | Moroccan zellige is assembled from hand-cut glazed pieces; each piece's glaze varies slightly. | Flat tile colours with a controlled ±3% lightness variance per instance (seeded), so large patterned areas feel crafted rather than vector-flat. |
| G6 · Two reading distances | Large stars read across a courtyard; fine fills reward the close viewer. | Every pattern specified at macro scale (≈1/6 viewport) and micro scale (≈1/60); the micro layer appears only on zoom or large displays. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** zellige cobalt #1F4FA0, turquoise #2A9D8F, green #1E6B4A, ochre #C8902E, white #F2EFE7, manganese brown #2A211C. Iranian tile turquoise and lapis.
- **Type:** frequently paired with inscription bands (see `islamic-calligraphy`); for UI: Amiri, Reem Kufi; Latin pairing a geometric sans.
- **Form:** star polygons, rosettes, interlace, muqarnas (stalactite vaulting) in three dimensions.
- **Composition:** field, borders and inscription bands; dado tiling below stucco above.

## Attitude

The patterns express unity in multiplicity and were transmitted as workshop knowledge, recorded in design scrolls such as the Topkapı Scroll. They are mathematics made visible and tactile. Owen Jones's study of the Alhambra (1842–45) and *The Grammar of Ornament* (1856) brought them into Western design teaching.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `owen-jones`, `art-deco`, `op-art` — geometric star systems and optical order.
- **Productive tension with:** `kuba-raffia-cloth` — perfect symmetry against deliberate interruption.
- **Clashes with:** `punk-graphics` — torn collage breaks the logic that is the whole point.

## Don't copy

Specific monument panels (Alhambra's Hall of the Ambassadors, Darb-i Imam spandrels, Bou Inania madrasa zellige); drawings from contemporary authors' books reproduced as-is; mihrab compositions.

## Respect

Geometric pattern is broadly regarded as shared heritage and open to study and use with credit to its regional source (Moroccan zellige, Nasrid, Mamluk, Ottoman, Persian, Mughal). Do not combine patterns with Qur'anic or sacred text as decoration, and do not put mosque imagery, mihrabs or the Kaaba on products. Avoid floors, mats and footwear for compositions taken from mosques. Not all art from Muslim-majority places is religious; name the region and period. Caricature is "Arabian Nights" orientalism: lamps, genies, onion domes and one undifferentiated "Islamic" style.
