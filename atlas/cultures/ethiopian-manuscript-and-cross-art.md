# Ethiopian manuscript and cross art

**Essence:** A modular script, rubricated pages with interlace headbands, flat radiant painting and pierced metal lattices, sustained by one of the world's oldest continuous Christian book cultures.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Modular abugida (fidel) | Ge'ez script forms each character from a consonant base plus a systematic vowel modification across seven orders; one logic generates hundreds of signs. | Icon and status families built as base glyph plus systematic modifiers (up to 7 positions: a mark left, right, top, foot, ring, stroke, loop); a matrix generated by rule, documented as a table. |
| G2 · Rubrication | Manuscripts write holy names, headings and key words in red within black text. | One "ink red" (#B22222) reserved for headings and key terms inside running text; no other emphasis colour in reading views. |
| G3 · Interlace headbands | Book openings and section starts carry ornamental bands of interlace in red, yellow, green and blue. | A single ornamental band marks each chapter or section start (12–20px high), then the page returns to plain two-colour text. |
| G4 · Pierced lattice within an outline | Processional and hand crosses are lattices of interlaced bands and openings held inside a strong outer contour. | Pierced-mark construction: brand marks and large icons built as lattices with open negative spaces inside a bold outline, working at one colour; never as cross shapes. |
| G5 · Flat frontal figures | Painting uses flat colour fields, strong outlines, frontal poses and large almond-shaped eyes. | Character illustration in flat colour with bold contour and direct, frontal gaze; patterned garments instead of shading. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red #B8322A, ochre yellow #E0A526, green #2E7D4F, blue #2C4F8C, black #1A1A1A on parchment #EADCBF.
- **Type:** Ge'ez fidel (also used for Amharic and Tigrinya). For UI: Noto Sans Ethiopic, Noto Serif Ethiopic; Abyssinica SIL.
- **Form:** interlace, lattices, flat figures, geometric headbands; cast and wrought metal crosses in brass and silver.
- **Composition:** two-column text blocks with red rubrics; framed full-page paintings; symmetrical crosses with smaller crosses nested inside.

## Attitude

Christianity became the faith of the Aksumite court in the 4th century, and monastic scribes have copied books on parchment ever since; radiocarbon dating places the Garima Gospels in Late Antiquity, among the oldest surviving illustrated Gospel books. Manuscripts and crosses are made for use in worship, not for display. The design lesson is a complete, durable system: script, page, colour and ornament working together for centuries.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `islamic-calligraphy` — script as the core of the visual system, rubrics and illumination.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — rubricated two-column reading inside a rational sans grid.
- **Clashes with:** `memphis` — ironic, random colour undercuts a sacred, ordered palette.

## Don't copy

Pages of the Garima Gospels; specific processional crosses (including those of the Lalibela churches); icons by named painters; objects taken from Maqdala in 1868.

## Respect

Crosses, icons and manuscripts are liturgical objects of a living church; do not use processional crosses or saints as fashion or décor motifs. Tabots (consecrated altar tablets) may be seen only by clergy; museums holding them keep them out of view. Many Ethiopian manuscripts and objects in Britain were looted at Maqdala in 1868 and their return is requested; acknowledge this context when citing them. Ge'ez is a living script: never use it as decorative pseudo-text, and have a reader check anything you set. Caricature is "exotic Africa" imagery or treating Ethiopian Christianity as generic tribal art.
