# Scottish tartan

**Essence:** A pattern that is fully defined by a string of numbers, where crossing threads mix their colours into new ones.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Threadcount as token | A tartan's sett is a sequence of colours and thread counts (for example black 4, red 24, black 24, yellow 4), woven identically in warp and weft. | Store stripe patterns as data strings that generate backgrounds, bars and chart fills; designers edit numbers in tokens, not pixels. |
| G2 · Crossing makes the palette | Where a warp colour crosses a weft colour in twill, a mixed tone appears, so a few base colours yield many visible tones. | Define only 3–5 base colours; generate secondary tones automatically by overlapping translucent stripes (50% multiply). Secondary colours are derived, never picked. |
| G3 · Mirror at the pivot | Symmetric setts reflect at pivot points; asymmetric setts simply repeat. | Spacing rhythms that mirror (8–24–8 │ 8–24–8) for symmetric layouts; repeating rhythms for feeds and lists. |
| G4 · Same sett, many renderings | One tartan can be woven in "modern", "ancient" (lighter), "muted" or "weathered" colourways, and in dress or hunting versions. | Theme variants generated by transforming one token set: saturated, lightened, muted and weathered versions of the same pattern data. |
| G5 · Registered identity | The Scottish Register of Tartans (Act of 2008, run by National Records of Scotland) records names, threadcounts and any stated usage restrictions. | Treat patterns as identity assets with a registry: each has an ID, threadcount, owner and usage note in the design system. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** dark green #1E4D2B, navy #1B2A4A, red #B22234, black #141414, yellow #F2C230, white #F2F0EA; mixed tones appear at crossings.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; for UI a sturdy transitional serif or a grotesk.
- **Form:** orthogonal stripes; the diagonal "twill line" visible at close range.
- **Composition:** square sett repeated in both directions; pivots and bands.

## Attitude

Tartan is both old craft and invented tradition. After the Dress Act of 1746 banned Highland dress (repealed 1782), the 19th-century romantic revival systematised clan tartans, helped along by the forged *Vestiarium Scoticum* (1842). Punk (Vivienne Westwood) and fashion houses have repeatedly reinvented it. Its gift to design is parametric thinking: pattern as code.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `josef-albers`, `anni-albers`, `bauhaus` — colour interaction and woven structure.
- **Productive tension with:** `punk-graphics` — establishment pattern turned into rebellion.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — organic curves have no place in the orthogonal sett.

## Don't copy

The Burberry check (a registered trademark); corporate and designer tartans registered by their owners; the Balmoral tartan, by custom reserved for the British royal family; specific historic pieces at the National Museum of Scotland.

## Respect

Tartan is largely open; by "tartan etiquette" anyone may wear most clan tartans, and anyone can design and register a new one. Some tartans carry restrictions recorded by their owners, which the register notes but no statute enforces; respect them. Do not present a clan's tartan as your brand pattern; design and register your own sett. Caricature is bagpipes, Nessie, "see you Jimmy" hats and shortbread-tin clichés.
