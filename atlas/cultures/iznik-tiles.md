# Iznik ceramics and Ottoman tilework

**Essence:** Semi-naturalistic flowers and serrated leaves glowing on a brilliant white ground, designed centrally at court and executed as modular wall programmes.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · White ground as light source | Fritware under a white slip and clear glaze makes colours glow; the ground is bright white, never neutral grey. | Background a warm, near-pure white (#FBFAF7); saturation lives only in motifs and key controls; no grey panels at all. |
| G2 · Tight palette with one signature colour | Cobalt, turquoise and green, then from the 1550s a raised bole red that stands slightly proud of the surface. | Four-colour palette where the "impossible" red is reserved for the single highest-priority signal; only that colour gets a 1px raised highlight. |
| G3 · The sweeping saz leaf | Long serrated leaves cross the field diagonally, overlapping blossoms and setting the whole composition in motion. | One dominant diagonal (≈30°) through a layout, as an image crop edge or graphic stroke, with smaller elements arranged along it. |
| G4 · Recognisable stylised flowers | The "four flowers" repertoire (tulip, carnation, hyacinth, rose) is drawn clearly enough to name, flat enough to repeat. | Botanical icon style: clear silhouettes, dark outline, flat fill, one highlight dot; each flower a named icon in the set. |
| G5 · Tile, border, corner | Walls are tiled from field units, border strips and corner pieces that assemble into panels of any size. | Components as 9-slice tiles: defined field, edge and corner pieces so panels grow without redrawing; square cards that join seamlessly. |
| G6 · Central studio, distributed makers | Designs were drawn in the imperial design studio (nakkaşhane) in Istanbul, associated with head designers such as Kara Memi, and fired by Iznik potters. | Treat ornament like a design system: one central library of motifs and tokens, consumed by many product teams; no local redraws. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** cobalt #1D3F8F, turquoise #2BA3A8, emerald #2E8B57, bole red #C3352B, black outline #1A1A1A, white #FAF8F2.
- **Type:** Ottoman calligraphy appears in tile inscription panels; for UI pair with a humanist serif and Amiri for Arabic.
- **Form:** saz leaves, rumi arabesques, cloud bands, cypresses, prunus blossom; the "rock and wave" dish rim.
- **Composition:** symmetrical panels with dense floral fill, framed by borders; dishes with central motif and rim band.

## Attitude

Imperial patronage, renewed during the building of the Süleymaniye mosque (1550–57), turned Iznik into a court-directed industry. Designers pushed towards naturalism while keeping every flower obedient to a decorative order. The work shows how a centrally authored visual language can scale across architecture and objects.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `william-morris`, `arts-and-crafts` — William De Morgan and Morris admired and echoed Iznik colour and flowers.
- **Productive tension with:** `scandinavian-modern` — cool restraint and lots of white space holding one rich floral moment.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism` — raw grey mass extinguishes the white-ground glow.

## Don't copy

Tile panels of the Rüstem Pasha Mosque (Sinan, 1561–63) and the Topkapı Palace; specific museum dishes (British Museum Godman Collection, V&A, Met); current designs of revival workshops.

## Respect

The style is widely reproduced in Türkiye and open to learn from; credit it as Ottoman Turkish. Mosque tile programmes often include Qur'anic inscriptions; never reproduce those (see `islamic-calligraphy`). Do not place compositions taken from mosques on floors or footwear. Caricature is orientalist "Turkish" clichés: harems, fezzes, evil-eye beads and Turkish-delight exoticism layered over tulips.
