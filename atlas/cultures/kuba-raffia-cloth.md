# Kuba raffia cloth

**Essence:** Angular geometry that keeps changing its mind: patterns shift scale, direction and motif mid-field, so the eye never settles.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Pattern that refuses to repeat exactly | Kuba panels set up a geometric order and then alter it: a motif changes, a band shifts, a scale jumps. The variation is the design. | Controlled interruption in repeating grids: every Nth item (e.g. 1 in 7, seeded) is a variant (rotated, rescaled, colour-swapped). Lists stay scannable but never monotonous. |
| G2 · Angular interlace on a diagonal grid | Linked lozenges, step-frets and angular meanders built on the woven grid. | Ornament and empty-state art drawn only on a 45° grid with 2px strokes and square joins; no curves. |
| G3 · Texture as hierarchy | Cut-pile embroidery (loops of raffia cut into a dense plush) stands against flat woven ground; emphasis is made by touch-like texture, not colour. | Emphasis through surface texture instead of hue: primary panels carry a fine raised pattern or noise, secondary panels are flat; colour palette stays tiny. |
| G4 · Many hands, one cloth | Men weave the raffia base cloth; women add embroidery and cut pile; panels are joined into long ceremonial skirts. | Collaborative canvases where many contributors add panels within one shared palette and stroke rule, so the whole stays coherent despite different hands. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** natural raffia #D8C49A, dark brown-black #2C1F17, camwood red #8E3B2A, ochre #B98A3E.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; for UI a plain grotesk that lets pattern carry voice.
- **Form:** lozenges, interlocking angular lines, step motifs, chequers; plush tufts against flat weave.
- **Composition:** allover fields with deliberate breaks and shifts; long assembled panels.

## Attitude

Kuba textiles were prestige goods, diplomatic gifts, burial wealth and even currency, judged by inventive design. Designs had names, and makers prized novelty within a shared vocabulary. Matisse and Klee admired these cloths; the lesson for designers is that a system can include planned deviation.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `henri-matisse`, `paul-klee`, both learned from Kuba rhythm and improvised geometry.
- **Productive tension with:** `islamic-geometric-pattern`, perfect infinite order against deliberate interruption.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity`, strict consistency dogma rejects the very deviation that makes Kuba work.

## Don't copy

Specific panels in museum collections (British Museum, Met, LACMA, North Carolina Museum of Art); named Shoowa and Kuba designs; royal regalia.

## Respect

Kuba cloth belongs to living Kuba communities and much of it entered Western museums under colonial conditions, including early 20th-century collecting expeditions. Use the structural principles (interruption, angular grid, texture hierarchy) rather than reproducing panels; credit "Kuba, Democratic Republic of the Congo". Burial and royal cloths carry status and should not be mimicked as status décor. Caricature is generic "tribal" geometric prints and safari styling.
