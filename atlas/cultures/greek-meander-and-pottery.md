# Ancient Greek vase painting and the meander

**Essence:** Ornament that obeys the body of the vessel: horizontal registers, a continuous right-angled meander, and stories told in silhouette.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The continuous meander | The meander (Greek key) is one unbroken line turning at right angles, forming an endless band that frames. | Section frames drawn as a single continuous SVG path on an 8px module with a constant 2px stroke; one meander band per page at most, at a structural boundary. |
| G2 · Registers proportioned to the body | Geometric-period vases are divided into horizontal bands of pattern and figures, sized to the swelling of neck, shoulder, belly and foot. | Page as registers: header (neck), navigation (shoulder), the widest and richest content at the "belly", narrow footer; each register with its own density. |
| G3 · Story by silhouette (black-figure) | Figures painted as black silhouettes on orange clay, details incised to show the clay beneath; poses must read from shape alone. | Illustration rule: every figure must read as a solid silhouette; inner detail only as 1px knockout lines. |

| G5 · Grid lettering (stoichedon) | Many Classical Athenian inscriptions align letters in a strict grid, each in its own cell, like monospace. | Monospaced or grid-aligned display type for headings and data labels, every character on a column grid; pairs well with tabular figures. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** terracotta #C2672F, gloss black #1C1714, added red-purple #7B2E2A, added white #EDE4D3.
- **Type:** Greek capitals of inscriptions; for UI a monolinear capitals-friendly sans or a grid mono (e.g. IBM Plex Mono for labels).
- **Form:** meanders, chevrons, rosettes, palmettes, figure friezes; incised and brushed lines.
- **Composition:** horizontal registers, framed panels (metopes) on vessel bodies.

## Attitude

Potters and painters treated decoration as an articulation of the object's structure; some signed their work, among them Exekias, who signed as both potter and painter. The meander and register logic have shaped classical and modern design ever since. The lesson: let ornament follow structure, and tell stories in shapes legible from a distance.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-deco`, `bauhaus` — right-angled ornament and structural honesty.
- **Productive tension with:** `postmodern-new-wave` — classical fragments quoted against contemporary layering, if done with intent.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — whiplash organic line fights the right-angled register.

## Don't copy

The Dipylon Amphora (National Archaeological Museum, Athens); Exekias's amphora of Achilles and Ajax playing a game (Vatican Museums); the Euphronios Krater; Versace's "Greca" border and Medusa marks; the Anthora coffee cup (Leslie Buck, 1963).

## Respect

The vocabulary is ancient, broadly open and part of shared classical heritage; credit it as Greek and, where possible, name period and place. Caricature is "toga party" Greece, faux-Greek Latin fonts that use Σ for E and Λ for A, and diner-cup kitsch. Be aware that modern Greek identity is not only antiquity.
