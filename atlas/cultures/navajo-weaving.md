# Navajo (Diné) weaving

**Essence:** Terraced geometry woven on the upright loom, from banded chief blankets to bordered rugs, balancing stark contrast with calm symmetry.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Stepped geometry from the loom | Weft-faced tapestry weave builds diagonals as terraces; diamonds and triangles have serrated or stepped edges. | Diagonals in ornament and icons only as 1:1 or 2:1 pixel steps on a strict grid; no curves; corners square. |
| G2 · Enrichment by phases | Chief blankets moved from simple stripes (first phase) to stripes with rectangles (second) to stripes with diamonds (third): complexity added in layers on the same banded base. | Progressive enrichment: one banded base layout; higher states (premium, completed, celebrated) add rectangles, then diamonds, on the same bands rather than switching style. |
| G3 · Natural-wool neutrals | Two Grey Hills weavers became known for undyed and carded blends of black, grey, brown and white wool. | A palette of five "fleece" neutrals with figure and ground separated by value alone; ideal for calm data-dense screens. |
| G4 · Optical vibration on purpose | From the 1880s, bright commercial (Germantown) yarns enabled Eye Dazzler weavings of serrated zigzags in clashing hues. | One deliberately vibrating screen (celebration, onboarding finale) with serrated zigzags in complementary colours; the rest of the product stays calm. |
| G5 · Bordered field with an exit | Trading-post era rugs often frame a central field with borders; some weavers run a fine contrasting line from the field through the border, a weaver's pathway. | Bordered containers with one deliberate break where the next action exits (a gap in a card border aligned to its link). Use the structural idea; do not name or market it as a "spirit line". |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Ganado red #9B1B1E, black #1B1B1B, natural grey #8C8C88, natural white #EEEAE2, natural brown #5B4636; vegetal-dye pastels in some regional styles.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; a sturdy grotesk keeps out of the way.
- **Form:** stripes, terraced diamonds, stepped triangles, serrated zigzags, crosses.
- **Composition:** horizontal bands (blankets); central medallions within borders (rugs); strong bilateral symmetry.

## Attitude

In Diné teaching, Spider Woman taught weaving; the work is tied to hózhó, balance and beauty. Trading posts such as Hubbell's at Ganado shaped regional rug styles for outside markets, and weavers adapted while keeping control of their craft. Today weavers such as D.Y. Begay and Barbara Teller Ornelas show the tradition as contemporary art.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `anni-albers`, `andean-textiles` — weave structure dictating stepped geometry.
- **Productive tension with:** `op-art` — the Eye Dazzler already anticipated optical vibration; pairing tests where vibration serves meaning.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau` — fluid organic line cannot live on the terraced loom grid.

## Don't copy

Historic chief blankets and rugs in museum collections; designs of named contemporary weavers; Yei and Yeibichai rugs; any sandpainting imagery.

## Respect

The Navajo Nation holds trademarks on "Navajo"; it sued Urban Outfitters in 2012 over "Navajo" products and settled in 2016. The US Indian Arts and Crafts Act (1990) makes it illegal to market goods falsely as Native-made. Yei and Yeibichai figures depict Holy People and come from ceremonial contexts; sandpainting designs must never be used. The whirling-log motif was renounced by Navajo and other Southwestern weavers in 1940 because of its resemblance to the Nazi swastika. Buy from or commission Diné weavers and credit them. Caricature is "tribal/Aztec print" fast fashion, dreamcatchers, headdresses and the word "Navajo" used as a pattern name.
