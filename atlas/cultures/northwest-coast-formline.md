# Northwest Coast formline

**Essence:** A single continuous, swelling and tapering line system that fills its field completely, ranked by colour into primary and secondary structure.

> **Use mode: structural principles only.** Formline is the visual language of living nations, and crest images belong to clans and lineages. Do not draw ovoids, U-forms, split-U forms, crest creatures or anything that resembles them. Only the abstract principles below transfer.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Everything connects | The primary formline is one continuous network; lines rarely end in open space, they join other lines. | Diagrams and illustrations where every stroke terminates on another stroke; no loose ends; connectors meet at clean junctions. |
| G2 · Line that breathes | Formlines swell and taper in width, tightening at junctions and opening along spans, giving tension and life. | Variable-width strokes in illustration and dividers: width ratio around 1:2.5 between junction and mid-span; no monoline outlines for key graphics. |
| G3 · Rank by colour | The primary formline is usually black, the secondary red, with a tertiary blue-green fill; each colour has a structural role. | Strict colour rank: primary colour only for top-level structure, secondary for internal detail, tertiary for fills; roles never swap within a screen. |
| G4 · Design adapts to the object | Designs are distorted and recomposed to fill the given surface (box side, blanket, paddle) completely. | Illustrations composed to their container: art fills the frame edge to edge and is re-composed for each breakpoint, never just scaled. |

## Visual facts

Described for understanding: black, red and blue-green on natural cedar or cloth; Bill Holm's 1965 analysis named the elements (formline, ovoid, U-form) and the primary/secondary hierarchy. These shapes are not for reuse.

## Attitude

Crests record the histories and rights of lineages; displaying them is a right affirmed publicly, historically at potlatch, which Canada banned from 1884 to 1951. The art revived powerfully through artists such as Bill Reid and Robert Davidson (Haida). The lesson for others is about systems: a complete, rule-based visual grammar with hierarchy, continuity and tension.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-nouveau` — structurally, the idea of a continuous living line.
- **Productive tension with:** `bauhaus` — monoline geometry against breathing, variable-width line.
- **Clashes with:** any imitation of the surface; "Native-style" graphics undercut the artists whose language it is.

## Don't copy

All crest imagery (Raven, Eagle, Killer Whale, Bear, Frog, Thunderbird and others); totem poles; Chilkat and Ravenstail weaving; works by Bill Reid (e.g. *The Spirit of Haida Gwaii*), Robert Davidson and other artists.

## Respect

Treat as closed to motif borrowing. Crests are owned by clans and lineages; sacred and ceremonial regalia are not décor. In the US, the Indian Arts and Crafts Act (1990) makes it illegal to market goods falsely as Native-made. Commission Northwest Coast artists and credit their nation. Caricature is kitsch totem poles, "tribal" sports logos and souvenir formline knock-offs.
