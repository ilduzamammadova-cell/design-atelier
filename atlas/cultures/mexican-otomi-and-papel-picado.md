# Otomí tenango embroidery and papel picado

**Essence:** Flat, joyful silhouettes in saturated colour: embroidered creatures floating on white cloth, and chisel-cut paper banners that turn light into pattern.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Flat silhouette bestiary | Tenangos fill white cotton with animals, birds and plants as flat filled shapes: no outline, no shading, no horizon. | Illustration system of silhouettes only, floating on white; density decreases near content areas so text keeps a clear field. |
| G2 · One colour or many | Many tenangos are worked in a single vivid colour; others go fully multicolour. | Two illustration themes: monochrome (all art in the brand colour) for everyday screens, multicolour reserved for celebrations and milestones. |
| G3 · Pattern as holes | Papel picado is cut with small chisels through stacks of tissue paper; the image is light passing through the cuts. | Header banners built from repeated "cut" flags using CSS masks; background colour or photo visible through the cut-outs; flags strung along a gently sagging line. |
| G4 · Made to flutter and fade | Paper banners are temporary, hung over streets and altars and replaced each season. | Event skins that appear for a limited time then retire; motion is a gentle flutter (2–3° rotation, 3s ease-in-out) applied only to festive elements. |
| G5 · Stacked repetition | Artisans cut many layers at once from one template, so identical forms appear in many colours. | Generate banner variants from one template with a colour per instance; never redraw per colour. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** tenango on white manta #F7F4EE with pink #E6427A, orange #F28C28, turquoise #1BA6A6, green #3FA34D, purple #6C3483; papel picado adds yellow and marigold orange #F39C12.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; let colour lead with a neutral sans. Mexican hand-painted sign lettering (rótulos) is a separate tradition with its own makers.
- **Form:** silhouettes of deer, birds, rabbits, flowers; symmetric cut lace, scalloped borders.
- **Composition:** scattered allover fields (tenango); horizontal strings and canopies (papel picado).

## Attitude

Tenango embroidery grew in the 1960s from Otomí blouse embroidery when drought pushed communities to make sellable flat panels; it is livelihood and identity. Papel picado is festive and civic, made in quantity in Huixcolotla, which Puebla declared the cradle of the craft in 1998. Both celebrate abundance with modest means.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `alexander-girard`, `luis-barragan`, `henri-matisse` — folk colour, cut-out silhouette, joy as a design value.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams` — playful silhouettes inside an otherwise austere product UI.
- **Clashes with:** `ulm-school` — grey rationalism mutes the colour logic that carries the meaning.

## Don't copy

Designs by named artisans and workshops of San Nicolás and Tenango de Doria, including designs registered at Mexico's IMPI; Alexander Girard's own textile designs; specific Día de Muertos ofrenda compositions.

## Respect

Tenango designs are collective heritage of Otomí communities. Mexico's Ministry of Culture has publicly challenged fashion brands, including Carolina Herrera's Resort 2020 collection (2019), and a 2022 federal law protects the cultural heritage of Indigenous and Afro-Mexican peoples, voiding deals signed with a single member instead of the community. Collaborate with and pay artisans, and name the community. Papel picado is widely made for sale and open to use, but Día de Muertos (UNESCO-listed) commemorates the dead and is not "Mexican Halloween". Caricature is sombreros, moustaches, sugar-skull face paint as costume, cacti and "fiesta" fonts.
