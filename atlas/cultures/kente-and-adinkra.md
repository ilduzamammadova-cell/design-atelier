# Kente and Adinkra

**Essence:** Narrow woven strips assembled into staggered polychrome rhythm, and a stamped symbol language where each sign carries a proverb.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Strip assembly, staggered blocks | Kente is woven on narrow horizontal looms in strips, then sewn edge to edge; motif blocks in neighbouring strips are offset, producing a woven checker rhythm. | A strip layout: content columns as narrow strips whose blocks deliberately do not align across columns (offset by half a block), giving a staggered, woven rhythm to dashboards and galleries. |
| G2 · Stripe interrupted by block | Long warp stripes run continuously and are interrupted at intervals by weft-faced blocks of motif. | Lists or timelines drawn as continuous coloured stripes punctuated at regular intervals by highlighted "blocks" (milestones, featured items). |
| G3 · Every pattern has a name | Each kente design and each adinkra symbol is named, often after a proverb, person or event; cloth is read, not only seen. | Name and define every pattern, icon and colour in the system with a one-line meaning; the documentation reads like a glossary of proverbs (written for your own product, not borrowed). |
| G4 · Stamped grid with combed rules | Adinkra cloth is divided into squares by multi-line combed rules, and each square is stamped with carved calabash stamps in dark dye. | Grids of square cells separated by triple 1px rules; each cell holds one icon from your own symbol set, rotated or repeated by rule. |
| G5 · Colour as stated meaning | Colours are commonly read symbolically (gold for status and wealth, green for growth, red for struggle, black for maturity and the spiritual, blue for peace). | Write colour semantics into the tokens themselves (e.g. `color.growth`, `color.alert`) and never use a colour against its stated meaning. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** kente gold #E1B12C, green #1F7A3A, red #B5281F, black #111111, blue #1F4E8C, white; adinkra dark brown-black dye on white, red or russet cloth.
- **Type:** adinkra are symbols, not letters; for UI a robust geometric sans.
- **Form:** rectilinear weave blocks, zigzags, ladders, checks; adinkra symbols are compact, mostly symmetric marks.
- **Composition:** checkerboards of strips; stamped grid fields with ruled divisions.

## Attitude

Kente developed under royal patronage; certain designs were reserved for the Asantehene, and colour and design still signal age, status and occasion. Adinkra cloth was historically associated with mourning and farewell. Both are languages: wearing them says something specific. UNESCO notes transmission through families, apprenticeship and schools.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `anni-albers` — woven structure as the generator of image.
- **Productive tension with:** `de-stijl` — primary blocks on a strict grid versus a staggered, polychrome woven rhythm.
- **Clashes with:** `japanese-wabi-sabi-and-ma` — muted austerity and celebratory chroma cancel each other.

## Don't copy

Named kente designs and the work of master weavers; adinkra symbols adopted wholesale as a brand's icon set or logo (e.g. Gye Nyame, Sankofa); Ghanaian state and royal regalia.

## Respect

Kente and adinkra are living heritage of the Asante and Ewe of Ghana; Ghana's copyright law treats national folklore as held in trust for the people of Ghana, and the government is pursuing stronger protection for kente. Gye Nyame and other adinkra have religious meaning; keep them off floors, footwear and throwaway goods. Printed "kente" is not kente: label it "kente-inspired" and buy woven cloth from Ghanaian weavers. Do not confuse kente with wax print (a Dutch-origin industrial cloth). Caricature is generic "African print" mash-ups, "tribal" fonts and safari imagery.
