# Madhubani (Mithila) painting

**Essence:** Every surface inside the frame alive with line: double outlines, hatching and natural symbols, painted by women as ritual and now as authorship.

## Visual facts

- **Color:** see G5; contemporary work also uses bright commercial colours.
- **Type:** Maithili was historically written in Tirhuta script; for UI pair with Devanagari-capable fonts (Mukta, Tiro Devanagari) and let illustration carry voice.
- **Form:** fish, peacocks, lotus, bamboo, sun and moon, frontal figures with large profile eyes, trees of life.
- **Composition:** central subject, often symmetrical, enclosed by borders; narrative scenes in registers.

## Attitude

Women of Mithila painted walls and floors for marriages and festivals. After a drought in the mid-1960s, a relief programme associated with Pupul Jayakar and the All India Handicrafts Board encouraged painting on paper for sale; artists such as Jagdamba Devi (Padma Shri, 1975), Sita Devi, Ganga Devi and Baua Devi became named authors. Dalit artists introduced the Godna style drawn from tattoo traditions. The tradition is ritual, livelihood and personal voice at once.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `arts-and-crafts` — handwork, border-and-field, nature as pattern.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — the "full inside, empty outside" rule lets dense illustration live on a rational grid.
- **Clashes with:** `minimalism-art` — the tradition's meaning lives in fullness; reduction strips it.

## Don't copy

Works of named artists (Ganga Devi, Sita Devi, Baua Devi, Mahasundari Devi, Jagdamba Devi) and pieces in the Mithila Museum (Tokamachi, Japan); Kohbar and deity paintings.

## Respect

"Madhubani painting" holds Geographical Indication status in India (2007); do not call non-Mithila work Madhubani. Kohbar (marriage-chamber) images and paintings of deities (Durga, Kali, Krishna) are ritual and devotional; never use them as product decoration. Credit the specific style and, where relevant, the caste community that created it (Godna). Commission living artists; many now paint contemporary subjects. Caricature is generic "Indian folk" fish clip art, or treating Madhubani, Warli and Gond as interchangeable.
