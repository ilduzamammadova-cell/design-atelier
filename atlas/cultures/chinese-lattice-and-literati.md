# Chinese lattice, gardens and literati aesthetics

**Essence:** Order generated by simple rules (the window lattice) set against cultivated spontaneity (the ink painting), with views framed rather than displayed.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Lattice as grammar | Daniel Sheets Dye catalogued over a thousand window lattices and grouped them by base figure (square, octagon, hexagon, key fret, ice-ray). Each is a rule applied to a frame, so variety never loses coherence. | Generate frames, dividers and backgrounds from rules: base cell + rotation + break. Publish the rule, not just the artwork; 1px lattice lines in a dark wood tone at panel edges only. |
| G2 · Ice-ray subdivision | "Cracked ice" lattice splits a panel into irregular straight-edged polygons; every line ends on another line. Irregular but fully structured. | Gallery and collage layouts as straight-line crack subdivisions: recursive splits at angles constrained to 30–60°, all panels touching the frame; no floating cards. |
| G3 · The framed view | Garden walls are pierced with moon gates and leak windows that frame a chosen scene; "borrowed scenery" pulls distant views into the composition. | Images revealed through one framing shape (circle, octagon) cut from a flat wall colour; scrolling moves the "frame" across a larger scene instead of swapping images. |
| G4 · Ink, emptiness and inscription | Literati painting uses monochrome ink, large empty silk, and integrates poem inscriptions and red seals into the picture. | Captions set inside the image block, not under it; one cinnabar seal colour (#B7322C) as the only accent, used for a single confirm action or a stamp-like status mark. |
| G5 · The handscroll's paced reveal | Handscrolls are unrolled an arm's length at a time, right to left, so a landscape is read as a sequence. | Horizontal narrative sections revealed one viewport at a time with snap points; a progress rail that shows the whole scroll in miniature. |
| G6 · Seals as provenance | Owners and connoisseurs added their seals to a painting over centuries, building a visible history. | Approval and edit history rendered as small square seal marks accumulating in a document corner, each with name and date on hover. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** ink black #1E1E1E and wash greys, aged silk #EFE6D2, cinnabar seal red #B7322C, rosewood #4A2C21, celadon #A6BFA8.
- **Type:** brush calligraphy (regular, running, cursive scripts). For UI: Noto Serif SC/TC (Song/Ming style) for text, LXGW WenKai (open source) for a brush voice; Latin pairing a humanist serif.
- **Form:** straight-member wooden lattice, rocks of eroded limestone, bamboo, brush strokes with dry and wet variation.
- **Composition:** symmetry in buildings, asymmetry in painting and garden paths; layered screens, zigzag routes.

## Attitude

Scholar-officials painted and wrote for themselves and for peers, prizing cultivated personality over technical display. Gardens were compressed worlds built for walking and changing viewpoints. Lattice-makers worked from craft rules that allowed endless variation. Together: freedom earned inside discipline.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `japanese-wabi-sabi-and-ma` (shared roots in ink and void), `frank-lloyd-wright` (screens, framed views).
- **Productive tension with:** `de-stijl` — orthogonal purity versus ice-ray irregularity within one straight-line language.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic` — fluid saturated colour erases both ink restraint and lattice logic.

## Don't copy

Specific masterpieces (Fan Kuan's *Travelers among Mountains and Streams*, National Palace Museum, Taipei); replicas of named gardens (Humble Administrator's Garden); I. M. Pei's Suzhou Museum (2006); historical seals with real names.

## Respect

Lattice patterns and garden principles are open, long-shared heritage. Calligraphy is not texture: never use characters you cannot read, and have any text checked by a fluent reader. Do not invent seals bearing real names. Five-clawed dragons were reserved for the emperor in Ming and Qing China; dragons and phoenixes carry meaning. Caricature is "chop-suey" faux-brush Latin fonts, red-and-gold dragons, lanterns and "Oriental" chinoiserie. Credit by dynasty and region where possible.
