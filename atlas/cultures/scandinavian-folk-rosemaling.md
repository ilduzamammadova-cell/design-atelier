# Scandinavian folk painting (rosemaling)

**Essence:** Baroque and rococo scrollwork translated by rural painters into warm, flowing ornament for the objects and rooms of everyday life.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Two-curve skeleton | Rosemaling grows from C- and S-scrolls branching from a root, with leaves and flowers budding off them; urban acanthus carving is its ancestor. | Decorative linework constructed from two primitives only (a C-curve and an S-curve), always branching from one root point at a frame corner or header centre. |
| G2 · Regional dialects | Telemark style is asymmetric and free-flowing; Hallingdal is symmetrical with bold flowers; Rogaland is symmetrical and more stylised. | One ornament system with three named "dialect" settings (asymmetric-organic, symmetric-bold, symmetric-geometric), chosen per product line or season. |
| G3 · Ornament on the edges of use | Trunks, ale bowls, cupboards, walls and ceiling beams are painted where hands rest and eyes fall; bowls often carry painted inscriptions. | Ornament only on containers and thresholds: headers, footers, card edges, empty-state frames; never under readable content. |
| G4 · Shading in one stroke | Painters load two colours on one brush so a single comma stroke carries both highlight and shadow. | Two-tone single-stroke icons and flourishes (dark edge plus light edge in one path) instead of gradients. |
| G5 · Coloured dark grounds | Ornament often sits on deep red, blue-green or blue-black grounds. | Dark themes built on warm or coloured darks (oxblood #6E1F1D, deep teal #1F4B4B) instead of neutral black, with cream ornament. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** oxblood #7A2621, deep blue #23395B, blue-green #2F5D5A, ochre #C99B3B, cream #EFE3C2, black #1E1B18. Swedish Dalarna painting adds bright reds and greens with large "kurbits" gourd-like flowers.
- **Type:** painted inscriptions in script and roman letters; for UI a warm humanist serif.
- **Form:** scrolls, tulips, rosettes, acanthus leaves, linework "veining".
- **Composition:** framed panels, central rosettes on bowls, flowing borders on walls and trunks.

## Attitude

Rural, often travelling, painters adapted court and church ornament for farmhouses, bringing colour into long dark winters. The work celebrated home, hospitality and festivity. Emigrant communities kept it alive, notably around Vesterheim in Decorah, Iowa. Ornament here is affection for ordinary objects.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `william-morris`, `arts-and-crafts` — love of hand-decorated domestic objects.
- **Productive tension with:** `scandinavian-modern` — the same region's later restraint; one painted object in a calm modern room.
- **Clashes with:** `swiss-international` — free scrolls fight the neutral grid unless strictly confined to frames.

## Don't copy

Specific painted rooms and objects in the Norsk Folkemuseum and Vesterheim collections; work of named contemporary rosemalers; the specific Dala horse designs of current Dalarna workshops.

## Respect

This is an open folk tradition; credit the region and style (e.g. "Telemark-style rosemaling"). Rosemaling has nothing to do with the Viking age; avoid "Viking" kitsch. Keep away from Norse runes and symbols that hate groups have adopted (othala, valknut, sun wheel). Sámi duodji is a distinct Indigenous tradition with its own rights and meanings; do not fold Sámi patterns into "Nordic folk". Caricature is trolls, horned helmets and "hygge" clichés.
