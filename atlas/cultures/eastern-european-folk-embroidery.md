# Eastern European folk embroidery

**Essence:** Counted-thread geometry placed at the thresholds of garment and home, where ornament marks identity, region and protection.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Counted-thread grid | Cross-stitch and counted satin stitch follow the weave of linen: every motif sits on the thread grid. | Pixel-grid ornament: motifs drawn on an exact 4px cell, scaled only by whole multiples and rendered crisp (`image-rendering: pixelated`). |
| G2 · Ornament at thresholds | Embroidery concentrates at collar, cuffs, chest opening and hem, the openings of the garment, traditionally read as protective. | Decorate entry and exit points only: onboarding start, checkout confirmation, sign-out, empty-state frames; the middle of flows stays plain. |
| G3 · Placement grammar | The Romanian ia has fixed zones: the shoulder band (altiță), a gathered band below it, and vertical rows down the sleeve; each zone has its own pattern type. | A placement map in the design system: decoration permitted only in named zones (top band, crease line, vertical guides), each with an allowed pattern type. |
| G4 · Two inks on linen | Many regional traditions use red and black on white linen; others work white on white or full polychrome. | Two-ink palette (#B3201E, #1A1A1A on linen #F4F0E6) as the default theme, with region-like variants (white-on-white for minimal mode, polychrome for festive). |
| G5 · Folded symmetry (paper-cut) | Polish wycinanki are cut from folded paper; Kurpie cuts are single-colour, Łowicz cuts layer many colours. | Illustration generator based on folds: draw half, mirror once or twice; layered flat colours stacked by size for festive variants. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** red #B3201E, black #1A1A1A, linen #F4F0E6; regional accents: ochre #C9962E, cornflower blue #3D5A99, green #3E7C4A; Hungarian Matyó embroidery in saturated reds, pinks and blues on dark ground.
- **Type:** Cyrillic and Latin; for UI a sturdy serif with Cyrillic support (PT Serif, Noto Serif).
- **Form:** rhombuses, eight-pointed stars, trees of life, birds, roses, zigzags; mirror symmetry.
- **Composition:** bands and borders, symmetrical end panels (Ukrainian rushnyk towels), vertical sleeve rows.

## Attitude

Embroidery recorded who and where a person was: village, region, marital status, occasion. Ritual towels (rushnyky) accompany weddings, funerals and icons. UNESCO's ia inscription stresses handwork and family transmission. Artists from Matisse (*La Blouse Roumaine*, 1940) to Sonia Delaunay drew on these traditions.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `susan-kare`, `sonia-delaunay`, `henri-matisse` — grid-born pixel motifs and folk colour.
- **Productive tension with:** `bauhaus` — rational sans and plain surfaces, with ornament confined to thresholds.
- **Clashes with:** `deconstructivist-type` — fractured layouts destroy the counted symmetry.

## Don't copy

Specific museum garments and rushnyky; designs of contemporary houses (e.g. Ukrainian designer Vita Kin); regional garments reproduced wholesale, such as the Bihor vest at the centre of the 2017 "Bihor Couture" campaign against a fashion house's copy.

## Respect

These are living national traditions with strong identity meaning; the vyshyvanka carries special weight in Ukraine today. Name the country and region precisely; never label Ukrainian patterns as Russian or merge all "Slavic" or "Balkan" folk into one. Ritual towels and wedding and funeral textiles are not decoration. Buy from or credit makers. Caricature is the "peasant blouse" boho look and generic folk florals stripped of place.
