# Andean textiles

**Essence:** Geometry born from thread counts, in which cloth was the highest form of wealth and woven bands and grids carried identity, status and time.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Grid of unique cells (tocapu) | Inca royal tunics carry grids of small squares, each with a different geometric figure; what they encode is still debated, but they clearly signal status and identity. | Identity matrices: a profile or project badge composed of a 3×3 grid of square cells, each filled from your own designed glyph set by data; every user gets a unique, legible combination. |
| G2 · Geometry from the weave | Warp-patterned weaves produce stepped diagonals, diamonds and mirrored motifs dictated by counted threads. | Icons and ornament on a thread grid: diagonals only as 1:1 or 1:2 steps, no curves, odd cell counts so every motif has a true centre. |
| G3 · Patterned band, plain field | Textiles alternate pattern bands (pallay) with plain coloured fields (pampa). | Page rhythm alternating dense patterned bands with solid fields at roughly 1:3; the plain fields hold the reading content. |
| G4 · Colour swap inversion | Motifs repeat with figure and ground colours exchanged (A on B, then B on A). | Alternating rows or tiles invert foreground and background colour instead of using a lighter tint. |
| G5 · Calendar woven as band | Taquile's calendar waistband sets the year's ritual and agricultural cycle as a sequence of woven panels. | Timelines as a horizontal woven strip: one motif cell per month or phase, read left to right, current period highlighted by colour inversion. |
| G6 · Natural dye spectrum | Cochineal reds, pinks and purples; indigo; plant yellows; undyed alpaca and sheep browns. | Palette tokens from those sources (below); dark neutrals are browns, never pure grey. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** cochineal red #B0193C, magenta #C2185B, indigo #233A6E, plant yellow #E2B13C, green #2F7F4F, alpaca brown #6B4A33, black #1A1715, white #F3EEE4.
- **Type:** none intrinsic; for UI a sturdy geometric sans with clear figures.
- **Form:** stepped diamonds, chevrons, interlocking hooks, birds and llamas rendered in steps.
- **Composition:** bands, stripes and grids; strong symmetry with deliberate colour shifts.

## Attitude

In the Inca state, fine cloth was made by specialists and given as gifts, tribute and offerings. Today weavers in communities such as Chinchero and Taquile (where men knit and women weave) keep technique and meaning alive, adapting to tourism. Anni Albers studied Andean weaving as the deepest school of textile structure.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `anni-albers`, `bauhaus` (weaving workshop), `navajo-weaving`, structure-first textile thinking.
- **Productive tension with:** `susan-kare`, pixel-grid icon craft meets thread-count geometry.
- **Clashes with:** `art-nouveau`, free organic curves cannot live on a thread grid.

## Don't copy

The Inca royal tunic at Dumbarton Oaks; Paracas mantles; designs identified with specific communities (Chinchero, Taquile, Jalq'a, Tarabuco); work of named contemporary weavers.

## Respect

Living Quechua and Aymara communities identify themselves through specific designs; do not lift village-specific motifs into products. Ritual cloths used in offerings and the chakana (stepped Andean cross) carry spiritual meaning and are not decoration. Buy or commission through weaver-led organisations such as the Center for Traditional Textiles of Cusco, and credit community and country. Caricature is llamas, pan pipes and "Inca" display fonts, or blending Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador and Guatemala into one "ethnic" print.
