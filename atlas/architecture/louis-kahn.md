# Louis I. Kahn

**Essence:** Rooms of pure geometry with the machinery moved out of sight, lit by indirect light, around great silent voids.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Served and servant spaces | At the Trenton Bath House (1955) and Richards Medical Research Laboratories (1957–65) the rooms people use ("served") stay clear, while stairs, ducts and utilities live in separate "servant" towers and hollow supports. | Split every screen into served zones (content) and servant zones (toolbars, filters, settings, status). Servant zones have fixed positions and their own material (a darker tone, monospace labels); they never invade the content as floating buttons or inline clutter. |
| G2 · Indirect light | At the Kimbell Art Museum (1966–72) a narrow skylight at the crown of each cycloid vault hits a perforated aluminium reflector, which spreads the daylight over the concrete so it arrives silver and even. | Highlight by lighting the container, not the item: a selected or focused element is shown by a soft tint on the surrounding surface (the "vault"), while the item itself stays unchanged. No hard drop shadows; emphasis is always reflected light. |
| G3 · Pure geometry cut at scale | Huge circles, triangles and squares punched through walls: the National Assembly in Dhaka (1962–83), the circular openings around the atrium of the Exeter Library (1965–72). | Oversized primitive cut-outs (a circle or square 40–60% of the section width) act as masks through which images or key numbers are seen. Only primitives, never freeform; one cut-out per section. |
| G4 · The room as the unit | He saw architecture beginning with the room: a defined space with its own structure and light, not a slice of open floor. | Every module (card, panel, modal) is a "room" with its own structure: its own header, its own light (tone), a clear edge. Pages are built as an aggregation of rooms rather than one endless surface. |
| G5 · Monumental void | The Salk Institute's courtyard (1959–65) is an empty travertine plaza with one thin water channel running toward the Pacific; Luis Barragán advised Kahn to leave it without trees. | Give each key page one large empty field with a single thin line (the "channel") leading the eye to the primary action or focal point. The emptiness is the hero; nothing decorative is allowed into it. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** warm concrete grey (≈ #A7A29A), travertine (≈ #DCD3C2), teak (≈ #8A5A36), lead and aluminium grey (≈ #9BA0A3), brick red (≈ #9C4A35, Dhaka and Ahmedabad), silver daylight.
- **Type:** no signature typeface; inscriptions in classical Roman capitals → free: "Cardo" or "Cormorant" for rare titles, a neutral sans for everything else.
- **Form:** massive walls with primitive-shaped openings, cycloid vaults, concrete left with formwork tie holes and pour joints visible (Salk), brick arches tied with concrete beams.
- **Composition:** symmetry and strong axes, nested squares, bands of served and servant space, central courts.

## Attitude

Kahn looked for "beginnings": what a school, a library or a laboratory essentially wants to be, before the brief and the budget. He spoke of silence and light, and took Roman and Egyptian ruins as models. Monumentality for him meant spiritual weight, not size.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `tadao-ando`, `luis-barragan` — light, silence and pure geometry; Barragán literally shaped the Salk plaza.
- **Productive tension with:** `sanaa` — heavy, defined rooms against weightless, scattered pavilions; gives a solid served/servant structure with airy content rooms.
- **Clashes with:** `psychedelic` — melting, agitated forms dissolve the solidity and silence the work depends on.

## Don't copy

The Salk plaza and water channel reproduced as a composition; the Dhaka assembly façade cut-outs; the Kimbell vault profile as a brand mark.
