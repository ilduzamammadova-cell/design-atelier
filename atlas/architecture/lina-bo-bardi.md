# Lina Bo Bardi

**Essence:** Raw, generous architecture that hands its ground floor to the public, meets art without labels first, and treats folk objects as equal to masterpieces.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The free span | MASP (1957–68) hangs its gallery block from two huge concrete frames to leave a 74 m open plaza beneath, free for markets, protests and concerts. | The ground floor is public: the first screen is genuinely usable without signing in, and the core value is reachable at once. Gated features live "upstairs". Keep a truly open area (a blank board, an open input) for the user's own use, not for marketing. |
| G2 · Glass easels | MASP's paintings stood on panes of glass set in concrete blocks, all on one open floor; labels were on the back so visitors met each work before its context. | Content first, metadata on the reverse: cards show only the work (image or text); title, author and date sit on the back (flip on tap/hover) or in a list behind the grid. All items share one plane and stay visible through each other, instead of being paginated room by room. |
| G3 · Holes, not windows | At SESC Pompéia (1977–86) irregular openings are torn into the concrete sports towers like cave mouths and closed with red sliding lattice screens. | Allow a few hand-cut, irregular masks inside a rigid system: image crops or section openings with deliberately uneven polygon edges (drawn once, not randomised on each load). Their frames and shutters take one bright red (≈ #C8312A). |
| G4 · Popular equals precious | She exhibited Brazilian folk objects and everyday tools as serious design (*A Mão do Povo Brasileiro*, MASP, 1969). | Vernacular and refined elements at equal hierarchy: user-made content, sketches or folk patterns set at the same size and in the same frame as official imagery. No visual split into "premium" and "community" tiers. |
| G5 · Adaptive reuse | SESC Pompéia kept a former factory's brick sheds and inserted new life: a river-stone "stream" across the hall floor, a fireplace, a library. | Redesign by insertion, not replacement: keep the existing product's recognisable structure and add new components that read clearly as new (a different material, the red), so long-time users keep their bearings. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** raw concrete (≈ #A19D95), signal red of MASP's frames and SESC's screens (≈ #C8312A), old brick (≈ #A0563B), river-stone grey (≈ #7E7A72), tropical planting green (≈ #3F6B3A).
- **Type:** her watercolour sketches carry handwritten notes → free: "Caveat" for annotation moments, a sturdy grotesque such as "Archivo" for text.
- **Form:** raw concrete, glass, irregular openings, cylindrical towers, concrete bridges between towers, hand-drawn watercolours.
- **Composition:** volumes lifted over open ground; free-standing objects in open halls; towers linked by bridges.

## Attitude

Bo Bardi saw architecture as social freedom and everyday life, not as monument. She took popular and vernacular culture as an equally intelligent design tradition and designed spaces for people to occupy and change, not to admire from a distance. She worked with Gio Ponti in Milan before emigrating to Brazil in 1946.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `brutalism`, `shigeru-ban` — raw materials and an architecture that serves the public.
- **Productive tension with:** `mies-van-der-rohe` — Mies's glass and free plan taken over by crowds and folk objects; gives a precise frame filled with lively, user-made content.
- **Clashes with:** `corporate-identity` — strict top-down brand control contradicts her equality of the vernacular and the official.

## Don't copy

The exact form of the glass easel as a product; the Bowl chair (1951); MASP's red frames as imagery or logo; the SESC tower windows as a mark.
