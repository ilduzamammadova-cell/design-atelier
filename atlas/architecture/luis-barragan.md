# Luis Barragán

**Essence:** Walls of pure saturated color that close out the street and turn a person inward toward light, water and silence.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Wall of saturated color | Flat planes of pink, ochre, violet and red, usually one color per wall, read as pure fields in strong sun. | One full-bleed, flat color field per section: no gradients, no textures, no overlays. A section *is* its color; content sits directly on it in one contrasting ink. At most four fields per page, and neighbouring fields differ strongly in hue. |
| G2 · Blind wall, inward life | High windowless walls face the street; life opens onto enclosed patios and gardens behind them. | Closed, inward-facing views: the opening screen has no outbound links and almost no navigation chrome beyond a small mark. The experience opens up only once you are "inside" (after the first scroll or step). Calm through enclosure. |
| G3 · Stair without a railing | At Casa Barragán a cantilevered timber stair floats off the wall with no handrail, reduced to its essence. | Controls stripped to slabs of flat color: no border, no shadow, no icon. Steppers and progress shown as floating blocks. Use only where actions are low-stakes and easily undone. |
| G4 · Tinted light | The Capuchinas chapel in Tlalpan (1952–55) fills with gold light through yellow glass; wooden shutters filter sun into the house. | Tint the whole interface's light rather than individual elements: a global color cast (e.g. warm yellow at 4–6% over every neutral) that shifts at set moments (time of day, mode). The atmosphere changes, the components don't. |
| G5 · Framed view | A single large window frames the garden like a picture, often divided by a simple cross-shaped frame. | One large image per page, held by thick flat-color margins (at least 10% of the width), with at most one cross-shaped divider. Images never float unframed. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Mexican pink (≈ #E3478F), ochre (≈ #D99A2B), lavender-violet (≈ #7A5A9C), terracotta red (≈ #B6402C), sun yellow (≈ #F2C230), cobalt (≈ #2D4E9E), lime white (≈ #F3EFE7), volcanic stone (≈ #3A3533). Approximate.
- **Type:** none; type should recede → free: "Karla" or "DM Sans", small, set directly on the color fields.
- **Form:** thick rendered walls with no mouldings, rough hand-applied plaster, lava stone, timber, still water.
- **Composition:** intersecting wall planes, enclosed courts, framed views, long horizontal pools, vertical towers (Torres de Satélite).

## Attitude

Barragán wanted architecture to be a refuge for serenity, emotion and contemplation, and turned away from the cool functionalism of his early career. He drew on Mexican vernacular, convents and haciendas, and the gardens of Ferdinand Bac. Color was not decoration but a way of giving a wall emotional weight. He received the second Pritzker Prize, in 1980.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `josef-albers`, `mark-rothko` — flat color fields as emotional, perceptual events.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international` — saturated, wordless color walls holding a strict typographic grid gives warm fields with cool, exact type.
- **Clashes with:** `glassmorphism` — blur, translucency and gradients destroy the flat, solid field that is the whole idea.

## Don't copy

The Torres de Satélite (with Mathias Goeritz); the Cuadra San Cristóbal composition of pink wall, pool and horses as an image; Casa Gilardi's red column in the pool; Armando Salas Portugal's photographs. Image and intellectual-property rights to Barragán's work are held by the Barragan Foundation in Switzerland; borrow principles, never the images.
