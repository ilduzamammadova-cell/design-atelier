# Carlo Scarpa

**Essence:** Every seam is a celebration: old and new kept visibly apart, each object given its own mount, and the joint treated as the most important part of the work.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · The joint as event | Wherever metal meets stone, new meets old, or floor meets wall, Scarpa marks the junction with a gap, an inlay or a brass connector. The seam becomes a designed detail. | Design the seams between components: a 2 to 4 px reveal (shadow gap) between header and content, an inlaid accent line where two panels meet, a small connector glyph where a card attaches to its list. Seams are tokens (`seam.gap`, `seam.inlay`, `seam.connector`) defined once and used everywhere. |
| G2 · Stepped profile | His recurring stepped (ziggurat) edge: surfaces and mouldings step back in repeated equal increments. | Hero containers end in stepped edges: a three-step offset corner (4 / 8 / 12 px) or stacked outline rings instead of a single rounded corner; headings sit on a stepped underline. Hero components only, never every card. |
| G3 · Display as ritual | At Castelvecchio, Verona, each sculpture has its own mount, angle and viewpoint; the equestrian statue of Cangrande stands on a cantilevered plinth seen from several levels of the route. | Featured content gets a bespoke "mount": its own plinth component with a distinct material, an off-axis position, and an approach (scroll sequence) that shows it from two vantage points, first glimpsed small in a list, then met large. |
| G4 · New kept distinct from old | At Castelvecchio he exposed layers of the castle's history and cut the new work in steel, concrete and plaster visibly apart from the medieval walls. | Make change visible: in revisions, migrated features and updates, old and new appear as distinct materials (a different surface tone, a gap between them) instead of being blended. Version history is a designed layer, not a red/green afterthought. |
| G5 · Water channel | Narrow channels and pools guide movement and sound (Fondazione Querini Stampalia garden; the Brion tomb). | One thin continuous channel line (1 to 2 px, accent color) runs through a page or multi-step flow, linking the steps; it bends with the layout and ends at the goal. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** polished Venetian plaster in deep red (≈ #8F2E2A) and ivory (≈ #EDE5D3), sea green (≈ #4F7F74), brass (≈ #B08D57), concrete grey (≈ #9A968E), black, gold mosaic tesserae.
- **Type:** no typeface of his own; his drawings mix pencil, colored pencil and notes → free: "Cormorant" for titles, "IBM Plex Mono" for small labels.
- **Form:** stepped profiles, brass and steel connectors, inlaid stone, murrine glass, cantilevered plinths, cut concrete, water.
- **Composition:** layered routes with multiple viewpoints; objects placed as islands; asymmetric but exactingly detailed.

## Attitude

Scarpa worked hand in hand with Venetian glassblowers, plasterers and metalworkers, and drew directly with them. For him time and history were materials, and a building was a dialogue between layers. Detail is where thought becomes physical. He admired Frank Lloyd Wright and the Vienna Secession throughout his life.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `frank-lloyd-wright`, `arts-and-crafts`, total, crafted design in which ornament comes from making.
- **Productive tension with:** `mies-van-der-rohe`, both obsess over junctions; Mies hides the joint in perfection, Scarpa celebrates it. Together: a minimal system that is expressive only at its seams.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalist-web`, its deliberate refusal of craft cancels the seam-level care Scarpa stands for.

## Don't copy

The interlocking-circles motif at the Brion tomb; the Olivetti showroom stair of floating stone slabs; the Cangrande mount; Scarpa's Venini glass series.
