# Eileen Gray

**Essence:** Furniture and rooms that move to meet the body, with instructions and jokes written directly on the surfaces they describe.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Adjust to the body | The E1027 side table (1927) has a telescoping height and an open C-shaped base that slides under a bed, so the top floats over the sleeper. | Components adapt to their context instead of having a fixed size: a sheet that slides partly over a list instead of replacing it, widgets the user can raise or lower, a "reading in bed" mode with larger type and a reachable control strip. The component goes to the user, not the reverse. |
| G2 · Words on the surface | At E-1027 she stencilled words onto walls and storage: an instruction to enter slowly in the hall, an ironic "no laughing" sign, labels for hats, pillows and pyjamas. | Microcopy printed on the surface it describes, never hidden in tooltips or a help page: the label inside an empty drawer (empty state), a caption painted on the drop zone, one dry line of wit at the threshold of a serious step. Stencil or small caps, at most one per screen. |
| G3 · Furniture as architecture | Built-in pieces that pivot, slide and fold: swivelling drawer cabinets, folding beds, the hinged lacquer Brick screen. | Panels pivot, slide and hinge from a visible edge: settings rotate out on an axis, drawers slide from the side, cards fold back. Motion reveals the mechanism (the hinge point stays visible) instead of fading things in and out. |
| G4 · Plan from daily movement | E-1027 was planned by tracing how one person moves, sleeps, reads, dresses and receives guests; each zone has its own light and degree of privacy. | Map screens by daily activities (arrive, work, rest, receive) rather than by feature list. Each zone has its own tone and privacy level, and the passage between zones is designed, not just linked. |
| G5 · Lacquer depth | Her early lacquer work, learned from Seizo Sugawara, built deep blacks from many hand-polished layers, with incised and silvered lines. | Near-black surfaces (≈ #141210) built from 3 to 4 stacked tonal layers 2 to 4% apart, with one incised metallic hairline accent (≈ #B9A27A). Depth comes from layering, never from drop shadows. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** lacquer black (≈ #151311), white walls (≈ #F3F1EC), deep navy (≈ #23345E), cork tan (≈ #B8946A), red lacquer (≈ #8E2A20), chrome.
- **Type:** stencilled capitals on walls and furniture → free: "Big Shoulders Stencil" or "Stardos Stencil" for labels, paired with a quiet sans for text.
- **Form:** tubular steel, cork, glass, celluloid, lacquer; asymmetric, adjustable pieces; nautical details (deck-chair forms, a stencilled sea chart).
- **Composition:** an open plan divided into activity zones, terraces facing the sea, storage built into partitions.

## Attitude

Gray treated a house as a living organism shaped around one person's rhythms. She distrusted the cold formulas of the avant-garde and wanted comfort, intimacy, humour and pleasure for the senses. Comfort and freedom of movement were the measure, not the architect's statement.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `alvar-aalto`, two humane modernisms that put the body before the manifesto.
- **Productive tension with:** `le-corbusier`, his standard system with her intimate, adjustable details gives a rigorous product that still bends to one person. (History note: he painted murals on her walls at E-1027 without her consent in 1938 to 39.)
- **Clashes with:** `brutalism`, monumental mass flattens her small, adjustable, personal scale.

## Don't copy

The E1027 table, Bibendum chair, Transat chair, Dragon chair and Brick screen (still produced under license); the E-1027 interior and its stencilled texts.
