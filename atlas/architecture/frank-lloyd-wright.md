# Frank Lloyd Wright

**Essence:** Space that squeezes and then opens, hugs the horizon, gathers around a central hearth, and is designed down to the last chair and pane of glass.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Compression and release | Low, dim entrances and corridors lead into tall, bright main rooms. The squeeze makes the release feel bigger than it is. | Before a large canvas or dashboard, pass through one narrow, focused step: a single 480 px column on a darker surface. Then open to full width on a light surface. The jump in width is the reward, so never land the user straight on the big view. |
| G2 · Horizontal datum | Prairie houses stress the horizon: long low roofs, deep eaves, continuous sills, and Roman brick with deeply raked horizontal joints and flush vertical ones (Robie House, 1909 to 10). | Horizontal rules run edge to edge and carry the structure; vertical dividers are suppressed and replaced by spacing. Row heights and baselines line up across columns so the eye reads long continuous lines. |
| G3 · Hearth at the center | The fireplace and chimney mass sit at the core of the plan, with rooms pinwheeling around it. | One anchor element (the primary document, feed or canvas) sits at the geometric center. All secondary panels arrange around it and are visibly lighter; no layout pushes the core to one side. |
| G4 · Nature abstracted to geometry | His art-glass "light screens" turn plants into straight leaded lines with small chips of color in mostly clear glass (Darwin D. Martin House, 1904 to 05). | Icons and illustration built from a geometric abstraction of one natural motif: straight segments at 90° and 45°, color only in small squares within a mostly empty field. Pattern lives in frames (borders, window-like panels), never as a full background. |
| G5 · Total design on one unit | Furniture, glass, lighting and textiles were drawn on the same planning module as the walls. | Icons, illustrations, charts, email templates and packaging all sit on one unit (e.g. 8 px, with motifs of 4 units). No asset enters the system unless it lands on that unit. |
| G6 · Cantilever | At Fallingwater (1936 to 39) concrete terraces reach out over the stream, anchored at one end. | One panel or image per page overhangs its container edge by one module, anchored on one side. It signals reach and confidence; more than one per page and it becomes noise. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Cherokee red (≈ #A0392B), sand ochre (≈ #C9A56B), moss green (≈ #6E7B4F), oak brown (≈ #7A5534), cream plaster (≈ #EDE3CC), clear glass with small gold and green chips.
- **Type:** his own squared Arts and Crafts lettering on drawings (the commercial "Eaglefeather" family is based on it) → free: "Josefin Sans" or "Federo" for display, a sturdy serif for text.
- **Form:** low hipped roofs, deep overhangs, cantilevered terraces, leaded art glass, patterned textile concrete blocks (1920s Los Angeles houses).
- **Composition:** cruciform and pinwheel plans around a hearth; flowing interiors; at the Guggenheim, one continuous spiral ramp.

## Attitude

Wright called his work "organic architecture": a building should grow out of its site, its materials and its use as a single whole. He held the architect responsible for the chair and the lamp as much as the roof. His Usonian houses aimed to give ordinary American families a dignified, affordable home.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `arts-and-crafts`, `alvar-aalto`, shared belief in total, human-scaled design and natural materials.
- **Productive tension with:** `swiss-international`, Prairie horizontals and warm materials inside a Swiss typographic grid give a warm editorial system.
- **Clashes with:** `brutalist-web`, raw browser defaults deny the total, crafted design that is Wright's whole point.

## Don't copy

Fallingwater's silhouette as imagery; the Tree of Life window and other art-glass patterns (licensed by the Frank Lloyd Wright Foundation); the Guggenheim spiral as a logo; Wright's name and signature, which are trademarks of the Foundation.
