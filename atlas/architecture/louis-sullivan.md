# Louis Sullivan (1856–1924)

**Essence:** Let the structure say what each part does — a plain, repeated body that rises — and spend ornament like grown foliage only where people enter and where the building ends.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Form follows function, in three zones | In "The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered" (*Lippincott's*, 1896) he gave the skyscraper a grammar: a public base, a shaft of identical office floors, and a distinct crowning attic. | Page anatomy by function: a "base" zone for entry (nav plus one primary action), a "shaft" of identical repeating rows or cards with fixed height and no per-item decoration, and a "crown" that visibly ends the page (footer with settings, legal, status) in a different surface colour. |
| G2 · Continuous verticals, recessed spandrels | The Wainwright Building (St. Louis, 1890–91, with Dankmar Adler) lets brick piers run unbroken upward while the horizontal panels between floors are set back and ornamented. | In tables and long layouts, vertical column lines dominate (1px, full height) and horizontal row separators are lighter, inset 8px from the columns. Height and continuity read first; rows read second. |
| G3 · Ornament at the thresholds | The cast-iron entrance canopies of the Schlesinger & Mayer store (Chicago, 1899–1904) and the Golden Door of the Transportation Building (1893) concentrate richness where people enter; walls above stay calm. | A decoration budget spent only at entry and edge: onboarding, the primary call to action, the hero frame and the footer crown. Working surfaces (forms, data, reading) stay plain. Audit: every ornamental asset must sit at a threshold. |
| G4 · Ornament grown from a seed | *A System of Architectural Ornament According with a Philosophy of Man's Powers* (1924, commissioned by friends via the Art Institute of Chicago) develops foliage out of simple geometric figures through a sequence of manipulations. | Generative ornament: start from one polygon, apply a fixed sequence of transforms (subdivide, rotate, swell edges into curves, branch) to make borders, dividers and empty-state art. Seed each instance with the content id so every one is unique but from the same family. |
| G5 · The jewel box | His late Midwestern banks, such as the National Farmers' Bank in Owatonna (1908), are simple brick boxes, each street front pierced by one great arched window framed by bands of rich ornament — small buildings given dignity. | For small products and single-purpose pages: one plain rectangular container, one large opening (a single hero image with a round-arched or rounded-top crop), and one band of ornament framing it. Craft signals trust, especially in finance. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** terracotta ≈ #B5603A, deep brick ≈ #8E3B2A, verdigris iron ≈ #4E7A64, gold leaf ≈ #C9A043, cream ≈ #EDE2C8, stencil green ≈ #6E8B4E.
- **Type:** the 1924 ornament book was typographically designed by Frederic W. Goudy → free: Goudy Bookletter 1911 or Sorts Mill Goudy for text; Sullivan's own drawings use fine hand lettering.
- **Form:** interlacing foliage on geometric armatures, round arches, continuous piers, cast-iron and terracotta relief.
- **Composition:** tripartite elevations; vertical bays; plain field with concentrated ornamental bands.

## Attitude

Sullivan saw the tall building as a new problem that deserved its own honest expression, not a stack of historical styles. Yet he never thought function meant bareness: ornament, grown organically from geometry, was the building's poetry. He trained the young Frank Lloyd Wright, who called him "lieber Meister" for life.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `frank-lloyd-wright`, `art-nouveau` — organic ornament and total design from the same Chicago lineage and era.
- **Productive tension with:** `mies-van-der-rohe` — Mies's bare steel grid with Sullivan's rule of ornament-only-at-thresholds gives a strict system with one rich, memorable entrance.
- **Clashes with:** `minimalism-art` — minimalism refuses ornament outright; Sullivan's whole argument is that structure and ornament grow from one seed.

## Don't copy

The plates of *A System of Architectural Ornament*; the Schlesinger & Mayer ironwork; the Golden Door; the Guaranty Building's terracotta skin; the Owatonna bank's arched windows and interior stencils. Take the three zones and the threshold rule, not the foliage.
