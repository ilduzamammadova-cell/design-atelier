# Shigeru Ban

**Essence:** Humble, cheap and recyclable parts, paper tubes, crates, fabric, engineered into dignified shelter that anyone can assemble.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Weak material made structural | Paper tubes, first used for an Alvar Aalto exhibition he designed in Tokyo in the mid-1980s, became columns, walls and roofs, up to the Japan Pavilion at Expo 2000 Hannover (with Frei Otto). | Build the premium experience from the humblest parts: system fonts, native inputs, plain HTML elements, kraft-toned surfaces, made strong by proportion and arrangement, not custom assets. Document why each "cheap" part is good enough for its load. |
| G2 · A kit anyone can build | The Paper Log House (Kobe, 1995): sand-filled beer crates as a foundation, paper-tube walls, a tent-membrane roof. Cheap, insulated, assembled by volunteers, recyclable. | A component kit small enough for non-designers: 12 or fewer components, each with a one-line assembly rule and fool-proof defaults, so a support agent or customer can build a valid page without design review. |
| G3 · Partitions that move or vanish | In the Curtain Wall House (1995) a two-storey fabric curtain is the outer wall; in the Naked House (2000) rooms are boxes on castors; his Paper Partition System gives families privacy in evacuation shelters with tubes and cloth. | Rooms on castors: users drag, resize and hide dashboard modules. Dividers are soft, a translucent "curtain" sheet that slides across to give privacy or opens for overview, and the arrangement is the user's, not fixed by the designer. |
| G4 · Temporary by intent, kept by love | The Cardboard Cathedral in Christchurch (2013) was built as a transitional church after the earthquake and stayed in use for years. | Design short-lived features (event pages, betas, seasonal tools) with the same care as core features, with an explicit teardown or upgrade path. Each component's metadata records its intended lifetime. |
| G5 · Woven structure | The Centre Pompidou-Metz (2010) is roofed by a hexagonal weave of laminated timber, where structure and pattern are the same thing. | Interlaced grids where structure is the pattern: grid lines in backgrounds or diagrams alternate over and under each other at crossings (alternating z-order), so the layout grid itself becomes the texture. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** kraft paper (≈ #C8A97E), cardboard brown (≈ #B48E5F), plywood (≈ #D9BF8F), membrane white (≈ #F2F0EA), galvanised steel grey (≈ #9EA3A6).
- **Type:** none → free: "IBM Plex Sans" or "Atkinson Hyperlegible", utilitarian and highly legible.
- **Form:** tubes, membranes, timber lattices, crates, fabric curtains, joints that can be undone.
- **Composition:** modular grids of repeated tubes; light enclosures; open plans with movable partitions.

## Attitude

Ban believes architects should serve people who have lost their homes, not only clients with money, and he has done relief work through his Voluntary Architects' Network since the 1995 Kobe earthquake. A material's strength comes from design, not from its price or status. Pritzker Prize 2014.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `lina-bo-bardi`, `alvar-aalto`, architecture as social service; humble, warm materials.
- **Productive tension with:** `mies-van-der-rohe`, Ban's Curtain Wall House answers the glass pavilion with cloth; Mies's precise modular frame built from humble parts.
- **Clashes with:** `art-deco`, luxury surface ornament denies the humility of the kit.

## Don't copy

The Paper Log House as an icon; the triangular A-frame façade of the Cardboard Cathedral; the Centre Pompidou-Metz roof form.
