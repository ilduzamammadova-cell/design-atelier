# Antoni Gaudí

**Essence:** Structure that grows like nature: curves found by gravity, skins of broken tile, and ornament that cannot be separated from what holds the building up.

## Genes

| Gene | The idea | In UI / product terms |
|---|---|---|
| G1 · Form found by gravity | For the Colònia Güell crypt (built 1908–14) he hung strings loaded with small bags of shot; the sagging web, photographed and turned upside down, gave arches and leaning columns that work purely in compression. | Let a simulation draw the curves: motion uses spring physics with mass and damping tokens; connector lines between related nodes hang as true catenaries whose sag is proportional to their "weight" (item count). No hand-tweaked beziers. |
| G2 · Trencadís | Broken tiles and crockery recomposed into a continuous skin over curved surfaces (the Park Güell serpentine bench, with Josep Maria Jujol). | A mosaic of fragments: photos, avatars or activity tiles cut into irregular pieces and fitted edge to edge to cover an irregular shape (a profile banner, an empty state), with a 2 px light grout gap between pieces. |
| G3 · Nature as structure | Tree-like branching columns in the Sagrada Família nave; bone- and shell-like forms at Casa Batlló (1904–06). | Hierarchies drawn as branching trees, not nested boxes: navigation and data structures shown as trunks that split into branches, with lines tapering from thick at the root to 1 px at the leaves. |
| G4 · Curves from straight lines | Ruled surfaces — hyperboloids, hyperbolic paraboloids, helicoids — look curved but are built from straight members. | Generate rich curved forms from straight primitives: backgrounds and illustrations drawn as families of straight lines whose envelope forms the curve (string-art logic), defined by a few parameters and crisp at any size. |
| G5 · Ornament shaped by the hand | Door handles and handrails at Casa Batlló are modelled to the grip of a hand. | Shape controls from the hand inward: touch targets at least 48 px, with organic, asymmetric forms where the finger lands (a slider thumb with a fingertip hollow, a drag handle shaped to the grip). Ornament comes from use. |

## Visual facts

- **Color:** Mediterranean ceramic — cobalt (≈ #1F4E9B), turquoise (≈ #3FA7A6), ochre (≈ #D49A2A), olive (≈ #7C8B3A), coral red (≈ #C8402F), sandstone (≈ #CDB48C), white tile shards.
- **Type:** Modernista lettering in tiles, stone and ironwork → free: "Federant" for rare display moments, "Crimson Pro" for text.
- **Form:** catenary arches, twisting columns, hyperboloid vaults, organic wrought iron, trencadís, stone carved without straight edges.
- **Composition:** almost no right angles; growth-like accumulation; strong vertical aspiration; structure, ornament and furniture fully integrated.

## Attitude

Gaudí read nature as the great model: structure follows the same logic as trees and bones, so ornament and structure are one thing. A devout Catholic, he gave more than forty years to the Sagrada Família and saw it as a collective work that would outlive him. His models, more than his drawings, were his design tools.

## Blends

- **Amplifies:** `art-nouveau`, `zaha-hadid` — organic curves; his hanging models are often cited as an ancestor of computational form-finding.
- **Productive tension with:** `dieter-rams` — a strict, quiet product system in which only the touch points are organic and hand-shaped.
- **Clashes with:** `swiss-international` — exuberant ornament against neutral, objective grids; the mix usually reads as noise rather than tension.

## Don't copy

The Sagrada Família towers and façades; the trencadís dragon at Park Güell; the Casa Batlló façade and roof; the La Pedrera chimneys. These are some of the most reproduced images in the world.
